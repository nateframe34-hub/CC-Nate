# Swipe File Deep Analysis — Origins Native + Meta Statics

> **Purpose:** Break down exactly HOW each winning ad in the swipe file works — creative + body copy synergy — so we can reverse-engineer the same mechanics for our own ads.
>
> **Key insight before we start:** Every single native/camouflage ad follows the same core architecture:
> 1. Creative stops the scroll (curiosity or pattern interrupt)
> 2. Body copy opens with a story that matches the creative's emotional setup
> 3. The story builds desire by naming a SPECIFIC pain the avatar lives with daily
> 4. A "doctor figure" or authority introduces new information/mechanism
> 5. The product is presented as the logical solution to the newly understood problem
> 6. Social proof + risk reversal close the sale
>
> The creative is the BAIT. The body copy is the HOOK, LINE, and SINKER.

### CAMOUFLAGE AD #1 — Uflora (BV after menopause)

**Creative:** Medical illustration of female reproductive anatomy (uterus cross-section). Clinical, educational-looking. NOT product-forward. Looks like a health article, not an ad.

**Body Copy Preview (visible with creative):** "I never had BV until after menopause. Now I get it every time I have sex. And it makes me feel... dirty. But I'm not dirty. I'm clean. - I wipe front to back. - I shower daily..."

**How the creative stops the scroll:**
- The medical illustration is JARRING in a Facebook feed. It looks like something from a health textbook, not an ad. This creates a "what am I looking at?" moment.
- It feels educational, not promotional — bypasses the brain's ad filter entirely.
- Women who have experienced this issue will immediately recognize the anatomy and feel a gut-level "this is about ME" reaction.
- The clinical nature lends CREDIBILITY — it looks like medical content, not marketing.

**Why someone reads the body copy after seeing the creative:**
- The medical image creates concern/curiosity: "What's wrong? What should I know?"
- It's the same impulse that makes people click health articles — fear + desire to be informed.
- The preview text "I never had BV until after menopause" IMMEDIATELY qualifies the audience. If you have this problem, you're reading.

**How the body copy builds desire and sells:**
1. **Opens with pain identification:** The first-person narrative names the EXACT shame cycle — doing everything "right" but still getting infections. This validates the reader's experience and eliminates self-blame.
2. **Escalates emotional investment:** Lists all the "clean" behaviors, then reveals they don't matter. This creates cognitive dissonance and curiosity: "If it's not my hygiene, WHAT is it?"
3. **Introduces the authority figure:** Dr. Patel provides the medical explanation (bacterial dysbiosis, Lactobacillus decline). This is the NEW INFORMATION that reframes the problem.
4. **The mechanism reveal:** The body copy introduces specific, clinical-sounding ingredients (HOWARU HN001, La-14, Cran d'Or, KSM-66). These aren't generic — they sound pharmaceutical-grade, which builds belief.
5. **Social proof from real women:** Named testimonials (DeAnna W., Deborah S., Masego B.) describing the exact same problem being solved.
6. **Timeline of results:** Day-by-day improvement journal (Day 7, Day 14, Day 21, Day 30). This is INCREDIBLY powerful because it sets realistic expectations while building anticipation.
7. **Emotional climax:** "My husband whispered, 'You're back. You're really back.'" — The sale isn't about the supplement. It's about getting your IDENTITY back.
8. **The close:** 90-day money-back guarantee, doctor-recommended, 50,000+ women, no auto-billing. Every objection is addressed.

**How the story relates to the creative:**
- The medical illustration set up the "clinical education" frame. The body copy DELIVERS on that promise with real medical terminology, named doctor, specific strains, pH levels, etc. The creative promised "medical truth" and the copy fulfills it.

**Why someone buys:**
- They feel UNDERSTOOD (shame validated)
- They have a NEW EXPLANATION for their problem (not hygiene — bacterial dysbiosis)
- They have SPECIFIC evidence (clinical strains, named doctors, real testimonials)
- The risk is eliminated (90-day guarantee)
- The emotional payoff is crystal clear (intimacy, confidence, marriage restored)

### CAMOUFLAGE AD #2 — Uflora (BV trade deal)

**Creative:** Romantic couple photo — man carrying woman in water, laughing, carefree. Looks like a relationship post or personal photo, NOT an ad.

**Body Copy Preview:** "My husband gets an orgasm. I get bacterial vaginosis. Every. Single. Time. He finishes with a smile. I finish calculating how many pairs of underwear I'll have to throw away this week."

**How the creative stops the scroll:**
- The romantic couple photo looks like a personal post or relationship content. It does NOT look like a product ad.
- Creates a "camouflage" effect — it blends with organic Facebook content. People engage because it looks like a friend's photo or a viral relationship post.
- The juxtaposition between the happy couple image and the devastating opening line ("My husband gets an orgasm. I get bacterial vaginosis.") creates a MASSIVE pattern interrupt.

**How the body copy builds desire and sells:**
1. **THE most powerful opening in the entire swipe file.** "My husband gets an orgasm. I get bacterial vaginosis. Every. Single. Time." — This is so raw, so specific, and so REAL that anyone who's experienced this will stop dead in their tracks.
2. **Vivid, visceral detail:** This copy doesn't hold back. It describes the smell, the discharge, the itch, the pain during sex with graphic specificity. This FILTERS the audience — if you don't have this problem, you leave. If you do, you're LOCKED IN because finally someone is saying what you've been living through.
3. **The Valentine's Day scene:** The emotional climax of the "before" section. Red lingerie she can't wear. Crying on the bathroom floor. "I can't do this anymore." This is masterful storytelling — specific details (La Perla lingerie, cold tile floor, ratty period underwear) make it REAL.
4. **Same mechanism reveal:** Dr. Patel, vaginal microbiome panel, Lactobacillus at 3% vs. healthy 90%. But this time it's presented through a SECOND story with different emotional texture.
5. **Different discovery path:** Reddit thread ("HOLY SH*T LADIES. 6 months infection-free after 4 years of BV hell"). This feels organic — like real social proof from a real community.
6. **Even more detailed timeline:** Day-by-day journal format with specific, believable details ("Wore white underwear. Stayed clean all day." "Started crying in the shower. Happy tears.")
7. **Emotional payoff:** "Wore the red La Perla lingerie. Booked Cabo vacation. Stopped carrying emergency underwear. Had shower sex (standing up!)." — Notice how this CLOSES THE LOOP on the Valentine's Day scene from the beginning.
8. **The PS:** "387 days since my last infection. 387 days of spontaneous sex. 387 days of white underwear. 387 days of freedom." — Specific number = believable. This isn't marketing language. This is someone who COUNTED.

**Key copywriting principle at work:** This ad is LONGER and MORE GRAPHIC than Ad #1, but it's the same product. Why? Because DIFFERENT ANGLES reach DIFFERENT SEGMENTS of the same avatar. Ad #1 approaches through the "medical validation" angle. Ad #2 approaches through the "relationship destruction" angle. Same product, same mechanism, but the emotional entry point is completely different.

### CAMOUFLAGE AD #3 — Provitalize (hip pain / medical gaslighting)

**Creative:** Woman lying in bed in pain, wearing casual clothes (pajamas/loungewear). Real-looking photo, not staged. Looks like a personal post about chronic pain.

**Body Copy Preview:** "When a man has hip pain, he immediately gets MRI scans, steroid shots, and insurance-covered rehab sessions... When a woman has hip pain after menopause? She's told to 'Just stretch more' or 'Take some Advil.'"

**How the creative stops the scroll:**
- Relatable image of a woman in visible discomfort — this is NOT a glamorous health ad. It looks like a real person sharing their pain story.
- The preview text hits a RAW NERVE for the female 50+ demographic: the gender gap in healthcare. This is an instant emotional trigger.

**How the body copy builds desire and sells:**
1. **Opens with OUTRAGE:** The gender comparison (men get MRIs, women get "try yoga") immediately validates the reader's lived experience and creates anger — an incredibly powerful motivator.
2. **Doctor journey arc:** Five doctors in 12 months. Each one dismissive. This builds rage and empathy simultaneously — the reader has probably experienced the exact same thing.
3. **The "different doctor" moment:** Dr. Parker says "This isn't in your head. I believe you." — After pages of medical gaslighting, these five words are CATHARTIC. The reader feels relief THROUGH the story.
4. **New mechanism:** Gluteal Tendinopathy — a condition the reader likely has never heard of. This is new information that reframes their pain from "aging" to "an actual diagnosable condition."
5. **The systemic critique:** "Isn't it funny how when a man can't get an erection, there are 7 FDA-approved drugs... But when a woman starts losing collagen?" — This isn't just selling a product. It's building an IDENTITY around being a woman who refuses to be dismissed.
6. **Product as vindication:** Provitalize isn't just a supplement in this story. It's the answer that the medical system REFUSED to give them.

**Why this approach is brilliant for native ads:** The creative + opening copy create a "medical injustice" narrative that feels like journalism or advocacy, NOT advertising. By the time the reader realizes it's selling something, they're emotionally invested in the outcome.

### CAMOUFLAGE AD #4 — Provitalize (GT diagnosis / doctor visit)

**Creative:** X-ray images of legs/hips with medical annotations. Looks like actual diagnostic imaging. Extremely clinical.

**Body Copy Preview:** "The most misunderstood condition affecting women over 50? Gluteal Tendinopathy. It's the breakdown of tendons in the hips and buttocks..."

**How the creative stops the scroll:**
- X-ray images look like REAL medical content. This bypasses the ad filter completely — it looks like health education.
- The annotations and measurements on the X-ray add credibility. This isn't stock photography; it looks diagnostic.

**How the body copy builds desire and sells:**
1. **Opens as education, not story:** Unlike Ads #1-3 that open with first-person stories, this one opens as an educational article: "The most misunderstood condition affecting women over 50?" — This is INFORMATION-FIRST, curiosity-driven.
2. **Different emotional entry point:** Instead of empathy (feeling the person's pain), this ad creates RECOGNITION ("OMG, is THAT what I have?")
3. **The doctor visit is different:** Dr. Smith here gives the diagnosis in the OPENING — not after pages of suffering. This gets to the mechanism faster.
4. **Uses humor:** Emoji use (rolling eyes, face palm, running emoji) and casual language ("What the f**k is going on with my hips?") creates a conversational, relatable tone.
5. **Product delivery is different:** Instead of finding the product through forums/research, the DOCTOR physically hands her a bottle. This is the highest form of authority-based selling.
6. **Transformation is physical and measurable:** "Walking without a limp. Sleeping through the night. Running long distances." These are specific, visual outcomes.
7. **Heavy social proof section:** Three named reviews (Janet G., Kim H., Jennifer S.) with specific details, ages, and outcomes. Trustpilot rating (4.3) adds third-party validation.

**Key insight:** Ads #3 and #4 sell the SAME PRODUCT (Provitalize) to the SAME AVATAR (women 50+ with hip pain) but through COMPLETELY DIFFERENT ANGLES. #3 uses rage/injustice, #4 uses education/diagnosis. This is exactly what Origins teaches about testing 3 angles per sub-avatar.

### CAMOUFLAGE AD #5 — Wilderglow (menopause hormones)

**Creative:** Multi-image collage: woman with doctor, product photos (jars of cream), lifestyle shots. The collage format looks like a blog post or health article, not an ad.

**Body Copy Preview:** "'Your Progesterone Levels Look Like a 35-Year-Old's' – My Doctor's Exact Words After Seeing My Blood Test"

**How the creative stops the scroll:**
- The collage format (multiple images) looks like a news article or health blog post. It doesn't read as "ad."
- The doctor-patient photo adds authority.
- Product shots are included but they're SMALL and unbranded — they look like something you'd find in an article, not an ad.

**How the body copy builds desire and sells:**
1. **THE headline is the proof:** "Your Progesterone Levels Look Like a 35-Year-Old's" — This is an OUTCOME that can be measured. Blood test results are undeniable.
2. **The 18-symptom list:** This is the longest "problem amplification" in the entire swipe file. Itching, mood swings, anxiety, panic attacks, night sweats, hot flashes, insomnia, depression, brain fog, weight gain, acne — it goes on and on. The repetition isn't a mistake — it's designed to make the reader think "I have 12 of these."
3. **The financial cost:** $12,000 on HRT over 4 years. This creates both outrage (the system is extracting money) and a price anchor (the product is $30/month vs. $1,500/year for HRT).
4. **The "conference overhearing" discovery:** This is a BRILLIANT copywriting device. The narrator "accidentally" overhears researchers at a conference. This makes the discovery feel organic, not marketed.
5. **The precursor vs. replacement frame:** "Synthetic hormones = forcing pre-made furniture. Diosgenin = giving a master carpenter the finest wood." This analogy is simple and memorable — it explains why previous approaches failed.
6. **12-week transformation diary:** Week-by-week format with specific, emotional milestones (husband moved back to bedroom, daughter invited her to volleyball, wore old jeans again).
7. **The doctor's silence:** The blood test results moment is the CLIMAX. The doctor's shock validates the entire story.
8. **The "why your doctor won't tell you" section:** This is aggressive but effective — it names the financial incentive for doctors to keep prescribing HRT. Creates an us-vs-them dynamic.

**This is the most sophisticated native ad in the entire swipe file.** It combines: medical authority, financial argument, systemic critique, emotional transformation, and measurable proof (blood tests). It's essentially a long-form sales letter disguised as a personal health journey.

### CAMOUFLAGE AD #6 — Bamboo Compression Sleeves (knee replacement)

**Creative:** POV photo of a man's legs on a couch — one leg in a post-surgery sleeve/bandage, casual setting. Looks like a real Facebook post from someone recovering from surgery.

**Body Copy Preview:** "After going through two knee replacements, I gotta share what made the second one so much easier."

**How the creative stops the scroll:**
- The first-person POV photo (your own legs on your own couch) is the MOST "native" looking creative in the entire swipe file. It looks EXACTLY like a real Facebook post.
- The casual setting (worn couch, mismatched socks) adds authenticity.

**How the body copy builds desire and sells:**
1. **Conversational, male voice:** "Won't sugarcoat it." "Figured what the hell." "No joke." — This reads like a real guy telling his buddies about something. The voice is VERY different from the female-targeted ads.
2. **Comparative structure:** First knee replacement (terrible) vs. second (easier). This creates a natural A/B test in the reader's mind.
3. **The authority figure is casual:** The SURGEON recommends the sleeves. But it's mentioned casually, not dramatically. This feels more real.
4. **Understatement sells:** "The pain was still there but duller. More in the background. I could actually function." — This is ANTI-hype copy. It doesn't promise miracles. It promises manageable improvement. This builds trust.
5. **Wife as social proof:** "My wife got herself a pair for her hands." — This casual endorsement is more powerful than any testimonial because it's embedded in the story.

**Key takeaway:** Sometimes the most powerful native ads are the most UNDERSTATED. This ad doesn't have a dramatic transformation, a villain doctor, or a systemic critique. It's just a guy sharing what worked. The simplicity IS the credibility.

### CAMOUFLAGE AD #7 — Frankincense Oil (chronic pain)

**Creative:** POV photo of a leg in a blue cast, casual setting (couch, colorful interior). Again, looks like a personal Facebook post.

**Body Copy Preview:** "I've battled fibromyalgia, degenerative disc, bone spurs, old back fractures, arthritis, now a broken leg 2 months ago..."

**How the creative stops the scroll:**
- Blue cast on a leg is immediately eye-catching and sympathy-inducing.
- POV angle makes it personal.
- The colorful interior (versus sterile medical setting) makes it feel like a real person's home.

**How the body copy builds desire and sells:**
1. **This is a TESTIMONIAL, not a narrative:** Unlike the other ads, this is literally a customer review that reads like a Facebook post. It's raw, unedited, and emotional.
2. **Pain credibility:** The opening lists EVERY condition this person has. This isn't some minor ache — this is someone in chronic, debilitating pain for 14 years. When THIS person says something works, it carries enormous weight.
3. **The medication journey:** Morphine, oxycodone, methadone — these are serious drugs. The reader understands the severity.
4. **The discovery moment:** "My husband's best friend told me about FRANKINCENSE." — Word-of-mouth discovery is the most trusted form of recommendation.
5. **The testing moment:** "I put about a quarter size drop in my hand... rubbed my lower back, and within 20-30 minutes... I'm not in that pain anymore!!" — Specific details (quarter size drop, 20-30 minutes) make it believable.
6. **Authenticity markers:** "Sorry this is so long, but I felt it was important to explain..." — A marketer would NEVER write this. This reads as 100% real.

**Key insight:** This is proof that you don't always need to write polished copy. Sometimes a REAL, raw testimonial is the most powerful body copy. The key is the creative must look equally real.

## ORIGINS META STATICS SWIPE — ANALYSIS

### Entertainment - Relatable (Bones Coffee)
- **Creative:** Bold white text on black: "THIS COFFEE TASTES LIKE YOU SHOULD SHUT UP UNTIL I FINISH IT."
- **Body Copy:** "What does your coffee taste like on Mondays? #bonescoffee #memes #funny #monday"
- **Why it works:** Pure engagement play. Makes you laugh, tag a friend, comment. Zero selling. The product sells through IDENTITY — "I'm the kind of person who needs this coffee." Builds reliability and community.

### Avatar Call Out (Norse Organics)
- **Creative:** "MOMS OF 15 YEAR OLDS" + real review from Kristina about her 15-year-old son's acne clearing.
- **Body Copy:** "MOMS OF TEENAGERS! This is how you should help your children with Acne"
- **Why it works:** Hyper-specific avatar callout. Not "parents" — MOMS. Not "teenagers" — 15 YEAR OLDS. The specificity makes it feel personally targeted. Review is REAL and matches the creative. Social proof landing page adds congruence.

### Indirect Avatar Call Out (FirstDay Vitamins)
- **Creative:** "TEEN ALWAYS GRUMPY?" + vitamin visual showing nutrients
- **Body Copy:** "Trouble sleeping...irritable attitude...dragging your moody teen out the door for school every morning...sound familiar?"
- **Why it works:** Calls out the PAIN (grumpy teen) not the product. Parents see themselves immediately. The reframe ("chemical imbalances from vitamin deficiencies") is new information that creates hope. Links to an advertorial — NOT a product page.

### Curiosity to Advertorial (Vitality Extracts)
- **Creative:** Detailed reflexology chart of human feet showing organ connections. Educational, curiosity-inducing.
- **Body Copy:** "Imagine Never Getting Sick Again... 3-5 drops of this essential oil can strengthen your immune system..."
- **Why it works:** The foot reflexology chart is FASCINATING — it makes you stop and study it. Links to advertorial, not product page. The curiosity gap between the chart and the body copy drives clicks.

### Relatable Hook (ComfortWear)
- **Creative:** "12-HOUR SHIFT SHOES" + bullet points of features for healthcare workers
- **Body Copy:** "I'm a nurse with plantar fasciitis. I've worn popular shoes which always ended up hurting my feet after 3 12s in a row..."
- **Why it works:** Customer-written, story-focused ad copy. The creative calls out the EXACT use case (12-hour shifts). The testimonial is from a nurse — high credibility within the target audience.

### Image With Context #1 (Living Urn)
- **Creative:** Photo of a small tree planted in a yard. Looks like a personal photo — no product, no branding.
- **Body Copy:** Heart-wrenching story about planting a tree with a loved one's ashes.
- **Why it works:** "The image makes NO SENSE here... Then you read the first headline and you're like: 'Holy shit.'" The disconnect between image and copy is the HOOK. The review can't be faked. Makes you FEEL.

### Image With Context #2 (VetSmart)
- **Creative:** Photo of an older black dog sleeping in a dog bed. Looks like a pet owner's photo.
- **Body Copy:** Story about a dog's cancer prognosis and finding a product that eliminated tumors.
- **Why it works:** Vulnerable older dog + devastating diagnosis + miraculous result. The emotional impact is enormous. Found the product "by a miracle" — organic discovery frame.

### Engagement Bait (Washable Rugs)
- **Creative:** Goat standing on a rug inside a house. Bizarre, attention-grabbing.
- **Body Copy:** "Washable rugs. Made for the whole family to enjoy."
- **Why it works:** "Why is there a goat in the house?" — EVERYONE stops to figure this out. Drives massive comments and engagement. The body copy is one line — the creative does ALL the work.

### Contradictory Static (Dog-Proof Washable Rugs)
- **Creative:** "Dog-Proof Washable Rugs" + product images + cute dog
- **Body Copy:** "Your dog's new favorite rug. Good thing it's washable!"
- **Why it works:** People KNOW dogs ruin rugs. "Dog-proof" is contradictory and curiosity-inducing. Washable Rugs = new mechanism for a familiar problem.

### Stealing Credibility (Gruns)
- **Creative:** "Ozempic's New Bestie" + army of gummy bears visual + nutritional stats
- **Body Copy:** "Are you on a weight loss journey? These 8 gummies are packed with 100% of your essential nutrients..."
- **Why it works:** STEALS credibility from Ozempic (a trusted, known mechanism). The "army" of gummy bears is visually arresting. The body copy completes the creative by explaining what the product actually does.

## UNIVERSAL PRINCIPLES EXTRACTED

### How Native/Camouflage Ads Work (The Architecture)

1. **The creative MUST look native to the platform.** Medical diagrams, personal photos, POV shots, memes — anything EXCEPT product photography or branded content. If it looks like an ad, it dies in the feed.

2. **The body copy opens in first person with a SPECIFIC, vivid pain point.** Not general — SPECIFIC. Not "I had health problems" but "I never had BV until after menopause. Now I get it every time I have sex."

3. **The story follows a THREE-ACT STRUCTURE:**
   - ACT 1: Pain + failed attempts (this is 40-60% of the copy)
   - ACT 2: Discovery of new information/mechanism via authority figure
   - ACT 3: Product solves the problem + transformation + CTA

4. **The product doesn't appear until ACT 2 or later.** The first half (or more) of the copy is PROBLEM — not solution. This is critical for native ads because it keeps the reader engaged through story, not through product interest.

5. **Every ad has an authority figure:** Doctor, specialist, researcher, surgeon, spouse-who-is-a-doctor. This person provides the NEW INFORMATION that makes the product make sense.

6. **Specific numbers and timelines build belief:** "Day 7 – No more burning." "387 days since my last infection." "$12,000 on HRT." "3% Lactobacillus." Numbers anchor the story in reality.

7. **The CTA section is heavy on risk reversal:** 90-day guarantees, no auto-billing, made in USA, FDA-registered facility. Every possible objection is addressed.

8. **Multiple angles for the same product:** Uflora has ads from the "shame/hygiene" angle (#1) AND the "relationship destruction" angle (#2). Provitalize has the "medical gaslighting" angle (#3) AND the "educational diagnosis" angle (#4). This is exactly what Origins teaches — test 3 different angles per sub-avatar.

### How Static Ads Work (The Architecture)

1. **The creative does 90% of the work.** Body copy is often just 1-3 sentences.
2. **Types that win:** Avatar callout, curiosity/pattern interrupt, engagement bait, stolen credibility, relatable humor, contradictory claim, image-with-context.
3. **Body copy is SHORT and either:** (a) a customer review/testimonial, (b) a one-line hook, or (c) a direct avatar callout.
4. **Statics work best for:** awareness, engagement, retargeting, product-aware audiences.
5. **Native ads work best for:** cold traffic, problem-aware/unaware audiences, longer sales cycles, higher AOV products.

### Creative-to-Copy Synergy Patterns (What Makes Them Work Together)

| Creative Type | Body Copy Style | Synergy Mechanism | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical illustration/diagram | First-person story with authority figure | Creative promises "education" → copy delivers medical truth | Cold traffic, problem-unaware |
| Romantic/lifestyle photo | Raw, visceral personal narrative | Creative sets emotional expectation → copy shatters it with reality | High-emotion pain points |
| POV personal photo | Casual, conversational testimonial | Creative IS the testimonial → copy just extends it | Broad reach, authenticity |
| Multi-image collage | Long-form sales letter disguised as journey | Creative looks like article → copy reads like article | Sophisticated buyers, higher AOV |
| X-ray/diagnostic | Education-first, mechanism-driven | Creative creates "what is this?" → copy names the condition | Recognition-based selling |
| Hotel/aspirational | Data-driven, optimizer-focused narrative | Creative creates contrast → copy explains why | Male audience, health-tech overlap |
| Bold text on dark/plain | Short testimonial or one-line hook | Creative IS the message → copy adds specificity | Statics, retargeting |

### Discovery Mechanism Inventory (How Characters Find the Product)

These are the different ways the winning ads introduce the product — each one feels organic in a different way:

1. **The Doctor Visit** — Authority figure diagnoses the problem and recommends the product (Ads #1, #3, #4)
2. **The 2AM Research Rabbit Hole** — Narrator finds published research late at night (can be adapted for our NMN stories)
3. **The Forum/Reddit Thread** — Narrator finds real people sharing results in an online community (Ad #2)
4. **The Conference Overhearing** — Narrator accidentally learns about the product through researchers (Ad #5)
5. **The Friend Who Looks Younger** — Social comparison triggers research (can be adapted for our reunion/friend stories)
6. **The Surgeon's Casual Recommendation** — Authority figure mentions it offhand, not as a prescription (Ad #6)
7. **The Word-of-Mouth** — Husband's friend, colleague, family member shares what worked (Ad #7)
8. **The Happy Accident** — Product discovered through an unrelated experience (Ad #8, hotel scene)
9. **The Data Discovery** — Health tracking data reveals the problem, research reveals the solution (Ad #8, Apple Watch)
