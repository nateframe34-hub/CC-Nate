If you're reading this, you've probably had this conversation with your doctor.

---

### There's a molecule your body is running out of. It's not a vitamin.

In 2013, a research team at Harvard published a study that changed how scientists think about cellular aging. They identified a molecule called NAD+ — nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide — and demonstrated that declining levels of it were directly connected to how cells function as we age.

NAD+ isn't a vitamin. It's not a mineral. It's a coenzyme — a molecule present in every single cell in your body. Your cells use it for energy production, for cellular maintenance, for DNA support. It's involved in hundreds of biological processes.

And here's the part that matters: **your body produces less of it every year after about age 40.**

Published research suggests NAD+ levels can decline by as much as 50% between your 30s and 60s. That's not a theory. That's measured. Documented. Replicated across multiple studies.

Think about what that means. The molecule your cells depend on for energy production is gradually becoming less available. Not because you're doing anything wrong. Not because your diet is bad or your sleep is off. Because your body's production of this specific molecule is declining with age.

**That's the thing nobody told you.**

Your thyroid is fine. Your iron is fine. But the fuel inside every cell — the thing mitochondria need to actually produce energy — is running lower every year.

If you're over 40 and you've tried to fix your energy, I already know the list.

None of it moved the needle.

Here's why: those supplements address the wrong layer of the problem.

B-vitamins support metabolic pathways — helpful, but not the bottleneck. Magnesium supports muscle and nerve function — important, but not the root cause. Iron supports oxygen transport — critical if you're deficient, but your labs already said you're not.

None of them address the cellular energy system itself. None of them support NAD+ production.

It's like putting premium gas in a car with a dying battery. The gas isn't the problem. The electrical system is.

### What the research says about NMN

NMN — nicotinamide mononucleotide — is the direct precursor your body uses to produce NAD+. Your cells convert NMN into NAD+ through a well-documented metabolic pathway.

This isn't fringe science. NMN has been studied in peer-reviewed, published human clinical research. The mechanism is straightforward: you provide the raw material, your cells do what they already know how to do — just with more to work with.

What makes NMN different from the supplements you've already tried:

- **It's not a stimulant.** It doesn't spike your energy and crash you two hours later. It supports the underlying system that produces sustained cellular energy.
- **It's not a vitamin stack.** You're not throwing 15 ingredients at the wall hoping something sticks. NMN targets one specific, well-researched pathway.
- **It works on a timeline.** Most people in published studies and in my practice report noticing changes around weeks 2-4 of consistent daily use. It's not instant — it's building something.

But here's what I've also learned, both from the research and from my own experience: NAD+ doesn't work in isolation.

---

### NAD+ is the foundation. But it needs support.

This is the part that most NMN brands don't talk about — probably because it's harder to put on a label.

NAD+ fuels your cells. But the systems that USE that fuel need their own support:

- **Mitochondria** — the power plants inside every cell — need CoQ10 to function efficiently. Your body produces less CoQ10 with age, just like NAD+.
- **Sirtuins** — the proteins that regulate how your cells age — are activated by resveratrol. NMN provides the fuel; resveratrol tells your cells what to do with it. This is one of the most-studied pairings in longevity research.
- **Methylation** — the process your body uses for DNA regulation and detoxification — uses methyl groups. NMN metabolism consumes methyl groups. TMG replenishes them. This is why knowledgeable NMN users pair the two.
- **Oxidative stress** — the cellular damage that accumulates with age — is managed by antioxidants like glutathione, astaxanthin, and quercetin. Supporting NAD+ without supporting the defense system is like running an engine harder without changing the oil.

When I first started researching NMN for myself, I ended up with five separate bottles on my counter. NMN from one brand. Resveratrol from another. CoQ10. Glutathione. TMG. Different doses, different schedules, $200+ a month.

It worked. I felt the difference. But the routine was unsustainable.

## Copy Notes

**What this advertorial does that the PDP can't:**

1. **Validates the reader's experience.** "Your blood work is normal. You still feel terrible." — this is the line that earns trust. Nobody on a product page says this.

2. **Explains WHY previous supplements failed.** This reframes the reader's history of failed supplements as "wrong layer, not wrong effort." It removes the objection "supplements don't work for me" without dismissing their experience.

3. **Builds the NAD+ case before mentioning any product.** By the time Baseline appears, the reader already understands the mechanism and wants a solution. The product is the answer to a question they now have — not an interruption.

4. **Uses Sarah's practitioner authority without being clinical.** "I'm trained in this. I read research papers for fun. And I still missed it." — this earns the right to educate without being preachy.

5. **Addresses the proprietary blend honestly.** "In a perfect world, every dose would be listed separately. But the primary ingredient — NMN at 500mg — is the foundation, and it's transparent." This preempts the trust break the PDP currently creates.

6. **Sets realistic expectations.** "Most people notice something around week 3-4. Some take 6-8 weeks. The people who quit at day 10 never find out." This reduces early returns AND builds credibility.

**What's intentionally NOT in this advertorial:**

- No price mentioned (that's the PDP's job)
- No subscription push (too transactional for this stage)
- No urgency/scarcity ("limited stock," "price going up") — doesn't fit the editorial voice
- No competitive brand attacks
- No before/after transformation language
- No "miracle," "revolutionary," "game-changing"
