## Format: Relatable Hook — Why This Format

The Relatable Hook format names the exact SITUATION the avatar lives in every day. The swipe file example is "12-HOUR SHIFT SHOES" — it doesn't name the product, it names the LIFE. The viewer sees their daily reality called out, self-identifies instantly, and reads the benefits to see if the product fits.

**Why this format for the 3pm Wall Hitter:**

1. **The 3pm Wall Hitter IS a situation.** This sub-avatar isn't defined by demographics (age, gender) or identity (mom, professional). They're defined by a daily experience — the afternoon energy crash. Relatable Hook is built to target situations.

2. **The swipe file match is almost 1:1.** "12-Hour Shift Shoes" targets people who work 12-hour shifts and need comfortable shoes. Our ads target people who crash at 3pm and need cellular energy support. Same format, same logic: name the situation → show the product → list the benefits.

3. **The format shows the product.** Every non-native format in the swipe file shows the product (13/13). Relatable Hook puts the product floating on the right with benefit bullets on the left. Clean, scannable, product visible — unlike our B1-B2 native ads where the product was hidden and people clicked out of curiosity instead of purchase intent.

4. **It works at Stage 2-3 market sophistication.** The 3pm Wall Hitter may be Stage 1-2 for NMN specifically — most people in this avatar have never heard of NMN or NAD+. Relatable Hook works for audiences actively seeking a solution to a known problem. They KNOW they crash. They don't know WHY or what to do about it. The headline names the problem, the bullets introduce the mechanism and product.

**Swipe file layout being copied:**
- Light/clean background
- Bold headline top (names the situation)
- Benefit bullets left side with colored accent markers
- Product floating right side
- Clean, informational, single-glance comprehension

## Sub-Avatar: The 3pm Wall Hitter

**Emotion:** Frustration, resignation ("this is just what getting older feels like")

- The 3pm crash is a DAILY pain point. Not occasional, not seasonal — every single day. Daily pain = high urgency.
- It's gender-neutral. B2 and B3 target women specifically. B4 opens up the male 40+ audience for the first time.

## Why We Think This Will Convert

1. **Product is visible.** Every non-native winner in the swipe file shows the product (13/13). Our B1-B2 ads hid the product and generated curiosity clickers, not buyers. This format shows them what they're buying.

2. **The headline names THEIR life.** Relatable Hook's power is that the viewer self-identifies before reading a single benefit. "THE 3PM WALL" / "3 COFFEES BY 2PM" — if that's your life, you can't scroll past it.

3. **Benefits are scannable.** Bullet points with the clinical dose, ingredient count, price, and guarantee — all visible in 2-3 seconds. No 500-word story required. The reader gets: what it is, why it's different, what it costs, and what the risk is, all in one glance.

4. **Price is anchored.** "$49.99 for a 60-day supply" is visible on the image. No surprise when they land on the PDP. The B1-B2 feedback loops identified "no price framing before the click" as a conversion killer.

5. **Risk reversal is visible.** "30-day money-back guarantee" on the image. Removes the "what if it doesn't work" objection before the click.

6. **Listicle pre-sell bridges the education gap.** The image and body copy are short — they grab attention and create the click. The listicle does the mechanism education (NAD+, why coffee doesn't work, why other supplements failed, why dose matters) before sending them to the PDP pre-sold.

## What We're Testing This Batch

**Primary test:** Does the 3pm Wall Hitter sub-avatar convert? We have zero data on this audience. If this batch generates ATCs or purchases, we know this sub-avatar has purchase intent, not just engagement.

**Secondary test:** Which angle resonates best with this sub-avatar?
- Naming the experience (THE 3PM WALL)
- Naming the behavior (3 COFFEES BY 2PM)
- Reframing the cause (NOTHING CHANGED. YOUR BODY DID.)

**Tertiary test:** Does the Relatable Hook format (product-visible, benefit-forward, short copy) convert better than native/camouflage (product-hidden, story-forward, long copy)? B3 and B4 run simultaneously — comparing format effectiveness.

## Learnings Applied from B1-B2

- Product visible in creative (B1C2 lesson: product-forward killed CPMs in native format, but the swipe file proves product visibility is REQUIRED in non-native formats — different rules for different formats)
- Price and guarantee visible on the image (B1-B2 had zero price framing before the click)
- Short, punchy headlines — 3-5 words max (B1-B2 had no on-image headlines)
- Benefit bullets give reasons to buy, not just reasons to click (B1-B2 generated curiosity, not purchase intent)
- "Same inputs, worse outputs" framework rescued from B1C3 (strongest insight from a killed ad, now in the right format)
- "I've tried everything" inventory from B2C3 ported into body copy (strongest trust device in the campaign)
- Listicle pre-sell for education (short-format ads can't do mechanism education — the listicle handles it)
