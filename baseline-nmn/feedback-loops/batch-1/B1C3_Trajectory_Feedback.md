## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why did CBO abandon this ad? (Ranked by confidence)

**1. The creative didn't earn initial engagement, so CBO never gave it a chance (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

CBO distributes budget based on early signals. In the first 24 hours, B1C3 got 4 impressions while B1C1 got 494 and B2C2 got 235. The early engagement signals (clicks, reactions, time-on-ad) were either absent or negative. CBO concluded this ad wouldn't perform and starved it of budget permanently.

This is a creative problem, not a copy problem. The CBO never delivered the ad to enough people to test whether the copy resonates. We literally can't evaluate the body copy because almost nobody saw it.

**2. The "data analyst tracking health metrics" angle is too niche for broad targeting (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The creative — described as a health tracking data/screenshot — appeals to a very specific subset: quantified-self biohackers who track HRV, sleep stages, and resting heart rate. That's a small audience within the already-specific 40+ demographic. When CBO tested it against broader-appeal ads (tired mom, doctor visit), the engagement signals confirmed it resonated with fewer people.

The copy opens with "I'm a data analyst. I track everything." — this immediately filters out anyone who doesn't identify as data-driven. Compare to B2C2's "My 7-year-old asked me to play catch" which is universally relatable to any parent.

**3. The copy is the longest and most technical in the batch (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

At ~435 words with terms like "heart rate variability," "deep sleep stages," and "resting heart rate," this ad requires the most domain knowledge to appreciate. The reader needs to understand what HRV and deep sleep tracking mean to care about the numbers declining. Most 40+ adults scrolling Facebook don't track these metrics and can't relate to the premise.

### What the ad DID do well (in theory — insufficient data to confirm)

- **The "inputs haven't changed, outputs are declining" framing is strong.** For people who DO track their health, this is the most intellectually honest description of age-related decline. Same diet, same exercise, same sleep — worse results. That's powerful.
- **The NAD+ decline curve mapping to personal data is a unique proof mechanism.** "The decline curve in the research mapped almost exactly onto my own tracking data" — this is the most evidence-based argument across all six ads.
- **The skeptic identity is built into the character.** "I'm still tracking. Still skeptical of most supplements." — This is good for the Reluctant Decliner avatar who distrusts supplement marketing.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes for B3-B4

### Keep
- The "same inputs, worse outputs" framework. This is a universal truth for the 40+ audience — you don't have to be a data analyst to experience it. Reframe it in everyday language: "I didn't change anything. My body did."
- The concept of measurable change as proof. People trust numbers. But the numbers need to be relatable (energy on a 1-10 scale, hours of sleep, afternoons without crashing) not technical (HRV, deep sleep stages).

### Change
- **Don't lead with the analyst identity.** "I'm a data analyst" is a filter that excludes 95% of the audience. If retesting this angle, lead with the universal experience ("something changed in my early 40s and I couldn't explain it") and reveal the tracking habit as a supporting detail, not the premise.
- **Simplify the metrics.** Instead of HRV and deep sleep stages, use metrics anyone can relate to: "My energy used to be a 7 out of 10. By 44, it was a 5. Nothing changed except my age."
- **The creative needs to be more natively social.** A health tracking screenshot looks like a tech review, not a Facebook post. If retesting, the creative should show something more universal — a person looking at their phone on the couch, a kitchen counter scene, anything that doesn't require the viewer to recognize an Oura Ring dashboard.

### Test Next
- This angle is deprioritized. The data-driven skeptic is a real sub-avatar but too narrow for broad CBO testing at this budget. If we return to it, it should be with simplified language, universal metrics, and a creative that doesn't filter the audience before they read the copy.

---

## Decision
Kill. CBO made this call on day 1. The angle is too niche for current broad targeting. Learnings (same inputs/worse outputs, measurable change) are portable to other ads.
