## PART 1: WHAT THE RESEARCH SAYS THESE ADS NEED TO DO

Before analyzing individual ads, here's what the combined research tells us makes or breaks a native/camouflage ad at $50/day:

1. **The 3-second rule** — If the creative doesn't stop the scroll in 3 seconds, Meta deprioritizes it almost immediately. Hook rate (3-sec views / impressions) above 35% = strong. Below 25% = rework immediately. This is the single most important early metric.

2. **Native feel is the #1 competitive advantage** — UGC/native ads deliver 4x higher CTR and 50% lower CPC than polished brand ads. Every creative must pass the "would a real person post this?" test.

3. **Creative IS targeting** — With Andromeda, the algorithm matches ad creative to individual users. Each ad is its own audience selector. The hook + visual naturally attract the right person. This means each creative needs to speak to ONE person so clearly that the algorithm can find more of them.

4. **Body copy length matters for native** — The Origins camouflage format uses long-form body copy (300-500+ words) because the format depends on the reader believing they're reading a real person's story. This is correct for the format. But the FIRST LINE is everything — it must be a pattern interrupt that earns the next line.

5. **At $50/day, you get ~3,400 impressions or ~70 clicks per day** — That's not much data per creative. With 3 ads per ad set and 3 ad sets, each creative gets roughly ~1,100 impressions and ~23 clicks per day. You need 7-14 days minimum before any creative has statistically meaningful data.

6. **Signal density matters more than anything at this budget** — Every dollar of data fragmentation hurts. The more consolidated the spend, the faster you learn.

#### B1C1 — "The Doctor Visit" (Lab Report)

**Creative review:**
- **Strengths:** The lab report on a kitchen table is excellent native creative. Coffee stain on the document, reading glasses nearby, glass of water — all authentic domestic details. The circled "NAD+ Coenzyme Level" with handwritten "LOW" in red pen is the scroll-stopper. This looks like something a real person would photograph and post with "just got my labs back." The 2nd option with more detailed formatting is equally strong.
- **The ALT versions** (laptop showing mitochondria diagram) are also strong but serve a different function — they're more "educational content I found" vs "my personal lab result." Both work for the angle but the lab report feels more personal and more native.
- **Scroll-stop potential:** HIGH. A medical document is inherently curiosity-triggering. People stop for health information. The red pen circling creates visual urgency.
- **Native score:** 9/10. This genuinely looks like someone photographed their kitchen table after getting concerning lab results.

**Body copy review:**
- **Opening line:** "I turned 48 and started keeping a list in my head of things that didn't work anymore." — STRONG opening. Specific age, relatable behavior, immediate identification.
- **Structure:** Classic narrative arc — pain → failed system → authority figure → mechanism → results. This is the most "Origins-standard" body copy in the batch. Well-executed.
- **Discovery mechanism:** The Doctor Visit is the strongest authority mechanism. A functional medicine practitioner who runs deeper labs is believable and aspirational (many people wish they had this doctor).
- **Results timeline:** Week-by-week progression is realistic and understated. "I stopped dreading mornings" and "stopped keeping that list in my head" are FELT outcomes, not clinical claims. This is excellent compliance-safe language that still communicates transformation.
- **Weakness:** At ~465 words, this is on the longer side. The middle section (visiting 3 doctors) could drag for some readers. The story earns its length, but every sentence in the middle needs to pull weight.
- **CTA:** "Support your baseline." — Clean, brand-consistent, understated. Works.

**Prediction:** This is the strongest ad in Batch 1. The creative-to-copy alignment is tight (lab report → doctor story → NAD+ discovery). The authority mechanism (doctor) is the most trustworthy discovery path. Likely to have the highest CTR in B1 because the lab report image has inherent curiosity pull.

## PART 3: CAMPAIGN-LEVEL PERFORMANCE PREDICTIONS

### The Math Reality

- **Budget:** $50 CAD/day CBO across 3 ad sets
- **Per ad set:** ~$16-17/day (CBO will skew toward perceived winners)

At this spend level, you will NOT have statistically significant purchase data for any individual creative after 7 days. You WILL have enough data to read directional signals: which ad sets are spending, which creatives are getting clicks, which body copies are getting engagement (comments, saves, shares).

### Predicted CBO Behavior

With CBO at $50/day across 3 ad sets, Meta will likely:
1. Test all 3 ad sets roughly equally for days 1-3
2. By day 4-5, start skewing spend toward 1-2 ad sets
3. By day 7-10, one ad set may be getting 50-70% of total spend

**The ad set that gets the most spend IS the signal.** That's Meta telling you which sub-avatar converts (or at least engages) most efficiently. Don't fight CBO's allocation — read it.

### What "Success" Looks Like at $50/Day

After 14 days (~$700 CAD spent):
- **Best case:** 1-2 purchases across the campaign, clear CTR winner among ad sets, one sub-avatar clearly outspending others via CBO
- **Realistic case:** 0-1 purchases, but clear engagement signals (CTR differences, comment quality, save rates) that indicate which sub-avatar resonates
- **Worst case:** Even spend distribution, no clear winner, low engagement across all 9 ads — which means the creative or the offer needs work, not just the targeting

You're looking for RELATIVE performance differences between batches, not absolute CPA targets.

## PART 4: STRUCTURAL & STRATEGIC CONCERNS

### 1. CBO vs ABO — Context-Dependent Choice

**The research says ABO for exiting learning phase. But that's not the goal here.**

The goal of these batches is to find the winning sub-avatar and angle — not to exit learning phase. That changes the calculus:

**CBO is the right choice for this test because:**
- CBO lets Meta's algorithm vote with spend. If Meta consistently pushes 60% of budget toward one ad set, that IS the signal — the algorithm is telling you which sub-avatar it can find efficient conversions for.
- ABO forces equal spend, which hides Meta's preference signal. You'd get equal data on all 3 sub-avatars, but you wouldn't know which one Meta WANTS to run.
- At this stage, Meta's preference signal is more valuable than controlled equal-distribution data. You're reading the algorithm's judgment, not trying to override it.

**Where ABO would be better:**
- If the goal were to exit learning phase (need ~50 conversions/week per ad set)
- If you wanted to guarantee each sub-avatar gets a fair shot regardless of early algorithmic noise
- If you were testing creative variations within a single sub-avatar (same audience, different angles)

**Caveat:** Watch for premature CBO allocation. Days 1-2 spend skews are noise, not signal. If by day 3 one ad set has <10% of spend, that may be CBO making a premature call. The real signal emerges day 5-7+. Don't make decisions until then.

**Advantage+ Shopping Campaign:** Still not recommended. ASC requires conversion data to perform well. A new account with zero purchase history gives ASC nothing to optimize against. Use a manual Sales campaign with Purchase optimization + CBO.

### 2. Two Creatives Are Missing

- **B2C3** (2AM phone screen) — Canva build not yet created
- **B3C2** (Caffeine vs Baseline infographic) — Canva build not yet created

This means B2 and B3 launch with only 2 creatives each. Meta recommends 3-4 ads per ad set at this budget level. Running 2 per ad set means:
- Less creative diversity for the algorithm to work with
- Faster fatigue on each creative (each gets more impressions)
- Less data on angle diversity (you're testing 2 angles instead of 3)

### 3. B1/B3 Audience Overlap

The batch plans flag this. B1 (Reluctant Decliner, 40+) and B3 (3pm Wall Hitter, 35+) target overlapping demographics with overlapping pain points. Under CBO, Meta may struggle to find distinct audiences for each.

This overlap means:
- B1 and B3 are partially competing for the same impressions
- CBO data on "which sub-avatar wins" is muddied between these two
- B2 (women 40-55) is the most differentiated audience

### 4. Optimization Event

The batch plans say "Purchase" optimization. The research says at $50/day, you mathematically cannot exit learning phase optimizing for purchases (need ~$286/day per ad set at $40 CPA).

Since the goal is NOT to exit learning phase but to find the winning sub-avatar and angle, Purchase optimization is actually fine here — you're using it to generate the highest-intent signals possible, not to hit the 50-event threshold. The algorithm will still learn from purchase-intent signals even without exiting learning phase.

However: if after 7 days you have ZERO add-to-carts across all 9 ads, that's a different problem (offer/landing page, not creative).

- Removed `[EDIT NOTE]` and replaced with 10-in-1 proof point: "500mg NMN plus nine supporting ingredients, everything listed on the label, nothing hidden behind a proprietary blend."

**4. Set up CAPI via Shopify before launching.**
- Native Shopify integration, Maximum data sharing, Advanced Matching ON
- 15-30 minutes. Free. Non-negotiable.

### HIGH PRIORITY (Significant Impact)

**5. Launch B1 + B2 first, B3 second. (DECIDED)**
- B1 and B3 overlap in audience. Launching all 3 simultaneously fragments $50/day across partially overlapping audiences.
