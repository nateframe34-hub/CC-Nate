## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why did this ad get clicks but no conversions? (Ranked by confidence)

**1. The ad is a complete story — it resolves the reader's curiosity before the click (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

This is the longest ad in the batch (~465 words). The reader gets the full emotional arc: three doctors, the functional medicine practitioner, the NAD+ explanation, the week-by-week results, and the resolution ("I stopped keeping that list in my head"). By the time they reach the CTA, they've already received the emotional payoff. They feel satisfied by the STORY, not compelled to buy the PRODUCT.

The click is curiosity — "let me see what this Baseline thing is" — not purchase intent. That's why 16 people landed on the PDP and 0 added to cart. They clicked to look, not to buy.

**2. The authority figure (functional medicine doctor) builds belief in NAD+, not in Baseline specifically (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The ad's strongest element — the doctor validation — creates trust in the MECHANISM (NAD+ decline is real, NMN is the precursor). But it doesn't create desire for THIS product. The doctor doesn't recommend Baseline. She says "look into NMN" and "give it 6-8 weeks." Baseline appears later as the narrator's independent choice, almost incidental: "I found Baseline because they didn't promise miracles."

This means the ad educates people about NMN and then sends them to Google/Amazon to shop for NMN — not necessarily to buy Baseline. The 16 LPVs who made it to the PDP were likely comparing Baseline against other options they'd already searched for.

**3. The CTA doesn't signal a product or offer (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"Support your baseline." — This is a brand tagline, not a purchase trigger. It doesn't tell the reader what happens when they click. Compare to "See the full formula" or "Try it risk-free for 30 days" — those set expectations for a product page. "Support your baseline" could lead to a blog post, a quiz, an about page. It creates ambiguity, not intent.

**4. CPM instability suggests creative may be borderline for camouflage (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

CPMs ranged from $45 to $101 across 4 days. The $65 average is 2.3x higher than B2C2 ($28) and 2.7x higher than B2C3 ($24). The creative — described as a cellular/medical diagram — looks more "health article" than "personal post." It passes as shareable content, but it's not as natively social as B2C2 (tired mom on couch) or B2C3 (dark phone screen at 2am). Meta's delivery system is giving it moderate reach but not scaling it.

**5. No price anchoring or offer framing in the ad (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

The reader has zero context for what Baseline costs when they click. They land on a PDP showing $49.99 and have no framework to evaluate whether that's good or bad. The ad mentions "500mg of NMN — the dose the research actually uses — plus a handful of supporting compounds" but doesn't frame the value ("10 ingredients you'd pay $200+ for separately" or "less than your afternoon coffee").

### What the ad DID do well

- **The three-doctor narrative is compelling.** "Normal for my age" repeated by two doctors, then the third doctor asking a different question — this is strong storytelling. It mirrors how the target audience actually experiences medical dismissal.
- **The "dimmer switch" metaphor is memorable.** "Someone had slowly turned the dimmer switch down on my entire body and nobody could find the dial" — this is the kind of language that makes people screenshot and share.
- **The mechanism explanation is clean.** The doctor's NAD+ explanation is the most credible mechanism presentation across all six ads. It doesn't read like ad copy — it reads like a real conversation.
- **Week-by-week timeline builds believability.** Starting with "Week 1-2: Nothing" is honest and sets realistic expectations. The gradual progression feels real.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes for B3-B4

### Keep
- The authority figure discovery mechanism (doctor/practitioner validates the cause). This angle works for the Reluctant Decliner avatar — they trust professional validation.
- The "nothing happened at first" honesty beat. This builds massive credibility.
- The three-doctor narrative structure (dismissed → dismissed → different answer). Deeply relatable for 40+ audience.

### Change
- **Don't resolve the story completely.** The character's arc should close, but the READER should be left with an open loop: "I don't know if your story looks like mine. But if you've been told 'you're fine' and you know you're not..." — the reader needs to feel like THEIR story is unresolved.
- **Name the product earlier and with more specificity.** "I found Baseline because they didn't promise miracles" is too vague. Replace with something that gives a concrete reason to click: "I found a formula called Baseline 10-in-1 — 500mg NMN plus 9 supporting ingredients. The same dose the research uses, not the 125mg most brands sell. And it replaced the 4 separate bottles I was already buying."
- **Add offer/price framing before the CTA.** "60-day supply. Less than my afternoon coffee. And a 30-day guarantee if it doesn't work." — This sets the reader's expectations so the PDP price isn't a shock.
- **Change the CTA from brand slogan to product signal.** "Support your baseline" → "See the full 10-in-1 formula" or "Try it risk-free for 30 days."
- **Consider shortening by 20%.** The ad is 465 words. The mechanism education section (doctor explaining NAD+) could be tightened — the reader doesn't need a biochemistry lesson, they need to believe the doctor and want to try the solution.

### Test Next
- Re-run the doctor/authority angle with tighter copy, unresolved reader loop, specific product bridge, and offer framing. Pair with the same creative if CPMs are workable, or test a new creative (e.g., POV of someone sitting in a doctor's waiting room, phone in hand).

## Decision
Keep running for now — CBO is still feeding it some budget and it has the second-highest LPV count. But not a winner. Copy learnings feed into B3-B4 briefs.
