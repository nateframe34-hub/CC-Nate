## What the Ad Did Well (High CTR)

### 1. The copy is the strongest in the batch
The 187 Days body copy is the most emotionally complete story across all six ads. It has:
- **Specificity that builds trust:** "187 days" is not a round number. It feels real, tracked, lived. Compare to DoctorVisit which uses vague "Week 6" and "Week 8" milestones.
- **The Reddit origin story:** The discovery mechanism (late-night Reddit rabbit hole) is deeply relatable for the 40+ research-driven audience. It mirrors how these people actually find supplements — not through ads, but through forums and peer recommendations.
- **The best "nothing happened" beat:** "Weeks 1 and 2 I felt nothing. Almost returned it." This is the most honest moment in any of the six ads. It builds massive credibility because it's what skeptics expect to hear.
- **The payoff line is perfect:** "It's not a lightning bolt. It's more like someone quietly turned the lights back up." This is the single best line across all six ads. It's poetic without being hyperbolic. It describes exactly what NMN does — subtle restoration, not dramatic transformation.

### 2. People who saw it were compelled to click
The high CTR (10.81% blended across 3 days) tells us that when this ad reached someone, it resonated hard. The copy-to-creative match worked — the POV hand-reaching-for-bottle image set up the testimonial frame, and the body copy delivered.

## What the Ad Did Poorly (High CPM)

### 1. The creative looks like a supplement ad
This is the core problem. Compare the creatives across the batch:

| Ad | Creative Description | Looks Like... |
|----|---------------------|---------------|
| B1C1 DoctorVisit | Cellular/medical diagram | Health article shared by a friend |
| **B1C2 187Days** | **Product bottle + coffee cup on counter** | **A supplement ad** |
| B1C3 Trajectory | Health tracking data/screenshot | Someone sharing their Oura Ring stats |
| B2C2 ExhaustedMom | Tired woman on couch with kids' stuff | A mom's real Facebook post |
| B2C3 2AMResearch | Dark screen with late-night search text | A relatable screenshot share |

B1C2 is the **only ad in the entire campaign where the product is the visual focus**. The NMN bottle is front and center, clearly labeled, clearly a supplement. The coffee cup and kitchen counter add some lifestyle context, but the composition leads with the product.

**This triggers Meta's ad detection at every level:**
- **Algorithmic:** Meta's image classification likely flags visible product/supplement packaging as commercial content, pushing it into more competitive (expensive) ad auction pools
- **User behavior:** When people see a product in an image while scrolling, their thumb behavior changes — even those who don't consciously register it as an ad. Lower engagement signals (scroll-past, hide, fewer reactions) tell Meta's delivery system the creative isn't resonating broadly, which restricts delivery to smaller, more expensive audience segments
- **Auction dynamics:** Ads that Meta classifies as more "commercial" compete in tighter auction pools against other supplement/health advertisers, driving CPMs up

### 2. The creative breaks camouflage
The whole batch strategy is native/camouflage — ads that look like organic social posts. Five of six ads achieve this. B1C2 doesn't.

The brief said: "POV photo — person's hand holding a supplement bottle next to their morning coffee on a kitchen counter. Casual, everyday, looks like a Facebook post someone would share."

The intent was right. But in practice, a supplement bottle with "NMN" clearly visible on the label next to a coffee cup reads as a product photo, not a personal moment. Real people don't photograph their supplements from this angle with this composition. They photograph their coffee, their view, their kid — and the supplement bottle might be in the background, incidental. Here, it's the subject.

### 3. Sparse visual environment amplifies the ad feel
The image contains: coffee cup, NMN bottle, hand, keys, glasses, mail, succulent. That's a curated countertop, not a lived-in kitchen. Compare to ExhaustedMom's brief (toys scattered, half-eaten snack, couch — chaos and reality) or 2AMResearch (dark screen, late night — pure emotion). B1C2's image is too clean, too composed, too intentional.

## The Paradox: Why High CTR + High CPM

This ad found a small, expensive-to-reach group of people who were already supplement-aware and ready to click. But Meta couldn't scale delivery because:

1. The product-forward creative limited which audiences Meta would show it to
2. Broad audiences scrolled past it (it looked like an ad), sending negative engagement signals
3. Meta responded by narrowing delivery to the tiny slice that engaged, driving CPMs through the roof
4. The CBO saw clicks happening and kept feeding it budget, creating a death spiral: more budget → same tiny audience → higher CPMs → more budget eaten

**In short:** The copy qualified the right people. The creative disqualified the ad from reaching them cheaply.

## Lessons for Future Creatives

### Keep
- The 187 Days copy structure — it's the best performing narrative arc in the batch. The specificity, the Reddit discovery, the "almost returned it" honesty beat, and the "lights back up" payoff are all worth preserving.
- The raw testimonial angle (A2) — the CTR proves the angle works. The problem was delivery, not resonance.

### Change
- **Never make the product the visual subject in a camouflage ad.** If the bottle appears at all, it should be incidental — background, partially obscured, one item among many in a messy real-life scene.
- **Reframe the creative to match the copy's discovery moment.** The copy is about a late-night Reddit session that changed someone's life. The creative should reflect THAT moment — a phone screen in bed, a laptop open on a couch at night, a kitchen counter with a laptop and coffee at midnight. Not the product itself.
- **Add visual chaos.** Real life is messy. The countertop was too clean. More clutter = more native = better camouflage.

### Test Next
- Re-run the 187 Days copy with a new creative that hides the product. Options:
  - POV of a phone showing a Reddit thread at night (matches the copy's origin story)
  - Morning kitchen counter with coffee, laptop, and the bottle barely visible in the background among other items
  - First-person couch POV with a coffee mug and phone (the "9pm Tuesday and I still have gas in the tank" opening)
- This isolates whether the copy can perform at scale when the creative doesn't sabotage delivery.
