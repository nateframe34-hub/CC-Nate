## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What this ad does RIGHT (Ranked by confidence)

**1. The hook is the best in the entire campaign (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

"My 7-year-old asked me to play catch last Tuesday. I said 'maybe after dinner.'"

This is the only hook across all six ads that creates an immediate emotional response without mentioning health, energy, aging, supplements, or science. It's a STORY. It looks like a real Facebook post. It doesn't trigger any health ad filters. And it hits the deepest identity threat for the target audience: not being present for your kids.

The CPM data proves it — $28.26 average is the lowest in the campaign (alongside B2C3 at $24.45). Meta's system sees this as organic-feeling content and delivers it broadly. The camouflage is working.

**2. CBO is voting for this ad with budget (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

B2C2 has consumed $74.10 of the total ~$215 campaign spend (34%). CBO feeds budget to the ad with the best engagement signals. This ad is winning the internal auction every day, consistently. It's not a fluke — it's 4 consecutive days of being the top performer.

**3. The parental guilt angle creates the strongest emotional stakes (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Every ad in the campaign describes fatigue. But only B2C2 ties fatigue to an IDENTITY THREAT — being a bad parent. "I keep a running count in my head. Three weeks since I said yes to the park." This isn't about feeling tired. It's about failing at the thing that matters most. That emotional weight creates a different kind of engagement — people don't just click, they NEED to find a solution.

This is likely why B2C2 is the only ad with an ATC. The reader isn't just curious about NAD+ — they're desperate for an answer to a problem that's eating at them.

**4. The Facebook group discovery mechanism is the most authentic (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"I'm in one of those private parenting groups on Facebook — the kind where moms actually say how they're doing." — This is the most believable discovery path in the campaign. The target audience IS in these groups. They've seen these posts. The mechanism mirrors their actual behavior, which makes the entire story feel real.

### What's STILL not working (Ranked by confidence)

**1. 43 LPVs and only 1 ATC = the ad isn't pre-selling the product hard enough (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The ad generates clicks. People reach the PDP. They leave. The 2.3% LPV-to-ATC rate is well below the 5-15% benchmark for supplement pages. This means the people clicking are interested in the STORY but not arriving ready to BUY.

The product section of the ad is thin: "I found Baseline because it came as a 60-day supply... Baseline gave me enough time to actually find out." That's a logistics differentiator (60-day bottle), not a desire-building differentiator. The reader doesn't leave the ad thinking "I need THIS specific product" — they leave thinking "NMN sounds interesting."

**2. The story resolves too completely (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

"He grabbed my hand on the way inside and said 'that was so fun, Mom.'" — This is beautiful writing. It's also a CLOSED emotional loop. The reader gets the payoff — the mom is present again, the kid is happy. The reader feels the warmth of that resolution vicariously. They don't need to buy anything to feel good.

The ad gives away the emotional reward for free. The product needs to be the VEHICLE to that reward, not a footnote after it's already been delivered.

**3. The CTA is a brand slogan, not a purchase trigger (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

"Function first. Everything else follows." — This is poetic and on-brand, but it tells the reader nothing about what happens when they click. It doesn't signal a product, a price, an offer, or an action. It's a sentence you'd put on a brand manifesto, not on a buy button.

**4. No price or offer framing before the click (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

The reader has zero context for cost. They click, see $49.99, and have to make a snap judgment on a brand they've never heard of. No anchor ("replaces $200+ in separate supplements"), no risk reversal ("30-day guarantee"), no accessibility framing ("less than your afternoon coffee"). The PDP has all of this — but it's below the fold, after the reader has already made their stay-or-leave decision.

**5. The 0-purchase problem could also be a PDP issue (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

1 person added to cart but didn't complete checkout. Possible reasons: checkout friction, shipping cost surprise, saw the placeholder reviews and lost trust, got distracted, wanted to compare with Amazon first. We can't diagnose this with one data point — but if the ATC-to-purchase gap persists with more ATCs, the PDP/checkout needs investigation.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes for B3-B4

### Keep — These are the campaign's best assets
- The "my kid asked me to play catch" hook pattern. Parental guilt + specific moment = unbeatable camouflage + emotional stakes.
- The parental identity threat angle. This is the sharpest emotional angle in the campaign. It works for moms AND dads 40+.
- The Facebook group discovery mechanism. Authentic, relatable, mirrors real behavior.
- The voice — raw, vulnerable, guilt-ridden but not self-pitying. "I'm not a different person... Present. Not perfect. Present."
- The CPM profile. $28 average CPMs prove the camouflage is working. Don't break what works for delivery.

### Change — Fix the conversion gap

**A. Strengthen the product bridge (the section between story and CTA):**

Current: "I found Baseline because it came as a 60-day supply."

Better: "I found a formula called Baseline — 500mg NMN plus 9 supporting ingredients in one serving. Not another vitamin I'd forget about. The full clinical dose the research is actually based on, plus the compounds that help your cells actually use it. It replaced the three bottles I was already juggling and cost less than half."

This gives the reader: product name, ingredient count, dose credibility, stack replacement value, and price anchoring — all in 3 sentences.

**B. Leave the reader's story unresolved:**

Current ending: "I have two kids who want me present. Not perfect. Present. And most days now, I can be."

Better: Keep this line (it's too good to cut) but ADD a reader-facing open loop after it: "I don't know if your story looks like mine. But if you're counting 'maybe laters' too — there might be a piece you haven't been told about yet."

The character's story resolves. The READER's hasn't. That gap is what drives purchase intent.

**C. Add offer framing before the CTA:**

Add before the CTA: "60-day supply. Less than your afternoon coffee. And if it doesn't change anything in 30 days, they refund you. No hoops."

This does three things: frames the price as accessible, sets a timeframe expectation (30 days), and removes risk (guarantee).

**D. Change the CTA:**

"Function first. Everything else follows." → "See what's in the full formula" or "Try it risk-free — 30-day guarantee"

Signal that clicking leads to a PRODUCT, not a brand philosophy.

### Test Next
1. **B2C2 V2:** Same hook, same story arc, same voice — but with stronger product bridge, open reader loop, offer framing, and product CTA. This is the highest-priority test for B3-B4.
2. **B2C2 hook variations:** Test the parental guilt hook with different specific moments: "My daughter asked me to paint with her. I said I was too tired." / "I fell asleep during my son's school concert." — Same emotional mechanism, different entry point.
3. **Dad version:** The parental guilt angle isn't gender-specific. Test a dad version: "My son asked me to shoot hoops after dinner. I said maybe tomorrow. It's been three weeks of tomorrows." — This opens a new audience segment within the same emotional framework.

---

## Decision
Keep running. This is the campaign's best performer and the foundation for B3-B4 iteration. The ad itself won't convert at scale — it needs the structural fixes above — but it's generating the data and signals we need.

The A/B test (PDP vs advertorial destination) should continue for 2-3 more days before drawing conclusions.
