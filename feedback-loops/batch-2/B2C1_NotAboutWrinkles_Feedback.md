## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why is this ad failing? (Ranked by confidence)

**1. High CTR + astronomical CPMs = same death spiral as B1C2, just slower (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The pattern is identical to B1C2 (187Days): people who see it click at a high rate (7.41% blended), but Meta can't find a broad audience to deliver it to cheaply. The $1,004 CPM on March 26 is the worst single-day CPM in the entire campaign.

This suggests the creative is triggering Meta's commercial content detection or narrowing delivery to a tiny, expensive audience segment. The ad is resonating with a SMALL group intensely, but CBO can't scale it.

**2. The "anti-aging industry" attack opening may be triggering Meta's health ad filters (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The ad opens with: "The anti-aging industry thinks I want to look younger. I don't." This is a strong hook emotionally, but it puts "anti-aging" as the first concept Meta's AI processes. Meta's health ad classification likely flags this immediately, pushing it into restricted health/beauty ad auction pools with higher CPMs.

Compare to B2C2 which opens with "My 7-year-old asked me to play catch" — zero health/beauty signals in the hook. B2C2 gets $28 CPMs. B2C1 gets $366 CPMs. The content is similar, but the framing triggers different algorithmic treatment.

**3. The copy is the longest in Batch 2 (~495 words) and the most combative in tone (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

The ad has a frustrated, sharp voice: "Normal for whom?" / "As if the problem was my skin" / "A jade roller." This tone resonates with women who feel dismissed by the wellness industry — but it also introduces friction. The reader has to process anger before getting to the solution. Compare to B2C2 which leads with guilt and vulnerability (parental identity threat), a softer emotional entry point that builds empathy faster.

**4. The "friend Liz" discovery mechanism is weaker than B2C2's Facebook group discovery (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"My friend Liz mentioned something she'd been reading about" is a secondhand recommendation with no specifics. B2C2's discovery — a mom posting in a private Facebook group, dozens of comments, one different reply — is more vivid, more specific, and more relatable (many women in this avatar ARE in those groups). The discovery moment in B2C1 doesn't carry the same weight.

**5. Same fundamental problem as all ads: story resolves, no product desire, soft CTA (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"I wasn't tracking it anymore. That was the sign." — The character's story resolves completely. The reader feels satisfied. The CTA "Function first. Everything else follows." is a brand motto, not a purchase trigger. The 4 people who made it to the PDP were curiosity clickers, same as every other ad.

### What the ad DID do well

- **The reframe from cosmetic to cellular is the batch's best strategic move.** "Every 'anti-aging' product is about wrinkles. I just want to function." — This positions Baseline in a category of one. Every competitor talks about looking younger. This ad says the problem isn't how you look, it's how you FUNCTION. That's a powerful differentiator.
- **The jade roller callout is specific and memorable.** It names the exact products this audience has been sold and dismissed. That specificity builds trust — "she understands my frustration."
- **The "both are real, but only one gets talked about" framing is excellent.** This acknowledges hormonal changes while introducing NAD+ decline as a separate, addressable issue. It's respectful and accurate — doesn't dismiss what women are going through, just adds a missing piece.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes for B3-B4

### Keep
- The cosmetic-to-cellular reframe. "Not cream. Fuel." — This is the strongest positioning line for the Invisible Woman avatar. Preserve this in future ads.
- The "both are real, but only one gets talked about" framework. This is honest, respectful, and differentiating.
- The jade roller / collagen powder callout. Specificity about failed products builds trust.

### Change
- **Remove "anti-aging" from the opening hook.** It likely triggers Meta's health ad classification. Reframe the opening around the FEELING, not the industry: "I don't want to look younger. I want to stop feeling like someone pulled the plug." — Same message, no health/beauty keyword in the hook.
- **Shorten the frustration section.** The "what the wellness industry offered me" rant is 4 sentences long (wrinkle cream, collagen powder, serums, jade roller, adaptogenic lattes). Pick the 2 strongest, cut the rest. The reader gets the point in 2 examples.
- **Same structural fixes as all ads:** unresolved reader loop, stronger product bridge, offer framing, product-signal CTA.
- **Test a softer emotional entry point.** B2C2 proves that vulnerability outperforms frustration for this audience. A version of this reframe that leads with exhaustion/loss rather than anger might deliver better.

### Test Next
- The cosmetic-to-cellular reframe is worth retesting with a cleaner hook (no "anti-aging" keyword), shorter frustration section, and the structural fixes from the batch-wide learnings. But this is lower priority than iterating on B2C2's direction, which has proven delivery and the only conversion events.

---

## Decision
Monitor for 1-2 more days. If CPMs stay above $100, kill it — it's following the B1C2 death spiral pattern. The reframe concept is valuable but needs a new execution.
