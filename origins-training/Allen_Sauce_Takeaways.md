# Allen Sauce - Dropshipping Key Takeaways

## 1. Customer Research is Non-Negotiable

- Dedicate serious time to manual customer research (84+ hours cited as a benchmark)
- It's boring by nature — the willingness to grind through it separates winners from quitters
- Research tells you how your customers talk, think, and feel so your ads resonate

### Why Customer Research Matters:
- **Language match**: If you don't sound like your customers, they tune out immediately
- **Angles**: Understanding how the problem affects their lives gives you selling angles
- **Tone & relatability**: Matching the voice and stories your avatar connects with drives conversions
- **Objection handling**: You can't overcome objections at each awareness stage without knowing what those objections actually are

---

## 2. The 5 Stages of Awareness (Eugene Schwartz Framework Applied to Ecom)

**Problem Aware → Solution Aware → Product Aware → Most Aware**

- Each stage has its own objection to overcome
- Selling to less-aware people is harder because there are more objections to clear (not just because they're "less aware")
- You must walk the customer through each stage — never skip stages

### The Bridge vs. Waterslide Analogy:
- **Bridge (bad)**: Dry, logical, feature-based copy that forces customers to climb toward the sale
  - "Our product is scientifically tested 10,000 times"
  - "Buy before they run out, low stock today"
- **Waterslide (good)**: Engaging, curiosity-driven copy that pulls customers smoothly toward the sale
  - "There's a weird reason most supplements fail before they even reach your bloodstream..."
  - CTA framed as a desire: "Get my teenage body back"
- People hate climbing bridges; people love waterslides — make your funnel feel effortless

### Product Aware Stage Example:
- Research Amazon reviews of similar products (even in adjacent categories)
- Find common complaints about the product form/delivery (e.g., "too chewy," "smells bad")
- Preemptively overcome those objections in your ad copy

---

## 3. Never Jump Awareness Stages

- Every buyer ultimately buys at the **Most Aware** stage (your PDP shows brand, product, offer)
- Sending a Problem Aware or Solution Aware ad straight to a Most Aware PDP **breaks the customer journey** — this is why many ads fail
- Use an **advertorial** to bridge the gap and properly walk customers through each stage
- This is why statics often underperform natives — it's not the format, it's the broken awareness journey

### TikTok Dropshipping Exception:
- TikTok ads that scale fast are usually Most Aware → Most Aware (e.g., "This hoodie is only $29 today!")
- Works because you're marketing to an already-aware audience and just pushing them over the edge with price

---

## 4. Ads Are Filters, Advertorials Are Funnels

- **Ad job**: Filter out your target avatar from everyone else in the first 0.5-3 seconds
- **Advertorial job**: Filter out the buyers from the viewers within that avatar
- Your headline or first line must immediately call out your avatar — if it doesn't, your targeting is broken regardless of spend
- Vague or "clever" headlines (e.g., "Marathon something something, Finish Line") fail because they don't filter

---

## 5. Sub-Avatars vs. Angles — Know the Difference

- A **sub-avatar** is a specific segment of your audience (e.g., "Menopausal BV sufferer")
- An **angle** is the reason that sub-avatar buys your product (e.g., "wants a better sex life," "wants to be intimate with husband again")
- Common mistake: creating "sub-avatars" that are actually just angles baked into the avatar definition
- Proper structure: Define the sub-avatar first, THEN test multiple angles for each sub-avatar
- You can't test an angle for an angle — keep them separate

---

## 6. Don't Over-Rely on AI for Ad Copy

- AI-generated headlines tend to be vague and don't properly filter your audience
- Always check: does the first line of your ad make your avatar immediately know "this is about me"?
- AI is a tool, not a replacement for deep customer understanding

---

## 7. Fear of Loss > Desire for Gain (Mindset & Ad Copy)

### Applied to Mindset:
- "Desire of magnitude" determines success — wanting fancy cars will never match the drive of someone who *needs* to succeed
- Comfort kills drive — living rent-free with no real stakes leads to half-effort and wasted years
- The cycle of lose money → go back to comfort → make more → waste it again traps most people
- Third-world entrepreneurs often outperform because they have everything to lose
- The mental reframe: stop craving success, start fearing the consequences of failure

### Applied to Ads & Funnels:
- Frame your messaging around what the customer stands to lose, not just what they'll gain
- Loss aversion is a more powerful psychological lever than aspiration
- This principle should permeate your entire sales funnel, not just your mindset

---

## 8. Hard Work is the Only Remaining Edge

- There are limited "secrets" in ecom — eventually everyone knows the same tactics
- The real differentiator is simply outworking everyone else
- If you're not willing to do the boring, tedious research work, you lack the drive to succeed in this space
