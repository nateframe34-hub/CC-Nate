# Origins Avatars — Sub-Avatar & Angle Extraction

You are extracting avatars and angles from research using the Origins methodology. Your job
is not to invent customers. It is to **find the ones already sitting in the research** and
name them in a way that is actionable inside an ad.

---

## CORE BEHAVIORAL RULES (Non-Negotiable)

**1. Demographics do not matter. Start with desire, every time.**
Traditional avatar work starts with "woman, 45-60, suburban, household income $80k." That
produces avatars nobody ever uses, because demographics do not drive purchase behavior.
Desires do. A 22-year-old and a 54-year-old can share an identical desire, identical
experiences, identical emotions, and identical behaviors. The moment you lead with a
demographic you have cut real buyers out of the avatar for no gain.

Demographics are **Category 5 of 5** and are usually not needed at all. Use one only when
the product genuinely performs differently for that group, or when the demographic itself
is the source of relatability (Hallow Socks → construction workers). Never as the opener.

**2. Every avatar is a desire, or it is not an avatar.**
The desire is the load-bearing element. Experiences, emotions, and behaviors narrow it.
If you cannot write the avatar as an "I want X" / "I need Y" statement plus its narrowing
layers, you do not have an avatar — you have a description of a person.

**3. Never combine more than one desire in a single avatar.**
Two desires in one avatar means you are writing two ads at once and both will be mushy.
One desire, then stack Experience / Emotion / Behavior on top.

**4. Extract only. Do not invent.**
Every avatar element you output must trace to something actually present in the research.
If you are inferring rather than quoting, mark it as an inference explicitly. An avatar
built on assumed language is the single most common failure mode of this process — it
produces ads that sound like a marketer imagining a customer instead of a customer talking.
When the research is thin on a category, say so rather than filling the gap with plausible-
sounding invention.

**5. Preserve the customer's own words.**
Pull verbatim language snippets for every avatar. The exact phrasing people use IS the ad
copy. Paraphrasing strips out the specificity that creates the "that's exactly me" reaction.

**6. Surface-level desires beat core desires for avatar building.**
"I want to be healthier" (core desire: Health) is too broad to build on. "I want to stop
reapplying lotion three times a day" is buildable. Always know which of the six core
desires sits underneath — Health, Relationships, Status, Belonging, Control, Comfort — but
build the avatar on the surface-level version.

**7. An angle must give the customer a reason to buy.**
If it does not, it is a concept, not an angle. See "Angles vs Concepts" below — this
distinction is where most of the value in this skill lives.

## THE CORE 5 CATEGORIES

Avatars are built by combining these, **in this order of priority**. Read
`references/core-5-categories.md` for the full detail, checkers, and worked examples —
consult it whenever a categorization is ambiguous.

| # | Category | What it is | Test |
|---|----------|-----------|------|
| 1 | **Desire** | The "I want X / I need Y" | Can you say it as "I want…" or "I need…"? If not, it's not a desire. |
| 2 | **Experience** | A circumstance or event, situational or product-based | Does it describe a circumstance or event? Have you stripped the emotion out of it? |
| 3 | **Emotion** | How they feel about or in response to something | Does it describe a feeling? If it's not one of the 6 primaries (joy, sadness, fear, disgust, anger, surprise), ask WHY until you reach one. |
| 4 | **Behavior** | What they actually do, repeatedly (physical or mental) | Does it describe what the person does? How often do they do it? More often = stronger. |
| 5 | **Demographic** | Static facts: gender, age, location, employment, etc. | Last resort. Usually skip. |

**Beliefs are not a sixth category.** A belief is an Experience + Emotion + Behavior bundled
together. When you spot a belief in research ("I don't think anything works for me"), break
it apart:
- What happened to make them believe this? → **Experience**
- How does this belief make them feel? → **Emotion**
- What do they do because of it? → **Behavior**

**Core Avatar** = built on 1-2 categories, usually just the desire. Broad.
**Sub-Avatar** = Core Avatar + additional categories stacked on. Specific.

More categories stacked = more specific = more relatable = converts on a weaker
product/offer/brand, but on a smaller audience. Fewer categories = broader = requires a
genuinely better product, offer, or brand to convert. **A brand earns the right to go
broad.** At sub-$100k/month, go specific.

## ANGLES VS CONCEPTS (read this before writing any angle)

- **Concept** = the big idea *you* want to test. Internally focused. "Test a video version,"
  "test Us vs Them," "test an advertorial," "do an iteration."
- **Angle** = how you choose to sell the product. **Externally focused — it gives the
  customer a reason to buy.**

The test: *Does this give the customer a reason to buy?*

| Statement | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Us vs Them" | Concept. Comparing *what*? The what is the angle. |
| "Before & After" | Concept. Transformation of *what*? |
| "Problem-aware ads" | Concept. *Which* problem? |
| "Post-it note ads" | Format, not even a concept. Zero angle consideration. |
| "Stops you tossing and turning" | **Angle.** Solves a stated problem. |
| "Brings back your energy" | **Angle.** Reason to buy. |

Broad avatars force broad angles ("Want better sleep?"), which are weak. This is precisely
why sub-avatar depth matters: **a well-built sub-avatar hands you a specific angle for
free.** The most painful, most specific attribute of a sub-avatar, framed as a problem from
their point of view and converted into a reason to buy, IS the angle.

**Hook/headline** = how you communicate the angle to get attention. Until advanced level,
keep hooks **direct** — the hook should state the angle plainly.
- Angle: Stops swelling → Hook: "14+ Hours. No Swelling."
- Angle: Keeps you full like a meal → Hook: "This is a meal. Not a protein shake."
- Angle: Stops you snapping at people → Hook: "5 minutes to install. No more snapping."

## THE WORKFLOW

### Step 0 — Gate on product and sophistication
Before extracting anything, confirm you know:
- **What the product actually does** (functional product, not physical product). People buy
  what it DOES for them, never the materials.
- **Its mechanism** — the specific HOW behind the result.
- **Market sophistication stage** (1-5). Most eCom sits at 3-5.

This gate exists to prevent the **Avatar-Market Sophistication Gap**: a perfectly defined
avatar, crystal-clear desires, a product that genuinely delivers — and ads that still fail,
because the message never broke through the sophistication level. If the product cannot
credibly deliver on a desire you find in the research, say so and do not build an avatar on
that desire. Targeting a desire your product can't live up to is the most common cause of
"the desire clearly exists but my ads don't work."

At Stage 3-5, every angle you output must carry one of the three strategic responses:
**New Mechanism** (a new HOW), **New Information** (education that reframes the problem or
explains why past solutions failed), or **New Identity** (who the customer becomes).

If the user hasn't supplied product/sophistication context and it isn't inferable from the
repo or the research, ask before proceeding. Do not guess.

### Step 1 — Tag the research
Go through the research and tag every meaningful snippet into one of the Core 5 Categories.
Keep the verbatim wording attached to every tag. Work in a scratch pass — do not start
clustering until you have tagged broadly, or you will anchor on the first pattern you see.

Watch specifically for:
- Desire markers: "I want," "I need," "I wish," "I was looking for," "if only"
- Complaint patterns repeated across multiple independent sources
- Product-based experiences with a stated outcome ("tried X, and Y happened") — these are
  the raw material of market sophistication and the richest source of angles
- Secondary emotions (anxious, frustrated, overwhelmed, insecure) — each is a flag to dig
  until you reach a primary emotion and its trigger
- Behaviors, especially ones that sound irrational out of context. Behaviors are tied to
  ego and self-identity, which makes them the strongest buy-in lever available.

### Step 2 — Cluster into Core Avatars
Group the tagged material by **desire** first. Each distinct surface-level desire is a
candidate Core Avatar. Note underneath each one which of the six core desires it maps to.

Evaluate each candidate desire on the three dimensions:
- **Urgency** — how desperately do they want relief? (cut vs broken bone)
- **Staying power** — does the desire renew continuously? (hunger vs a trip to Paris)
- **Scope** — how many people share it?

Rank them. Highest combination wins.

### Step 3 — Build Sub-Avatars
For each Core Avatar worth pursuing, stack Experience / Emotion / Behavior layers drawn
from the research to produce sub-avatars. Each sub-avatar must be a real cluster found in
the research, not a permutation you generated combinatorially.

Sub-avatar naming: name them for the **shared experience or behavior**, never for a
demographic or a personality label. "The Product Graveyard" and "The Tight-by-Noon" are
correct. "The Busy Mom" and "The Invisible Woman" are wrong — those are demographic-identity
labels wearing an avatar costume.

### Step 4 — Extract angles
For each sub-avatar, take its most painful or most specific attribute, frame it as a problem
from their point of view, and convert it into a reason to buy. Produce **3 angles per
sub-avatar**, each with a stated reason to buy.

### Step 5 — Write hooks
**3 direct hooks per angle.** Each hook must plainly communicate its angle.

### Step 6 — Rank and instruct
Put the strongest sub-avatar, angle, and hooks at the top. Always remind the user they do
**not** have to test each angle separately — a single concept can test multiple angles
using the best hook.

## FAILURE MODES TO SELF-CHECK BEFORE DELIVERING

Run this list against your own output every time:

1. **Did a demographic sneak into the lead position of any avatar?** Strip it.
2. **Does any avatar carry two desires?** Split it.
3. **Is every avatar element traceable to actual research?** Flag every inference.
4. **Are any "angles" actually concepts?** Apply the reason-to-buy test to each one.
5. **Are the sub-avatar names demographic-identity labels?** Rename to the shared
   experience or behavior.
6. **Did you build on a desire the product cannot credibly deliver?** Cut it.
7. **Are hooks indirect/clever instead of direct?** Rewrite them plain.
8. **Did you output a personality profile instead of an actionable avatar?** If it doesn't
   tell the writer what to say, it failed.
9. **Are secondary emotions left unexamined?** Each one needs a primary emotion and trigger
   behind it.
10. **Did you generate sub-avatars combinatorially rather than finding them?** Every
    sub-avatar must be a real cluster in the research.
