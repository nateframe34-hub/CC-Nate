# Phase 1 — Research (Before You Write a Single Word)

## The Prime Research Rule

Most ads fail not because of bad copy, but because the writer is guessing. Know the person, not just the product. Skip this phase and the copy will "sound like ads" no matter how well-crafted.

## The 6 Diagnosis Questions

Answer these before writing anything. Pull answers from the avatar research files. Do not make them up.

1. **Who is this for exactly?** Not demographics — psychographics. What moment are they in (noticing, frustrated, resigned)? What are they doing when they see the ad?

2. **What have they already tried?** List specifics: products, doctors, advice. These become credibility markers when mentioned in the copy.

3. **What are they emotionally tired of?** Not tired of the problem — tired of what specifically. Being dismissed? Failed solutions? Feeling like a failure? Being told it's "just aging" or "just stress"?

4. **What do they believe is the cause?** Their theory may be wrong, but they hold it. The copy must either work with this belief or shift it.

5. **What do they believe is NOT the cause?** The belief they're missing is the opening. This is where the new mechanism lives.

6. **What are they afraid to try next?** More wasted money? More crushed hope? Looking stupid for believing another ad? The copy should name and defuse this fear.

## Awareness Levels

Native ads only target three levels. Unaware = too expensive to educate. Most Aware = already buying from retention channels.

- **Problem Aware:** Feels something is wrong, doesn't fully understand why. Not searching for solutions yet. Lead with PAIN. Show you understand.
- **Solution Aware:** Knows solutions exist, tried some, been disappointed. Comparing options. Lead with WHY OTHER THINGS FAILED. Show you're different.
- **Product Aware:** Knows your product specifically. Lead with PROOF and URGENCY. Give a reason to act now.

Match the hook to the awareness level. A Solution Aware hook fed to a Problem Aware audience reads as confusing or pushy.

## Believability Quadrant

Ask: (1) do they believe in this category? (2) does the mechanism align with their existing beliefs?

- **Easy Sell:** Believes in category + mechanism aligns. Minimal belief shift needed.
- **Education Sell:** Doesn't believe in category + mechanism aligns. Lead with teaching.
- **Reframe Sell:** Believes in category + mechanism conflicts. Their job is to show why what they're already doing is the wrong version of right.
- **Hard Sell:** Doesn't believe + mechanism conflicts. Hardest. Needs Authority character + extensive proof.

## Dominant Emotion (The 2am Thought)

Surface pain is what they'd say out loud. Real pain is what they think at 2am. The ad speaks to the real pain.

Find it by asking:
- What relationships does this affect? (Deeper than identity alone.)
- What situations make it spike? (Specific moments — getting tagged in a photo, foundation cracking, a comment from their partner.)
- What do they feel underneath? (Shame, grief, invisibility, loss of control, fear.)

Name the real pain. Match the emotional level. That's what makes them stop scrolling.

## Audience Language

Pull 15–20 verbatim phrases from real customers. Reddit comments, Amazon reviews, Facebook groups.

Look for:
- Phrases that repeat across multiple people (signal, not noise)
- Exact words they use for the problem (not paraphrased)
- How they describe failed solutions
- Emotions underneath the complaints

Use these phrases directly in the copy. Do not "improve" them into marketing speak. The exact words running through their head are the words that make them feel seen.

## Gender Psychology

- **Women:** See the problem through relationships and identity.
- **Men:** See the problem through status and capability.

Both need specificity, proof, and real pain. Frame differently depending on who's reading.
