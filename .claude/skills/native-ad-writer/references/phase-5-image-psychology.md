# Phase 5 — Image Psychology (Nano Banana Pro Prompts)

The image must stop the scroll BEFORE the reader reads a word of copy. Polished beauty photography gets scrolled past. Documentation gets studied. Create prompts that look like documentation, not marketing.

## The Prime Question

Before generating any image prompt, ask:

> **"If someone experiencing this problem grabbed their phone right now to document their reality — what would they actually photograph?"**

NOT: "What would look good?"
NOT: "What would tell the story clearly?"

What would THEY capture in a private, frustrated, or desperate moment? That's the image.

## The Three Image Types

Pick ONE. Don't mix them. One gut punch per image.

### Type 1: The Object
**The thing they're staring at daily.** The object of their frustration. The physical thing that reminds them the problem isn't solved.

Examples:
- Pill bottle they hate taking
- Scale showing weight that won't move
- Drawer full of failed skincare products
- Cluttered bathroom counter with half-used creams
- The single product that finally worked, sitting alone

When to use: When the copy's "fuck that's me" moment is about an OBJECT or a collection of objects.

### Type 2: The Evidence
**The private proof the problem is real.** What they'd show a doctor, partner, or friend to say "look, I'm not making this up."

Examples:
- Close-up of dry/thirsty skin on a cheek
- Cracked hands in harsh winter light
- Bloodshot eyes in the bathroom mirror
- Ingredient list on the back of a product, finger pointing at it
- Medical/anatomy diagram showing the mechanism

When to use: When the reader needs to SEE the evidence of the problem to believe it exists. Physical symptoms. Mechanism diagrams. Dismissed-problem documentation.

### Type 3: The Isolation
**The moment they're alone with it.** Not performing emotion — just existing in the reality of dealing with it.

Examples:
- Woman at 11pm in her bed, phone lighting her face, researching her own problem
- Empty bathroom counter at 6am before the day starts
- Empty bedroom after everyone else is asleep
- Feet on a bathroom floor, towel on the counter
- Kitchen table at dawn with a coffee mug

When to use: When the copy's "fuck that's me" moment is about FEELING ALONE or DISMISSED. The isolation IS the point.

## The POV Rule

Shoot from THEIR perspective, not an observer's.

- **First-person:** Looking down at own hands, own body, what they see in a mirror
- **Observer:** Looking AT a woman from across the room

First-person = I'm in this moment = this is me
Observer = I'm watching someone else's problem = not my problem

Default to first-person unless there's a specific reason to break it.

## The Reddit Caption Test

Before finalizing any image prompt, ask: **"Would this image make sense posted to Reddit with 'dealing with this shit again' as the caption?"**

- If YES → native, keep it
- If NO → too polished, too staged, too much production value → rewrite

## Prompt Language — What to Include

Use words that anchor the AI to documentation style:
- "candid phone photograph"
- "iPhone quality"
- "documentary-style"
- "unretouched"
- "real bathroom lighting"
- "slightly off-center framing"
- "not styled"
- "not staged"
- "looks like a reference photo"
- "extreme macro close-up" (for evidence shots)
- "medical illustration" (for anatomy diagrams)

## Prompt Language — What to AVOID

Never use these words. They push the AI toward beauty photography:
- "beauty photography"
- "editorial"
- "studio lighting"
- "professional"
- "8K"
- "f/2.8"
- "shallow depth of field"
- "magazine shoot"
- "cinematic"
- "dramatic lighting"
- "golden hour" (unless documenting a specific moment)

## Common Image Mistakes

1. **Showing the solution instead of the problem moment** — No. Show the problem. The product photo doesn't belong in the ad image for most native ads.
2. **Making it pretty** — No. Real life isn't pretty. Harsh lighting. Messy spaces. Unflattering angles.
3. **Adding text or labels** — No. The copy explains. The image just shows.
4. **Faces performing emotion** — Usually no. Performed sadness looks like acting. Physical reality works better. Show the body part where the problem lives, not a face trying to feel the problem.
5. **Stock-photo composition** — No. Think phone camera. Think quick documentation. "I need to show someone what I'm dealing with."

## Don't Over-Exaggerate the Problem

For skin/body/health images: Normal dryness, not a medical condition. Normal frustration, not crisis. The reader should think "that's my skin" — NOT "thank god my skin isn't THAT bad."

Words to avoid in problem-state prompts:
- "cracked" (unless actually cracked in reality)
- "flaking heavily"
- "severe"
- "inflamed"
- "medical condition"
- "worst-case"
- "dermatology textbook"

Words to include for appropriate dial:
- "normal everyday"
- "the kind a real woman notices in the mirror"
- "dull and tight but not damaged"
- "end-of-winter skin"
- "late 40s, healthy but thirsty"

## Default Aspect Ratio

**4:5 (1080 × 1350)** for Meta feed. Use this unless the user specifies otherwise.

## Self-Check Before Delivering the Prompt

1. Is this image showing ONE thing (object, evidence, or isolation)?
2. Would the target audience actually take this photo themselves?
3. Can I look at it for 1 second and know what problem this is about?
4. Does it pass the Reddit caption test?
5. Is the problem visual appropriately dialed (not too extreme)?

If any answer is no, rewrite the prompt.
