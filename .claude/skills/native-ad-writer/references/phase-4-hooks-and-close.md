# Phase 4 — Hooks and Close

The hook gets them in. The close gets them to act. Everything in between only matters if these two moments land.

## What a Hook Does

**The one job:** Create enough curiosity or tension that the reader HAS to keep reading.

A good hook does at least one of:
- Creates an open loop (unanswered question)
- Creates recognition ("that's me")
- Creates fear or urgency
- Creates a pattern interrupt (breaks scroll rhythm)
- Signals authority or confession (unusual voice worth listening to)

A hook does NOT need to:
- Mention the product
- Introduce the solution
- Hint at what the ad is selling

Its only job is to make scrolling past feel like leaving something unfinished.

## Hook Formats

Pick ONE format per ad. Match it to awareness level.

1. **Unfinished story** — "Last month, a woman came in to my clinic..." (classic open loop)
2. **Direct threat** — "Your body is a ticking time bomb. And no one is going to warn you." (fear + pattern interrupt)
3. **Confession opener** — "I'm not supposed to be writing this. I'm going to anyway." (authority unusual voice)
4. **Scene opener** — "I was standing in my bathroom, putting on foundation. It cracked." (pulls into moment)
5. **Failure inventory** — "Retinol. HA. Ceramides. Vitamin C. None of them." (solution-aware recognition)
6. **Statistic shock** — "After 40, your body produces 60% less of something your skin needs." (new information)
7. **Skeptic framing** — "I thought this was insane. I was wrong." (overcomes category skepticism)
8. **External dialogue** — "My husband looked at me and said 'your skin looks different.' He never notices anything." (our proven winner)
9. **Pattern interrupt claim** — "Your moisturizer isn't failing. Your skin is." (reframe)
10. **Time reversal** — "Three months ago, I stopped using moisturizer. On purpose." (curiosity via opposite action)
11. **Identity callout** — "If your skin changed in your 40s and nobody told you why, read this."
12. **Unexpected reversal** — "I used to have oily skin. I'd give anything for that problem back."

## Matching Hooks to Awareness Levels

- **Problem Aware:** Lead with PAIN or FEAR. Show you understand. Best formats: Scene opener, identity callout, direct threat, statistic shock.
- **Solution Aware:** Lead with WHY OTHER THINGS FAILED. Show you're different. Best formats: Failure inventory, pattern interrupt claim, unexpected reversal, skeptic framing, external dialogue.
- **Product Aware:** Lead with PROOF or URGENCY. Give reason to act now. Best formats: Statistic shock, unfinished customer story, confession opener.

Wrong awareness-level match = they scroll past.

## What a Close Does

**The one job:** Transition from story to sale WITHOUT breaking native feel.

Done wrong: Reader enjoys the story, senses the pitch coming, leaves.
Done right: Clicking feels like a natural next step. Not a sale. A decision they already made in their mind.

**Critical rule:** The close must connect back to the hook and theme.
- Hook opened a loop → Close resolves it
- Hook raised a fear → Close offers the path out
- Hook promised transformation → Close shows how to get it

## Close Formats

1. **Fork in the road** — Paint two futures. "You can keep doing what's not working, or you can try this." Lets the reader choose.
2. **Risk reversal** — "60 days. If it doesn't work, send it back. You have nothing to lose except another month of the same problem."
3. **Intimate recommendation** — "This is what I told my sister. I'm telling you the same thing." (Warm, trusted-friend frame.)
4. **Permission close** — "Don't take my word for it. Go read the reviews. Trust them, not me."
5. **Direct decision** — "Here's what I'd do if I were you." (Takes decision burden off the reader.)
6. **Confession close** — "I'm not supposed to be writing this. But I'm tired of watching people suffer from something preventable." (Mirrors hook if hook was also confession.)

## Close Requirements

Every close must include:
- **Risk reversal** (guarantee, refund policy — something that lowers the perceived cost of trying)
- **Multiple reasons to act** (not just one — stack permission + urgency + proof + identity)
- **Natural CTA** (not "BUY NOW" — "here's where to try it" or "link below")
- **Callback to the hook or theme** (emotional closure)

## Common Mistakes

Hook mistakes:
- Vague opening ("I was struggling with...")
- Product mention in the hook (kills curiosity immediately)
- Generic pain ("Are you tired of being tired?")
- Clickbait disconnect (hook promises something the copy doesn't deliver)

Close mistakes:
- Pitchy language ("BUY NOW! LIMITED TIME!")
- Breaking native tone (sudden shift from story to sales page)
- Forgetting the hook (close that doesn't connect to opening)
- Weak or missing CTA (no clear next step)
- All 6 closes in a batch look identical (close should match its own hook)
