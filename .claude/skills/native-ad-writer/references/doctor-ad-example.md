# The Doctor Ad — Canonical Example with Annotations

This is the reference example of a fully-formed long-form native ad. Study it when writing a new ad for an unfamiliar category or when the mechanism is unusual. Every element of the 5-phase framework is applied.

**Product (redacted):** Grounding sheets / electrically-conductive bed sheets
**Character:** ER physician + Skeptic Convert combo
**Awareness level:** Problem Aware → Solution Aware
**Believability quadrant:** Reframe Sell (reader believes sleep matters, mechanism is unusual)
**Dominant emotion:** Fear of inevitable health collapse
**Theme:** The system won't save you. You have to save yourself.
**Approximate length:** 1,400–1,800 words

---

## The Full Ad (Annotated)

> **If you've been running on bad sleep for years, I need you to hear this. Your body is a ticking time bomb. And no doctor is going to warn you until it's too late.**

**[HOOK — Direct threat + pattern interrupt. 5 mechanisms stacked: threat, authority pre-seeding, confession, open loop, recognition. No product mention.]**

> My name is Dr. XX. I've been an ER physician for 20 years.

**[Character establishment — Authority. 20 years = credibility anchor.]**

> And I'm writing this because I'm tired of watching the same thing happen over and over.

**[Confession tone — reason to share. Not "I want to help you" but "I'm tired of watching this." Personal stakes for the narrator.]**

> Last month, a woman came in. 52 years old. Healthy weight. No heart history. She collapsed in her bathroom getting ready for work. Her husband found her on the floor. He thought she was dead.

**[SETTING + INCITING INCIDENT — Specific scene. "Last month" = recent. "52 years old, healthy weight, no heart history" = specificity that proves truth. "Bathroom getting ready for work" = mundane, familiar. Then the collapse — the moment normal life breaks. Readers can SEE it.]**

> When I looked at her chart, I saw it. The same pattern I've seen hundreds of times.

**[Open loop — what pattern? Also, stakes escalation: this is not unusual. It's routine.]**

> She hadn't slept properly in years. Four, maybe five hours a night. Always exhausted. Always pushing through. And her body finally said enough.

**[Recognition moment for the reader. Everyone scrolling has had nights of 4-5 hours of sleep. "Her body finally said enough" = inevitability language.]**

> I wish I could tell you she was unusual. She wasn't.

**[Stakes escalation — this happens to everyone like her.]**

> I see this every single week. Sometimes multiple times a week.

**[Pacing — short sentences stacking. Pattern building tension.]**

> Someone gets rushed in. Heart palpitations. Chest pain so bad they think they're dying. Fainting out of nowhere. Stroke-like symptoms. Sometimes actual strokes.

**[Fragments creating urgency. Each symptom adds weight.]**

> And when I sit with them afterwards — when they're stable enough to talk — I always ask the same question.

**[Slower sentence — anticipation. Setting up a reveal.]**

> "How's your sleep been?"

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — one line of dialogue on its own line. Format shift. The eye notices. Attention resets.]**

> And they look at me like I'm crazy. Like what does sleep have to do with anything? I just had a heart scare.

**[Reader psychology — making the doctor's question feel important by showing how much it surprises patients.]**

> But then they tell me. "I mean... I haven't slept well in years." "Maybe four or five hours a night. If I'm lucky." "I can't remember the last time I woke up feeling rested." "I've tried everything. Melatonin. Pills. Nothing works."

**[Secondary characters — multiple patient voices. Each one validates something slightly different. Failure inventory: melatonin, pills, "everything."]**

> And I'm sitting there thinking... that's why you're here. That's exactly why you're in my ER right now.

**[Dramatic irony — the doctor knows what the patients don't. This is the belief shift priming.]**

> But nobody ever told them. No doctor ever connected the dots for them.

**[Theme emerging: the system failed them. The question is being set up: who's going to save you if the system won't?]**

> They thought they were just tired. They didn't know their body was falling apart from the inside.

**[Recognition + fear. The reader who's been ignoring their own exhaustion is now worried.]**

> Let me explain what's actually happening. Because this is the part no one talks about.

**[NEW INFORMATION transition. The belief shift begins.]**

> When you don't sleep properly, your body goes into a state of chronic stress. It thinks something is wrong. It thinks you're in danger. So it stays on high alert.

**[Mechanism explanation — slower pacing, longer sentences, because the reader needs to understand.]**

> Your cortisol — that's your stress hormone — stays elevated. All day. All night. It never comes down.

**[Specificity — cortisol is a named thing. "All day. All night. It never comes down." is fragment-based urgency inside a mechanism explanation.]**

> And when cortisol stays high for too long, it triggers inflammation. Everywhere.

> Now, inflammation isn't always bad. If you cut your finger, inflammation helps it heal. That's acute inflammation. It does its job and goes away.

> But chronic inflammation? That's different. That's your body attacking itself, day after day, with no off switch.

**[Trust ladder — "cortisol causes inflammation" is an observable/common-knowledge truth. Reader nods along. Once they're nodding, the next step lands.]**

> And here's what chronic inflammation does to your body over time:
> Your blood vessels get inflamed. The walls that are supposed to be smooth and flexible? They get stiff. Damaged. Rough.
> Your heart has to work harder to pump blood through these damaged vessels. That's high blood pressure. That's the chest tightness people feel but ignore.
> Your brain gets inflamed. That's the brain fog. The forgetting. The feeling like you're losing your mind.
> Your immune system gets confused. It starts attacking healthy tissue. That's autoimmune issues.

**[Escalating consequences — each layer adds to the stakes.]**

> And the worst part? This happens slowly. Over years. You don't notice it until something breaks.

> Until you collapse in your bathroom and your husband thinks you're dead.

**[Callback to the opening scene. Closes one loop but keeps the main thread open.]**

> Now here's what makes me angry.

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — emotional shift. Snaps the reader back if they were drifting.]**

> When these patients come in, what do we do? We run tests. We check the heart. We prescribe medications. And then we send them home and tell them to "reduce stress" and "try to sleep more." That's it. That's the advice. We treat the crisis. We don't fix the cause.

**[Theme moment — the system fails people. The villain is identified.]**

> I got tired of it.

**[Confession repeated — the narrator has personal stakes.]**

> So I started researching. Outside the textbooks. Outside what they taught us in medical school.

**[The search begins — pulls the reader forward into the discovery.]**

> And that's when I found something that changed everything I thought I knew.

**[OPEN LOOP — what did he find?]**

> It's called grounding. Or earthing. Whatever you want to call it. And I know how it sounds. Trust me, I know. I'm a trained physician. I was skeptical as hell.

**[SKEPTIC CONVERT moment — addresses the reader's likely objection BEFORE the reader can raise it. "I know how it sounds" is doing enormous work here.]**

> But the science is real. And once I understood it, I couldn't unsee it.

**[Then mechanism explanation continues. Electricity in bodies. Free radicals. Inflammation neutralization. Studies exist.]**

**[PRODUCT MENTION — appears roughly 60% through the ad. Only after the belief shift is complete and the reader understands why their past approaches failed.]**

> I was skeptical. Of course I was. So I tried it myself first.

**[Second skeptic convert beat — doubled down because belief was hard.]**

**[TRANSFORMATION — progressive timeline:]**
> First couple nights, nothing dramatic.
> Third night, I slept through without waking up at 3am. That hadn't happened in years.
> By the end of the first week, I was waking up actually feeling rested. Not groggy. Not dragging myself out of bed. Actually rested.
> By week two, this tension I'd been carrying in my shoulders — I didn't even realize how bad it was until it was gone.

**[UNEXPECTED DISCOVERY — shoulder tension. Not tracked. Not expected. Adds credibility because fake transformations don't include things you weren't looking for.]**

> By week three, my husband looked at me and said, "What is going on with you? You seem like a different person."

**[EXTERNAL VALIDATION — the spouse. Mirrors B3C3 in NMN, "husband noticed before I did."]**

> I felt like myself again. The self I forgot existed somewhere around year five of residency.

**[IDENTITY-LEVEL PAYOFF — not "my sleep improved" but "I felt like myself again."]**

**[Then: more secondary characters. Each one different.]**
- Patient with 58-year-old heart issues — medical proof, long timeframe
- Woman on sleep meds for 11 years — extreme case resolved
- Mother with joint pain — different use case
- Son with stress — different demographic

**[Each validates something different. Not redundant testimonials.]**

**[CLOSE]**

> I'm not supposed to be writing things like this. Doctors aren't supposed to recommend products. The medical board wouldn't be happy.

**[Confession close — mirrors the confession hook.]**

> But I'm tired of watching people suffer from something preventable. I'm tired of treating crises that didn't need to happen.

**[Theme landing — system failure, personal responsibility to save yourself.]**

> If you've been running on no sleep for years... if you're exhausted all the time... if you've started feeling things in your body that scare you...
> Don't wait until you're in my ER. Do something now.

**[Fork in the road close — two futures. The close connects back to the opening threat.]**

> Link is below. Go check it out for yourself.

**[Permission close element — don't take his word, check yourself.]**

> Your body has been waiting for this.

**[Final identity-level hook closes the arc.]**

## Key Lessons From This Ad

1. **Hook stacks 5 mechanisms** — most ads use 1-2
2. **Specific inciting incident** opens the story (woman collapsing)
3. **Product doesn't appear until 60% through**
4. **Stakes escalate** from one patient → pattern → mechanism → inevitability
5. **Multiple secondary characters** each validating different objections
6. **Trust ladder built carefully** — observable truths → new info → claims
7. **Pattern interrupts** every few paragraphs ("How's your sleep?", "Now here's what makes me angry")
8. **Skeptic convert** addresses objection BEFORE reader raises it
9. **Transformation is progressive** with unexpected discovery and external validation
10. **Close mirrors hook** — confession-in, confession-out

## When to Use This Reference

Load this reference file when writing ads for:
- Unusual or unfamiliar product mechanisms (category skepticism)
- High-stakes topics where readers need authority
- Complex mechanism explanations that require trust ladder building
- Ads that need length for full belief shift (1,200+ words)
- Reframe Sell quadrant situations
