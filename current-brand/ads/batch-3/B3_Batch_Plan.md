## Purpose

Test whether fixing the copy structure (adding the 6 missing Origins 12-step elements) converts the ad that already showed a conversion signal. B2C2 (ExhaustedMom) generated the only ATC in the entire campaign. This batch iterates on that proven angle with structurally complete copy.

---

## Why We Need to Test This

B2C2 proved three things:
- Parental guilt hook creates the deepest identity threat for our audience
- $28 avg CPM = camouflage works, Meta delivers broadly
- CBO voted for it with 34% of total budget

But 43 LPVs → 1 ATC → 0 purchases. The feedback loop identified the gap: the ad tells a story but doesn't presell the PRODUCT. It resolves the reader's curiosity without building purchase desire.

**What was missing from B1-B2 copy (Origins 12-step structure):**
- Step 5: Social proof stack (embedded testimonials from other people)
- Step 6: Product introduction (Baseline got ONE sentence — needs 3-4 with dose, ingredients, stack replacement, price anchor)
- Step 9: Reframe + validation ("It's not your fault. It's not willpower.")
- Step 10: Competitor teardown ("Most NMN is 125mg. Research uses 500mg.")
- Step 11: CTA + risk reversal (30-day guarantee, price anchor, supply framing)
- Step 12: Urgency close (reader's story left unresolved, not narrator's)

Also missing: OBJECTION → CLAIM → PROOF → BENEFIT cycles (need 2-3 per ad).

---

## Why We Think This Will Be Successful

The hook pattern is proven — parental guilt creates the deepest identity threat in our audience. The CPMs are proven — camouflage works. The ONLY thing that didn't work was the bottom half of the copy. We're fixing the bottom half, not reinventing the top.

If adding product bridge + risk reversal + urgency close moves LPV-to-ATC from 2.3% to even 5-8%, we have a viable ad.

## Sub-Avatar: The Exhausted Parent

**Desire:** "I want enough energy to actually show up for my family"
**Experience:** Can't keep up with kids by 4pm, cancels plans, falls asleep during activities
**Emotion:** Guilt, shame, identity threat ("I'm failing at the thing that matters most")
**Behavior:** Keeps a mental count of "maybe laters," sits in the car before pickup to gather energy
**Demographics:** Women 40-55, 1-3 kids, married

**Why this sub-avatar:** The only sub-avatar that produced a conversion event. Parental guilt elevates the desire from "Comfort" (feel better) to "Relationships" (be present for your family) — the highest-urgency positioning in Origins' desire hierarchy.

## Copy Structure (Applied to All 3 Ads)

Each ad follows the full Origins 12-step native body copy structure:

1. **Personal hook** — The specific parental guilt moment (unique per ad)
2. **Symptom stack** — Everything she's tried, the daily exhaustion, the countdown of "maybe laters"
3. **Turning point** — Discovery moment (Facebook group, friend, late-night research)
4. **Mechanism reveal** — NAD+ decline explained simply, separate from hormonal changes
5. **Social proof stack** — 2-3 short embedded testimonials from other moms (NEW)
6. **Product introduction** — "Baseline 10-in-1: 500mg NMN + 9 supporting ingredients. The clinical dose. One serving replaces 5 separate bottles. Under $50 for 60 days." (EXPANDED from 1 sentence to 3-4)
7. **Results timeline** — Week-by-week, starting with "nothing" (keep)
8. **Emotional resolution** — Narrator's story resolves, but NOT fully ("Most days now, I can be present. Not every day. But most days.")
9. **Reframe + validation** — "It's not willpower. It's not laziness. It's not 'just your age.' There's a piece nobody told you about." (NEW)
10. **Competitor teardown** — "Most NMN supplements are 125mg. The research uses 500mg. Most brands hope you don't notice the difference." (NEW)
11. **CTA + risk reversal** — "See the full 10-in-1 formula. 60-day supply. Less than your afternoon coffee. 30-day money-back guarantee — if nothing changes, you get your money back. No hoops." (NEW)
12. **Urgency close / open reader loop** — "I don't know if your mornings look like mine did. But if you're counting 'maybe laters' too — there might be a piece of this you haven't been told about yet." (NEW)

**OBJECTION → CLAIM → PROOF → BENEFIT cycles (2 per ad):**

*Cycle 1:*
- OBJECTION: "I've tried supplements. Nothing works."
- CLAIM: "Most NMN is underdosed — 125mg when published research uses 500mg."
- PROOF: "Baseline delivers 500mg plus 9 compounds that support how your cells use it."
- BENEFIT: "Imagine not reaching for coffee at 2pm. Not willpower — your cells actually have what they need."

*Cycle 2:*
- OBJECTION: "How is this different from the $15 NMN on Amazon?"
- CLAIM: "One serving replaces 5 separate bottles."
- PROOF: "NMN + resveratrol + CoQ10 + glutathione + TMG + quercetin + astaxanthin + apigenin + grape seed extract + vitamin C."
- BENEFIT: "Two capsules. One bottle on your counter instead of five. Under $50 for 60 days."

## Learnings Applied from B1-B2

- Full 12-step copy structure (added missing steps 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12)
- 2 OBJECTION → CLAIM → PROOF → BENEFIT cycles per ad
- Product bridge expanded from 1 sentence to 3-4 (10-in-1, 500mg, stack replacement, price anchor)
- Story resolves for narrator but READER's story left open
- CTA changed from "Function first" to product-signal + risk reversal
- No product visible in creative (B1C2 lesson: product-forward = CPM death spiral)
- Kept the "nothing happened at first" honesty beat (strongest credibility device)
- Kept Facebook group / friend discovery mechanism (most authentic for this avatar)
