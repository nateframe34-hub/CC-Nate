## Image 1 (NEW): Morning Counter — Product in Her Life

**Placement:** Top of page, right after the title. Small-medium size. Sets the scene immediately.

**Purpose:** She just came from a B3 native ad showing a kitchen counter / car / couch. This image continues that exact visual language — the product is in HER morning, not a studio. It grounds her: "this is real, this is someone's actual kitchen."

**Prompt:**

```
A photorealistic overhead photograph taken on an iPhone of a kitchen counter in soft morning light. A white supplement bottle with a purple accent label sits casually next to a steaming ceramic coffee mug on a light quartz countertop. Two small white capsules rest on the counter beside the mug. Around them: car keys on a striped dish towel, a crumpled school permission slip, a banana, the corner of a blue kids backpack leaning against the backsplash. Everything is slightly messy and real — not styled, not arranged. Morning sunlight from a window on the left catches the coffee steam. The bottle is one item among many, not the focal point. Shot from slightly above at a natural angle, iPhone camera quality, warm golden light, slight grain. No text overlay. 4:5 aspect ratio.
```

**Post-production:** Crop to a smaller inline size (not full-width). On mobile it should feel like a photo embedded in a text conversation, not a hero banner.

## Sizing Notes

All three images should be **small to medium inline** — NOT full-width hero banners. Think text-message-sized photos embedded in the flow of copy. On mobile:
- Width: 70-80% of the screen, centered
- Rounded corners (8px border-radius)
- Subtle, not disruptive — they break up the text visually without making the page feel image-heavy

The goal is scanability. Text → small image → text → small image → text → CTA. The images give her eyes a place to rest and ground the copy in reality without making her think "this is a long article."
