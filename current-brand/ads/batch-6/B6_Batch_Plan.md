# Batch 6 Plan — Accidental Ideal Outcome: The Reluctant Decliner

> **Status:** Planned
> **Format:** Accidental Ideal Outcome ("I accidentally got [result]")
> **Sub-Avatar:** The Reluctant Decliner (adults 40-55, gradual energy decline)
> **Pre-sell:** YES — Short listicle ("How a 47-Year-Old Accidentally Replaced Her Entire Supplement Shelf")
> **Kill Rule:** $75 spend per ad with no purchase = dead

---

## Purpose

Test indirect desire channeling — whether implying effortless results converts better than describing the struggle. Every ad in B1-B2 led with pain (Identity/Relief channeling). This format leads with the OUTCOME and works backwards (Indirect Implication channeling). Tests a fundamentally different psychological mechanism: curiosity + "that could be me" instead of "that IS me."

Also retests the Reluctant Decliner avatar, which produced zero conversions in B1 — but with a completely different format and approach.

---

## Why We Need to Test This

Every B1-B2 ad used Identity/Relief desire channeling (reader sees themselves in the narrator's suffering). It worked for engagement but not conversion. Accidental Ideal Outcome uses Indirect Implication — the reader sees the RESULT and infers the promise themselves.

Origins Mastery Guide: "Self-generated conclusions are more persuasive than stated claims."

We haven't tested this psychological mechanism at all. The Reluctant Decliner avatar was our B1 target and produced zero conversions — but the angles were wrong (data-driven niche, product-forward creative). This format gives the avatar a fresh approach.

---

## Why We Think This Will Be Successful

The Mastery Guide specifically calls out this format for Stage 3-4 markets "with multiple benefits where you can lead with a surprising secondary outcome." Baseline's 10-in-1 formula IS multiple benefits.

The "accidental" framing disarms skepticism — it implies the narrator wasn't even trying, which bypasses the "too good to be true" objection. Specific, non-round numbers (47, not 50 / 43 days, not "a month") build believability.

First-person framing keeps a native feel even in a non-native format structure.

---

## Pre-sell: Listicle

**Title:** "How a 47-Year-Old Accidentally Replaced Her Entire Supplement Shelf (And Got Her Afternoons Back)"
**Length:** 500-800 words
**Tone:** Casual, effortless, discovery-oriented — mirrors the "accidental" framing
**Structure:** 5-7 numbered points, built around the discovery narrative

1. She wasn't looking for a new supplement (establishes "accidental" frame)
2. The afternoon crash was getting worse every year (relatable pain)
3. A friend mentioned something about cellular energy (discovery mechanism)
4. NAD+ decline after 40 — the piece nobody talks about (mechanism education)
5. One formula that covers what used to take 5 bottles (product bridge)
6. What actually changed in 6 weeks (results preview without full resolution)
7. Price, guarantee, CTA

**Ends with:** CTA + risk reversal. UTM-tagged links to PDP.

**Why a listicle:** Accidental Ideal Outcome creates maximum curiosity — "how did she accidentally get her energy back?" The click is curiosity-driven. Without a listicle, the reader clicks, sees a product page, and the curiosity dies — they wanted the HOW, not a buy button. The listicle catches that curiosity and converts it into product education before the PDP.

## Sub-Avatar: The Reluctant Decliner

**Desire:** "I want to feel normal again — not 30, just not like I'm falling apart"
**Experience:** Gradual energy decline over 2-5 years, no specific medical cause found
**Emotion:** Resignation, quiet frustration ("I guess this is just getting older")
**Behavior:** Adjusts life around declining energy (cancels plans, avoids commitments, lower expectations)
**Demographics:** Adults 40-55, men and women

**Why this sub-avatar:** Largest potential audience — anyone 40+ experiencing gradual decline. B1 targeted them but failed because of niche angles (data analyst) and product-forward creative (bottle image). This format gives the avatar a completely different treatment — outcome-first, not pain-first. Tests whether the Reluctant Decliner responds better to aspiration or pain.

## Creative Direction (3 Images)

**B6C1 — Dark/premium background + serif text:**
Clean, elegant layout. Large serif headline (Cormorant Garamond): "I Accidentally Got My Energy Back at 47." Below: 3-4 bullet points with specific claims (500mg NMN, 10 ingredients, 60-day supply, $49.99). Small product image at bottom, secondary to the text. Feels premium, intentionally designed — the "accidental" framing provides the authenticity, so the creative can be polished.

**B6C2 — Bathroom shelf comparison:**
Before/after composition: left side shows 5 separate supplement bottles (cluttered shelf), right side shows one Baseline bottle (clean shelf). Text: "I Accidentally Replaced 5 Bottles With 1 at 52." The visual proves the claim. Product IS visible here — it's the punchline, not the subject. Different from B1C2 (product as subject) because here the product is the ANSWER to a visual comparison.

**B6C3 — Bold text on simple background:**
Large, confident text: "I HAVEN'T HIT THE 3PM WALL IN 43 DAYS." Below in smaller text: "Here's what changed." No product, no imagery. Pure text. Curiosity gap forces the click. Clean, minimal.

**All creatives:** 1080×1350 (4:5) or 1080×1920 (9:16). Mix of premium-designed and text-minimal approaches.

## Learnings Applied from B1-B2

- B1 (Reluctant Decliner) produced zero conversions with pain-first native format — testing outcome-first approach for same avatar
- B1C2 "lights back up" payoff line was the campaign's best — "accidentally" framing captures that same subtle, non-dramatic energy
- B1C3 "same inputs, worse outputs" insight portable — B6C3 ("43 days") uses the data-tracking feel without the niche format
- Product differentiation front-loaded in body copy (500mg, 10-in-1, stack replacement, price)
- Specific non-round numbers throughout (47, 52, 43, $49.99) — Mastery Guide: odd/specific numbers feel measured, round numbers feel manufactured
- Risk reversal in every body copy (30-day guarantee)
- B6C2 shows product intentionally as the PUNCHLINE (shelf comparison), not as the subject — B1C2 taught us product-forward kills CPMs, but product-as-proof-of-claim is a different mechanic
- First-person framing maintained even in non-native format to keep authenticity
