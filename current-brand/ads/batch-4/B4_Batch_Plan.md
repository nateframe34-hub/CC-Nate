# Batch 4 Plan — Avatar Call Out: The 3pm Wall Hitter

> **Status:** Planned
> **Format:** Avatar Call Out (bold headline naming a specific demographic)
> **Sub-Avatar:** The 3pm Wall Hitter (adults 40+, universal afternoon crash)
> **Pre-sell:** YES — Short listicle ("5 Reasons the 3pm Crash Gets Worse After 40")
> **Kill Rule:** Hard kill at $50 (2x CPA). Soft kill at $37.50 (1.5x CPA) if metrics are bad (CPM >$80, CTR <1%, 0 LPVs, or LPVs with 0 ATCs). Exception: if ad has ATC/IC before $50, extend to $75 to see if it converts.

---

## Purpose

Test whether a completely different format converts where native long-form didn't. Avatar Call Out is bold, direct, and immediate — no story, no 500-word buildup. Just name the avatar in their face and let the body copy + listicle do the selling. Also tests a NEW sub-avatar we haven't run at all yet (3pm Wall Hitter — our broadest avatar).

---

## Why We Need to Test This

Every ad in B1-B2 was the same format (native/camouflage long-form). The Mastery Guide says test multiple formats via Marksman. We have zero data on whether a different format converts better.

Our hypothesis from the feedback loops: native long-form creates curiosity clickers, not buyers. Avatar Call Out is the opposite approach — direct, specific, no story. If it converts, the problem was format. If it doesn't, we know native is the right format and the copy structure fix (B3) is the real solution.

---

## Why We Think This Will Be Successful

Our audience may be Stage 1 for NMN specifically even if they're Stage 5 for supplements generally. Many 40+ adults have never heard of NMN or NAD+. A direct "WHEN YOUR 3RD COFFEE STOPS WORKING BY 2PM" bypasses the sophistication problem — it doesn't claim NMN is special, it just names the person and invites them to learn.

The 3pm Wall Hitter is the broadest avatar — virtually everyone 40+ has experienced the afternoon crash. Higher scope = more potential for Meta to find converters.

The listicle bridges the education gap between the short ad and the PDP.

## Pre-sell: Listicle

**Title:** "5 Reasons the 3pm Crash Gets Worse After 40 (And the Cellular Fix Nobody Told You About)"
**Length:** 500-800 words
**Tone:** Educational, curious, clean
**Structure:** 5 numbered points, each building toward Baseline as the answer

1. NAD+ levels decline after 40 (mechanism education)
2. Caffeine masks the problem, doesn't fix it (reframe)
3. Most supplements miss the actual cause (competitor teardown)
4. The research uses 500mg NMN — most brands sell 125mg (differentiation)
5. One formula covers what used to take 5 bottles (product bridge)

**Ends with:** Product name, price, stack replacement value, CTA + risk reversal (30-day guarantee). UTM-tagged links to PDP.

**Format:** Shopify blog post (same method as V2 advertorial).

**Why a listicle not an advertorial:** Avatar Call Out creates "that's me" recognition clicks, not story clicks. A listicle matches the energy — scannable, factual, numbered. An advertorial (another long story) would be incongruent with the bold, direct ad format. The flow is: attention (bold image) → education (listicle) → conversion (PDP). Clean escalation, no story reset.

## Creative Build Notes (Canva — Not AI Generated)

Avatar Call Out creatives are text-dominant designed graphics. Build in Canva, not Nano Banana Pro.

### B4C1 — "WHEN YOUR 3RD COFFEE STOPS WORKING BY 2PM"

**Canvas specs:** 1080×1350 (4:5)
**Background:** Solid deep teal (#0c3b3f)
**Typography:**
- Main headline: Plus Jakarta Sans Bold (or similar heavy sans-serif), white, ALL CAPS, 60-72pt, centered
- Line breaks: "WHEN YOUR / 3RD COFFEE / STOPS WORKING / BY 2PM"
- Subline: Plus Jakarta Sans Regular, white at 60% opacity, sentence case, 20pt, centered below headline with 40px gap: "There's a reason. And it's not caffeine."
**Layout:** Headline vertically centered in the upper 60% of the canvas. Subline below. Bottom 15% is clean space. No imagery, no product, no logo. The bold white text on dark teal IS the entire creative.
**Mood:** Confrontational, direct, impossible to scroll past. Feels like a public service announcement, not an ad.

### B4C2 — "THE 3PM CRASH ISN'T A COFFEE PROBLEM"

**Canvas specs:** 1080×1350 (4:5)
**Background:** Nano Banana Pro generated lifestyle image (see prompt below), dimmed to 30-40% brightness with dark overlay
**Typography:**
- Main headline: Plus Jakarta Sans Bold, white, ALL CAPS, 60-72pt, centered
- Line breaks: "THE 3PM CRASH / ISN'T A / COFFEE PROBLEM"
- No subline — the body copy in Meta provides the answer
**Layout:** Headline centered vertically over the dimmed image. Text has a subtle drop shadow or dark text-shadow for readability.
**Mood:** The lifestyle image provides warmth/context, the text provides the punch. The image doesn't distract — it grounds the statement in reality.

**Nano Banana Pro Prompt (background image):**

```
A photorealistic photograph of a half-drunk coffee cup on a cluttered office desk in harsh fluorescent afternoon light. The coffee is cold — no steam, dark surface slightly reflective. Around the cup: a laptop with the screen showing a blurred spreadsheet, a phone face-down, scattered papers, a pen with the cap off, a granola bar wrapper. The desk is standard office beige or gray laminate. The lighting is flat and institutional — overhead fluorescent panels, no natural light. The time of day feels like 2-3pm. The mood is monotonous, draining, stuck. No people visible. Shot from slightly above at a natural desk-level angle, smartphone camera quality. Muted color palette — no vibrancy, everything feels washed out and tired. 4:5 aspect ratio.
```

**Post-production:** Apply a dark overlay (black at 60-70% opacity) over the generated image. Place headline text on top. The image should be visible but muted — a texture, not a focal point.

## Learnings Applied from B1-B2

- Testing a format we've NEVER tested (Marksman principle — multiple formats to find direction)
- Medium body copy with OBJECTION → CLAIM → PROOF → BENEFIT structure (B1-B2 had zero)
- Product named early with specific differentiators (500mg, 10-in-1, stack replacement)
- CTA + risk reversal in the body copy itself (30-day guarantee, price anchor)
- Listicle presell bridges the education gap that short-format ads create
- Testing broadest avatar (3pm Wall Hitter) vs narrowest (Exhausted Parent in B3)
- "Same inputs, worse outputs" framework rescued from B1C3 (killed ad's best insight, freed from niche format)
- B2C3's "I've tried everything" inventory ported into Body Copy 2 (strongest trust device in campaign)
