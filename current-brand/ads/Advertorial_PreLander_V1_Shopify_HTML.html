But here's what most doctors aren't looking at.

There's a molecule your body is running out of. It's not a vitamin.

In 2013, a research team at Harvard published a study that changed how scientists think about cellular aging. They identified a molecule called NAD+ — nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide — and demonstrated that declining levels of it were directly connected to how cells function as we age.

NAD+ isn't a vitamin. It's not a mineral. It's a coenzyme — a molecule present in every single cell in your body. Your cells use it for energy production, for cellular maintenance, for DNA support. It's involved in hundreds of biological processes.

And here's the part that matters: your body produces less of it every year after about age 40.

Published research suggests NAD+ levels can decline by as much as 50% between your 30s and 60s. That's not a theory. That's measured. Documented. Replicated across multiple studies.

Think about what that means. The molecule your cells depend on for energy production is gradually becoming less available. Not because you're doing anything wrong. Not because your diet is bad or your sleep is off. Because your body's production of this specific molecule is declining with age.

That's the thing nobody told you.

Your thyroid is fine. Your iron is fine. But the fuel inside every cell — the thing mitochondria need to actually produce energy — is running lower every year.
