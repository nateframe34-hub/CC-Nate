There's a molecule your body is running out of. It's not a vitamin.

In 2013, a Harvard research team published a study that changed how scientists think about cellular aging.

They identified a molecule called NAD+ — nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide. It's a coenzyme present in every single cell in your body. Your cells use it for energy production, cellular maintenance, and hundreds of other biological processes.

It's not a vitamin. It's not a mineral. It's the fuel behind the fuel — the thing your mitochondria need to actually produce energy.

And here's the part that matters:

Your body produces less of it every year after about age 40.

Think about what that means.

The molecule every cell depends on for energy production is gradually becoming less available. Not because you're doing anything wrong. Not because your diet is bad or your sleep is off. Because production of this specific molecule declines with age.

What I found when I followed the research

NMN — nicotinamide mononucleotide — is the direct precursor your body uses to produce NAD+. Your cells convert NMN into NAD+ through a well-documented metabolic pathway.

This isn't fringe science. It's been studied in peer-reviewed, published human clinical research. The mechanism is straightforward: you provide the raw material, your cells do what they already know how to do — just with more to work with.

What makes NMN different from everything else I'd tried:

It's not a stimulant. No spike. No crash two hours later. It supports the underlying system that produces sustained cellular energy.

It's not another vitamin stack. NMN targets one specific, well-researched pathway — the one that was actually the bottleneck.

It works on a timeline. Most people in published studies notice changes around weeks 2-4. It's not instant — it's building something.

But here's what I also learned, both from the research and from working with clients: NAD+ doesn't work in isolation.

The systems that use NAD+ need their own support:

Mitochondria need CoQ10 to function efficiently — and your body produces less with age, just like NAD+.

Sirtuins — the proteins that regulate how cells age — are activated by resveratrol. NMN provides the fuel. Resveratrol tells your cells what to do with it.

Methylation consumes methyl groups, and NMN metabolism uses them up. TMG replenishes them. This is why knowledgeable NMN users pair the two.

Oxidative stress is managed by antioxidants like glutathione, astaxanthin, and quercetin. Supporting NAD+ without supporting the defense system is like running an engine harder without changing the oil.
