## POST 4 (Thursday) — PERSONAL HEALTH JOURNEY
**Text post/graphic:** List of supplements tried and quit

### Caption A
The irony of being a trained naturopathic practitioner who still got played by the supplement industry is not lost on me.

I've tried the overpriced greens powders. The adrenal support blends with 47 ingredients. The collagen that tasted like chalk and did exactly nothing. Adaptogens stacked on adaptogens. I wanted them to work. Some of them I *told patients* would work.

Here's what I look for now: a clear mechanism of action — how does it actually work in the cell — and third-party verification of what's actually in the bottle. That's it. Two questions. Eliminates about 90% of what's on the shelf.

### Caption B
A partial list of supplements I've quit: a mushroom blend that cost $78 a month, two different "energy support" formulas that were basically B vitamins in a fancy bottle, an adaptogen stack I couldn't even explain the mechanism for, and a greens powder my kids called "swamp juice."

I have a degree in this. I ran a practice. And I still bought things based on marketing instead of mechanism. That's how good the branding is.

Now my filter is simple: explain how it works at the cellular level, and show me independent proof of what's inside. No exceptions.

### Caption C
Confession from a former naturopathic practitioner: I have a drawer full of supplements I quit.

Not because I'm anti-supplement — I'm literally trained in this. But the industry makes it shockingly easy to waste money on things that sound good and do nothing. Proprietary blends with undisclosed doses. Trendy ingredients with no real mechanism of action. Beautiful branding, zero accountability.

The frustrating part? I should've known better. I did know better. I just wanted shortcuts like everyone else.

My criteria now are ruthlessly simple: proven mechanism of action and independently verified contents. That's the whole checklist.
