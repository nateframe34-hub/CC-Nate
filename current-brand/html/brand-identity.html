Our Recommendation
Baseline
"Your baseline dropped. This brings it back."

Baseline is the only name that speaks to BOTH the core desire AND the core differentiator. When a customer says "I just want to feel normal again," they're describing their baseline. When we test every batch, we're establishing a baseline of proof. The name does double duty.

In a market full of hype names — Wonderfeel, Alive By Science, GenuinePurity — a grounded, measured name stands out. It says: we don't need to shout because we have the data.

For The Restorer, "Baseline" is permission to want what they once had. For The Optimizer, it's the metric they're already tracking. One word. Two audiences. Zero wasted connotation.

02 — Color System
Modern Apothecary

A palette that bridges science and health in a single glance — ownable, differentiated, and warm enough for both avatars.

Primary Deep Teal #0C3B3F
Secondary Verdigris #165C5A
Accent Copper #C07840
Background Cream #F5F0E8
Text Near Black #1A1A1A

Teal bridges science + health

Blue signals scientific credibility. Green signals health and vitality. Teal is both in one color. No NMN competitor uses deep teal as a primary — this is immediately ownable shelf space.

Copper signals precision

Copper evokes lab instruments, measured quality, and artisan craft. It's warm without being playful, premium without being cold. The "precision" connotation ties directly to batch-specific COA testing.

Cream keeps it human

White backgrounds feel clinical. Cream feels warm, natural, and trustworthy — critical for The Restorer (45-65) who isn't a biohacker and doesn't want to feel like they're in a lab. It says: science you can trust, made for real people.

Palette A: "Trusted Signal"

Strong but generic

Navy is the default trust color — which is exactly the problem. Every bank, every insurance company, every "we're trustworthy" brand uses navy. In the NMN space it would work, but it wouldn't differentiate. We'd be fighting for recognition against decades of blue-means-safe conditioning. Teal gets the same trust signal while being ownable.

Palette B: "Quiet Authority"

Premium but misread

Charcoal + sage reads as a luxury tech brand or upscale skincare. Premium, yes — but it lacks any health or science association. A customer scrolling past would not think "supplement" or "health." The sage accent alone can't carry the health story. This palette works better for a future product line extension, not the flagship.

One mark to own.

Each direction takes a different angle on the brand story. All are designed to work at any scale — from app icon to billboard.

The Measure
