## 2. Mindset Foundation
**[Source: `Origins mindset.docx`]**

### The Emotional Cycle of Change (5 Stages)
1. **Uninformed Optimism** - Everything seems exciting and easy
2. **Informed Pessimism** - Reality sets in, doubt creeps in
3. **Valley of Despair** - The hardest point; most people quit here
4. **Informed Optimism** - Skills develop, evidence of progress appears
5. **Success & Fulfillment** - Compound results from sustained effort

### Core Mindset Principles
- **10,000 Hours of Deliberate Practice**: Mastery comes from reps, not shortcuts
- **Feedback Loops are the growth engine**: Do → Get Output → Analyze Output → Change Input → Repeat
- **White Belt Mentality**: Hardness = Scarcity = Value. The difficulty IS the moat
- **Bias Toward Action**: Chase high-intent reps, not perfection
- **Track Everything**: Every product tested, every creative tested, and WHY you tested it

## 3. Product Selection & Understanding
**[Source: `How To Find Product Module (1).docx`, `Origins - Product Overview Document (2).docx`]**

### The Dual Nature of Every Product
Every product is actually TWO products:
- **The Physical**: Carbon fiber, wires, motherboard, ingredients, materials
- **The Functional**: What it DOES - shiny hair, smooth skin, celebrity-style results

The customer purchases what the product DOES (functional). The physical only justifies the price or documents quality.

### Product Criteria Checklist
- **AOV**: $60+ after bundles to ensure profitability
- **Margin**: Minimum 3x COGS + Shipping (ideally more)
- **Trend**: Quick Google Trends check - make sure the niche isn't trending down
- **Weight**: Avoid heavy items (Shoebox Rule)

### Myth-Busting
- There is NO "winning product." Any product can be a winner
- Spam testing is BS (relying on luck)
- Instant success is a lie - building a brand takes time
- Saturation = Big audience. Don't fear competition

### Product Overview Template (Fill for every product)
1. **Basics**: Product name, price point
2. **Product In Action**: HOW does it work? Describe the process/method and outcome. Then write a 6th-grade reading level version
3. **Features**: List all main features
4. **Assumed Benefits**: For each feature → what does it DO for the customer?
5. **Assumed Desires**: For each benefit → what deeper desire does it attend to? ("Why does someone want/need that benefit?")
6. **New Mechanisms (USPs)**: What makes the product unique? Any new mechanisms?
7. **Hidden Mechanisms**: What does it do that you DON'T advertise? What ingredients/components do competitors lack? If it worked TOO well, what would customers complain about? How would someone explain it to a friend?

### Two Ways to Stand Out in Competitive Markets
1. **Innovate the product** - New mechanism that solves an existing problem better
2. **Innovate on the avatar** - Target an underserved avatar

### Case Studies

**Shilajit (New Mechanism):** When Shilajit was only resin form, it was Stage 1-2 sophistication claims. Brands introduced new delivery mechanisms (gummies, capsules) to reset sophistication.

**Stanley (New Avatar):** Originally made for construction workers ("Keeps ice 2 hours longer than YETI"). Sophistication reset by targeting Gen Z women: "Your emotional support water bottle." Old market Stage 5 → New market Stage 1.

**TrueClassic (New Avatar + Mechanism):** Everyone targeted hypebeast, gymbros, bargain hunters, athletic dudes. TrueClassic targeted the underserved "Dad Bod" avatar (age 30-55). First to acknowledge this avatar exists. Body-optimizing fit that hides gut, enhances shoulders.

## 4. Market Sophistication
**[Source: `Market Sophistication Document (2).docx`, `Avatar Module Notes (4).docx`]**

Eugene Schwartz's Market Sophistication from *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966), adapted for modern eCom. The rate of sophistication is now FASTER than ever due to global commerce and instant feedback.

### The 5 Stages

| Stage | Definition | Goal | Action |
|-------|-----------|------|--------|
| **Stage 1** | First to market - market has never seen your product | Be simple, be direct | Name the claim and prove it works |
| **Stage 2** | Competition emerging - market starting to see it | Enlarge successful claims, one-up competitors | Make bigger and stronger claims |
| **Stage 3** | Market has heard all the claims | Introduce a NEW MECHANISM | Focus on HOW the product delivers the SAME claim in a NEW and better way |
| **Stage 4** | Market now knows the mechanisms | Enlarge your mechanism | Make it easier, faster, solve it better, overcome limitations |
| **Stage 5** | Market no longer believes you | Focus on IDENTIFICATION with your prospect | Show the market the version of themselves they want to become |

### Critical Rules
- Most eCom markets operate in **Stages 3-5** due to low barriers to entry and information abundance
- Market Sophistication is **NOT something you test** on ads. It just EXISTS. You don't test "different levels of sophistication"
- Market Sophistication is **NOT** Market Awareness (those are different concepts)
- This is what makes Facebook advertising extremely hard when you have no true USPs
- This is why bad marketers can get rich quickly finding products with new mechanisms or in early Stage 1-2 markets

### The Avatar-Market Sophistication Gap
Even when your avatar is perfectly defined, desires are crystal clear, and your product actually delivers claims - **you fail** because you can't communicate in a way that breaks through the sophistication level. This gap is what kills most brands.

### How To Determine Your Market's Sophistication
1. Analyze your product and the problems it solves
2. Identify all competitors selling the same product
3. Identify all businesses solving the same problem
4. Create a Market Sophistication board (use Foreplay)
5. During competitor research, build "boxes" and see which ones resonate and how to attack an angle the market hasn't seen

## 5. Three Strategic Responses
**[Source: `Avatar Module Notes (4).docx`, `How To Find Product Module (1).docx`]**

Three ways to break through sophisticated markets:

### 1. New Mechanism
Introduces a NEW process, technology, or delivery system that makes familiar promises fresh and believable. Rather than claiming bigger benefits, reveal the "secret thing" behind your solution.

- **Psychology**: Sophisticated customers crave a clear understanding of HOW products work
- **Examples**: Purple's GelFlex Grid, Seed Health's ViaCap dual-capsule (95% probiotic survival vs competitors' 20%), Oura Ring
- **Apple iPod Story**: South Korea created the MP3 first. Only when Apple marketed it as "1,000 songs in your pocket" did people buy
- **Pitfalls**: Over-technical communication that alienates, claiming false uniqueness for industry-standard processes, explaining mechanisms without connecting to customer value
- **Requires**: R&D investment, patent/trade secret protection, technical communication capabilities, demonstration/proof systems

### 2. New Information
Reframes product understanding through consumer education. Position as trusted authority rather than seller. Reveal problems customers didn't know existed.

- **Psychology**: Education builds authority and trust. Reciprocity principle makes customers feel obligated to support brands providing genuine value
- **Examples**: AG1 invested $5M+ in clinical studies (built $600M+ business), The Ordinary (radical ingredient transparency), Ritual vitamins (interactive supply chain maps)
- **Pitfalls**: Surface-level education undermines credibility. Overly promotional content backfires
- **Requires**: Real subject matter expertise, content creation resources, long-term commitment to authority building

### 3. New Identity
Creates emotional connections through lifestyle movements. Focus on WHO customers become rather than what products do. Builds communities around shared values.

- **Psychology**: People purchase to maintain identity consistency. Belonging is a core human need (Maslow). Emotional responses have 23% greater influence on purchase intent than rational content
- **Examples**: Liquid Death ($1.4B water brand through punk rock identity), Glossier ("skin first, makeup second"), Peloton (status symbol + competitive community)
- **Pitfalls**: Positioning feels inauthentic, values misalign with actual brand behavior, trying to appeal to everyone = meaningless positioning
- **Requires**: Brand strategy expertise, influencer relationships, authentic storytelling

### Quick Decision Framework
| Response | Core Promise | Positioning Language |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|
| New Mechanism | New HOPE through new technology | "Not another supplement, but the first one that..." |
| New Information | Awareness of hidden problems | Previously unknown "why" behind the problem |
| New Identity | Belonging and aspiration | "Not another [category], but for people who..." |

## 6. Desire Hunting
**[Source: `Avatar Module Notes (4).docx`, `Origins - Desire Hunting Template (2).docx`]**

### What Is Desire
- The FORCE that makes advertising work
- Strong feelings of wants or needs
- Desire is the demand that ALREADY EXISTS and is flowing through the market
- **You CANNOT create desire. You can only CHANNEL it.**

### Types of Desire
- **Permanent Desires**: Health, Wealth, Relationships - desires that never leave us
- **Trending Desires**: Crypto, Valentine's Day, AI - desires that come and go

### Desire Strength
Your desire's strength determines your ad's success. Find the dominant desire based on:
- **Intensity**: Minor headache vs. severe migraine
- **Scope**: Total number of people who share this desire

### The 6 Mass Instincts (Permanent Forces)
Listed in order of scale and emotional urgency:
1. **Health** - Survival, wellness, longevity, physical capability
2. **Status** - Social standing, respect, recognition, perception
3. **Relationships** - Connection, love, intimacy, social bonds
4. **Comfort** - Ease, convenience, pain avoidance, stress reduction
5. **Control** - Autonomy, mastery, independence, security
6. **Belonging** - Acceptance, group identity, tribal connection

### The Elevation Strategy
Moving products UP the hierarchy (Belonging → Health) increases scale and emotional urgency. Moving DOWN cheapens positioning.

**Example**: A mattress positioned for "Comfort" (sleep better) has less urgency than positioned for "Health" (chronic pain, cognitive decline from poor sleep) or "Relationships" (stop keeping your partner awake).

### Desire Evaluation Formula
**Urgency x Staying Power x Scope**
- Urgency: How badly do they need it NOW?
- Staying Power: Will this desire persist?
- Scope: How many people share this desire?

### Seasonal Desire Mapping
| Month | Primary Desires | Key Opportunities |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| January | Transformation, Control | "New Year, New Me" - highest purchase intent of the year |
| February | Relationships, Status | Valentine's Day, connection-focused messaging |
| March-April | Renewal, Action | Spring cleaning energy, fresh start messaging |
| May | Belonging, Status, Comfort | Mother's Day, Memorial Day, outdoor living prep |

### Desire Hunting Template
Track for your brand:
- **Desires** (Wants/Needs): "I want X" statements
- **Customer Language Snippets**: Exact words they use
- **Ad Ideas**: Concepts sparked by research
- **New Information**: Anything learned during research

### Research Methods for Finding Desire
- **Research**: Look for "I wanted", "I was looking for", "I was in need of" in reviews, Reddit, Facebook groups, Amazon Q&A
- **Testing**: Test each desire in your ad creatives. Example: "Find a style" vs. "I want to look wealthy" (right side won)

## 7. Avatar Creation - The Core 5 Categories
**[Source: `Avatar Module Notes (4).docx`]**

### What Is an Avatar
A group of specific people most likely to buy your product. Goal: create instant relatability - when they read your ad, they think "that's exactly me."

### The Problem With Traditional Avatars
Most avatars are demographic-based, generalized profiles created once because someone in marketing said they were important. They are NOT actionable. But you ARE targeting avatars with every ad you write - the problem is your avatar is usually too broad and not relatable.

### Meta Targeting Reality
Meta targeting is now **99% creative-based**, not audience-selection-based. If you want to target an avatar, it has to be done through the creative, not by selecting interests at the adset level. Meta rewards relevance - the MORE your creatives resonate with a specific avatar, the lower your costs and higher your conversions.

### Spencer's Core 5 Categories (IN ORDER OF IMPORTANCE)

**Critical insight: Start with desires, NOT demographics. The order matters.**

#### Category 1: Desires (Start Here)
"I want X / I need Y" statements. The emotional pull that drives purchase behavior.

- Use SURFACE-LEVEL desires for avatar building (not core desires)
- Core desires (Health, Status, Relationships) are too broad for initial avatars
- But you MUST understand which core desire the surface desire maps to

**Examples of Surface-Level Desires:**
- "I want to get better sleep" (Core: Health)
- "I want my dog to stop pulling" (Core: Control/Comfort/Status)
- "I need to lose this baby fat" (Core: Health/Status)
- "I want to perform better in the bedroom" (Core: Relationships/Control)

**How to check it's a Desire:** Does it start with "I want" or "I need"? Can you connect it to one of the 6 core desires?

#### Category 2: Experiences
Things that have happened TO someone. Two types:
- **Situational**: "My bedroom gets bright at 5am" / "I had a panic attack at the gym"
- **Product-based**: "I tried X product and it didn't work" / "I bought a harness but my dog still pulls"

**Key rule:** ISOLATE the event. Strip away the emotion, desire, and behavior. Just the raw thing that happened.

**How to check it's an Experience:** Rewrite as "X happened." If it works, it's an experience.

#### Category 3: Emotions
How someone FEELS about or in response to something.
- **Primary 6**: Joy, Sadness, Fear, Disgust, Anger, Surprise
- **Secondary**: Anxious, Frustrated, Overwhelmed, Embarrassed, Hopeless, Insecure, etc.

**Key rule:** If it's a secondary emotion, dig deeper. Ask WHY and WHAT to find the root primary emotion.

**How to check it's an Emotion:** Does it describe how a person FEELS about or in response to something?

#### Category 4: Behaviors & Habits
Regular action patterns people develop as responses to experiences, emotions, desires, or demographics. How people ACT.

- Behaviors are often specific solutions to problems: Experience = "I tried X and Y happened" → Behavior = "So now I do Z instead"
- Tied to ego/self-identity - incredibly powerful for targeting
- If you understand behaviors, you can predict them and "tell them the future" in your ads
- Can be physical OR mental ("constantly overthinking" is a behavior)

**Examples:**
- Experience: Dog keeps pulling → Behavior: Only walks dog at 5am to avoid crowds
- Desire: Wants to lose 20lbs by summer → Behavior: Meal preps every Sunday
- Emotion: Feels insecure about skin → Behavior: Constantly orders new skincare products

**How to check it's a Behavior:** Does this describe what the person DOES? How often might they do it? (More frequent = stronger = more relatable)

#### Category 5: Demographics (Use LAST)
Static, factual characteristics. Gender, age, location, income, employment, etc.

- Most demographics are ASSUMED from the other 4 categories
- "Giving birth" experience → assumes female demographic
- Demographics drive NOWHERE NEAR as much purchase motivation as the other 4 categories
- Use only to add 1 extra bit of relatability, or when your product works specifically for a demographic

**The biggest mistake:** Isolating buyers based ONLY on demographics. A 22-year-old woman and a 54-year-old man can share the same desire, experience, emotion, and behavior.

### Rules of Thumb
- Avoid combining more than one Desire when building your avatar
- More categories used = more specific avatar
- More BROAD = better product/offer/marketing/brand required to convert
- More SPECIFIC = worse product/offer/marketing/brand can be and still convert
- The lower the revenue, the MORE specific you need to be
- As a brand, you EARN the right to go broader

### What About Beliefs?
Beliefs are NOT a separate category. They are Experiences + Emotions + Behaviors tied together.

"People who believe they can't lose weight" breaks down to:
- Experience: "Tried multiple diets and gained weight back"
- Emotion: "Feels hopeless about losing weight"
- Behavior: "Avoids starting new diets because expects failure"

When you spot beliefs, ask: "What happened to make them believe this?" (Experience), "How does this belief make them feel?" (Emotion), "What do they do because of this belief?" (Behavior)

## 8. Sub-Avatars & Avatar Scaling
**[Source: `Avatar Module Notes (4).docx`]**

### Core Avatar vs Sub-Avatar
- **Core Avatar**: Built from 1-2 categories (usually desire-based). Broad.
- **Sub-Avatar**: Core avatar + additional categories stacked on. Specific.

Avatars exist on a spectrum with infinite combinations. More categories combined = more specific = more relatable = easier to convert (but smaller audience).

### How to Build Sub-Avatars
Start with Core Avatar (desire), then layer on:

**Example - Blackout Blinds:**
- Core Avatar: "Wants better sleep" (Desire)
- Sub-Avatar 1: Wants better sleep (Desire) + baby wakes at 5am from morning light (Experience) + feels exhausted as a parent (Emotion)
- Sub-Avatar 2: Wants better sleep (Desire) + works night shift (Experience) + wears nose strips to bed (Behavior)
- Sub-Avatar 3: Wants better sleep (Desire) + snaps at family from exhaustion (Behavior) + feels guilty about being short-tempered (Emotion)

### How Many Avatars Should You Have?

| Revenue Stage | Recommendation |
|---------------|---------------|
| $0-100k/month | 1 Core Avatar, build Sub-Avatars around it. 99% should focus on a SINGLE desire-based Core Avatar |
| $100k-500k/month | Can expand to 2-3 Core Avatars with Sub-Avatars |
| $500k+/month | Can leverage core desires as starting points, broader avatars |

**If stuck after 2-4 weeks:** Change to a NEW desire-based Core Avatar. But focus and depth compound - the deeper you go, the better you get.

**Pro tip:** Look at your biggest competitor to see WHAT desire they scale with, then go deeper on sub-avatars to "Out-Avatar them" rather than "Out-Market them."

## 9. Copywriting Principles
**[Source: `Copy of Understanding the importance of copywriting (1).docx`]**

### Why Copywriting Is Non-Negotiable
"The Power of Your Copy Is In Direct Proportion To The Depth of Your Research" - Eugene Schwartz

Copywriting is not a nice-to-have. It's a MUST-HAVE skill. It's like learning a new language - rough at first, but flows naturally with practice. If there's one skill that will keep food on your table for the rest of your life, it's copywriting.

### The 7 Rules of Copy

**Rule 1: Nobody Cares About You, Your Company, or Your Product**
Frame everything through THEIR lens - their problems, desires, outcomes.
- Bad: "We've been in business for 20 years"
- Good: "For 20 years, we've helped people like you solve [problem]"

**Rule 2: Results Are All That Matter. Remove Your Emotion.**
The market decides what's good. Test, measure, let data tell you what converts. Never argue about copy - test it.

**Rule 3: Write How People Talk, Not How They Read**
Use contractions. Short sentences. Start with "And" or "But." Read it aloud - if it sounds stiff, rewrite it. Target 6th grade reading level (use hemingwayapp.com).
- Bad: "This supplement facilitates enhanced metabolic function"
- Good: "This tells your body to burn fat instead of storing it"

**Rule 4: Copy Is Never Too Long, Only Too Boring**
People interested in what you sell will read EVERYTHING - as long as it's engaging.

**Rule 5: Every Sentence Should Create a "Slippery Slide"**
Once they start reading, they can't stop. Use open loops, curiosity gaps. Never give them a natural place to stop until the CTA.

**Rule 6: Research Is 80% of the Work**
Great copy comes from great research. The writing is just organizing what you learned.
- Customer reviews (their exact language)
- Competitor claims (what's already been said)
- Amazon Q&A sections (questions = objections)
- Reddit/Facebook groups (raw, unfiltered pain points)
- Product mechanism (understand it deeply)
- Spend 2-3 hours researching for every 1 hour writing

**Rule 7: The Daily Practice (Joe Schriefer)**
Do this for 90 days and be better than 90% of copywriters:
1. **Read** a piece of copy (active reading - analyze the subtext)
2. **Write** a page of copy (for anything - ad, landing page, tweet)
3. **Come up with an idea** (keep asking "what if...")

### Copywriting Strategies for $100k/mo+

**Strategy 1: Understand What Humans Value**
- What is their dream outcome?
- How likely are they to get it?
- How long does it take?
- How much effort does it require?
- How much sacrifice does it require?

**Strategy 2: Use "Frames"**
Every claim should hit: NEW, EASY, SAFE, BIG
- Bad: "Lose weight fast" (only FAST)
- Good: "12,000 women blocked the enzyme destroying their appetite hormone and ate 24% less in week one - without trying. Try it risk-free." (all four)

**Strategy 3: Avoid Categorizing Yourself**
Don't say what you're "like." Position as the FIRST thing that does X, not another version of Y.
- Bad: "Better than other collagens" (categorized as collagen)
- Good: "The first supplement that blocks the enzyme destroying your appetite hormone" (new category)

**Strategy 4: Show, Don't Tell (Even With Words)**
TELLING triggers the logical brain (skepticism). SHOWING triggers the emotional brain (belief).
- Bad (TELLING): "You'll have more energy"
- Good (SHOWING): "Imagine waking up without hitting snooze three times. Walking past the vending machine at 3pm without even thinking about it."

**Strategy 5: Body Copy Structure - OBJECTION → CLAIM → PROOF → BENEFIT**
Cycle through this pattern multiple times for different objections:
1. **Objection**: What they're thinking/doubting (address head-on)
2. **Claim**: Your counter-statement
3. **Proof**: Evidence (study, testimonial, mechanism)
4. **Benefit**: Emotional payoff (what life looks like after)

**Strategy 6: Market Awareness Levels**
For under $100k/mo, focus only from Most Aware down to Problem Aware:
- Problem Aware → Solution Aware → Product Aware → Most Aware
- Unaware is NOT recommended under $100k/mo (saved for EVOLVE)

### Recommended Reading
- *$100M Offers* by Alex Hormozi
- *Take Their Money* by Kyle Milligan
- *Breakthrough Advertising* by Eugene Schwartz (core of EVOLVE program)

## 10. Angles & Hooks
**[Source: `Origins - Angle Identifier (2).docx`, `How To Create Ads (4).docx`]**

### Definitions

| Term | Definition | Focus |
|------|-----------|-------|
| **Concept** | The big idea / test strategy | Internally focused (what YOU want to test) |
| **Angle** | How you choose to sell the product | Externally focused (the customer's REASON TO BUY) |
| **Hook/Headline** | How you communicate the angle | How you get attention |
| **Variation** | Different executions of the angle | Different ways to say/show the same selling reason |
| **Format** | How you deliver the ad | Video type, image type, etc. |

### The Critical Distinction: Concept vs. Angle
Most marketers confuse these. This is why most ads fail.

**These are CONCEPTS, not angles:**
- "Us vs Them" → Comparing WHAT? The "what" is the angle
- "Before & After" → Transformation of WHAT? The "what" is the angle
- "Problem-Aware Ads" → WHAT problem? The "what" is the angle
- "Post-It Note Ads" → This is a FORMAT, not even a concept

**These ARE angles (customer reasons to buy):**
- "Stops tossing and turning"
- "Brings back your energy"
- "Buy it for life"

**When concept = angle:** "Test Buy It For Life Angle" → Concept AND Angle = "Buy It For Life"

**Golden rule:** For EVERY concept, always write down the angle (the reason someone should buy). Even if your concept IS an angle, jot it down separately.

### How to Extract Angles from Sub-Avatars
The KEY: Keep angles ACTIONABLE.

**Process:**
1. Look at the sub-avatar's **desire** (what they want)
2. Look at their **behavior** (what they currently do to get that desire)
3. Identify the **gap** (they're doing X to get Y, but it's not working)
4. The angle = how your product fills that gap

**Example:**
- Sub-Avatar: Nose Strip Sleeper
- Desire: Wants restorative sleep
- Behavior: Wears nose strips to bed
- Gap: Still not getting restorative sleep despite nose strips
- Angle: "Better than nose strips"

### Angles Exist on a Spectrum
Like avatars, angles range from broad to specific:
- Broad angle: "Want better sleep?" (technically an angle, but weak)
- Specific angle: "5 minutes to install. No more snapping at your kids by 3pm." (targets specific sub-avatar)

Broad avatars → broad angles → harder to convert.
Specific sub-avatars → specific angles → easier to convert.

### Hooks/Headlines
How you communicate the angle. As a junior writer, always keep hooks DIRECT (not indirect).

**Direct hook examples:**
| Angle | Hook |
|-------|------|
| Stops swelling | "14+ Hours. No Swelling" |
| Keeps you full like a meal | "This is a meal. Not a protein shake" |
| Stops snapping at people | "5 minutes to install. No more snapping" |
| Nutella turns bread into breakfast | "This is bread. This is breakfast" |
| Getting rid of double chin | "Drains double chin in 7 days" |

### AI Angle Identifier Prompt
There is a complete AI prompt in `Origins - Angle Identifier (2).docx` that:
- Takes product info + sub-avatar profiles as input
- Generates 3 potential marketing angles per sub-avatar
- Provides 3 strategic hooks/headlines per angle
- Ranks strongest angles and hooks first
- Reminds user they can test multiple angles in one concept
- Outputs in a structured format with testing priority recommendations

## 11. Ad Creation & Testing Methods
**[Source: `How To Create Ads (4).docx`]**

### The 3:2:2 Testing Structure
- **3** = 3 creative variations of 1 concept
- **2:2** = 2 headlines + 2 body copies for that concept inside Meta

### The 3 Testing Methods

#### Marksman Method (Use for Images)
Test multiple angles at once to find direction FAST, in a controlled environment.

**How it works:**
- Test 3 different angles congruently in your 3:2:2
- Pick the 1 best hook for each angle/sub-avatar

**Pros:** More fluid, less rigid, find direction FASTER when you lack data
**Cons:** Can't go as deep, harder to say definitively "this is why it's working"

**For video (Marksman):**
- Hooks = Call out the specific sub-avatar/angle (specific)
- Hold = Demonstrates the shared desire of the core avatar (generic/universal)
- Example: Hook 1: "POV: You work night shift" / Hook 2: "POV: You're a light sleeper" / Hook 3: "POV: You have insomnia" → Hold: Shows blinds closing → complete darkness → peaceful sleep
- IMPORTANT: If your hold is TOO specific to one angle's context, it won't work for other hooks

#### Sniper Method (Use for Videos)
Test different executions of ONE specific angle to optimize it.

**How it works:**
- Once you find a winning angle, go all-in testing variations of that angle
- 3 variations = different executions of the same angle

**Pros:** Clearer understanding of what works, more confidence in validated angles
**Cons:** Less fluid, restricts creativity, can put you in iteration overload

#### The Flow
1. **Marksman** → Find direction (which angle resonates)
2. **Sniper** → Go deep on winning angle (optimize variations)
3. **Marksman again** → When performance plateaus, test new angles

### The Simplified Rule
If confused about methods:
- **Marksman Method = Image Ads**
- **Sniper Method = Video Ads**
- Just make the BEST ad you can for every angle/sub-avatar
- Complete feedback loops so you know what to go deeper on

### The 3-Step Process
1. Test multiple angles initially to find something that gets sales or wins
2. Make hypotheses on WHY something is winning
3. Test those hypotheses to narrow down and optimize

**Your superpower:** Most people take broad learnings like "video ads work" or "good hook rate." YOU will identify the sub-avatar, the angle, and do more research to write customer-first ads.

**The faster you figure out why something IS NOT winning, the FASTER you can scale.**
**The faster you figure out why something IS winning, the FASTER you can scale.**

## 13. Offers
**[Source: `How To Create Offers Module  (3).docx`, `_Origins _ Offer Mastery (1).pptx`]**

### Why Offers Matter
Offers are the most important part of the landing page.

### Critical Warnings
1. **Offers are addictive.** They can spike ROAS from 2x to 4x overnight, pump conversion rates - but can become IMPOSSIBLE to get off and be OVERLY relied on for customer acquisition
2. **ALWAYS use the profit calculator BEFORE setting up any offer.** Establish new ROAS & CPA targets so you know if it's actually successful. A 30% offer that keeps you at 2x ROAS but drops gross margin from 70% to 55% can DESTROY profit even while revenue increases.

### Offer Types (Ranked by Recommendation)

| Type | Examples | Recommendation |
|------|----------|---------------|
| **Bundle & Save** | "The Staple 6-Pack \| Regular $265 Now $186", "Rat Protection Bundle \| Rat Vac + Capz + Vent + eBook \| Regular $371 now $249" | Highly Recommended (Favorite) |
| **$X Amount Off** | "Save $20!", "Save $30 On First Orders!", "Save Up To $80 Sitewide" | Highly Recommended |
| **%X Amount Off** | "Save 10%", "Save 25% On First Orders!", "Save Up To 80% Sitewide" | Highly Recommended |
| **Buy X Get X Free** | "Buy 1 Get 1 Free", "Buy 2 Get 1 Free", "Buy X Get Another Product Free" | Highly Recommended |
| **Buy X Get Y% Off** | "Buy 1 Get 1 50% Off!", "Buy 1 Get X Product 15% Off" | Recommended if bad margins |
| **Free Gift** | "Free Mystery Gift when you spend X", Free complementary product, Free eBook, Free gift wrapping (Q4) | Add-on, not core offer |
| **Free Shipping** | "Free Shipping On All Orders", "Free Shipping On First Orders", "Free Expedited Shipping" | Add-on, not core offer |

### Bundle & Save Best Practices
- Group commonly purchased products together at a discount
- Provide a REASON for the bundle
- Think of a NAME for the bundle
- Can raise AOV if you contact supplier and ship add-ons in one package

### Law of Large Numbers
If the percentage looks bigger than the dollar amount, use percentage. If dollar amount looks bigger, use dollar amount. Vice versa.

## 14. Feedback Loops
**[Source: `Origins _ Feedback Loops.docx`, `Ad Creatives feedback loop cheatsheat .docx`]**

### The Core Loop
**Do → Get Output → Analyze Output → Change Input → Repeat**

This is THE growth engine. Complete feedback loops on EVERY ad - whether it works OR doesn't work.

### Feedback Loop Template
**Step 1: Hypothesis**
- Why did this work? OR Why did this NOT work?
- Rank hypotheses by confidence level

**Step 2: Action**
- What am I doing differently next time?
- Specific changes based on hypotheses

### Ad Creatives Feedback Loop Cheatsheet (15 Checkpoints)
Before submitting any brief, ask:
1. Does the hook grab the RIGHT user?
2. Is it native to the platform?
3. Does it feel authentic?
4. Does it educate creatively?
5. Does it tell a story?
6. Is the angle clear and specific?
7. Does it target a specific sub-avatar?
8. Is the copy at 6th grade reading level?
9. Does every sentence create a slippery slide?
10. Is the CTA clear?
11. Does the body copy follow OBJECTION → CLAIM → PROOF → BENEFIT?
12. Are we showing, not telling?
13. Is the format appropriate for the angle?
14. Have we defined what we're testing and why?
15. Do we have a hypothesis for what will win and why?

### Processing Results
When something WINS, ask:
- What sub-avatar did this resonate with?
- What was the angle?
- What was the hook pattern?
- How can we go deeper on this direction?

When something LOSES, ask:
- Was it the angle, the hook, the format, or the execution?
- What would we change?
- Is there a different sub-avatar we should target?
