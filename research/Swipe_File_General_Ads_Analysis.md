## SECTION 1: The 13 Named Framework Ads

### 1. ACCIDENTAL IDEAL OUTCOME

**Example:** Happy Mammoth — "I Accidentally Lost 12 Pounds At 52"

**Why it works:**
- The word "accidentally" creates massive curiosity ("How do you accidentally lose weight?")
- Call-out points use vivid visual language: "blocking fat cells," "dissolving old fat"
- Uses the **Law of Unique Numbers** — "12 pounds," "52," "56.4%" feel more real than round numbers

**NMN Ad:**
> "I Accidentally Started Sleeping Like I Was 28 Again At 53"
> - Restores NAD+ levels that decline with age

> - 47.3% of users reported better sleep in the first 2 weeks

## SECTION 5: Key Takeaways

1. **The swipe file reveals 13 distinct static ad frameworks.** Not all are equal for supplements — the top 5 for NMN are: Pain Point Question Hook, Us vs Them, Don't Get Scammed, Testimonial Screenshot, and Ingredient Breakdown.

2. **Your QR-to-COA is your killer differentiator.** Multiple swipe ads use "3rd party tested" as a trust badge. You can go further — "Scan the bottle. See your batch's lab results." That visual of a QR code on the bottle is an ad in itself.

3. **"Don't Get Scammed" is your category-defining opportunity.** The NMN market on Amazon is a mess of untested, questionable-purity products. You can own the "transparency" position the way Fodzyme owns "more than Lactaid."

4. **The Accidental Ideal Outcome and Unique Numbers patterns should be in every testimonial ad.** Don't say "I lost weight" — say "I accidentally dropped 12 pounds at 52." Don't say "I have more energy" — say "I went from 2 coffees to zero by day 11."

5. **Run cold traffic humor/meme ads to build the pixel.** The Venn diagram and meme formats get massive engagement and shares — they build your retargeting audience cheaply so the conversion ads (testimonials, offers, urgency) have someone to target.

6. **Match the ad framework to the funnel stage.** Pain Point Question Hook stops cold traffic. Ingredient Breakdown educates warm traffic. Classy Offer closes hot traffic. Never run a flash sale to someone who's never heard of you.
