# Native Course Analysis — Part 2: Phase 1 — Before You Write a Single Word

> **Source file:** `Native Course/02-Phase 1_ Before you write a single word.docx`
> **What this module is:** The research framework you MUST complete before writing any copy. The course's argument: most ads fail not because of bad copy, but because the writer is guessing about the audience. This module replaces guessing with diagnosis.

---

## The Core Thesis

> **"You can't write a native ad that feels real if you don't know who you're talking to. Most ads fail before the first word is written — not because the copy is bad, but because the writer is guessing. They know the product. They don't know the person."**

This is the course's direct challenge to the "just start writing" approach. The promise: 30-60 minutes of research upfront replaces 10+ hours of copy rewrites later.

Critical distinction: the course says "skip it and your ads will sound like ads." Meaning the research isn't optional polish — it's the thing that determines whether the final ad passes the camouflage test.

---

## Section 1: The 6 Diagnosis Questions

These aren't generic marketing questions. They're specifically designed to extract the RAW material you need to write native copy that sounds like it was written by the customer herself.

### Question 1: "Who is this for exactly?"

**The course explicitly rejects demographics.** It wants psychographics.

What it asks for:
- What does their day look like?
- What MOMENT are they in? (Starting to notice the problem, deep in frustration, ready to give up?)
- What are they doing when they see your ad?

**Application to tallow:**
We have the 5 sub-avatars (Invisible Woman, Product Graveyard, Overnight Changer, Eczema Fighter, Chemical Refugee) but we haven't mapped them to specific "moments." This is a gap.

For Batch 1 (Invisible Woman), the moment is: **she's standing in front of her bathroom mirror in the morning, noticing her face feels different, scrolling Instagram while her coffee brews.** That specific moment should inform the tone. The copy should feel like someone WHO IS IN THAT MOMENT, not someone reflecting back on it from a distance.

### Question 2: "What have they already tried?"

**Why it matters:** "They're not a blank slate. They're skeptical. If your ad sounds like the same stuff they've tried, they scroll."

The course is clear — listing failed attempts in the ad copy is what establishes credibility. When you mention the specific products they've tried, they feel SEEN.

**Application to tallow:**
Our Product Graveyard ads already do this well. B2C1 specifically lists "retinol, hyaluronic acid, ceramides, vitamin C" — these are the exact products the avatar has tried. That list IS the credibility move. We should expand this pattern to B1 ads too.

### Question 3: "What are they emotionally tired of?"

**Critical distinction the course makes:** Not tired of THE PROBLEM — tired of the EMOTIONAL EXHAUSTION that comes with the problem.

Examples the course gives:
- Being dismissed by doctors
- Advice from people who don't get it
- Trying things that work for everyone else but not them
- Feeling like a failure

**Application to tallow:**
Our Invisible Woman avatar is emotionally tired of being DISMISSED. "It's just aging." "That's just menopause." "You need more sleep." "Drink more water." She's not tired of dry skin — she's tired of being told her problem doesn't matter or is her fault.

B1C3 (dermatologist) hints at this but doesn't make it the emotional core. The course suggests this emotional exhaustion SHOULD be front and center. I'd rewrite B1C3's opening to lean harder on the dismissal angle — not "I asked my dermatologist" but "I was tired of being told this was just aging."

### Question 4: "What do they believe is the cause?"

The course acknowledges these beliefs might be wrong — but they're real and they shape how the customer sees solutions.

Examples the course gives:
- "My metabolism slowed down"
- "It's genetic"
- "I don't have willpower"
- "It's stress"

**Why it matters:** "If they believe the cause is genetics, they'll be skeptical of anything that claims to help — because how can you fight genetics? Your ad either works WITH this belief or shifts it."

**Application to tallow:**
Our Invisible Woman avatar likely believes:
- "It's aging, there's nothing I can do"
- "I need better products"
- "I need to drink more water"
- "I need a more expensive moisturizer"

Our ad needs to SHIFT these beliefs, specifically belief #2 (she thinks she needs better products — we need to show her it's the TYPE of product, not the brand). We partially do this in B2C2 ("all the same thing in different bottles").

### Question 5: "What do they believe is NOT the cause?"

**This is where the course says the opportunity lives.**

> **"If they think it's willpower but it's actually hormonal, you've found your angle. The belief they're missing is your opening."**

This is the **new mechanism** reframe in disguise. The course is saying: find the thing they're NOT considering, and that becomes your belief shift.

**Application to tallow:**
Our Invisible Woman avatar is NOT considering:
- That her body has literally stopped producing the oil her skin needs (sebum decline)
- That water-based moisturizers fundamentally can't fix the problem
- That her skin needs a FAT, not a lotion

This is exactly the angle we're already using in Section 4 of the PDP and in the mechanism section of B1C3. The course validates this approach.

### Question 6: "What are they afraid to try next?"

The course calls out the fear that stops clicks even when belief exists:
- More money wasted
- More hope crushed
- Looking stupid for believing another ad

**Application to tallow:**
Our Invisible Woman has spent $300+ on skincare this year. Her fear is: "this is going to be another $50 jar that does nothing." The course suggests we should NAME this fear inside the ad copy itself.

**I don't think we currently do this.** B2C1 hints at it ("at this point, what's one more jar?") but it's a throwaway line, not an addressed fear. The course implies this should be a deliberate moment in the copy.

## Section 2: Male vs Female Psychology

The course's summary is short but important:

> **"Women see the problem through relationships and identity. Men see it through status and capability. Write to the wrong lens and they'll just leave."**

Both genders need specificity, believable proof, and real pain. But the frame is different:
- **Women:** How does this affect who I am and who I love?
- **Men:** How does this affect what I can do and how I rank?

**Application to tallow:**
100% a women's product. Our frame should be identity + relationships. Our best-performing B1C2 hook ("My husband looked at me and said your skin looks different") is exactly this — it's identity (her skin as part of her identity) filtered through relationships (her husband's perception).

This validates our existing angle. We should keep leaning into relationship-filtered identity for this audience.

## Section 3: Awareness Levels

The course narrows the universe to **three** awareness levels that native ads should target. Unaware and Most Aware are explicitly excluded.

### Problem Aware
- They KNOW something's wrong, they FEEL it daily
- They don't fully understand why
- Not searching for solutions yet
- Looking for someone who GETS what they're going through
- Skeptical of products because they don't trust their own diagnosis

**When they see your ad:** They're thinking about their situation, not products. They're wondering if anyone else feels this way.

### Solution Aware
- They know solutions exist
- They've tried some and been disappointed
- Comparing options, reading reviews, looking for proof
- Don't need education on the problem

**When they see your ad:** They're asking "Why should I believe THIS one? What makes it different?"

### Product Aware
- They know YOUR product specifically
- Seen your ads before, maybe visited your site, maybe abandoned cart
- Need the final push

**When they see your ad:** They're looking for a reason to act NOW. Social proof, risk reversal, urgency.

### Why only these three?
The course is explicit:
- **Unaware audiences = too expensive to educate.** You're paying to teach people they have a problem.
- **Most Aware audiences = don't need ads.** They've already bought. Retention lives in email/SMS.

**Application to tallow:**
Our Invisible Woman and Product Graveyard are both **Problem Aware** to **Solution Aware**:
- Invisible Woman: Problem Aware (she knows her skin changed but doesn't know why) → Solution Aware (she's tried products and is evaluating)
- Product Graveyard: Solution Aware (deep in solution fatigue) → borderline Product Aware (she's just looking for a reason to stop searching)

Our current ads mostly target Solution Aware. **We don't have a pure Problem Aware ad.** A Problem Aware ad would NOT mention tallow or sebum or moisturizer. It would just name the experience ("your skin changed and you don't know why"). That's a future batch.

## Section 4: The Believability Quadrant

Two questions determine how hard the sell is:
1. Do they believe in this TYPE of solution? (Have they bought this category before?)
2. Does your mechanism align with what they already believe? (Does your explanation make them nod, or raise an eyebrow?)

The four quadrants implied by these two axes:
- **Easy Sell:** Believes in category + mechanism aligns
- **Education Sell:** Doesn't believe in category + mechanism aligns
- **Reframe Sell:** Believes in category + mechanism conflicts
- **Hard Sell:** Doesn't believe in category + mechanism conflicts

**Application to tallow:**
Our avatar:
- **Believes in skincare category?** YES (she's bought many moisturizers)
- **Does tallow mechanism align with her existing beliefs?** NO (she believes skincare should be water-based, clean, chemical — tallow is the opposite)

We're in the **Reframe Sell** quadrant. She already buys skincare but her beliefs about what GOOD skincare is conflict with ours. Our job isn't to sell her on skincare (she's sold). It's to **reframe her understanding of what works**.

This matches our current strategy in B2C2 ("all the same thing in different bottles") — we're reframing her understanding of what she's been buying. The course validates this is the right angle for our believability situation.

## Section 5: Dominant Emotion

**The course's sharpest distinction:**

> **"Surface pain is what they'll say out loud. Real pain is what they think about at 2am."**

The ad must speak to the REAL pain.

### How to find the dominant emotion:

1. **"What relationships does it affect?"**
   A problem that affects how they see themselves = one thing. A problem that affects their marriage, kids, friendships = DEEPER.

2. **"What situations make it spike?"**
   When does it go from background noise to unbearable? Getting dressed for an event. Being tagged in a photo. A comment from their partner. **These are the moments your copy should describe.**

3. **"What do they feel underneath?"**
   Shame, loss of control, fear, grief, invisibility — which one?

### The Emotional Map
The course says every reader exists on a spectrum, mostly in the "lower half" (more negative emotions). The ad's job is to move them up one level at a time. **Not jumping from grief to joy in one paragraph** — moving one notch.

**Application to tallow:**
Our Invisible Woman's real pain isn't "dry skin." It's **invisibility**. She feels like she's disappearing. Nobody notices her anymore. Her husband used to notice her, her kids used to want her in photos, her reflection used to show someone she recognized. Now all of that is gone.

**The 2am thought is NOT "my skin is dry."** It's **"I'm becoming invisible and I don't know how to stop it."**

Our B1C2 ad (husband noticed) accidentally hits this. The husband noticing is the REVERSAL of invisibility. But we got there by luck, not design. The course suggests this should be the DELIBERATE emotional anchor for the whole Invisible Woman batch.

**Specific spike situations for our avatar:**
- Getting tagged in a photo from a reunion
- Putting on foundation and watching it cake into fine lines
- Her daughter asking why she doesn't FaceTime anymore
- Looking at a photo from 5 years ago

Any of these moments should appear in our ad copy as the spike. **Our current ads don't have these specific moments.** That's a gap to fix in Batch 3+.

## Section 6: Extracting Audience Language

**The course's rule:** If your copy sounds like marketing, they tune out. If it sounds like the words in their own head, they lean in.

### Where to find it:
The course references finding exact phrases in real customer sources. For us, that's:
- Reddit (r/30PlusSkinCare, r/menopause, r/SkincareAddiction)
- Amazon reviews of competing products
- Facebook group comments
- YouTube video comments on menopause/skincare content

### What to look for:
- **Phrases that repeat across multiple people** (that's signal, not noise)
- **Exact words THEY use** (not your words)
- **How they describe failed solutions** ("I tried X and it made me feel...")
- **Emotions underneath the complaints** (angry? defeated? embarrassed? hopeful?)

### Example phrases the course gives (women's weight loss):
- "I'm doing everything right and the scale won't budge"
- "I don't even recognize myself anymore"
- "My doctor just told me to eat less and move more"
- "Nothing works the way it used to"
- "I feel like a stranger in my own body"
- "I'm so tired of trying"

**Application to tallow:**
We ALREADY DID THIS WORK. Our `tallow-cream/avatars/Avatar_Language_Research.md` file has 40+ verbatim quotes from Reddit and Amazon reviews. Rereading it through this lens, the strongest phrases we collected were:

- "My skin doesn't look tired anymore"
- "I feel like I actually look my age — not ten years older"
- "Can I really have glass skin at this age?"
- "I have tried every single moisturizer under the sun"
- "I'm so tired of trying"
- "Nothing works the way it used to"

**The course validates what we did.** But it also reveals we haven't USED this language aggressively enough in our current ads. B1C1 uses "desert dry" which is strong but not pulled from real customer language. We should do a pass through the ads and make sure every emotional moment is anchored in a phrase we pulled directly from the research.

## Key Takeaways From Phase 1 For Our Tallow Launch

1. **We have most of the research done.** Our Avatar_Language_Research.md and Avatar_Blueprint.md cover the majority of what Phase 1 asks for. We didn't waste that work.

2. **The biggest gap is the "fear of trying next" question.** We never explicitly asked what our avatar is afraid of when clicking another skincare ad. She's afraid of wasting another $50 and feeling stupid. Our ads should address this head-on.

3. **We're Solution Aware too heavily.** All 6 of our current ads target Solution Aware. We need Problem Aware ads in Batch 3+ for cold top-of-funnel traffic that hasn't been hit with skincare ads before.

4. **Invisibility should be the deliberate emotional anchor of B1.** Not just "tired skin" but "feeling invisible." The dominant emotion is stronger than we've been writing to.

5. **The believability reframe is our angle.** She believes in skincare, but not in tallow. The course confirms this is the exact frame we should be running.

6. **Spike moments are missing.** The course says to describe SPECIFIC situations where the pain spikes (getting tagged in a photo, foundation caking into lines). Our current ads describe general frustration but not these specific trigger moments.
