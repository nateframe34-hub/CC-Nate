## Part A: The Doctor Ad Case Study — Full Deconstruction

The course uses one ad as a running case study across every module. I read the full ad to understand what a "complete" native ad looks like when every principle is applied.

### The Ad's Setup
- **Product:** Grounding sheets (electrically conductive bed sheets)
- **Character:** ER physician with 20 years of experience
- **Awareness Level:** Problem Aware → Solution Aware (the ad works to shift the reader through these)
- **Believability Quadrant:** Reframe Sell (reader knows about sleep problems, the mechanism is unusual)
- **Dominant Emotion:** Fear (of inevitable health collapse)
- **Theme:** "The system won't save you. You have to save yourself."

### The Full Structure Mapped

**Hook (opening 3-4 paragraphs):**
> "If you've been running on bad sleep for years, I need you to hear this. Your body is a ticking time bomb. And no doctor is going to warn you until it's too late."

Then the narrator introduces himself (ER physician, 20 years) and tells a specific scene: last month, a 52-year-old woman collapsed in her bathroom.

**What this hook does:**
- Direct threat ("ticking time bomb")
- Authority establishment (20-year ER doctor)
- Inciting incident (woman collapsing)
- Open loop (what happened to her?)
- Pattern interrupt (ER doctor giving health advice on Facebook)

Five mechanisms in four paragraphs. Our current ads use 1-2 mechanisms per hook. The Doctor Ad stacks five.

**Body Section 1 — The pattern expansion:**
The doctor describes that this isn't unusual. He sees it every week. He lists the symptoms he sees. Uses dialogue from patients ("How's your sleep been?").

**What this does:**
- Stakes escalation (it's not just one person, it's a pattern)
- Dialogue interrupt (breaks up narration)
- Expert credibility (he's the one asking the question)

**Body Section 2 — The mechanism reveal:**
He explains the science. Chronic stress → cortisol → inflammation → blood vessels → brain → autoimmune issues. The whole chain.

**What this does:**
- New information (most readers don't know this chain)
- Trust ladder (verifiable medical facts)
- Fear escalation (stakes get bigger)

**Body Section 3 — The doctor's frustration:**
> "Now here's what makes me angry."

He pivots to his own frustration with the system. Doctors treat the crisis, not the cause. Patients come back sicker. This is a pattern interrupt (emotional shift) and it earns the narrator's authority further (he's not selling, he's angry about a problem).

**Body Section 4 — The discovery:**
> "That's when I found something that changed everything I thought I knew."

Open loop. Then he reveals the mechanism (grounding / earthing) and explicitly addresses his own skepticism: "I'm a trained physician. I was skeptical as hell. But the science is real."

**What this does:**
- Skeptic convert frame (overcomes reader's likely objection)
- Open loop closed
- Scientific credibility layer

**Body Section 5 — The mechanism explained:**
Bodies run on electricity. Every cell has a charge. You need to discharge. Studies exist. Physics, biology, real.

**Body Section 6 — The product introduction:**
Only NOW does the product get named (it's redacted in the case study doc but the structure shows the product doesn't appear until probably 60% through the ad).

**Body Section 7 — His personal story:**
He tried it himself. Was skeptical. ER shifts are brutal. Describes his own low point ("accepted I was going to be tired for the rest of my career"). Then the transformation unfolds by day:
- Day 1-2: Nothing dramatic
- Day 3: Slept through without waking at 3am
- Week 1: Waking up rested
- Week 2: Shoulder tension gone (unexpected discovery)
- Week 3: Husband noticed (external validation)
- Final: "I felt like myself again"

**Body Section 8 — Social proof from patients:**
The "frequent flyers" stopped coming back to his ER. He names specific patient stories — different demographics, different conditions, different use cases. Each one validates something different.

**Body Section 9 — The warning:**
> "Now, here's the important part. And I need you to hear this. Not all [products] actually work."

He warns against cheap alternatives. Explains why his specific product is different. Says he's bought 7 himself for family members.

**Close:**
Urgency (sale happening, inventory low), risk reversal (90-day guarantee), confession ("I'm not supposed to be writing things like this"), fork-in-the-road ("don't wait until you're in my ER"), CTA.

### The Word Count
The full ad is approximately **1,400-1,800 words.** Our current ads are 600-900 words.

**We are roughly HALF the length of the reference ad.**

### What the Doctor Ad Has That We Don't

1. **More mechanisms stacked in the hook** (we use 1-2, it uses 5)
2. **A specific inciting incident** (woman collapsing in bathroom)
3. **Stakes escalation** (patient becomes pattern becomes chain of consequences)
4. **Pattern interrupts** ("Now here's what makes me angry")
5. **Full mechanism explanation** (the science taught, not hinted at)
6. **Skeptic convert frame** (explicitly addresses his own skepticism)
7. **Multiple secondary characters** (4-5 patients, each different)
8. **Warning against alternatives** (addresses "which one should I buy" objection)
9. **Longer transformation arc with unexpected discoveries**
10. **Confession close** ("I'm not supposed to be writing this")

**Our ads have maybe 3 of these 10. The Doctor Ad has all 10.**

This is the biggest gap analysis I can do. If we want to write ads at the level of what the course considers "working," we need to roughly double our word count AND add 5-7 of these elements to each ad.

## Part B: The AI Prompts Bonus

### What It Is
A template for generating native ads with AI. Four input categories, one mega prompt.

### The Four Input Categories
1. **Research** — 6 diagnosis questions, awareness level, believability quadrant, dominant emotion, audience language
2. **Story elements** — character, theme, setting, low point, transformation, secondary characters
3. **Product info** — what it is, mechanism, differentiation, proof, offer
4. **Examples** — 2-3 full ads that match the style/tone you want

### The Mega Prompt Structure
The prompt tells the AI:
- You're a direct response copywriter
- Write a long-form native primary text ad
- Match the awareness level
- Use the character/theme/story elements I specify
- **Sound like the examples I've provided** (match rhythm, structure, tone)

### The Critical Insight
> **"Examples are what show AI the rhythm, structure, and tone you want."**

The course says the prompt only works if you include 2-3 full example ads as input. Without examples, the AI produces generic copy.

### The Swipe File Problem
The course references a Foreplay board of 200+ winning ads: `https://app.foreplay.co/share/boards/kmgyoPjhabnyItWhZDZ4`

**This is the most valuable asset in the course, and we don't have access to it unless you sign up for Foreplay.**

### Application to Our Workflow

**What we can do right now without the swipe file:**
1. Use the Doctor Ad case study as Example 1
2. Use the BV ad and hip pain ad from `origins-training/swipe-file/origins-native-swipe` as Example 2 and 3
3. Fill out the research and story element sections for each sub-avatar
4. Run the mega prompt

**What we should add to the workflow:**
- A "winning ads" folder in the repo where we save screenshots of ads we see in the wild that match our style
- A system for tagging them by character type, hook format, and awareness level
- Build our own swipe library over time

### The Meta Lesson About AI Copy Generation

The course is effectively saying: **AI is only as good as your inputs, and your inputs must include examples.** Generic prompts produce generic copy. Stacked examples + detailed research + specific story elements produce copy that matches the examples' quality.

This validates what we've been doing — we keep reading the research files before writing copy. The course says we should go further: feed the AI the research files AND example ads simultaneously.

2. ⚠️ **All 6 closes are interchangeable** — write unique closes per ad, each connecting to its own hook

### Gaps to Fix in Batch 3+ (Medium Priority — Real Improvements)
1. **Add specific inciting incidents** to every ad (currently summaries, should be scenes)
2. **Identify and deploy THEME** for each sub-avatar (Invisible Woman = invisibility, Product Graveyard = hope vs helplessness)
3. **Escalate stakes** throughout copy, not just in the problem section
4. **Add explicit low-point moments** of resignation (not crisis)
5. **Stack 3-4 secondary characters** per ad instead of 1
6. **Add unexpected discovery moments** to transformation sections
7. **Build explicit trust ladders** — observable truths → new information → claims
8. **Insert open loops** that pull the reader forward
9. **Add pattern interrupts** every 200-300 words
10. **Vary pacing deliberately** (short, long, fragment, pause)
11. **Embed contrasts throughout** — not just before/after at the end
12. **Answer objections inside the ad** (smell, greasy, breakouts) instead of saving them for the PDP
13. **Target Problem Aware AND Product Aware** audiences — not just Solution Aware
14. **Test 3-4 new hook formats** — statistic shock, skeptic framing, confession opener, direct threat
15. **Approximately DOUBLE word count** from 600-900 to 1,200-1,800

### Gaps in Image Strategy for Batch 3+
1. Test **Type 3 isolation images** (woman alone at 11pm researching)
2. Test **first-person POV shots** (her hands in a bathroom mirror)
3. Make the **product graveyard messier** (more chaos, less styled)

## The Single Most Important Insight From the Entire Course

> **"The reader isn't evaluating your product. They're evaluating the person talking to them."**

Every technique in the course serves this one principle. The character choice, the story elements, the pacing, the hooks, the images — all of it is in service of making the reader trust the NARRATOR.

**If the narrator feels real, the product gets trusted by extension.**
**If the narrator feels fake, the product gets rejected regardless of how good the copy is.**

Our NMN postmortem missed this. We built ads around the PRODUCT (NMN, cellular energy, NAD+ decline). We should have been building ads around the NARRATOR (a real woman with real frustration, speaking to a real friend).

The tallow launch has a better chance because the product itself is more narrator-compatible (you CAN'T talk about tallow without sounding like a person — "cow fat on my face" is inherently first-person). But we still need to lean harder into the narrator being believable.
