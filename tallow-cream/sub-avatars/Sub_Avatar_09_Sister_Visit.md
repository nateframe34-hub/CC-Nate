# Sub-Avatar 9: The Sister Visit

**Status:** Written, awaiting launch (B15C1 — launch delayed to ~May 27)
**Ads built for this Sub-Avatar:** B15C1 (The Sister Visit)
**Lifetime sales:** 0 (not yet tested)

---

## Anchor
- **Core Avatar:** Invisible Woman
- **Core Desire:** I want to be seen again — and I want my sister to look at me on Saturday morning and not have anything alarming to say

---

## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want to stop avoiding my own sister."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, anchored to family blood. The Sub-Avatar's specificity: the sister is the family member from whom you can't hide. Friends will be polite. Husbands will rationalize. Sister will tell you the truth, and you know it before she opens her mouth. The desire is to be able to receive the sister's gaze without flinching — to be in the kitchen at coffee on Saturday and not be assessed by someone who has known your face for forty years and will say what she sees.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Relationships (primary — blood family) + Belonging (secondary — family closeness restored after avoidance)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience:** Sister Lisa drove up from Cleveland for the long weekend. They hadn't been in the same room in 18 months. Saturday morning at the kitchen table with coffee. Lisa looks at her for what felt like a long time. Then says: "What's happening with your skin? You look ten years older than me." Lisa is 49. The narrator is 53. Lisa didn't say it to hurt her. She said it because she was alarmed.
- **Product Experience:** CeraVe blue tub for six months — nothing held past noon. Retinol from a six-minute dermatologist visit, burned for two weeks, cracking got worse. Hyaluronic acid serum from a coworker, $80 jar gone in six weeks. By 52 she'd given up. Told herself it was fine. It wasn't fine.
- **Active or past?** Active — the Saturday morning + the 18-month avoidance is the immediate context

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Sadness (specifically: avoidant grief — she stopped meeting Lisa for lunch when they were in the same city, twice; told herself she was busy, wasn't busy)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Alarmed-recognition stemming from Sadness. The hook isn't shame — it's a sister's alarm landing exactly because shame had been preventing the conversation for 18 months. Per Origins, the secondary emotion here is "Witnessed" stemming from Sadness: the moment the family member you can't hide from looks at you and names what you've been failing to name yourself. The grief that hits is for the avoidance, not for the skin.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit:** Turns off the overhead bathroom light before doing her makeup. Three years. The harsh light bothered her. She didn't want to see what she'd look like under it. Done every morning, automatically, for so long she'd stopped thinking about it.
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (the cracking foundation, the tight cheeks) + Emotion (avoidance of being witnessed by her own mirror)
- **Frequency / strength:** Daily, automatic, three years. Strength = the cessation is invisible to her until Steve flips the light on without thinking and she doesn't notice — and only realizes in traffic on the way to work that she'd done her makeup with the overhead light on like a normal person.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 50-58
- Has a sister (blood, younger or close in age) — without the sister, the Sub-Avatar doesn't exist
- **Married 22+ years** (Steve flips the light on without thinking because they've shared a bathroom for over two decades — without the long marriage, this habit-cessation beat doesn't land)
- Geographic separation that creates visit-gaps (Pittsburgh / Cleveland in B15C1 — the 18-month gap is what makes the visit a discrete event)

## Verbatim Language Patterns

- **Phrases she uses to describe her problem:**
  - "What's happening with your skin? You look ten years older than me." (Lisa, the hook)
  - "She didn't say it to hurt me. She said it because she was alarmed."
  - "I'd just been avoiding her, in the small way you avoid people who'd see something you don't want them to see."
  - "I stopped meeting Lisa for lunch when we were in the same city. Twice. I told myself I was busy. I wasn't busy."
  - "That was the thing I wanted back. Not the soft cheeks. The not-needing-the-light-off."

- **Internal-monologue patterns:**
  - Quietly identifies the avoidance behavior to herself but doesn't tell anyone
  - Categorizes the sister's bluntness as alarm, not cruelty
  - Does the per-use cost math when given the chance (Lisa runs the math at the kitchen island: $100/month → $20/month)
  - Sends a selfie back without a caption when day-21 arrives

- **Words she'd embrace:** alarmed, sister, the kitchen table, blood, the not-needing-the-light-off, look like yourself again, fifty cents a day
- **Words she'd reject:** menopause, hormones, anti-aging, hiding, shame, self-acceptance, body positive, glowing, radiant, lifting

## Notes

The structural hypothesis behind this Sub-Avatar is that family-blunt witness creates a different conversion pathway than peer-friend or professional witness. Friends can be tactful. Sisters cannot. The Sub-Avatar's emotional engine is the recognition that she's been avoiding someone whose love is precisely what makes them say what they see.

This is the first Sub-Avatar to include both the per-use cost math AND the proven B5C1 husband-notice phrase ("your skin looks different") in the same ad. The 12-variable test matrix for B15 deliberately layered new variables (per-use cost mention, mechanism delivered with cited outside authority, sister-as-discovery-character) on top of the locked converter formula.

What we expect to learn:
- Whether sister-as-witness reaches a different sub-segment than friend-as-witness. Hypothesis: yes, because the sister carries family-system weight that no peer can. If CTR is similar to converters but conversion is higher, the sister frame is doing close-the-deal work that peer-witnesses don't.
- Whether the per-use cost math ($100/mo → $20/mo, ~$0.55/day) lifts conversion. B3C2 is the only existing converter with per-use cost mention and is also the converter with the lowest sale price ($19.99 discount error). Whether per-use math actually converts or just appeals to the analytical sub-segment is the open question.
- Whether the light-avoidance habit-cessation beat (turning off the bathroom overhead light before makeup) reads as functional/environmental rather than vanity. If it reads as vanity, the beat needs to be replaced with something more clearly functional in future ads.
- Whether the cited-outside-authority mechanism delivery (Lisa relays what her own GP Dr. Trent told her at her annual physical) is more credible than peer-direct mechanism delivery (Lisa just explains it herself). If yes, future Sub-Avatars should bake in cited-physician-via-peer as a structural element.

Why it's awaiting launch: launch was delayed from May 15 to ~May 27 due to an unexpected car repair expense consuming the ad budget. B15 ads (this Sub-Avatar + Group Text + Old Notebook) are ready when ads resume.

The Sub-Avatar's name in shorthand is "Sister Visit." The defining feature is family-blunt witness — specifically a younger sister whose love is the source of the alarmed honesty. Future variants could swap sister for a sister-in-law she's close to, a cousin she grew up with, or any blood relative who occupies the "won't pretend not to see" position.
