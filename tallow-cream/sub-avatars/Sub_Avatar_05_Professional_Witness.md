# Sub-Avatar 5: The Professional Witness

**Status:** Parked, written, not launched (B12C1)
**Ads built for this Sub-Avatar:** B12C1 (The Hairdresser)
**Lifetime sales:** 0 (not yet tested)

---

## Anchor
- **Core Avatar:** Invisible Woman
- **Core Desire:** I want to be seen again — and specifically by the person who has been looking at my face closer than anyone outside my marriage

---

## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want my hairdresser to stop looking at me with that expression I think she's been holding back."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, anchored to professional witness — the discovery character who has been observing her face for twelve years under harsh fluorescent salon light is the most qualified observer in her life. Her noticing carries occupational weight: "the salon is where you see a face for what it actually is. No filter. No evening light. No mercy." When THIS person notices a change, it counts.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Status (primary — be respected by the professional whose job is reading faces) + Relationships (secondary — the 12-year hairdresser-client relationship is a long-running social tie)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience:** In her hairdresser Renee's chair on a Thursday afternoon in March. Renee has been cutting her hair since she was 40 (12 years). Renee has the color brush in her hand and is about to paint the first section. Renee stops and leans in. Renee says, "What happened to your skin?" — Renee doesn't ask about her skin. Ever.
- **Product Experience:** Drugstore, then mid-range, then the retinol serum her sister-in-law swore by, then the $140 jar Renee herself recommended years ago (back when Renee's own skin was different). Every one felt nice for a week. Then her face went back to tight and dry by 10am.
- **Active or past?** Active — the salon chair moment is the discovery moment, recent

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Sadness (specifically: dreaded inevitability before the appointment)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Anticipatory shame stemming from Sadness. For two years she'd been dreading those appointments. Per the ad: "I'd sit in her chair and see myself and look away. I'd catch her looking at my face in the mirror and I'd catch myself thinking she was noticing what I was noticing." The Origins secondary emotion here is dread of being witnessed by the person whose job is witnessing — the salon's unflattering light is the antagonist, not Renee.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit:** Checks her face in her phone's front camera at stoplights — between meetings, before dinner, multiple times a day for over a year. Specifically the stoplight version (B12C1's habit is location-specific: "in the car waiting at a light").
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (no external confirmation she still looks like herself) + Emotion (anticipatory dread of the salon)
- **Frequency / strength:** Multiple times daily. Strength = she didn't decide to start; the habit formed sideways. Stops when she stops needing to check.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 50-58
- Has a long-standing hairdresser relationship (10+ years with the same stylist) — without this, the discovery character doesn't exist. The Sub-Avatar requires a professional witness whose observation span IS the credibility
- Goes to the salon at a regular cadence (every 6 weeks in B12C1) — establishes the witness-frequency

## Verbatim Language Patterns

- **Phrases she uses to describe her problem:**
  - "What happened to your skin?" (Renee's verbatim, the hook)
  - "The salon is where you see a face for what it actually is. No filter. No evening light. No mercy."
  - "I'd been dreading those appointments."
  - "The dry patch on my chin that foundation would never cover, just sit on."
  - "Forty-one photos on my phone. I'm in two of them." (Grand Canyon family trip)

- **Internal-monologue patterns:**
  - Catalogues what Renee can see that she can also see ("The skin at my temples going papery. The crease between my eyebrows that doesn't go away when I stop frowning.")
  - Doesn't bring up what she suspects Renee is noticing
  - Counts photos of herself across vacations
  - Trusts hands as evidence (Renee's healed hands prove the product works before Renee says anything else)

- **Words she'd embrace:** calm, tight, papery, the salon light, see it, no mercy, you look like you slept
- **Words she'd reject:** anti-aging, hormonal, menopause, wrinkles, fine lines, glowing, radiant, lifting, plumping, anti-wrinkle

## Notes

The structural hypothesis behind this Sub-Avatar is that occupational close-range observation is the most credible peer-adjacent authority available — more credible than a friend (because Renee doesn't lie out of social politeness), more credible than a sister (because Renee sees her under salon light at 6-inch range every 6 weeks), and definitely more credible than a doctor (because Renee isn't selling treatment; she's been quietly watching).

The B12C1 ad introduces a unique element no other ad in the project has: hands-as-mechanism-proof. Renee says "look at my hands" and shows the narrator hands that hairdressers shouldn't have — not red, not cracked at the knuckles, not raw between thumb and finger. The visible evidence on Renee's hands does the conversion work before Renee delivers the mechanism. This is the only Sub-Avatar in the roster where the discovery character's own body is the evidence package.

What we expect to learn from launching B12C1:
- Whether the occupational-witness Sub-Avatar pulls a different audience pool than the friend/sister-witness Sub-Avatars (Hairdresser vs Lisa, Karen, Elaine). If CPM and CTR differ meaningfully, the hairdresser hook is reaching a structurally different sub-segment.
- Whether the hands-as-proof beat is doing measurable work. If B12C1 converts at a similar rate to B5C1/B7C1/B9C3, the hands beat may be a low-cost structural addition for future ads. If higher, the hands frame is its own conversion lever.
- Whether "you look like you slept" as a husband-notice phrase competes with "your skin looks different." The latter is the validated B5C1 phrase. The former is a different category of skin-specific verbal — less explicitly about the skin, more about a derivative state. Open question whether this works equally well.

Why it's parked: B13 (Photo Avoider) was selected for the next launch slot because it tests a fundamentally NEW Sub-Avatar (vs B12C1 which is a converter-formula variant). B12C1 layers in after B13 has 5-7 days of post-launch data.

The Sub-Avatar's name in shorthand is "Professional Witness." The defining feature is not the hairdresser specifically — it's any long-running professional whose job is close-range face observation. Future variants could swap hairdresser for esthetician, dental hygienist, makeup artist, optometrist, or any other observer with 10+ years of pattern-recognition on the narrator's face.
