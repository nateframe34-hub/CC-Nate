## Anchor
- **Core Avatar:** Invisible Woman
- **Core Desire:** I want to be seen again by the person who has known me longest

---

## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want my husband to look up from his laptop and notice me the way he used to."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, with the relational specificity of long-married spouse as the canonical witness whose noticing matters most. Her husband is the person whose attention has been withdrawn the longest — recovering it is the truest measure that she is visible again.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Relationships (primary — be attractive to the partner you chose, feel desired) + Status (secondary — be respected as still-noticeable inside your own marriage)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience:** Husband has slowly stopped commenting on her appearance over decades of marriage. A woman friend (Amy at the engagement party, four years unseen) asked "are you sleeping okay?" when she'd been sleeping fine. A coworker said "long week?" when she'd had a normal week. Daughter Ellie stopped asking for selfies on weekly FaceTime calls — and she can't remember when. Something is visible to other people that she can't see in her own mirror.
- **Product Experience:** Years of CeraVe, then a $120 La Roche-Posay cream her dermatologist recommended, then a retinol that burned for two weeks, then a TikTok vitamin C serum, then a ceramide cream from Reddit (two jars), then collagen supplements + a bedroom humidifier. Nothing held past lunch.
- **Active or past?** Active and compounding — three external-noticing moments in six weeks. The accumulation is the experience.

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Sadness (suppressed, normalized)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Quietly grieved-out / resigned. The narrator names it directly: "I'd made peace with it in a quiet way I hadn't noticed myself making. That was the worst part — I'd stopped expecting anything to work." This is Origins' secondary emotion of Disappointed stemming from Sadness (unmet expectation that her appearance would not become unsigned over the years), layered with a resignation that has set in so quietly she didn't notice it form.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit:** Opens phone's front camera every morning before getting out of bed. Checks her face. Automatic. Hasn't been doing it deliberately — just doing it.
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (no external confirmation she still looks like herself) + Emotion (low-grade anxiety about what's changed)
- **Frequency / strength:** Daily, automatic, "for about a year." Has become unconscious — the strength of relatability is that she didn't decide to start and didn't notice when she stopped.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 45-58
- Married 20+ years (long-marriage specificity matters — the entire emotional engine depends on a spouse who has had decades to develop non-noticing patterns, then breaks the pattern)
- Has at least one adult or college-age child (provides the second external witness — daughter on FaceTime who has also stopped asking for selfies)

## Verbatim Language Patterns (from the ad copies)

  - "He never notices anything."
  - "Nothing held past lunch."
  - "I'd made peace with it in a quiet way I hadn't noticed myself making."
  - "Are you sleeping okay?" (the question that didn't match her reality)
  - "Something was visible to other people. Something I couldn't see in my own mirror."

- **Internal-monologue patterns:**
  - Catalogues failed products in her head (CeraVe → La Roche-Posay → retinol → vitamin C → ceramide → collagen → humidifier)
  - Reads everything that came in the mail at 1am after a TikTok video
  - Files things and doesn't bring them up — "I didn't cry. I didn't bring it up. I just filed it."
  - Feels stupid for trying again

- **Words she'd embrace:** soft, rested, different, finally, soft underneath, the not-checking
- **Words she'd reject:** anti-aging, menopause, perimenopause, wrinkles, fine lines, hydration, serum (as a category word), self-care, luxury, spa, glowing, radiant, youthful, revolutionary

## Notes

The B6 hypothesis-test produced one of the project's clearest insights: this Sub-Avatar's hook requires SKIN-SPECIFIC VERBAL noticing. "Your skin looks different" works. Wordless touch (B6C1), candid photo (B6C2), and generic-compliment-to-the-room (B6C3) do not. The verbal-specific moment is structurally non-negotiable for this Sub-Avatar.

The Husband-Noticed Sub-Avatar is the highest-confidence one in the entire roster because it has both a validated converter (B5C1) AND three falsified hypothesis variants (the B6 batch). We know what this Sub-Avatar's hook needs to be more precisely than we know any other Sub-Avatar's. The minimum viable opening is: husband, kitchen-table-equivalent moment, looks up from what he's doing, says something specific about her skin.

The conversion mechanism for this Sub-Avatar is the moment the husband notices something she had stopped expecting him to notice. The relief — and the structural payoff — is the act of being seen by the person whose noticing matters most. The "What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the Tuesday" identity payoff is doing the conversion work alongside the mechanism explanation.

Note that the B6 close beats are also useful raw material: each B6 falsified variant has an "I'm lucky" preemptive-apology opener and the "small uninterrupted possessions" close, both of which generate strong emotional resonance even though the hooks themselves did not earn the click. These framing devices likely survive into future B16+ ads under different Sub-Avatars.
