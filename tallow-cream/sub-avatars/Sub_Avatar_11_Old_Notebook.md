## Anchor
- **Core Avatar:** Invisible Woman
- **Core Desire:** I want to be seen again — and I want to inherit my mother's instinct about her own face that she figured out without knowing why

---

## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want my husband to walk into the kitchen and tell me I look like my mother."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, anchored to heritage-recognition. The Sub-Avatar's specificity: the mother is deceased; the connection runs through artifact (a 1979 notebook from when the mother was 47, the narrator is 52). The desire is to look like the woman who raised her — to be recognized as continuous with a line — and for the husband to be the one to name the recognition. The 5am alarm she set for 18 months to do her skincare routine was a betrayal of what her mother had figured out simply.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Relationships (primary — heritage, lineage, the mother-daughter bond after the mother is gone) + Belonging (secondary — inherit what mother knew)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience:** Sunday in February. Found her mother's notebook in a box in the attic. The box had been under the eaves for three years since the mother's death in 2018. The notebook is from 1979. The mother had written her skincare routine on one page. Six lines. The mother was 47 in the notebook. The narrator is 52. That morning the narrator had spent 45 minutes in her bathroom with seven jars open on the counter.
- **Product Experience:** CeraVe, a retinol that burned, a vitamin C serum, a hyaluronic acid serum, an eye cream, a lip treatment, a brightening spot serum bought online at midnight. Seven products. Setting the 5am alarm for 18 months for the routine. Forty-five minutes most mornings. Told herself it was working. It wasn't working.
- **Active or past?** Active (recent attic discovery) anchored in heritage (the mother's lifetime of nice skin, deceased)

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Sadness (heritage grief — the mother is gone, the answer was in her hallway cabinet, the daughter didn't know)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Reverent-grief stemming from Sadness. The Origins secondary register here is the specific feeling of holding an artifact written by a parent who is no longer alive, in handwriting you recognize, about something you didn't know they had figured out. Not regret in the heavy sense — closer to wistful gratitude. The mother's six-line entry isn't a recipe, it's a directive: "She had nice skin until the end. I hadn't thought about that in years."

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit:** Sets the alarm for 5am instead of 6am. Eighteen months. So she'd have the extra hour for her skincare routine. Cleanser, toner, vitamin C serum, hyaluronic acid, eye cream, moisturizer, sunscreen, with waiting time between each. Monday through Friday, religiously, almost two years.
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (the chronic failure of the products despite her commitment) + Emotion (the conviction that more discipline must be the answer)
- **Frequency / strength:** Daily, 5am, weekdays. Strength = she has built her sleep schedule around the routine. She gives up the hour of sleep willingly because the routine = the work = the seriousness. Cessation hits hard precisely because the time investment was so large and so consciously chosen.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 50-58
- **Mother deceased** (without this, the artifact-discovery doesn't work the same way — a living mother would just have been asked. The Sub-Avatar specifically requires the inheritance frame triggered by death.)
- Mother kept journals, notebooks, recipe cards, or any documentary trace of her daily life (B15C3 specifies six notebooks brought home from the mother's house in Cleveland after she died — the daughter has the artifacts but hadn't opened them)
- The mother's best friend (Sheila in B15C3) is still alive — provides the second-degree chain of authority connecting the artifact to the product

## Verbatim Language Patterns

- **Phrases she uses to describe her problem:**
  - "Wash face evening. Pond's cold cream. Two fingers. Forehead, cheeks, jaw. Glass of water before bed. Sleep eight hours." (Mother's 1979 notebook — six lines)
  - "Your mother had really nice skin." (Steve at the table, the hook beat)
  - "You look like your mom." (Steve at the close, the inheritance moment)
  - "Your mother had it figured out without knowing why."
  - "Your mother's instinct was right. The science just caught up later."
  - "That was the thing I wanted back. Not the soft cheeks. The hour of sleep."

- **Internal-monologue patterns:**
  - Reads the notebook out loud once before she calls anyone
  - Knows the answer to her own question before she finishes asking it (asks Karen what Sheila said about her mother's skin — Karen pauses — the answer arrives before the explanation)
  - Frames the discovery as something that takes three years to surface (the box has been in the attic for three years; she hadn't opened any of them)
  - Counts what her mother counted differently — eight hours of sleep, two fingers, six lines

- **Words she'd embrace:** mother, notebook, instinct, the right thing, simple, two fingers, eight hours, look like your mom, the science caught up
- **Words she'd reject:** menopause, hormones, anti-aging, regimen, routine (in the marketing sense), serum, layering, optimize, brightening, peptides, plumping

## Notes

The structural hypothesis behind this Sub-Avatar is that artifact-discovery (a physical document the mother wrote when she was the narrator's current age) is the highest-leverage discovery hook in the project library. The artifact does work no human discovery character can do: it speaks in the mother's own voice, in handwriting the daughter recognizes, about a daily ritual the daughter never knew about. The Karen-Sheila chain is the mechanism delivery, but the notebook is the emotional engine.

This is also the only ad in the project that names the product by full brand name in the body copy ("Whipped Tallow and Honey Balm"). Other ads use "tallow balm" or "the jar" or "four ingredients." B15C3 names it twice — once in the mechanism scene (when Karen says what she's been using) and once at the close. The hypothesis: brand naming when paired with this specific heritage frame doesn't read as sales copy; it reads as the answer to a question the mother set 47 years earlier.

The 3-layer chain (mother → Karen → Sheila) is the deepest authority chain in the project. B9C3 runs 2 layers (Elaine → Aunt Winnie). B15C3 runs 3 (notebook → Karen who heard Sheila → Sheila who knew the mother). If this converts at a similar or better rate than B9C3, depth-of-authority is doing measurable conversion work. If it doesn't, the chain may be a structural ceiling beyond which credibility no longer compounds.

What we expect to learn:
- Whether the heritage-artifact discovery hook is its own audience. Hypothesis: yes — it specifically pulls women whose mothers have passed and who have boxes of inherited material they haven't opened. This is a narrower audience pool than peer-friend hooks but potentially a higher-converting one because the emotional resonance is unrepeatable.
- Whether the 5am-alarm time-overinvestment habit cessation lands. Other Sub-Avatars have product-accumulation cessation (one jar vs fourteen, throwing out nine bottles, $680 shelf). This one has time-investment cessation (45 minutes of routine, dies in 3 weeks without her noticing). If it converts, time-investment cessation may be a stronger conversion lever than product-inventory cessation because it touches sleep.
- Whether the adjacent-rather-than-skin-specific husband-notice ("Did you cut your hair") works inside this Sub-Avatar. B6 falsified the adjacent-noticing hypothesis in the Husband-Noticed Sub-Avatar. B15C3 retests it here. The close ("You look like your mom") is a heritage-recognition phrase that is technically face-specific but emotionally about lineage. If this converts, adjacent noticing may work when paired with heritage framing.
- Whether brand name in body copy increases or decreases conversion vs no-brand-name. First test of this variable.

Why it's awaiting launch: same as B15C1 and B15C2 — launch delayed from May 15 to ~May 27 due to car repair expense.

The Sub-Avatar's name in shorthand is "Old Notebook." The defining feature is heritage-artifact discovery — a deceased mother's handwritten daily-ritual document found by accident in inherited storage. Future variants could test other heritage artifacts (a recipe card, a Christmas card from the 1980s, a vanity-top object kept on display, a letter to a now-adult child written when the mother was the narrator's current age).
