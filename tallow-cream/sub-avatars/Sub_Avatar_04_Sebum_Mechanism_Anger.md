## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want the missing piece of information that explains why ten years of products didn't work."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, layered with a sharper sub-desire: be told the truth. She wants the mechanism. She wants the diagram. She wants the textbook explanation her dermatologist withheld. The relational payoff still matters — but this Sub-Avatar buys when the intellectual loop closes, not when the husband notices.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Status (primary — be respected as someone smart enough to handle the real explanation) + Health (secondary — understanding her own body / skin biology)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience:** Thanksgiving morning. Her niece (second-year med student) left her bag at the house over the holiday. The niece's dermatology textbook is sitting on the kitchen counter with a sticky note in it. The narrator wasn't trying to read it. She opened it "the way you open a magazine in a waiting room." Page 412. Sebum production over the human lifespan, graphed. Peaks in 20s, drops sharply at 40, cliff at menopause. She stared at it for a full minute.
- **Product Experience:** Seven years of dermatologist visits twice a year. Prescribed retinol. Recommended hyaluronic acid serum. Suggested she drink more water. Spending $90/month on a routine that wasn't working. Never once told her skin makes less of its own oil and water-based creams can't replace it.
- **Active or past?** Active — recent Thanksgiving, recent textbook moment, recent 5-day delay before ordering

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Anger
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Intellectual betrayal stemming from Anger. She opens the ad with: "I'm 53. I figured it out three months ago. I'm still angry about it." This is the Origins secondary emotion of "Betrayed" stemming from Anger — specifically, anger at a system (the skincare industry + her own dermatologist) that had the information all along and never shared it. Not directed at any one person. Directed at the gap between what was knowable and what she was told.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit:** Flips bottles over to read the back label. Counts ingredients. Does the math on monthly spend ($90/month routine that wasn't working → $25/month routine that is). Reads everything she can find when an idea grabs her — Googled "What's the closest thing to human sebum?" and read until she had the answer.
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (the textbook moment) + Desire (wanting the mechanism) + Emotion (anger at the gap)
- **Frequency / strength:** Episodic but intense — once a question grabs her she follows it for hours. Strength = she has a working analytical sub-segment of the Invisible Woman avatar; she's the one who reads to the bottom of every Reddit thread.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 50-58
- Either has a profession that values analytical thinking or has a relative in medicine/science (the textbook moment requires a niece-in-med-school equivalent — the textbook arrives in her house because of who she's related to)
- Tracks personal-care spend at a per-month granularity (the $90/month → $25/month math only lands if she's the kind of person who knows what she spends on creams)

## Notes

This Sub-Avatar is the most distinct of the four converters — and also the most under-tested. B3C2 was the brand's first conversion and was killed prematurely (Apr 9, after one day, with unlogged spend). The customer reached the PDP, intended to pay full price, and got a misconfigured 60% discount applied at checkout. The conversion itself is real signal; the negative ROAS is an operational artifact.

What makes this Sub-Avatar structurally distinct from the other three converters:

1. **Anger as the emotional engine.** No other converter uses anger as the opening register. The other three are quiet, observant, slightly resigned. This one opens with explicit anger and never softens. The reader who matches this register is the analytical sub-segment — the woman who wants to feel like she's been vented to by a peer who's mad about the same gap.

2. **Mechanism-first, not story-first.** The other converters bury the mechanism around the 50-60% mark behind a relational scene. B3C2 leads with the statistic. The reader gets the WHY in the first sentence. The story arrives to validate it.

3. **Direct-to-reader close.** B3C2 closes with "Flip over your moisturizer. Read the first ingredient." — direct address, imperative voice, no husband-noticing close. The other three converters all close on relational outcomes. This one closes on a personal experiment the reader is invited to run.

4. **Per-use cost mention.** The only converter that explicitly does the spend math ($90/month → $25/month). The other three never name a number besides $49.99. Whether the per-use math is doing real conversion work or just appealing to this specific analytical sub-segment is the open test.

5. **Habit-cessation beat absent.** B3C2 has a cost-reduction beat instead of a habit-cessation beat. It's also the weakest converter (one $19.99 sale, killed after one day). Whether the missing habit-cessation beat is the structural weakness — or whether the overall density (6-7/10) is — can't be separated from this data.

What to test next: a densified B3C2 v2 rebuild that keeps the anger-opener + niece-textbook + spend-math + direct-to-reader close, but adds the 10-element skeleton including day-numbered transformation, habit-cessation beat, and close-mirrors-hook. This would isolate whether the Sub-Avatar's distinctive elements survive when paired with the converter formula, or whether the bare-bones intellectual register IS the conversion mechanism for this sub-segment and adding warmth dilutes it.

Hypothesis to validate: the analytical sub-segment of Invisible Woman is real but smaller than the relational sub-segment. B3C2 likely converts a narrower audience pool. Cost-per-conversion may be higher; lifetime value (because the buyer is solution-aware and intellectually committed) may also be higher. Untested.
