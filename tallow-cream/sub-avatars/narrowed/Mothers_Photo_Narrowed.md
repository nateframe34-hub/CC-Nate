# Mother's Photo — Narrowed Sub-Sub-Avatars

**Parent Sub-Avatar:** Sub-Avatar 2 (The Mother's Photo) — validated converter (B7C1)
**Parent profile:** Woman whose mother had good skin into old age, found a photo/artifact (Pond's jar, 1994 photo), generational mirror, narrator is now her mother's age in the memory. Counts products on her own counter, compares to mother's simple routine.
**Why narrow this one:** Converter with 2 sales. Parent describes ~5-10M women. Variants stack the specific life-relationship-to-the-mother to reach ~1-3M each.

> Each variant = parent + a specific current relationship to the mother (deceased-and-handling-estate / alive-and-declining / new-grandmother-three-generations).

---

## 2A: The Executor

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** Mother died recently. Narrator is the one cleaning out the house (sibling lives far away). Found the cold cream / routine artifact during the cleanout.
- **Emotion (+):** Grief plus the strange intimacy of handling a dead parent's belongings.
- **Behaviour (+):** Keeping certain objects she can't throw away. The cold cream jar is one of them.

**Why it converts:** The estate cleanout is a high-emotion, high-nostalgia moment where the mother's skincare artifact literally turns up in the narrator's hands. The discovery is physical, accidental, and grief-charged.

**Hook concept:** "We're selling my mother's house. I'm the one cleaning it out because my brother lives in Arizona and 'can't get away.' I found her cold cream in the medicine cabinet, the same blue jar from my whole childhood. She was seventy-nine when she died and her skin was better than mine at fifty-two."

**Audience size:** Women 48-60, mother died in last 2 years, handling the estate. ~2-3M.

**Verbatim language:** "I'm the one who had to clean it out" / "the same jar from when I was a kid" / "her skin was better at seventy-nine than mine is now"

**Image trigger:** An old Pond's-style cold cream jar held over a half-packed cardboard moving box.

---

## 2B: The Caregiver

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** Mother still alive but in assisted living or being cared for, declining cognitively, but her skin is STILL good.
- **Emotion (+):** Anticipatory grief plus the quiet irony that her mother's skin has outlasted her memory.
- **Behaviour (+):** Visits weekly, applies lotion to her mother's hands, notices the contrast with her own.

**Why it converts:** The weekly caregiving ritual (applying lotion to mom's hands) creates a recurring, intimate moment of skin-comparison that's impossible to ignore. The emotional weight of caring for a declining parent makes the "her skin is still soft" observation devastating and motivating at once.

**Hook concept:** "My mother is eighty-one and she doesn't always know my name anymore. But her skin is still soft. I noticed it last Sunday putting lotion on her hands at the home. Her hands are softer than mine and I'm fifty-three. I drove home and cried for a different reason than usual."

**Audience size:** Women 50-62, mother in care, weekly visits. ~1-2M.

**Verbatim language:** "she doesn't always know me anymore" / "her skin is still soft though" / "I do her hands every Sunday"

**Image trigger:** Two pairs of hands, an older woman's and a middle-aged woman's, with a tube of hand cream nearby. Soft care-home lighting.

---

## 2C: The New Grandmother

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** Just became a grandmother. Sees her daughter, the new baby, and (if alive) her own mother in four-generation photos.
- **Emotion (+):** Joy plus the jolt of the "I'm the grandma now" identity shift, plus accelerated self-comparison.
- **Behaviour (+):** In all the new-baby photos. Sees herself positioned in the generational line. The contrast with her young daughter is stark.

**Why it converts:** The four-generation photo is a literal visual ranking of where she falls on the aging line. Becoming a grandmother is a sharp identity marker that intensifies the desire to not look "old" in the new-baby photos that will be kept forever.

**Hook concept:** "My daughter had her first baby in March. There's a photo of four of us. My mother, me, my daughter, and the baby. Four generations. I looked at it and I could see exactly where I fell on the line. Right in the middle. And my mother at eighty looked better than I expected to look at fifty-four."

**Audience size:** Women 50-60, new grandmother, multi-generational family. ~1-2M.

**Verbatim language:** "four generations in one photo" / "I'm the grandma now" / "I could see exactly where I fell on the line"

**Image trigger:** A four-generation family photo (faces non-identifiable, generational structure clear) propped on a shelf.

## Test Notes

- All three inherit the proven generational-mirror mechanism and the Pond's-as-positive-validator framing (Pond's appears only as the mother's historical choice, never as a current competitor).
- 2A (Executor) is closest to the original B7C1 (found artifact) but adds the estate-cleanout grief layer.
- 2B (Caregiver) is the most emotionally heavy and the narrowest. Watch that grief framing stays functional and doesn't tip into medical/illness language about the mother.
- 2C (New Grandmother) is the most "forward-looking" (next generation) vs the others' backward-looking grief. Different emotional register, may reach a slightly younger skew (50-56).
