# One-Jar Discovery — Narrowed Sub-Sub-Avatars

**Parent Sub-Avatar:** Sub-Avatar 3 (The One-Jar Discovery) — validated converter (B9C3), closest to winner
**Parent profile:** Woman who saw a friend's near-empty bathroom counter (one jar where there used to be many), friend looks visibly better, curious comparison. Carries lotion packets in multiple places, accumulates products.
**Why narrow this one:** Strongest converter by CTR and ROAS. Parent describes ~5-8M women. Variants stack the specific relationship-to-the-better-looking-woman to reach ~1-3M each.

> Each variant = parent + a specific type of person whose skin she's comparing herself to (old friend at a reunion / divorced friend who glowed up / older work mentor).

---

## 3A: The Reunion

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** Attended a recent reunion (high school, college, or a funeral gathering) and saw a peer she hadn't seen in 10+ years who aged dramatically better.
- **Emotion (+):** Shock plus competitive comparison plus "we were exactly the same once."
- **Behaviour (+):** Went home and searched online for what the peer might be doing. Compared old yearbook photos to now.

**Why it converts:** A same-age peer is the most threatening and motivating comparison point that exists. "We were the same at eighteen, why does she look forty-five and I look fifty-three" is a sharper sting than comparing to a celebrity or a younger woman.

**Hook concept:** "I went to my thirty-fifth high school reunion in October. Tammy Boyd and I graduated the same year, same age, fifty-three. She looked forty-five. I looked exactly fifty-three. We used to be the same. I spent the whole drive home trying to figure out what she knew that I didn't."

**Audience size:** Women 50-58, attended a recent reunion. ~1-2M.

**Verbatim language:** "we're the same age" / "we graduated together" / "what does she know that I don't"

**Image trigger:** A reunion name tag, or two yearbook-era photos next to a phone showing a recent photo.

---

## 3B: The Divorced-Friend Glow-Up

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** A close friend went through a divorce in the last year or two and came out looking noticeably better, not worse.
- **Emotion (+):** Surprise plus a little envy plus genuine curiosity.
- **Behaviour (+):** Has been watching the friend rebuild herself, noticing the change across several gatherings before asking.

**Why it converts:** The "divorce glow-up" defies expectation (everyone expects her to fall apart), which makes the change impossible to attribute to anything obvious. The narrator HAS to ask, and the answer being tallow (not a younger boyfriend or surgery) lands as believable.

**Hook concept:** "My friend Sandra got divorced last spring. Everyone expected her to fall apart. Instead she showed up to book club in February looking ten years younger. I cornered her in the kitchen and asked what she was doing. I figured she'd say Botox or a new boyfriend. She said one word. Tallow."

**Audience size:** Women 48-58, has a recently-divorced close friend. ~1-2M.

**Verbatim language:** "everyone thought she'd fall apart" / "she looks ten years younger" / "I figured it was Botox"

**Image trigger:** Two wine glasses at a book-club gathering, one woman's hand visible reaching toward a jar on a counter.

---

## 3C: The Work Mentor

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** An older woman at work (58-65) looks remarkably good and becomes an informal mentor figure. Everyone assumes she's had work done.
- **Emotion (+):** Admiration plus aspiration ("I want to age like her").
- **Behaviour (+):** Watches the older colleague over months, eventually asks (often after a drink at a work event).

**Why it converts:** The older-mentor comparison is aspirational rather than competitive (unlike the reunion peer). She's not threatened by Dolores, she wants to BECOME Dolores. The "everyone assumes she's had work done but she hasn't" reveal builds the believability.

**Hook concept:** "Dolores has worked in our office for thirty years. She's sixty-one and everyone assumes she's had work done. She hasn't. I know because I finally asked her at the holiday party after two glasses of wine. She laughed and told me what she actually uses, and it cost less than my monthly serum."

**Audience size:** Women 45-58, has an admired older female colleague. ~2-3M.

**Verbatim language:** "everyone assumes she's had work done" / "I want to age like her" / "she's sixty-one and looks better than me at fifty"

**Image trigger:** An office break room or holiday-party setting, two women's hands, one passing the other a phone with a product page.

---

## Test Notes

- All three inherit the proven object-discovery mechanism and the multi-character authority chain that made B9C3 the highest-CTR ad in the project.
- 3A (Reunion) has the sharpest competitive sting (same-age peer). Highest emotional intensity, narrowest audience.
- 3B (Divorced-Friend) has the strongest believability mechanism (defies the expected "fall apart" narrative).
- 3C (Work Mentor) is aspirational rather than competitive, reaches a slightly broader and possibly younger-skewing audience (45-58). The "assumed she had work done, but she didn't" reveal is a strong New-Information beat.
- 3C's "cost less than my monthly serum" line carries a per-use-cost echo (the B3C2 converter element). Worth noting as a cross-pollination.
