# Husband-Noticed — Narrowed Sub-Sub-Avatars

**Parent Sub-Avatar:** Sub-Avatar 1 (The Husband-Noticed) — validated converter (B5C1)
**Parent profile:** Long-married woman, husband stopped commenting on her appearance, wants to be seen by him again. Checks phone front camera every morning. Married 20+ years, 45-58, adult/college-age children.
**Why narrow this one:** Highest-data converter (1 sale + 3 falsification variants in B6). Parent describes ~10-15M women. The variants below stack additional life-moment layers to reach ~1-3M each, where the ad reads as "she's reading my mind" instead of "relatable."

> Per Origins: at <$1k lifetime sales, we earn conversions through specificity, not reach. Each Sub-Sub-Avatar below = parent + 1-2 additional Experience/Emotion/Behaviour layers.

---

## 1A: The Empty-Nest Husband-Noticed

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** Youngest child moved out in the last 6-18 months. House is quiet. She and her husband are alone together for the first time in 20+ years.
- **Emotion (+):** Identity-shift anxiety. She was "Mom" for two decades. Now she's not sure who she is to her husband when the kids aren't the buffer or the topic.
- **Behaviour (+):** Has started cleaning out the kids' rooms. Catches herself setting the table for four.

**Why it converts:** The empty nest forces the couple to actually SEE each other again after years of kid-focused parallel living. Her skin-change anxiety spikes because now there's nothing between her and her husband's attention (or its absence).

**Hook concept:** "My youngest left for college in August. For the first time in twenty-two years it was just me and Dave at the dinner table. He looked at me across it like he was seeing me for the first time in a while. Then he said, 'When did you start looking so tired?'"

**Audience size:** Women 48-56, married 20+ years, youngest child departed in last 18 months. ~2-3M.

**Verbatim language:** "the house is so quiet now" / "I don't know what we talk about anymore" / "it's just the two of us now and I don't know what he sees"

**Image trigger:** A dinner table set for two where it used to be four. Or a kid's emptied-out bedroom with the door open.

## 1B: The Grieving-Daughter Husband-Noticed

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** Her mother died in the last 1-3 years. She was the one who handled the caregiving and the estate.
- **Emotion (+):** Generational dislocation ("I'm the older generation now") layered onto grief that flattened her self-care entirely.
- **Behaviour (+):** Stopped doing anything for herself during the caregiving and grief period. Let herself go while caring for everyone else.

**Why it converts:** Grief and caregiving are exactly when women most abandon their own appearance. The husband-noticing moment becomes a "you're coming back to yourself" signal that lands harder because she'd disappeared so completely.

**Hook concept:** "My mother died fourteen months ago. I spent the year before that driving back and forth to hospice. I didn't really look in a mirror for a year. Then one morning my husband watched me make coffee and said, 'There you are. You look like you again.'"

**Audience size:** Women 50-60, married, mother died in last 3 years. ~3-4M.

**Verbatim language:** "I let myself go while I was taking care of her" / "I didn't have time to think about my own face" / "I'm the matriarch now and I don't feel ready for it"

**Image trigger:** A sympathy card still standing on the mantel months later. Or a mother's photo held with a coffee cup nearby.

---

## 1C: The Anniversary Husband-Noticed

**Added layers:**
- **Experience (+):** A milestone anniversary (25th, 30th) is coming in the next few months. There's a planned trip or party.
- **Emotion (+):** Nostalgia plus low-grade anxiety about whether she still looks like the woman he married.
- **Behaviour (+):** Pulled out the wedding album recently. Comparing then-vs-now photos.

**Why it converts:** The anniversary creates a deadline AND a comparison point (the wedding photos) that intensifies the desire to be seen. The milestone makes the desire urgent and time-bound.

**Hook concept:** "Our thirtieth anniversary is in June. Dave booked the same inn where we honeymooned. I pulled out the wedding album last week to show the kids. I looked at the woman in those photos, then I looked in the mirror, and I sat down on the edge of the bed."

**Audience size:** Women 48-58, married 24-31 years, milestone anniversary approaching. ~1-2M.

**Verbatim language:** "thirty years" / "the woman he married" / "I want to look like myself in the anniversary photos"

**Image trigger:** An open wedding album on a bed. Or a printed hotel booking confirmation on the kitchen counter.

## Test Notes

- All three inherit the proven Husband-Noticed conversion mechanism (skin-specific verbal noticing from the spouse, per the B6 falsification finding).
- 1B (Grieving-Daughter) has the largest audience and the deepest emotional well, but grief framing must stay functional per compliance ("I let myself go," not "I was depressed").
- 1C (Anniversary) has the built-in urgency a deadline provides, which the other two lack.
- When testing: run the parent Husband-Noticed (B5C1) as control against one narrowed variant to measure whether added specificity lifts conversion.
