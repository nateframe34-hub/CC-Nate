# Sub-Avatar 7: The Photo Avoider

**Status:** Written, deferred (B13 batch — three ad variations within ONE Sub-Avatar)
**Ads built for this Sub-Avatar:** B13C1 (The Camera Roll), B13C2 (The Christmas Card), B13C3 (Behind The Camera)
**Lifetime sales:** 0 (not yet tested)

---

## Anchor
- **Core Avatar:** Invisible Woman
- **Core Desire:** I want to be seen again — and specifically, I want to be in the documentary record of my own family's life again

---

## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want to be in the Christmas card / the camera roll / the family group chat photo. Without thinking about it. Without deleting three tries."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, anchored to documentary evidence. The Sub-Avatar's desire is to be PHOTOGRAPHED — to exist in the family record. Not as performance (a posed photo, a deliberate selfie) but as default (just there, in the frame, the way she used to be). The relational payoff is the photo itself becoming the proof.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Belonging (primary — be part of the family's documentary record) + Status (secondary — be present in the record of your own life)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience (across all three B13 ads):**
  - **B13C1 (Camera Roll):** Daughter Hannah is home from college, making a photo book for Grandma's 75th birthday. Scrolls 847 photos from the past year. Looks up: "Mom. You're in nineteen of them. That's two percent."
  - **B13C2 (Christmas Card):** Four consecutive Christmas cards since 2022. Three Martins in the photo. No Diane. She stopped running back into the frame four years ago — her skin "looked tired."
  - **B13C3 (Behind The Camera):** Husband Mark scrolls Google Photos 2023 year-in-review on the couch on Saturday night. Slows. Scrolls back. "When did you stop being in pictures?" 84 photos from Banff — she's in 3.
- **Product Experience:** CeraVe blue tub, $60 retinol from the internet, sister's drugstore balm with the sticky note saying "I promise this is the one." Doctor: "That's very common at your age." Quit makeup on weekends. Took the camera at every family event.
- **Active or past?** Active and accelerating — the photographic absence has become smoother and smoother over 2-4 years

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Sadness (specifically: hidden invisibility — the kind nobody notices because she's gotten good at it)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Quiet shame that she has rationalized as practicality. The Origins secondary emotion here is Disappointed-with-herself stemming from Sadness, but it's a low-grade, normalized shame — she's stopped naming it. "I told myself it was a phase. It wasn't a phase." Plus the layered shame of having gotten SMOOTH at avoiding the camera. The skill of the avoidance is what makes it shameful when it's seen.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit (varies by ad within the Sub-Avatar):**
  - **B13C1:** Grabs the phone before anyone else moves when someone says "group photo." Hand moves toward her bag automatically. Gotten very smooth at it. Nobody has to ask anymore.
  - **B13C2:** Sets up the tripod, takes the test shot, deletes every shot she's in. Sends husband and sons to stand in front of the tree. Clicks the remote from behind the tripod.
  - **B13C3:** Shifts to her left automatically before any picture — to be on her better side, to hide the part of her jaw she doesn't like. Not consciously. An automatic adjustment.
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (the moment she stopped — the 2022 Instagram beach photo for B13C3, the first Christmas card without her for B13C2, the gradual selfie-attrition for B13C1) + Emotion (the rationalized practicality)
- **Frequency / strength:** At every photo opportunity. Strength = the smoothness. She has stopped having to decide.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 50-58
- Has a documented family-photo ritual: an iCloud library, an annual Christmas card, a Google Photos year-in-review, an active family group text. The Sub-Avatar depends on photographic infrastructure that surfaces the absence as data
- Has at least one external observer who can do the audit: an adult daughter home from college, a husband who scrolls Google Photos, a charge nurse who notices the microwave-reflection check

## Verbatim Language Patterns

- **Phrases she uses to describe her problem:**
  - "I'd gotten very smooth at it. Nobody had to ask me. I just moved."
  - "I'd been doing a thing for months I hadn't named. Stepping back. Angling toward the edge of the frame."
  - "The other thing I stopped was being in pictures. It happened slowly enough that I never noticed I'd decided anything."
  - "When did you stop being in pictures?"
  - "Three Christmas cards. I wasn't in any of them."
  - "I'm in two of them. That's two percent."

- **Internal-monologue patterns:**
  - Names the behavior matter-of-factly without emotional commentary ("I just moved.")
  - Tracks years and counts photos ("Fourteen-month gap," "847 photos," "84 photos, I was in three")
  - Rationalizes the avoidance as practicality, then catches the rationalization
  - Notices the smoothness of the habit as a kind of pride that turns to grief

- **Words she'd embrace:** in the frame, in the picture, just there, the not-thinking-about-it, smooth, behind the camera, the one with the camera out
- **Words she'd reject:** menopause, perimenopause, anti-aging, hormonal, hiding, wrinkles, fine lines, body shame, mom guilt, self-acceptance, body positive

## Notes

This is the first Sub-Avatar in the project that is being tested with three parallel ad variants under a single Sub-Avatar umbrella. The three ads differ by hook mechanism (quantitative audit / annual ritual / spousal detective) and discovery character (adult daughter / sister-in-law / professional colleague) but share the same underlying Sub-Avatar definition: an Invisible Woman whose visible-absence pattern is photographic.

The structural hypothesis behind this Sub-Avatar is that the photographic-absence pattern is a wide-base behavioural specificity that crosses multiple Invisible-Woman sub-pools. Hannah's quantitative audit reaches the data-comfortable subsegment. The Christmas card reaches the family-tradition subsegment. Mark's couch-scroll reaches the long-marriage observation subsegment. If any of the three converts, we learn which mechanism within the Sub-Avatar's emotional surface is the load-bearing one.

What we expect to learn:
- Whether the Photo Avoider Sub-Avatar can convert at all. This is the first NEW Sub-Avatar test outside the four validated converters. If none of the three converts, the Sub-Avatar may not be a buying audience even though it generates strong recognition.
- If one of the three converts, which hook mechanism is doing the work: the quantitative shock (2% → 45%), the ritual frame (annual Christmas card), or the spousal-detective frame (husband scrolling Google Photos).
- Whether the habit-cessation beats translate. Each ad has a different one: stopping the auto-grab for group photos / stopping the tripod-and-step-back / stopping the better-side shift. If the cessation beats don't land, the Sub-Avatar's behavioural specificity may be more diffuse than predicted.
- Whether the husband's noticing in this Sub-Avatar (which is structurally about photos rather than skin) competes with the "your skin looks different" verbal phrase that B5C1 validated. In B13C3, Mark's notice is the inversion of the hook ("When did this start happening again?") — a meta-observation of her presence in the documentary record, not a skin-specific comment.

Why it's deferred: the launch was delayed two weeks (from May 15 to ~May 27) due to an unexpected car repair expense consuming the ad budget. B13 is the queued first launch when ads resume.

The Sub-Avatar's name in shorthand is "Photo Avoider." The defining feature is documentary self-exclusion — the gradual, unconscious, then smoothed-out absence of self from the family record. Future variants could test additional photographic-evidence mechanisms (the year-in-review video, the social media tag-untag pattern, the wedding photographer's group shot).
