## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want to look the way my mother looked at fifty-two with one jar — not the way I look at fifty-two with ten products."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, anchored to a generational mirror. The mother is both the comparison standard (her skin at this age was visibly better) AND the source of permission to do less (she had it figured out without knowing why). Being seen by her own sister at Sunday dinner, by her husband, by herself in the mirror — through the lens of looking like her mother did.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Relationships (primary — inherit, belong to a line) + Status (secondary — she spent more in a month than her mother spent in a decade and looks worse for it)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience:** Found a photo of her mother (or a Pond's jar in her mother's medicine cabinet) by accident while cleaning out a box she'd been avoiding for three years. The mother in the photo is the same age the narrator is now. The mother's skin in the photo is observably better than the narrator's skin in her own mirror right now. Has counted ten products on her bathroom counter that morning.
- **Product Experience:** Cleanser, toner, vitamin C serum, hyaluronic acid serum, retinol she can't use anymore because it burns, day cream, night cream, eye cream, face oil, lip treatment. Ten things. Her mother used one — Pond's Cold Cream, the blue lid, scooped with two fingers, pressed in like kneading bread, thirty seconds.
- **Active or past?** Active discovery (recent Saturday) compounded by past generational observation she's had her whole life

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Sadness (grief — for the mother, for the time spent on the wrong tools)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Curious-grief. The Origins secondary register here is the strange mix of grief at losing fifteen years to water-based products PLUS the curiosity of suddenly understanding why her mother's skin worked. "We've all been sold the wrong tool." This sits as the slow-burn emotional engine of B7C1: not anger, not shame — a quiet curious grief at finally seeing what her mother had figured out without ever knowing the word for it.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit:** Cycles ten products morning and night. Specifically, reaches for the hyaluronic acid serum first thing — it's the cornerstone of the stack, the thing she trusts most because Reddit told her to. Cabinet is full.
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (the failure of every single bottle) + Emotion (the conviction that she just hasn't found the right one yet)
- **Frequency / strength:** Daily, automatic, layered over and over for years. Strength = she has stopped questioning the routine because it's become identity.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 50-58 (matches her mother's age in the photo — the generational mirror requires age symmetry)
- Mother either deceased or alive in her 70s-80s with visibly better skin (mother is the comparison standard — if her mother had bad skin too, this Sub-Avatar collapses)
- Has at least one sibling (sister calls back in two minutes when she texts the photo — sister-witness is part of the discovery scene's emotional weight)

## Verbatim Language Patterns

- **Phrases she uses to describe her problem:**
  - "My skin is tight by noon every day."
  - "We spend more in a month than Mom spent in a decade on skincare. And she looked better than both of us."
  - "I wasn't using the wrong brand. I was using the wrong kind of thing."
  - "I kept putting water on an oil problem. For fifteen years."
  - "My skin just didn't look tired anymore." (the word "tired" is the load-bearing word)

- **Internal-monologue patterns:**
  - Counts products. Counts decades. Counts the gap between what her mother spent and what she's spent.
  - Talks to her sister late at night about it. Sends the photo first, talks second.
  - Calls a sentence "the thing that made me put my phone down" when the mechanism finally lands.
  - Frames her mother as "doing the right thing without knowing why" — heritage frame

- **Words she'd embrace:** soft, rested, simple, four ingredients, the right thing, soft underneath, like Mom's, finally
- **Words she'd reject:** anti-aging, menopause, wrinkles, fine lines, hydration, regimen, routine, optimize, glowing, radiant, anti-wrinkle, brightening, plumping

## Notes

This Sub-Avatar is the second-highest-confidence one in the roster: B7C1 has two confirmed full-price sales plus unattended banned-BM portion, with the cleanest historical click-to-LPV funnel in the project. The hook (mother's 1994 photo + counter of ten products) is broad — almost every woman 50+ has a mother memory that anchors this Sub-Avatar emotionally.

The known structural problem is the Pond's-objection comment pattern: 3 of 5 B7C1 comments are "this is just expensive Pond's" variants. The hook creates a logical opening (mom used Pond's, narrator should just use Pond's) that the body doesn't fully close because it doesn't explicitly disambiguate tallow-vs-current-Pond's. The B12C2 variant exists to fix exactly that: it explicitly frames the Pond's formula as having changed (mineral oil replaced animal fat at some point), and positions tallow as the return to what cold cream was supposed to be. B12C2 is the structurally-tighter test of this Sub-Avatar — same Sub-Avatar, mechanism that closes the objection directly.

Note that B12C2 introduces Margaret (AP Chemistry teacher) as the mechanism deliverer rather than Karen — same Sub-Avatar, different discovery character, more clinical/structural framing of the mechanism. This is a useful tested permutation: the Sub-Avatar can hold both a "Sunday morning coffee with Karen" warm framing AND a "school lunch table with the chemistry teacher" cool framing.

Open question: whether the LPV → ATC drop on fresh BM is a Sub-Avatar problem (this avatar doesn't convert on fresh-BM CPMs) or an ad problem (this specific ad needs a stronger mechanism-to-purchase handoff). B12C2 once launched will isolate the answer.
