## Anchor

---

## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want to put something on my face that doesn't burn / doesn't cost $680 a year / doesn't require my dermatologist to shrug and tell me less is the answer."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, layered with a sharper anti-system desire: be done being patient with treatments that promise gold-standard results and deliver damage. The Sub-Avatar's emotional engine is the trust she gave the system (dermatology, the Sephora shelf, the Reddit boards) and the slow-motion betrayal. The relational payoff arrives as relief from the bracing reflex, not as husband noticing.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Health (primary — actual physical relief from a damaged face) + Control (secondary — autonomy from the prescribing-then-dismissing authority)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience (varies by ad within the Sub-Avatar):**
  - **B14C1 (It Started Burning):** Tuesday night at the bathroom sink. Three drops of retinol serum, same bottle, six years. Her face burns. Not the tingle Marian told her meant working. Sunburn coming in. She holds a cold washcloth to her left cheek for three minutes.
  - **B14C2 (The Shelf):** Sunday afternoon in February. Reorganizing the cabinet above the bathroom sink — annual ritual. Pulls everything out onto the counter. Twelve bottles. Starts adding up. $680 before she stops. "And I still reapply foundation at lunch."
  - **B14C3 (My Dermatologist Said):** Thursday. Fourth office visit in twelve months. Tretinoin is burning her. The dermatologist who prescribed it shrugs. Says "that's normal at your age" and offers to cut the dose again. Sits in the parking lot for ten minutes after.
- **Product Experience:** Retinol on dermatologist recommendation starting at 44-48. Worked for about a year. Then sting. Then burn. Layered: vitamin C serum to "optimize" it, peptide serum to buffer the sting, barrier repair balm for "recovery," two different recovery creams. Twelve products = $680/year for B14C2's narrator.
- **Active or past?** Active — recent failure moment is the trigger

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Anger (specifically: betrayed by the experts)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Disillusioned-trust stemming from Anger. The Origins secondary emotion here is the specific kind of anger that follows trust being weaponized. B14C3's narrator says: "I trusted dermatology because I work in medicine. I understand how clinical recommendations are made." The breaking of that trust is the emotional engine. B14C2's secondary is closer to weary-defeat (the $680 inventory shock) — anger expressed through math rather than confrontation.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit (varies by ad within the Sub-Avatar):**
  - **B14C1:** Flinches before applying anything to her face. Shoulders tight, eyes squinting slightly, a half-second of preparation before the sting. For 18 months. Before recovery cream, before sunscreen, before anything.
  - **B14C2:** Browses Sephora and Ulta on her phone almost every day. Three years. Not always buying — just looking. New launches. Reviews. Twenty minutes during lunch break.
  - **B14C3:** Reads r/SkincareAddiction on her break. Every day, sometimes twice. Two years. Looking for the ingredient she's missing, the application order she's getting wrong, the product that will make tretinoin finally work the way it did at the beginning.
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (the chronic failure of the prescribed system) + Emotion (the wish to find the missing piece that makes the system work)
- **Frequency / strength:** Daily. Strength = each of these habits is a search behavior, looking for the answer the system promised but didn't deliver.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 48-55 (note: this Sub-Avatar skews younger than the others — she's been on retinol long enough for it to turn on her, which puts her at the front edge of the 45-65 range)
- High disposable income / spends in the $500-1000/year range on skincare specifically (the dollar-shock works only if the dollar inventory is real)
- Trusts authoritative recommendations — dermatologist, esthetician with credentials, evidence-based subreddits. The Sub-Avatar's betrayal requires prior investment of trust

## Notes

The structural hypothesis behind this Sub-Avatar is that the retinol-burnout pattern is its own buying audience, distinct from the broader Invisible Woman avatar. These women aren't asking "why has my face changed?" — they already know. They're asking "why has the SOLUTION I trusted stopped working?" The Sub-Avatar requires a discovery character who can credibly explain why retinol stopped working (the new cells need oil to surface safely; after 40 the oil isn't there). Marian (esthetician), Trish (zero-industry rural authority), and Denise (clinical RN) all deliver the same mechanism through different credibility paths.

What we expect to learn from launching B14:
- Whether the Retinol Burnout Sub-Avatar can convert. Hypothesis: this is the most actively-frustrated audience in the project. The other Sub-Avatars have given up on their products quietly; this one is angry. If anger converts at higher CTR but lower conversion, we learn that anger is a hook fuel but not a purchase trigger for this Sub-Avatar.
- Which authority pathway works best: peer-professional (Marian the esthetician), peer-civilian (Trish the rancher's wife), or peer-clinical (Denise the RN). All three are non-medical-prescriber peer-adjacent authorities, but they index against different credibility frames.
- Whether the dollar-shock hook (B14C2's $680) competes against the physical-shock hook (B14C1's burn) or the medical-shock hook (B14C3's shrug). All three are SHOCK hooks — first time the project has tested shock-led openers as a category.
- Whether B14C3's narrator-is-medical-professional framing (Lauren the NP) lands differently than the civilian-narrator framing. This is the only ad in the project where the narrator herself has clinical training. If it converts, the medical-professional-burned-by-medicine angle may be its own subsegment worth future variants.

Why it's not yet launched: B14 was queued alongside B11 (CPM diagnostic, Working Hands Man — different Core Desire, parked), B12 (parked converter variants), and B13 (Photo Avoider, first to launch when ads resume). B14 is the second-launch slot after B13 has 5-7 days of data.

The Sub-Avatar's name in shorthand is "Retinol Burnout." The defining feature is system-betrayal: she did everything she was told and got burned (literally) for her trouble. Future variants could test other system-betrayal vectors (the Sephora consultant who told her this was the one, the spa chain that pushed treatments, the influencer whose product caused breakouts).
