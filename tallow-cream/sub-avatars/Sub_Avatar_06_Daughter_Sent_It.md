# Sub-Avatar 6: The Daughter Sent It

**Status:** Parked, written, not launched (B12C3)
**Ads built for this Sub-Avatar:** B12C3 (My Daughter Sent It)
**Lifetime sales:** 0 (not yet tested)

---

## Anchor
- **Core Avatar:** Invisible Woman
- **Core Desire:** I want to be seen again — and to be cared for, in the direction I had given up expecting

---

## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want my daughter to keep noticing me the way I've been noticing her for twenty-five years."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, with a generational-flip specificity: the daughter she raised has been watching her on FaceTime for three years and acted on what she saw. The Sub-Avatar's surface desire is the cream; the deeper desire is for the lifelong care-direction to occasionally reverse. To be the recipient of attention from a child she taught to wash her face at four.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Relationships (primary — the parent-child bond, late-stage) + Belonging (secondary — being known well enough by family that they intervene without being asked)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience:** Her 54th birthday. A package on the kitchen counter when she got home from work. Small, six inches square. Her daughter Claire's handwriting on the address label in blue ballpoint pen. Tape on both corners the way Claire always does when she wraps things herself. Inside: an amber jar wrapped in tissue paper that wasn't quite straight, plus a note: "Mom. Just try it. Trust me. I'll explain when you call. — C."
- **Product Experience:** CeraVe because a coworker said so. Olay Regenerist because her sister-in-law had been using it for a decade. $95 on a retinol a dermatologist's website recommended — used it for a week until it started burning. Linda stood in line at a pharmacy in Paris for something her daughter had told her about and brought her a jar. Used every drop. Tight again before the jar even ran out.
- **Active or past?** Active — the package on the counter is the present-moment trigger

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Surprise (the direction-of-care reversal)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Disoriented-grateful stemming from Surprise. The narrator names it: "I taught Claire how to wash her face. She was four years old. For twenty-five years the direction went that way. From me to her. She said trust me. That was the first time that sentence had ever gone that direction." The Origins secondary register: the strange emotional dislocation of being the recipient rather than the giver of care, from the person you spent 25 years giving care to. Not embarrassment — more like recalibration.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit:** Opens FaceTime in selfie mode before every call with Claire. For two years. Just to look. Check her face. Give herself a few seconds to decide whether she was ready before Claire saw her. Doesn't think of it as a habit — "I thought of it as being practical."
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (the slow narrative of her face changing) + Emotion (the wish to control how her daughter sees her)
- **Frequency / strength:** Every FaceTime call for two years. Strength = she has rationalized it ("practical") rather than recognized it as avoidance — which is exactly why the cessation lands when she finally calls without checking.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 50-58
- Has at least one adult daughter, ideally 22-28, living elsewhere — the FaceTime-with-distant-daughter pattern is the entire structural mechanism. Without the geographic separation, there's no FaceTime check; without the age range, the daughter isn't yet adult enough to send the package
- Optional but characteristic: a profession that involves caring for others (B12C3's narrator is a librarian who'd worked a 10-hour day with 80 people through the building — care-giver framing reinforces the role reversal)

## Notes

The structural hypothesis behind this Sub-Avatar is that discovery agency can sit entirely OUTSIDE the narrator. Every other ad in the project family has the narrator discovering the product through her own action (going to book club, finding a photo, sitting in a salon chair, walking into a sister's bathroom). B12C3 is the only one in which the narrator's role is reception, not seeking. This is structurally aligned with the Origins principle that the highest-converting hook is the accidental discovery — and B12C3 takes "accidental" to its logical limit: the discovery is literally chosen by someone else.

The Sub-Avatar's emotional engine is the direction reversal. The narrator has spent 25 years giving care to her daughter. The product arriving is not just a product — it's the first instance of Claire choosing what her mother applies to her face. The close (one year later, the second birthday package arrives, and this time it feels "normal" rather than reversed) is the identity-level payoff: she has accepted the new direction.

What we expect to learn from launching B12C3:
- Whether the generational-flip hook reaches a different sub-segment than peer-friend or hairdresser hooks. Hypothesis: this Sub-Avatar's audience pool overlaps significantly with the Mother's Photo (Sub-Avatar 2) audience but adds the women who have adult daughters specifically (rather than mothers who passed nice skin down).
- Whether "trust me" as the daughter's pull-quote in the hook can compete with "what's happening with your skin" or "your skin looks different." The latter two are skin-specific. "Trust me" is relational-specific.
- Whether the FaceTime selfie-mode habit-cessation beat resonates as broadly as the front-camera-checking beat. Hypothesis: yes, because the FaceTime version layers in the relational consequence (the daughter sees the not-checking, because she's the call recipient).

Why it's parked: same reason as B12C1 — B13 was selected for the next launch slot to test a NEW Sub-Avatar (Photo Avoider) rather than another converter-formula variant. B12C3 layers in after B13.

The Sub-Avatar's name in shorthand is "Daughter Sent It." The defining feature is reverse-direction discovery — care flowing UP the generational ladder. Future variants could swap daughter for daughter-in-law, niece, or adult son's girlfriend (any younger woman in the narrator's life who has the standing to send something unsolicited).
