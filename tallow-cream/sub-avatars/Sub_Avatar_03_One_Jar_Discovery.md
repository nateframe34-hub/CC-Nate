## Origins 5-Category Layering

### Category 1: Desire (Origins #1 priority)
- **Surface Desire:** "I want to walk into someone's bathroom and have one jar on the counter the way Elaine does."
- **Core Desire mapping:** Be seen again, anchored to a moment of object-discovery in a friend's home. The Sub-Avatar's desire is specifically RELIEF FROM ACCUMULATION — she's surrounded by her own bottles, sees a peer's counter with one jar, and her body knows before her brain does that the one jar is the answer. The relief is twofold: visible result + the not-carrying.
- **Mass instinct activated:** Belonging (primary — her friend at the same age has figured this out) + Control (secondary — the relief of stopping the accumulation cycle)

### Category 2: Experience(s) (Origins #2 priority)
- **Situational Experience:** At a friend's house (book club, going upstairs to the guest bathroom). The friend's counter used to have seven things on it eighteen months ago. Now it has one amber jar. Hands wet at the sink, stared at it too long, dried hands, came down without saying anything. Three weeks later asked at her own kitchen island while pouring wine.
- **Product Experience:** Fourteen things on her own bathroom counter: cleanser, toner, vitamin C, hyaluronic acid, retinol that burns, day cream, night cream, eye cream, lip treatment, Instagram face oil, brightening serum, neck cream, two different lip balms. Plus travel-size lotion packets in purse, car console, and bottom desk drawer.
- **Active or past?** Active and recent — three-week gap between the observation and asking

### Category 3: Emotion(s) (Origins #3 priority)
- **Primary emotion:** Surprise (the counter inventory mismatch is the emotional click)
- **Secondary emotion + WHY:** Curious-comparison shading into quiet shame she doesn't name. Per Origins: the secondary emotion stems from the realization that her friend has solved something she hasn't, AND the relief of imagining her own counter looking like that. Not envy. Not anger. The specific Origins secondary emotion of "Curious" stemming from Surprise — the visible evidence that less can be more, witnessed in a friend's lived-in bathroom.

### Category 4: Behaviour(s) (Origins #4 priority)
- **Specific habit:** Buys travel-size lotion packets — the foil envelopes from the drugstore — and keeps them in three places (purse, car console, bottom desk drawer at work). For about two years. Reapplies when skin gets tight. Has stopped tracking how many she's bought.
- **Source:** Stems from Experience (her skin getting tight by noon every day) + Behavior-self-soothing (the act of carrying = the act of feeling prepared)
- **Frequency / strength:** Daily reapplication minimum, restock cycle every 2-3 weeks. Strength = she doesn't know it's a habit. Daughter Lily asks her for one out of her purse and the absence is what surfaces it.

### Category 5: Demographics (Origins #5 — sparingly)
- Woman 50-58
- In a long-running social group (book club, coffee group, exercise class) with at least one peer the same age — the discovery requires a same-age friend whose counter is observably different
- Optional but characteristic: works a desk job and travels for it occasionally (the lotion packets in three places imply mobile reapplication)

## Notes

The B9 batch was the cleanest hook-isolation test the project has run: same Sub-Avatar, same body, same mechanism, same length, three different opening mechanisms. The object-discovery hook (B9C3) outperformed both the statistic-shock hook (B9C1) and the external-dialogue hook (B9C2) by a meaningful margin. This tells us something specific about THIS Sub-Avatar: she converts on visual-physical evidence (the empty counter) more than on stated information (the statistic) or peer praise (the diner dialogue). The image triggers her, not the words.

The unique structural element of B9C3 is the three-layer authority chain. Most converters have a one- or two-step authority structure (narrator → discovery character). B9C3 runs three steps: narrator → Elaine (book club friend) → Aunt Winnie (Elaine's aunt, 76, Vermont beekeeper, using tallow since 1987). Winnie's specificity (beekeeper, 1987, Brattleboro farmer's market) is treated by the feedback file as one of the load-bearing elements — the older peer-adjacent authority carries weight for this avatar because she represents living proof of a decades-long outcome, not just a 6-week experiment.

Untested but worth noting: a B9C3 v3 with Winnie removed or replaced by a younger authority would isolate whether Winnie's age and specificity (vs the mere fact of a third-layer authority) is doing the work. Hypothesis: Winnie's age matters specifically because the Sub-Avatar's underlying desire includes a long-runway projection. She isn't asking "will this work for me?" — she's asking "is this still working at 76?" Winnie answers the second question.

The habit-cessation beat for this Sub-Avatar (travel-size lotion packets in three places, stopped buying, daughter Lily can't find one) is the strongest in the project. It's functional (not vanity), unconscious (she didn't decide to stop), and externally witnessed (Lily asks for one and the absence is the data). Reproducing this kind of accumulation-cessation beat in future Sub-Avatars is the structural lesson.

The husband-notice close (outdoor restaurant table, Saturday in May, candle, husband puts his hand on her face and says "I can see it") is the highest-craft close in the project. Outdoor evening + candle + hand-on-face is the specific image that has converted twice.
