# First-Purchase Offer Design: Replacing BOGO Without Going Naked

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Context:** The Offer + Iteration Council demoted BOGO from the first-purchase headline (a loyalty mechanic mismatched to a pre-trust, one-jar, relief-oriented buyer). That left a hole. "Free shipping + 60-day guarantee" is table stakes in DTC, not an offer that makes her feel she is getting a lot for her money. This doc designs the replacement.

---

## The Reframe That Fixes This

We have been thinking about the offer as a DISCOUNT problem (how much do we knock off). For this buyer it is a RISK and VALUE-PERCEPTION problem. She is not price-sensitive in the way a coupon-hunter is. She told us so in the data: she will spend $49.99 on a 4-ingredient cream from a brand she has never heard of, on the strength of a Facebook story. Her real cost is not the $50. Her real cost is the fear of buying product number 9 that does not work, after she threw out the first 8.

So the offer has to do two jobs at once:
1. **Remove the actual barrier** (the fear of another dud), via risk reversal.
2. **Make $49.99 FEEL loaded with value**, via a stack and an anchor, NOT a price cut that bleeds the already-thin margin and trains discount-seeking.

A discount solves a problem she does not have. A value-stack plus aggressive risk reversal solves the problem she does have.

---

## The Margin Reality (so every option below is honest)

- Retail: $49.99, anchor $59.99
- Jar COGS: $17.01
- Shipping cost absorbed (free ship): roughly $6
- **True net contribution today: ~$26-27 per jar**, not the $32.98 gross figure

This is the number every offer below has to respect. We have about $26 to work with. A $10 discount cuts that to ~$16 and makes a $25-30 CPA underwater on the first order. A $3-4 bonus item barely dents it. That asymmetry is the whole argument.

---

## The Recommendation: "The Starter Jar" Value Stack

Keep the price at $49.99. Change what she perceives she is getting for it. Present the offer as a named set, not a bare product:

**THE STARTER JAR, $49.99 (anchor $59.99)**
1. **The full jar** (60-90 days of mornings) - the hero
2. **The keep-it guarantee** - "Use the whole jar. Scrape the bottom. If your skin is the same in 60 days, we refund you and you do not mail anything back." (Upgrade from the current plain 60-day return.)
3. **Free shipping** (counted IN the stack so it reads as a line-item benefit, not an assumed default)
4. **One earned bonus** (see the two versions below - one needs a new SKU, one does not)
5. **The anchor made visible**: "$59.99 launch price $49.99"

Framed this way, she sees roughly $75-80 of value plus zero financial risk for $49.99. That is the "I am getting a lot" feeling, built without a margin-killing discount and without asking her to buy two of an unvalidated product.

### Why the keep-it guarantee is the load-bearing piece

The empty-jar, keep-the-jar guarantee is doing double duty:
- **Risk removal:** it deletes her actual objection (another dud).
- **Value SIGNAL:** "they are so sure it works they will let me use the entire jar and still refund me" reads as generosity and confidence, not just a policy. It FEELS like value, which is exactly what you said free-shipping-plus-guarantee was missing.

Real-world refund abuse on empty-jar guarantees is low. Most people who would refund do not bother once the product is gone, and the ones it converts at the point of purchase outweigh the few who claim. Budget for maybe +2-4% refund rate. On ~$26 net that is a rounding error against the conversion lift.

---

## The Bonus Item: Two Versions (the only real decision)

### Version A (ideal, needs a new SKU): The Purse Mini

A 0.5oz travel-size tallow, free with the jar, "$14.99 value."

Why it is the best-fit bonus:
- It is narratively EARNED. B9C3's habit-cessation beat is that she stopped buying travel lotion packets in three places. The purse mini is literally the brand-voice version of that payoff: "the little one that lives in your bag, so you can stop buying those gas-station packets." It is not a generic gift-with-purchase, it is the story made physical.
- It does NOT violate the one-jar identity. The full jar is the counter jar. The mini is the bag. She is still the woman with one thing on her counter.
- Low COGS (~$3-4).

The catch: we do not have this SKU. It needs a fill run at the co-packer, which is capital and lead time the brand does not have this week. So this is the v2 of the offer, queued for when cashflow allows a small mini run.

### Version B (ship NOW, no new SKU): The Applicator + The Reset Card

For the launch we can run without a new fill:
- **A small wooden applicator/scoop** (sourced in bulk, ~$0.40-0.80 each, hygienic application, fits the plain-and-practical voice)
- **A printed insert: "The 4-Ingredient Reset"** - a tasteful card on what she can stop using now that she has this (on-brand with the threw-it-all-out identity, ~$0.30 printed)
- Both ride inside the existing jar packaging, no new SKU, negligible COGS

This is a weaker value-feeling than the mini, so for the NOW launch the guarantee + anchor + free shipping carry most of the perceived value, and the applicator/card are texture. When the mini SKU is feasible, it replaces the applicator as the headline bonus and the felt value jumps.

## Three Offer Architectures, Compared

| Offer | Cart | Net margin | Value-feeling | Fit to buyer | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bare** (free ship + 60-day) | $49.99 | ~$26 | Weak (table stakes) | Neutral | Rejected (your point) |
| **First-order discount** ($39.99) | $39.99 | ~$16 | Medium (cheap, but cheapens hero) | Poor (solves a problem she does not have, trains discounting) | Rejected |
| **The Starter Jar stack** (keep-it guarantee + bonus + anchor) | $49.99 | ~$22-26 | Strong (loaded, zero-risk) | Strong (hits her real objection) | **RECOMMENDED** |

---

## How This Stays Clean Against The B16 Test

Critical, because we just demoted BOGO partly to avoid contaminating B16: the value stack applies to ALL B16 cells equally, held constant, from day one. Because the offer is identical across C1 and C3, the internal "which ad wins" comparison stays perfectly clean. The only thing it changes is the absolute conversion level versus B9C3's historical 17% bar, and that bar is already a soft n=2 benchmark from CBO, not a hard control. We WANT the better offer live, because the goal is a winner, not a museum-accurate replay of B9C3's conditions.

The rule that must hold: do not run different offers on different cells, and do not change the offer mid-test. One offer, all cells, held constant for the full run.

---

## The Honest Case Against The Recommendation

- **The keep-it guarantee raises refund exposure on a thin margin.** If abuse runs higher than the assumed 2-4% (say a coordinated return wave, or a heavier-than-expected legit dissatisfaction rate because the product genuinely underdelivers for some skin), the net margin compresses fast. Mitigation: cap it as "first jar only," track refund rate weekly, pull back to standard 60-day if abuse exceeds ~8%.
- **A value stack can tip into QVC/gimmick territory**, which is wrong for a premium real-woman brand. If we stack five freebies with fake "$ value" tags, she smells it and it cheapens the hero. Mitigation: keep the stack tight (guarantee + one bonus + free ship + anchor), brand-voice the value tags honestly, never inflate.
- **The mini could blunt repurchase** (she has a backup). Minor at 0.5oz, and it accelerates her seeing it work on the go, which speeds the reorder. Net positive, but worth watching.
- **The applicator/card version may not move the value-feeling needle enough** to satisfy the exact complaint that started this. If the NOW-launch stack still feels thin in testing, the real fix is accelerating the mini SKU, not adding more cards.

---

## Verdict For The Founder

1. **Ship "The Starter Jar" for B16:** $49.99, keep-it 60-day guarantee, free shipping, anchor $59.99, plus the applicator + reset card (Version B, no new SKU). Held constant across all B16 cells.
2. **Queue the Purse Mini (Version A)** as the offer upgrade for when a small mini fill run is affordable. It is the strongest version and it is narratively earned.
3. **BOGO stays demoted to post-purchase** (thank-you page, day-30 reorder email), unchanged from the prior council.
4. **The keep-it guarantee is the load-bearing lever.** If you change nothing else, change the guarantee from "return it" to "keep it." That single line does more for this buyer than any discount.
