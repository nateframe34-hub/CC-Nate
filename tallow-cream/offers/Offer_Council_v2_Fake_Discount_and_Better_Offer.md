# Offer Council v2: The Fake-Discount Question + The Better Offer

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Method:** 5 in-house agents, mandatory challenger included. Anchor-Discount Architect (evaluates the founder's idea) / Core-Desire Offer Designer / DTC Mechanics Scout / Compliance & Trust Challenger / Conversion Skeptic. The question: design a better first-purchase offer than "free shipping + 60-day guarantee," and evaluate the founder's idea of showing a "15% off / $20 off" discount off an inflated list price that lands at the same $49.99.

---

## The Founder's Idea, Judged Straight

The idea: raise the list to ~$59.99-$69.99, show a struck-through "discount" landing at $49.99. Justification given: "not really lying, I plan to raise the price in the future."

**Verdict: do NOT ship the fake strike-through. Ship the honest version, which earns identical revenue with zero risk.** This is unanimous across all five agents, and the two challengers were the most emphatic.

### Why the future-price justification does not hold (Compliance Challenger)

A reference price has to be a price you ACTUALLY charged, in the past or present, for a meaningful period. Intent to charge it later does not make a never-charged price legitimate. Both regimes that apply to you test this:

- **FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing:** a "was $X" claim is legal only if $X was a bona fide price the product was actually offered at, in good faith, for a reasonably substantial time. A price invented to be crossed out is the textbook "fictitious former price" the Guides prohibit.
- **Competition Act Canada, s.74.01 ordinary-selling-price:** the reference price must pass a volume test (substantial volume actually sold at or above it) OR a time test (offered in good faith at it for a substantial period). With 9 lifetime sales all at $49.99, a struck-through $69.99 fails BOTH on day one. You are a Canadian seller, so this applies to you directly, on top of FTC exposure for your US buyers. The Competition Bureau has pursued large retailers for exactly this.

### Why it is especially dangerous for THIS brand and THIS buyer

- **Platform risk:** Meta and Shopify both prohibit deceptive strike-through/discount claims. You JUST took a platform false-positive ban and are mid-warmup on a fresh BM. The fake discount would hand Meta a real, accurate reason to act, exactly when you have zero disruption budget.
- **Trust risk (the bigger one):** your buyer's whole identity is "I do not buy beauty-industry nonsense," and she has been burned by 8 overpromising products. The fake "was $69" strike-through is THE most beauty-industry-coded tactic there is, the same con on the serum she already threw out. Your converters trusted a STORY precisely because it did not feel like a sales pitch. A fake anchor snaps her back into "they are running the standard discount con on me." You would spend your scarcest asset (her belief you are different) to save zero dollars.

### The compliant version that gets the identical outcome

**Founder's launch pricing, stated honestly and forward-looking:**

> "Founder's launch price: $49.99. Price goes to $59.99 after launch."

Same $49.99 at checkout. Same urgency. Every number on the page is true. Then ACTUALLY raise it to $59.99 on a stated date or unit count ("first 500 jars" / "through [month]"). Once $59.99 is a real sold-at price for a meaningful period, you can later run genuine "$59.99 / now $49.99" sales legitimately. The honesty is what makes it land with a skeptic, and it reinforces the honest-brand position instead of betraying it.

## The Better Offer: Where All Five Agents Converged

Four of five (and the fifth in its narrower form) landed on the SAME hero lever, arrived at from four different directions:

**The keep-it / empty-jar guarantee is the offer.**

> "Use the whole jar. Scrape the bottom. If your skin is the same in 60 days, we refund you and you keep the jar. Nothing to mail back."

Why every agent landed here:
- **Core-Desire Designer:** her purchase is blocked by ONE emotion, anticipated regret (dud number 9). Discounts, bundles, and pricing all optimize a decision she has not made yet. The guarantee is the only lever that operates ON the wall, transferring the risk of being wrong from her (burned 8 times) to the brand (confident).
- **Architect:** it solves her ACTUAL cost (fear of a dud), where an anchor solves a problem she does not have (price).
- **Mechanics Scout:** it is the one mechanic that attacks the dud-fear; refund exposure is ~2-4% on ~$26 net, a rounding error against the conversion lift.
- **Conversion Skeptic:** it is the ONLY genuine lever on the table, the only offer element that changes the expected value of the click-to-buy decision. Everything else is texture.

It also FEELS like value, which was your original complaint: "they are so sure it works they will let me empty the jar and still pay me back" reads as confidence and generosity, not a return policy. That is the value-feeling, delivered through trust instead of through a discount that bleeds the ~$26 margin.

### The supporting stack (held tight, not QVC)

- **Honest launch anchor:** "$49.99 launch price, going to $59.99" (the compliant version above). Zero margin cost, the only clean urgency lever available.
- **Free shipping, line-itemed IN the stack** so it reads as a benefit, not an assumed default.
- **GWP bonus: DEFERRED.** The applicator + reset card is cheap but the Conversion Skeptic and Compliance Challenger both flagged it as theater plus added fulfillment complexity. The Purse Mini is the better bonus but needs a SKU you do not have. Hold both until after a winning angle locks.

## The Real Tension The Council Surfaced (and how to resolve it)

The Conversion Skeptic raised the sharpest point in the whole council, and it reframes this entire exercise:

> "At 9 lifetime sales we have ZERO evidence the offer is the bottleneck. We have evidence of exactly one thing: ad-level intent variance. B9C3 converts ~17% of its LPVs; B5C1 converts 0% of 12 LPVs on the SAME PDP, same price, same offer. The offer cannot explain why two ads on the identical checkout perform 17% vs 0%. That is an ad-angle problem, full stop. This offer work is a distraction from B16, which is the lever the data points at."

This is correct and it is the most important sentence in the council. The resolution is not "ignore the offer," it is **sequencing and scope discipline:**

1. **Make exactly ONE offer change now: the keep-it guarantee** (plus the honest anchor framing, which is free). This is the one change grounded in the burned-8-times buyer, and it is a trust upgrade, not an offer experiment.
2. **Freeze everything else.** No discount, no fake anchor, no mini, no value-stack engineering. The Skeptic is right that elaborate offer work at n=9 is polishing a knob the data has not asked you to touch.
3. **Hold that offer DEAD CONSTANT across all B16 cells** so the C1-vs-C3 ad test reads clean. Changing the offer and the ad against the only external benchmark (B9C3's 17%) at the same time makes any lift unattributable.
4. **Real offer testing is Phase B,** after B16 locks a winning angle and we have 10+ purchases. THAT is when anchor-vs-no-anchor, mini-vs-no-mini, and price probes get isolated, one variable at a time, against a proven ad.

## The Decision

| Element | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Fake strike-through discount | **KILL.** FTC + Competition Act violation, live Meta/Shopify risk, trust-poison for the skeptic buyer. Unanimous. |
| Honest "launch price $49.99, going to $59.99" | **SHIP.** Same revenue, same urgency, every number true, reinforces the honest-brand position. |
| Keep-it / empty-jar 60-day guarantee | **SHIP as the hero offer.** The one genuine lever, attacks the dud-fear, delivers the value-feeling through confidence not discount. Cap to first jar, track refund rate weekly, pull back if abuse exceeds ~8%. |
| Free shipping framed in-stack | **SHIP.** Zero cost, reads as a benefit. |
| GWP (applicator/card or Purse Mini) | **DEFER** to post-winner Phase B. Theater at this stage plus fulfillment complexity. |
| Discount / lower price / BOGO at first purchase | **DO NOT SHIP.** Solves a problem she does not have, bleeds margin, contradicts the one-jar identity. |
| Offer held constant across B16 | **LOCKED.** One offer, all cells, unchanged for the full run. |

---

## The Two-Line Answer

**Kill the fake discount: it is illegal under both the FTC Guides and the Competition Act, it risks the fresh BM, and it poisons the exact trust your skeptic buyer converts on. The better offer is not a discount at all, it is the keep-it empty-jar guarantee as the hero, with an honest "launch price, going to $59.99" anchor for clean urgency, held dead constant across B16 so the ad test can finally tell you which angle wins.**
