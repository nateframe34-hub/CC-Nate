## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What we know without numbers

**1. The hiding-behavior low point is a compliance liability (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The opening describes a year of avoidance: "I started washing my face after he fell asleep. I kept the bathroom door half-closed when I did my skincare in the morning. If we were in the car together and the sun hit my visor wrong, I'd turn my head toward the window." This is exactly the kind of internal-shame / hiding-behavior framing that `compliance-and-readability.md` Rule 2 was written to ban. Meta's 2026 scanner penalizes negative self-perception sequences. We didn't have the rule yet when we wrote this — it was BORN from ads like this one underperforming.

**2. The hook competes with B1C2 directly and loses (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

B1C2 opens with "He never notices anything" — instant character beat, instant reader recognition, instant open loop. B3C1 opens with "I had been hiding from him in bad light for almost a year before he caught me." This is more literary but slower to land. B1C2's hook is built for a Facebook scroll. B3C1's hook is built for a memoir.

**3. The "your skin feels different" payoff is good but arrives late (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

The husband-noticing moment is the same emotional beat as B1C2, but B3C1 buries it after 200+ words of hiding behavior. The reader gets the hiding-behavior hit (which makes them feel worse) before the validation hit (which makes them feel hopeful). Wrong sequence — confession ads should land hope before they land shame.

**4. The "in bad light" frame is itself shame language (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"Bad light" implies the narrator believes she looks worse than she is. That's a self-perception statement which `compliance-and-readability.md` Rule 4 ("first-person discovery, not second-person diagnosis") was designed to flag — and the underlying emotional posture (shame about being seen) is what triggers Meta's content scanner.

**5. Element density estimated 5-6/10 — predates the density rule (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Single secondary character (husband). No trust ladder beyond the moment of being touched. No skeptic convert frame. Mechanism is light. Same structural gap as the entire pre-B5 library.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The "moment of being touched" beat — repurposed and used as the identity-level payoff in B5C3 ("his palm on my cheek, his thumb near my ear. Absent-minded. Comfortable.").
- The 24-year marriage specificity. Long-marriage detail makes the husband character believable.

### Change
- **Open with the moment of validation, not the year of hiding.** B5C1 takes the same emotional concept and inverts the structure — the husband's noticing comes FIRST, then the backstory. That's the right order.
- **Replace hiding behaviors with external observations.** Instead of "I started washing my face after he fell asleep," use "My friend Lisa asked me what was wrong at lunch." External witness > internal shame.
- **Densify to 8-of-10 elements.** This was rebuilt as B5C1 and produced the brand's first FULL-PRICE sale. The lesson stuck.

### Test Next
- B5C1 is the live successor. The "Caught In Bad Light" specific framing isn't being re-tested directly — its core moment (husband's hand on her face) lives on in B5C3 as the identity-level payoff.

---

## Decision

Killed correctly. This ad was a casualty of three problems we hadn't solved yet on April 8: (1) no compliance rules around hiding behaviors, (2) no element density rule, (3) no understanding that hope before shame is the right emotional sequence. Each of those gaps is now formalized in the skill. The ad's contribution is that it taught us what NOT to write at the structural level.
