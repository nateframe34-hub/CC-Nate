## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why this ad converted (when nothing else in B1-B3 did)

**1. The mechanism opener taps the "intellectual click" segment of the avatar (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

"After 40, your body produces more than half less of the only thing that keeps your skin from feeling dry." This is a frontloaded MECHANISM hook — it gives the reader the WHY immediately. It's the same energy as a science-Twitter thread or a Reddit r/SkincareAddiction post. The avatar segment that converts on this is the analytical sub-segment — women who want to UNDERSTAND before they buy. They don't need the emotional journey; they need the missing piece of information. That's exactly what B3C2 hands them.

**2. The niece-with-textbook discovery character is novel and credible (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Med student niece + accidentally-spotted textbook = peer-adjacent authority without violating the doctor-character rule. She's not endorsing the product; she's just the conduit for the information. This is the same structure that makes the doctor reveal in the course's Doctor Ad work. We accidentally hit the pattern here without naming it as a rule yet.

**3. The "I'm still angry about it" emotional anchor (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Anger is the most CTR-converting emotion for this avatar — the deep research found that the "nobody told me" anger is the dominant 2am thought. This is the only ad in B1-B3 that named the anger directly in the opening 50 words. That emotional honesty is what makes the analytical content land — the reader doesn't feel like they're being lectured, they feel like they're being VENTED TO by a peer who's mad about the same thing.

**4. The compliance posture is significantly cleaner than B3C1/B3C3 (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

No hiding behaviors. No "your body stopped" diagnostic claim — it says "your body produces more than half less" as a third-person observation, then shifts to first-person discovery. No menopause word. No symptom strike-list words. This was accidentally compliant because the mechanism focus pulled the writing away from the personal-attribute land mines.

### Why ROAS was negative

**1. Discount configuration was wrong — $19.99 instead of $49.99 (HIGH CONFIDENCE — operational, not creative)**

The customer reached the PDP, saw the price, intended to buy at full price, and got a misconfigured 60% discount applied at checkout. This is not a creative problem — it's a Shopify configuration issue. Now fixed. This is the single biggest reason the ad shows negative ROAS in the data.

**2. We don't know the spend behind the conversion (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

CSV doesn't log B3 spend. Could be $20 spend → $19.99 sale (1.0 ROAS, breakeven), could be $80 spend → $19.99 sale (0.25 ROAS, brutal). Without the spend number, we can't compute true ROAS. This is a data hygiene gap to fix going forward.

**3. The conversion may be a stat-of-one, not signal (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

One sale is one data point. Could be a true winner OR could be the one analytical buyer who would have bought regardless of ad copy. We didn't run it long enough to know.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The mechanism-frontload hook structure. "After 40, your body produces less of X" is a formula that delivered the brand's first conversion. Worth replicating.
- The "I'm still angry about it" anger anchor in the opening. This is a unique-to-avatar emotional trigger.
- The peer-adjacent authority structure (med student niece, dermatology sister, etc.) — credible without triggering the doctor-character compliance rule.
- The "I figured it out from [unexpected source]" discovery mechanism. Beats "my doctor told me" every time on compliance.

### Change
- **Always log spend per ad even if killing fast.** The data gap on B3 means we can't compute B3C2's true ROAS or compare CPM behavior. Going forward, log every ad's metrics before kill — even if the kill is correct.
- **Densify to 8-of-10 elements.** B3C2 was probably 6-7/10 (mechanism, character, anger emotion, discovery scene, light trust ladder). Adding 3-4 named secondary characters and a progressive 4-week transformation timeline could turn this into a real winner.
- **Test a re-run with proper Shopify config.** The ad converted at $19.99. Will it convert at $49.99? That's the unanswered question worth re-testing.

### Test Next
- A high-priority candidate for the "fresh campaign with 3 converters" experiment proposed for April 13. B3C2, B5C1, B7C1 are the only ads that have produced sales — running them in a clean campaign isolates whether the auction problem is campaign-level pollution.
- A densified B3C2 v2 would be a strong B9 candidate: same mechanism opener, same anger anchor, same niece discovery, but built to 10-element density with multiple named characters and a 4-week transformation arc.

---

## Decision

Killed in the April 9 sweep to clear runway for B5 — defensible at the time. In retrospect, this ad deserved a longer run. It is one of three ads in the project history to convert, and it converted on the FIRST sale the brand ever made. The structural lesson is: mechanism + anger + peer-adjacent authority is a formula. The operational lesson is: log every ad's spend, always, even on kill day.
