## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What this ad proved

**1. The generational-mirror hook converts (HIGH CONFIDENCE — 2 lifetime sales)**

This is the only ad in the project library to convert TWICE pre-April 12 ($99.98 total). The "my mother used one jar at 52, I'm 52 with ten products" frame creates instant recognition for women in the 48-58 band whose mothers used a single iconic product (Pond's, Vaseline, Nivea blue tin). The hook does double duty: it's an emotional opener (the photo) AND a logical setup for the mechanism (oil-based products worked because they were the right category, even if the user didn't know why).

**2. Pond's-as-anchor is the strongest peer-adjacent authority in the library (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The mother is a peer-adjacent authority the narrator already trusts unconditionally. There's no skeptic-convert work to do — the reader already accepts that the mother had nice skin, and the mother used Pond's. The mechanism explanation lands harder because it ratifies what the reader's own mother might have done. Karen (the discovery character) is almost a redundant authority by the time she appears — she just supplies the modern product (tallow) that does what Pond's accidentally did.

**3. Engages and EARNS the read at scale (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

7.69% CTR (Apr 13, 117 imp) and 19.51% CTR (Apr 21, 82 imp on fresh BM) put this in the top tier of project CTRs. 100% click-to-LPV on Apr 13 (9/9) means the hook is honest — every clicker stayed long enough to load the landing page. The body holds attention through the first 10 seconds.

**4. Lower CPM than other Invisible Woman ads (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — fresh-BM data confirms)**

Old BM: $209-240 CPM range, lower than B5C1 ($228-457) and B9C3 ($263-552). Fresh BM: $123 CPM vs. B5C1 $53 and B9C3 $552 (CBO-skewed). The directional signal is consistent — Meta's auction prices this hook lower than the other Invisible Woman hooks. Possible reason: the Pond's nostalgia hook attracts an older / less skincare-saturated audience pocket, which is less competed-for in the auction.

### What this ad did NOT prove

**1. Sustained day-over-day conversion (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Both lifetime sales pre-date the detailed tracker. Across 4 logged days (Apr 13, 14, 16, 21), zero same-day purchases despite 29 total clicks and 23 LPVs. The funnel breaks at LPV → ATC consistently. Either the body needs work at the mechanism-to-purchase handoff, or the people clicking are not the people who buy (high-engagement, low-intent traffic).

**2. The mechanism stage closes the buyer (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

100% click-to-LPV but ~5% LPV-to-ATC across recent runs. The hook attracts; the body does not consistently close. Compare to B9C3 on Apr 21: 5 clicks → 4 LPV → 2 ATC (50% LPV-to-ATC). B7C1's body may be losing readers at the Karen kitchen-table mechanism scene — it's the section where the Pond's nostalgic warmth has to hand off to a new product and a new mental model.

**3. Whether the Pond's anchor is replaceable (UNKNOWN)**

We don't know if "my mother used Pond's" is the load-bearing element, or if any iconic single-product generational anchor would work (Vaseline, Oil of Olay, Nivea, Noxzema, cold cream generic). Hasn't been tested.

**4. Fresh-BM purchase volume (LOW CONFIDENCE)**

1 ATC and 0 purchases on Apr 21 ($10.15 spend) is a sample of one day. CBO concentrated 69% of fresh-BM budget on B9C3, starving B7C1. B7C1 needs 3-5 days of $20+ daily spend on fresh BM before its true conversion rate can be measured.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep

- **The hook (the 1994 photo + "she was 52, I'm 52 now").** Three different runs (pre-tracker, Apr 13-16, Apr 21) all produced top-tier CTR. The opening is locked.
- **The Karen kitchen-table discovery scene** — the line "Your mom used Pond's. Was that complicated?" defuses the skeptic-convert objection without effort.
- **The "I keep that photo on my nightstand now" identity close.** It echoes the hook's photo, gives the ad a clear emotional through-line, and lands the identity payoff (doing what mother did, with understanding she didn't have).
- **The mother's hand motion** — "she'd scoop it out with two fingers and press it into her face like she was kneading bread." This sensory detail is doing real work; it's tactile, specific, and gives the reader an actionable image of how to use the product.

### Change

- **Body needs a closing-pass rewrite at the mechanism handoff (LPV → ATC).** Karen's kitchen scene is the section to interrogate. If 100% of clickers reach the page and only 5% add to cart, something between "I sat with that photo" and "I ordered it that night" is losing them. Possibly the mechanism is too compressed (one short paragraph), or the ordering decision happens too fast (no skeptic objection beat between Karen's pitch and the order).
- **Reallocate budget on fresh BM so B7C1 gets fair test.** Manually push $15-25/day to B7C1 in the next fresh-BM run; don't let CBO starve it for B9C3.
- **Don't keep it in CBO with B9C3 long-term.** They cannibalize each other — both are Invisible Woman, both are object/photo discovery hooks, CBO will always pick the one with the most recent purchase signal. Run them in separate ad sets if both are kept active.

### Test Next

- **B7C1 on fresh BM at $20-30/day for 5 days minimum.** This is the real test. If sustained ROAS lands above 1.5x gross over 3 days, it joins the "validated converter" tier.
- **B7C1 v2 with a different generational anchor.** Same body structure, swap Pond's → Vaseline OR Oil of Olay OR Nivea blue tin. Tests whether the Pond's-specific nostalgia is load-bearing or whether the generational-mirror MECHANIC is what carries.
- **B7C1 v3 with a tightened mechanism scene.** Same hook + Karen scene, but add a 60-word skeptic-objection beat between Karen's pitch and the order to slow the decision moment down.
- **A "B7C1 image variant"** — replace the Pond's-jar-in-hand image with a side-by-side of the 1994 photo and the narrator today (no faces, just the two photos held side by side on a kitchen table). Tests whether the photo IS the image hook.

---

## Decision

**Reactivate on fresh BM. Protect from CBO starvation. Test for sustained 2+ ROAS over 3 days.**

This ad is the most CPM-efficient hook in the Invisible Woman library and produced the most lifetime sales (2). The funnel-closure question is real — the body is losing buyers between LPV and ATC — but the engagement signal (CTR, click-to-LPV) is strong enough that the body can be tuned without rewriting the hook. This belongs in the next fresh-BM rotation alongside B9C3 and B5C1, with budget protected so it gets a fair test.

If after 5 days at $20-30/day on fresh BM it still has a broken LPV → ATC handoff, the body needs the rewrite described under "Test Next."
