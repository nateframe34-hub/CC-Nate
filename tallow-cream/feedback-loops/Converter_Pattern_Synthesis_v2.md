**Purpose:** Two things. (1) Cross-reference every ad that has ever converted, across the account's full history, to isolate the actual conversion mechanism, not just "the angle." (2) Answer the question this doc exists to answer: **which of those converting elements have already been translated into a cheap-CPM format, and which haven't been tried yet.** That second half is the actionable output: it's the brief for the next batch.

The obvious next move isn't "pick a format": it's "find every element that makes people buy, then find the cheapest wrapper for each one."

## The Portability Question: What's Been Translated to Cheap Formats, What Hasn't

This is the part that matters for where to point B21+.

### ✅ Already ported, already proven to work in a cheap format

**The mechanism (water evaporates, fat absorbs).** This is the ONE element that has now been independently proven twice: once buried inside B9C3's narrative (via Winnie's dialogue), and once completely stripped of narrative and run as a pure diagram (B19C2). It converted BOTH ways. This is strong evidence the mechanism itself is the load-bearing conversion element, not the story wrapped around it. **This is also, not coincidentally, the cheapest thing we've ever run ($11-77 CPM).**

**Structural comparisons (counter-count, cost, label-length).** B20's three cells port the STRUCTURE of comparison (many→one, expensive→cheap, long→short) from B9C3's "fourteen things → one jar" beat and B3C2's cost framing. Early signal is positive (B20C3 got an ATC on day one) but not yet proven at volume: this is mid-test, not proven.

### ❌ NOT yet ported to any cheap format: this is the actual opportunity

**1. The named, hyper-specific peer-adjacent authority.** Winnie (76, Vermont, beekeeper, 1987, farmer's market) is the single most original, credible element in the entire converter library, and NOTHING in whiteboard has attempted to carry a specific human authority. Every whiteboard cell so far is either pure diagram (C2) or pure comparison (B20). Nobody has tried putting a specific person's photo/quote/credential into a cheap-format diagram.

**2. The habit-cessation beat.** "Stopped carrying travel-size lotion in three places, didn't notice until her daughter asked for one." This is B9C3's most original line and has never been tried as a diagram. B19C3 (the timeline that failed) was a GENERIC results timeline with no specific habit and no character: it's very possible it failed not because timelines don't work, but because it lacked the one thing that made B9C3's timeline convert: a concrete, specific, relatable habit disappearing.

**3. The progressive day-marker transformation, done WITH a witness at each marker.** B19C3 tried a bare timeline (Week 1/2/3/4, generic claims) and it failed: cheap clicks, zero ATCs. B9C3's version isn't just day markers, it's day markers PAIRED with specific witnesses (Sylvia at Day 9, husband at Day-whatever). The C3 failure may be diagnostic: a timeline without a witness is a claim; a timeline with a witness is proof. Worth testing the fix, not abandoning the format.

**4. The external-witness close (someone else notices, unprompted).** Never been diagrammed. This is arguably better suited to something closer to the "screenshot/chat mockup" formats you found and flagged last (the comment-mining IG-story format, the WhatsApp chat mockup) than to a whiteboard: a fake-but-plausible text-message screenshot ("him: you look different. what are you doing?") could port this exact beat into a completely different cheap wrapper.

**5. The named emotional anchor.** Every converter has ONE dominant emotion, and it's different each time (anger, surprise, nostalgia, relief). Nothing in whiteboard or B20 currently leads with an emotion: they're all logic/comparison-first. Untested: does a whiteboard diagram that opens on an emotional beat (not a comparison) convert differently?

## Recommendation: Concrete Next Cells (working name: B21, Portability Batch)

Each ports exactly ONE untested element from the list above into a cheap format, isolated, so we learn which elements survive translation and which don't: same discipline as every batch before this.

**Cell A: "Winnie's Card."** A whiteboard/index-card style diagram built around ONE specific named authority (Winnie or an equivalent), her photo (real or illustrated), her specific credential ("76, keeps bees, been doing this since 1987"), and a short quote, paired with the mechanism. Tests whether specific-human-authority survives translation into a static/diagram format, or whether authority requires narrative to land.

**Cell B: "The Habit That Left."** A whiteboard diagram built specifically around the habit-cessation beat: before/after, not generic ("used to carry lotion in 3 places → stopped, didn't notice for a week"): NOT a generic product-count comparison like B20C1, but the exact specific habit-disappearing insight from B9C3. Tests whether a specific, concrete before/after habit outperforms B19C3's generic timeline.

**Cell C: "He Noticed" screenshot.** Build the fake-text-message-screenshot format (from the reference images) around the external-witness beat specifically. Different wrapper entirely from whiteboard: tests the format, not just the element, since this may be a better home for witness-based proof than a diagram is.

**Cell D: Witnessed timeline (fixes C3, doesn't abandon it).** Rebuild the failed B19C3 concept but with ONE specific witness quote embedded at the peak day-marker, instead of a bare generic claim. Directly tests the diagnostic hypothesis that C3 failed for lack of a witness, not because timelines as a category don't work.

## What This Doc Does NOT Conclude

- It does not conclude whiteboard "should" carry every element: some (the external-witness close specifically) may belong in a different cheap format entirely (screenshot/chat mockup), which is why Cell C is a different wrapper, not another whiteboard cell.
- It does not override the active B20 checkpoint (still running, 3-4 days / $40-50 more spend before judged). This is the next batch queued behind that decision, not a replacement for it.
- Sample sizes on every converter here are still small (1-7 purchases each). Treat every "why it worked" as a working hypothesis to keep testing, not settled fact: same posture as every individual feedback loop this synthesizes.
