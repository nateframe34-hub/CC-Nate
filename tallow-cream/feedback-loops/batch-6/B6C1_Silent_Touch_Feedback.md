## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What this data isn't telling us (and why we initially misread it)

**1. A 20% CTR on 5 impressions is statistical noise. Period. (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

I initially flagged this as a possible breakout — "$18 CPM and 20% CTR" sounds like a winner. It isn't. 1 click out of 5 impressions has a 95% confidence interval of roughly 1%-72%. The "true" CTR could be anywhere in that range. Treating this as evidence of a winner was the same regression-to-mean trap that bit us on B1C1's "18.18% CTR" and B4C1's "$70 CPM, 15.38% CTR" day-1 reads. We need 50+ impressions before any signal claim is even directional.

**2. The $18 CPM is also unreadable (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

5 impressions at $0.09 spend means a single ad-served event was priced at less than $0.02. That's a placement-pricing artifact, not a CPM read. Could have been a single low-quality Reels impression at a fire-sale auction price.

**3. CBO has not committed budget (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

$0.09 spend in a day = the algorithm took a microscopic test and didn't continue. We don't know what would have happened if it had been fed budget because the budget never came.

**4. The wordless-touch hook concept is sound (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — qualitative)**

The B6 batch hypothesis was: vary the husband-noticing mechanism (touch vs. phone reaction vs. spoken compliment) to isolate which variant drives engagement best. B6C1 used a wordless touch — same identity-level beat that B5C3 used as the close, here moved to the opening structure. Conceptually defensible. We just never got the impressions to test it.

**5. We can't compare to B1C2 / B5C1 yet (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

5 impressions vs B5C1's 107 vs B1C2's 70. B6C1 hasn't earned its place in the comparison set. The "is wordless-touch better than verbal-comment?" question remains genuinely open.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The wordless-touch hook concept. Worth keeping as a candidate test if/when the campaign-level CPM problem is solved and ads are getting fairer delivery shots.
- The husband-character framework — proven at the structural level across B1C2 and B5C1.

### Change
- **No more calling 5-20 impression numbers "promising signals."** Hard rule. Both B1C1, B4C1, and B6C1 cost us interpretive capital by reading signal into noise. From here forward, no engagement claim under 50 impressions, no CPM claim under 100 impressions.
- **Actively test against B5C1 in the same campaign at the same budget.** If both ads are running at $20+/day budget split evenly, then the comparison is meaningful. Currently B5C1 dominated B6 budget and B6C1 starved.
- **Don't take "keep" status as meaningful.** If CBO won't fund it, "keep" and "kill" produce identical outcomes.

### Test Next
- Move B6C1 into the fresh-campaign test alongside the 3 converters as a 4th candidate. If CBO funds it in a clean campaign, the "wordless touch vs verbal comment" comparison becomes possible.
- A B6C1 v2 with a slightly more curiosity-baited hook ("My husband did something Tuesday night he hadn't done in years and I almost asked him to stop because I was afraid he'd realize what he was doing") could test whether a richer hook makes CBO commit budget.

---

## Decision

Effectively dead via starvation. Not killed, not tested — just absent from the campaign in any meaningful way. The wordless-touch concept survives intact and is a strong candidate for the fresh-campaign isolated test. The bigger lesson is operational: stop reading signal into 5-impression CTR numbers. That mistake has cost us interpretive credibility three times now (B1C1, B4C1, B6C1).
