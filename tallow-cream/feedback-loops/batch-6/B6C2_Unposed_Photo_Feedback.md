## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why this ad got 0 clicks

**1. The "unposed photo" mechanism may be too subtle for the hook (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The B6C2 hook concept — husband picks up his phone and takes a candid photo — requires the reader to understand WHY that's significant before it lands. Unlike "your skin looks different" (immediate validation) or "He never notices anything" (immediate character beat), "he took a photo" needs interpretation. The reader has to translate "candid photo = he wanted to capture how she looks right now = he sees her" before the emotional payoff arrives. That translation step happens too slowly for scroll-velocity reading.

**2. Photo-as-validation is a generational mismatch (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

The Invisible Woman avatar (Gen X, 45-65) doesn't experience candid phone photos the same way millennials/Gen Z do. For her, a husband taking a phone picture isn't necessarily a romantic gesture — it could be utilitarian (showing someone something, sending to a sibling, etc.). The mechanism doesn't carry the same emotional weight in this avatar's mental model that it would for a younger audience.

**3. CPM at $135 is mid-range — not the worst, not great (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The auction priced this ad reasonably. The starvation isn't an auction-side rejection; it's CBO seeing zero engagement in the first impressions and pulling back. Same pattern as B1C3 / B2C3 / B5C2 / B5C3 / B6C1.

**4. The B6 batch hypothesis is being tested by absence (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

B6 was supposed to test variations on the husband-noticing mechanism. What we're learning is: the mechanism that drove B5C1 (an explicit verbal comment about her skin) is doing more lifting than we credited. The variants (touch, photo, spoken compliment) are all underperforming. The verbal-comment-about-skin is the load-bearing element.

**5. Element density estimated 8/10 — built under the density rule (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Body construction is sound. Same problem as B5C2/B5C3 — body never got read because the hook didn't earn the click.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The B6 batch's hypothesis-testing intent — varying one element to isolate the load-bearing piece is the right experimental design.
- The "husband doing something he stopped doing" emotional beat as a body element. It could work as a mid-ad pattern interrupt.

### Change
- **Don't lead with subtle/interpretive gestures.** The hook needs to land emotionally in 1-2 sentences. Photo-as-validation requires too much reader processing.
- **Conclude that the verbal-comment mechanism is doing the work.** B5C1's success and B6C2/B6C3's failure together strongly suggest the explicit verbal comment ("your skin looks different") is the load-bearing element of the husband-noticed pattern. This is a useful negative result — we now know what NOT to vary.
- **Stop trying to vary the husband-noticed mechanism.** The hook is locked. The variation hypothesis was worth testing and the test produced a clear answer: the original wins.

### Test Next
- Don't re-run B6C2 as-is.
- The "unposed photo" beat could survive as a 30-50% pattern interrupt inside a different ad — e.g., an ad about losing identity in long marriages where the candid photo is the moment in the middle, not the opener.

---

## Decision

Killed correctly. The B6 batch's negative results are themselves a positive learning: the verbal comment is the load-bearing element of the husband-noticed pattern, and we can stop varying it. Future iterations of the husband-noticed hook should preserve the verbal-comment mechanism and only vary peripheral elements (the body content, the secondary characters, the close).
