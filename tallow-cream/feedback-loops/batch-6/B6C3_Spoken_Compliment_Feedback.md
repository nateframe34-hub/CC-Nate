## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What we can read from this data

**1. The "spoken compliment" hook earned some clicks but no LPV (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

1 click on 37 impressions = 2.70% CTR is below B5C1's 3.23% and B1C2's 5.71%, but it's not zero. The reader engaged with the hook. But 1 click → 0 LPV is a 100% click-to-LPV failure — either the user closed the page before it loaded, the destination URL had a load issue, or they bounced from the headline. With sample size of 1, we can't diagnose the click-to-LPV gap.

**2. CBO funded this ad MORE than the other B6 variants (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

$6.36 spend vs B6C1's $0.09 vs B6C2's $1.76. Within the B6 batch, this was CBO's pick. The algorithm read SOME engagement signal here that it didn't see in the other two variants. But $6.36 is still half-starved compared to B5C1's $21.21 day-1 spend.

**3. The "you look beautiful" comment is the WEAKER variant of the verbal-noticing pattern (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

This is the diagnostic insight. B5C1's husband says "your skin looks different. What are you doing?" That comment SPECIFICALLY references the SKIN — it's the visible result of the product the ad is selling. B6C3's hook uses a generic "you look beautiful" type compliment. Generic compliments ≠ skin-specific noticing. The reader can't extrapolate "this product → that compliment" from a generic comment. The compliment has to be ABOUT THE THING THE PRODUCT FIXES.

**4. CPM at $172 is in the campaign's normal range (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Same range as B1C2 ($166), B2C2 ($213). Auction priced this ad reasonably for the campaign. The lower performance vs B5C1 is not a CPM problem — it's a click-quality problem.

**5. Element density estimated 8/10 — built under the density rule (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Body construction is sound. The hook is the structural weak point.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The verbal-noticing mechanism — but the noticing must be SKIN-SPECIFIC, not generic.
- The husband-character framework — proven across B1C2 and B5C1.

### Change
- **Locked rule:** future husband-noticed variants must have the husband comment SPECIFICALLY on the skin. Not "you look great." Not "you look beautiful." Has to be "your skin looks different" / "what are you doing to your face" / "your skin feels softer." The ad sells skincare; the noticing has to be about skin.
- **Conclude the B6 hypothesis test:** verbal-comment mechanism is necessary but the comment content is also load-bearing. Generic compliment < skin-specific comment. This is a refined version of the B6C2 conclusion (the verbal mechanism is doing the work) — now refined to "and the verbal mechanism only works when the comment is product-relevant."
- **Don't re-run B6C3 as-is.**

### Test Next
- A future variant could test DIFFERENT skin-specific comments to find the highest-converting phrasing: "your skin looks different" vs "your skin feels softer" vs "have you been getting more sleep" vs "what changed?" — all are skin/face-specific noticing, all could be tested.
- Lower priority than fixing the upstream auction problem. No point varying which skin-specific comment converts best when no comment variant is getting fairly delivered.

---

## Decision

Killed correctly. B6C3 closes out the B6 batch's husband-variation hypothesis with a refined answer: the verbal comment is necessary AND must be skin-specific. Generic compliments don't carry the same conversion weight. Going forward, every Husband Noticed variant must keep the SKIN-SPECIFIC comment intact and only vary the body, characters, or close.
