# Converter Pattern Synthesis — What the 4 Early Converters Share

**Date:** 2026-04-25
**Author:** Derived from B3C2, B5C1, B7C1, B9C3 feedback loops + ad copy

> **Status Disclaimer:** These are early converters, not validated winners. A winner = sustained 2+ ROAS over 3 consecutive days. None of these four ads have hit that bar yet. Sample size: 4 ads, approximately 6 lifetime sales ($319.94 total conversion value). The pattern below is a working hypothesis extracted from a small sample — not a locked formula. It should inform B11-B14 creative, not constrain it.

## What They Share — The Working Converter Pattern

Elements present in all 4 are noted 4/4. Elements in 3 of 4 are noted 3/4 and should be treated as strong signals, not locked requirements.

**1. Solution-Aware avatar — the Invisible Woman who has already tried things (HIGH CONFIDENCE — 4/4)**

All 4 narrators are the same character: 48-54, spent money on products that stopped working, no longer expecting anything to fix it. The reader doesn't need to be sold on the problem — she already lives it. The ad's job is to hand her a new explanation for why what she's tried failed, then give her the one thing she hasn't tried. None of these ads explain that skin changes after 40. They skip past that and land directly on the "here's why everything you've been using was the wrong category" reframe.

**2. Accidental discovery — never "I went looking" (HIGH CONFIDENCE — 4/4)**

- B3C2: Niece's dermatology textbook sitting on the counter Thanksgiving morning. "I wasn't trying to read it."
- B5C1: Friend Lisa texted an unsolicited selfie. "She sent a selfie. Not a product photo."
- B7C1: Found mother's photo while cleaning out a closet box she'd been avoiding for three years.
- B9C3: Went upstairs to use a friend's guest bathroom at book club and noticed one jar where there used to be seven.

The discovery always arrives sideways. The narrator is never a researcher or a seeker. She stumbles into the information. This matters because it's the difference between "someone tried to sell me something" and "I figured something out that wasn't meant for me." The accidental framing makes the reader lean in instead of lean out.

**3. Peer-adjacent (not medical) authority — zero medical authority endorsing the product (HIGH CONFIDENCE — 4/4)**

- B3C2: Med student niece + dermatology textbook. The niece is the authority, but she's not recommending tallow — she's just the source of the mechanism information.
- B5C1: Lisa, the college friend "who returns things." Her credibility comes from her consumer behavior, not any credentials.
- B7C1: Karen, a friend over coffee — whose credibility is borrowed from the narrator's mother's six decades of good skin.
- B9C3: Elaine → Aunt Winnie. Winnie is 76, has kept bees in Vermont since 1987, and found her first jar at a farmer's market by accident. Zero professional qualifications. Maximum credibility.

No dermatologist recommends tallow in any of the 4 converters. The one doctor who appears (B3C2, B5C1) is the dismissing authority — present only to explain why the narrator didn't already know the answer. Medical authority is the foil, not the endorser.

**4. Water-vs-oil mechanism delivered around 50-60% of the body (HIGH CONFIDENCE — 4/4)**

All 4 ads deliver the mechanism in roughly the same structural location — after the hook, the failed-products history, and the discovery scene, but before the day markers and the close. The mechanism block is roughly 150-200 words in each converter. It always covers the same three beats: (a) skin makes its own oil, (b) after 40 it makes much less, (c) every cream she's been using had water as the first ingredient, and water evaporates. The tallow connection arrives as the natural conclusion of that logic, not as a sales claim.

B3C2 delivers this through the sebum graph and niece conversation. B5C1 through Lisa's link and "the part I want you to read slowly." B7C1 through Karen's kitchen-table explanation. B9C3 through Elaine relaying what Winnie told her at the wedding. The deliverer changes. The content is nearly identical across all 4.

**5. Progressive transformation arc (HIGH CONFIDENCE — 4/4, but day-numbered markers only 3/4)**

All 4 ads have a multi-week transformation sequence. B5C1, B7C1, and B9C3 use specific day numbers (Day 1, Day 4-5, Day 9-10, Day 14, Day 16-21). B3C2 uses week markers ("by the second week," "by the third week") without numbered days — which is one reason its feedback file called it 6-7/10 density. The progression structure is present 4/4. The day-numbered specificity is 3/4. Worth noting because the day numbers appear to be doing real work — they give the reader a vivid projection sequence and make the transformation feel observable, not claimed.

**6. Compliance posture — no menopause, no symptom strike-list, no shame framing (HIGH CONFIDENCE — 4/4, with one exception noted)**

All 4 converters avoid menopause, perimenopause, and hormonal language in their main framing. Low points are functional ("foundation cracked by noon," "nothing held past lunch") not emotional (hiding behaviors, shame, social withdrawal). B3C2 includes one line — "It fell off a cliff at menopause" — in the sebum graph description, which the feedback file notes but classifies as accidentally compliant because the mechanism focus pulled the copy away from personal-attribute framing. Three of the four are clean. One has a minor compliance flag that may not have triggered Meta's review.

**7. Skeptic-convert framing — "I made the face you're making" (HIGH CONFIDENCE — 4/4)**

All 4 ads name the objection explicitly and resolve it before the reader raises it. The phrase structure varies slightly but the move is the same in every converter: the narrator acknowledges that tallow sounds absurd, demonstrates that she or a trusted proxy had the same reaction, and then explains why she ordered anyway. This is the hinge moment in each ad — the three or four sentences that turn skepticism into curiosity. None of the 4 converters skip this beat.

**8. Habit-cessation beat — "I didn't notice I stopped" (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — 3/4)**

- B5C1: Stopped opening the phone's front camera every morning.
- B7C1: Stopped reaching for the hyaluronic acid. Just walked past it.
- B9C3: Stopped buying travel-size lotion packets in three places. Daughter came looking for one and there weren't any.
- B3C2: No habit-cessation beat. Has a cost-comparison beat instead ("I was spending $90 a month on a routine that wasn't working. I'm now spending $25").

The beat is present in 3/4. B3C2 is the outlier and is also the weakest converter (lowest price, unlogged spend, killed after one day). Whether that's because B3C2 is missing this specific element — or because it was missing several elements — can't be separated from this data.

**9. Husband-notice close (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — 3/4)**

B5C1, B7C1, and B9C3 all end with the husband noticing. B3C2 ends with a direct-to-reader practical CTA ("Stop refilling the bucket. Get the right bucket"). This is worth tracking but not worth treating as a rule yet — the husband-notice close may reflect that these three ads were written to the same formula (the densified 10-element skeleton), not that the husband-notice element itself is the load-bearing variable.

## Open Questions — What We Don't Know

**1. Are these converting because of craft, or because Meta is showing them to a narrow audience pocket that would convert on any halfway-decent ad? (UNKNOWN)**

All 4 converters are Invisible Woman ads. The account has not validated a non-Invisible-Woman converter. It's possible that Meta has found a very specific sub-segment of women (call her Lisa-Lisa-Lisa) who are already at decision-threshold, and the hook doesn't matter much because she'd convert on almost any ad in this category that clears a minimum craft bar. If that's true, the "pattern" is an artifact of Meta's targeting, not a structural formula. B11 (Working Hands Man) is the first opportunity to test whether a completely different avatar can also convert — that result will tell us whether the Invisible Woman avatar is uniquely matchable or whether the account has a broader conversion capacity.

**2. Husband-noticing close appears in 3/4. Feature or coincidence? (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE that it matters — not proven)**

The three ads with husband-notice closes (B5C1, B7C1, B9C3) are the three that were explicitly written to the 10-element densified skeleton. B3C2 was written earlier, before the skeleton was fully defined. It's possible the husband-notice close is load-bearing. It's also possible it's incidental — that these three ads share the close because they were written from the same template, not because the close itself drives conversion. Testing a 10-element ad with a different external witness at the close (adult daughter, longtime friend, coworker) would isolate this.

**3. Discovery character ages span mid-20s (niece in B3C2) to 76 (Aunt Winnie in B9C3). Does age matter? (UNKNOWN)**

B9C3's most distinctive structural choice is Winnie — and Winnie's age and specificity (76, 1987, Vermont beekeeper, Brattleboro farmer's market) is treated in the feedback file as one of the load-bearing elements. But we haven't tested a version of B9C3 where Winnie is removed or replaced with a younger authority. B9C3 v3 (Winnie isolation test) would answer this. The hypothesis worth testing: older peer-adjacent authorities carry more weight for this avatar because they represent living proof of a decades-long outcome, not just a 6-week experiment.

**4. Does the discovery setting matter? (UNKNOWN)**

Settings across the 4 converters: Thanksgiving morning kitchen counter (B3C2), unsolicited text message (B5C1), Sunday morning kitchen table (B7C1), book club guest bathroom (B9C3). The settings are genuinely different — two domestic, one digital, one semi-public. There's no obvious pattern in where the discovery happens. What appears consistent is that the discovery is always unplanned and contextually plausible. Whether any specific setting converts better than others hasn't been tested.

**5. Is the water-vs-oil mechanism load-bearing, or is the 4-week transformation doing the actual conversion work? (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE that both are required, but untested)**

The mechanism block is structurally present in all 4 converters. But so is the transformation sequence. A version of the body with the mechanism compressed to 30 words (instead of 150-200) and the transformation expanded hasn't been tested. The two elements may function as a unit — mechanism creates the logical permission to try it, transformation gives the reader a specific projection of what "trying it" looks like. Separating them in a test would require writing an ad that's mechanistically thin but transformation-dense, and one that's the reverse.

## What This Means for B11-B14 and Future Ads

These recommendations are based on what 4 ads share. They should be treated as strong defaults, not unbreakable rules, until the sample is larger.

- **Body formula carries across hooks — hooks can and should vary, body should not.** All 4 converters have different hooks but similar bodies. The discovery → mechanism → transformation sequence is the structural constant. Write experimental hooks freely. Do not experiment with the body structure until there are 10+ converters to work from.

- **Always include the 4-week transformation with day-numbered markers.** B3C2 used week markers and is the weakest converter. B5C1, B7C1, B9C3 all use day numbers. The day numbers give the reader a specific timeline to project herself into. Use them.

- **Always include peer-adjacent (not medical) authority.** The credibility chain in all 4 converters runs through real women with no professional qualifications — their authority comes from relationship length, specificity, or longevity of results. Never assign mechanism delivery to a dermatologist, doctor, or nurse practitioner endorsing the product.

- **Always include the water-vs-oil mechanism compressed to approximately 60-100 words.** Longer mechanism sections may be unnecessary. B9C3's mechanism block (via Winnie) is about 120 words. B7C1's (via Karen) is about 100 words. B3C2's (via the sebum graph) is the longest — around 250 words — and B3C2 is also the only ad that may have compliance-flagged language in the mechanism section. Shorter may be better.

- **Always include a habit-cessation beat.** The beat is present in 3/4 converters and absent in the weakest one (B3C2). The beat does specific work: it proves the change is real without the narrator having to claim it, because she discovers it accidentally. The habit being extinguished should be functional (reapplying, checking, carrying) not vanity-based (looking in the mirror more often, taking selfies).

- **Default to husband-notice close unless the avatar requires a different external witness.** For Homesteader Mom, the household-utility close (husband uses it on cracked hands, dog's dry paws, cast-iron seasoning) is more resonant. For Working Hands Man, the functional-performance close (gripped a steering wheel and realized he wasn't wincing). For the Invisible Woman, the husband-notice close has shown up in 3/4 converters and hasn't been outperformed by anything else yet.
