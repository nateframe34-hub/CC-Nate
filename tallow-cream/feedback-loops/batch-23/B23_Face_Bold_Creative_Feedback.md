# B23 Feedback Loop: Face-Reveal + Bold Swipe-Style Creative Test

**Killed:** 2026-07-05 EOD (founder), whole campaign, ~2 days of delivery.
**Lifetime totals:** ~$90 spend, ~26 clicks, ~22 LPV, **0 add-to-carts, 0 purchases** across all four cells. C3 never delivered a single impression.
**Test purpose:** lower CPM by testing faces (C1/C2 candid, C3/C4 bold produced-style) on proven copy. **It failed at exactly that purpose:** day-two CPMs ran $442-595, the most expensive delivery ever recorded on this account, worse than the plain faceless native baseline it was supposed to beat.

---

## Step 1: Hypothesis (why it didn't work, ranked most to least confident)

**1. Under purchase optimization, CPM prices predicted PURCHASES, not engagement, and Meta's model decided these creatives attract lookers, not buyers.** This is the big lesson and it reconciles the weirdest part of the data: the face creatives got the highest CTRs the account has ever seen (15-25%) while simultaneously getting the highest CPMs (up to $595). Under an engagement objective that combination is impossible; under purchase optimization it makes perfect sense. Meta watched the first day of clicks produce zero carts, concluded the ads generate curiosity without intent, and repriced delivery accordingly. The behavioral data agrees with Meta: at native's historical 11.5% click-to-ATC rate, ~26 clicks should have produced ~3 ATCs; producing zero has roughly a 4% probability by chance. The face clicks are genuinely different, and worse, than native's clicks.

**2. Faces changed WHO clicked, not just how many.** A warm face invites "who is she?" clicks; an intriguing object (the jar, the counter) invites "what is that?" clicks. The second kind carries product intent, the first doesn't. This is the same lookers-not-buyers failure family as whiteboard (cheap clicks, ATC collapse), arrived at from the opposite direction (expensive clicks this time).

**3. The engagement-lever theory misread what the swipe file's faces were doing.** The reference ads' faces worked as proof (a woman in real pain, a genuine after-state) attached to long conviction copy in categories with a clear problem-solution frame. B23 borrowed the face without the proof function: a pleasant candid face next to a skincare story is decoration, not evidence. The creative-gap analysis ranked faces as the top untested lever based on engagement research; engagement was the wrong currency to optimize, see #1.

**4. (Lower confidence) Two days and $90 is a thin read, and the 7/4-7/5 holiday auction may have inflated everything.** Kept honest: the kill was made before any cell hit the $66 line, on batch-level behavioral signal rather than per-cell thresholds. Given 0-for-26 clicks AND inverted CPMs, the kill is defensible, but the sample is small enough that "faces can never work" is NOT a licensed conclusion. What is licensed: THESE four executions failed on both metrics they were built to improve.

## Step 2: Action

**KEEP:** the hook-first copy resequencing, CTA bullet block, and P.S. line (untested as isolated variables here since the images confounded everything; they ride along in B24's advertorial and B17C1's future variants at zero cost).

**CHANGE (the strategic takeaway):** stop chasing CPM through "bolder/more engaging" imagery. Two independent tests (whiteboard, B23) now show the same failure from opposite directions: creative that wins attention without carrying purchase intent gets punished by purchase optimization, either as cheap clicks that don't cart or expensive impressions. The creative lever that matters is whether Meta's model predicts BUYING, and the only creative family it has ever priced favorably while converting is the proven native story. B17C1's relaunch at $94-135 CPM (vs $200 historical) suggests the real CPM lever was the stale ad object / fresh-object reset, not the imagery at all.

**TEST NEXT:** nothing in this direction until B24's advertorial test reads out. If a face ever gets retested, it must function as proof (a real texture/result/moment tied to the claim), not as warmth, and it should be tested as a thumbnail variable under the SAME long-copy conviction machine, not as a standalone bet.
