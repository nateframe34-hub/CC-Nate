## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why we have no real read

**1. 20 impressions / 0 clicks across 2 days = the hook didn't earn engagement (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

CBO sampled this ad lightly, saw zero engagement signal in the first impressions, and pulled back. 0% CTR on 20 impressions is a stronger negative signal than 0% on 5 impressions — at this sample, we can say with reasonable confidence that the hook isn't earning clicks in cold paid distribution.

**2. The "time reversal" opener may be too counter-intuitive for scroll-velocity reading (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

"I gave up on moisturizer three months ago. Not because I found something better. Because I gave up." This is brilliant on a second reading. On a first half-second scan, it reads as "I gave up" → reader's brain registers "this person quit something" → no curiosity hook because nothing PROMISES a payoff. B1C2 / B5C1's "He never notices anything" promises the reader something is about to happen. "I gave up" implies nothing is about to happen.

**3. The body is arguably the strongest in the project (HIGH CONFIDENCE — qualitative)**

Reading the body in isolation, this is the most structurally complete ad we've written. Sister Claire as the discovery character, Meg as the external witness, the husband-touch identity payoff at week 4, the "stopped counting products on the counter" unexpected discovery, Jan at the gym ordering one too. The mechanism explanation ("you can't fix an oil problem with water") is the cleanest in the library. 10/10 density. None of it matters because the hook didn't pull the reader in.

**4. CPM in the $90-130 range is actually reasonable for the campaign (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

Compared to B2C2 ($213), B5C1 ($228), B1C2 ($166) — B5C3's $117 average is on the lower end of the campaign range. This suggests the creative + mechanism content reads cleanly to Meta's scanner. The auction would have priced this ad reasonably IF anyone had clicked. The starvation isn't an auction-side issue; it's a CBO-side decision based on zero engagement.

**5. Element density is genuinely 10/10 (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

This is the most rigorously density-counted ad in the library (full self-audit in the ad doc). Multi-mechanism hook, specific inciting incident (the November morning), 4 named secondary characters each doing different work, 5-rung trust ladder, 7 pattern interrupts, narrator-as-skeptic frame, 4-week progressive transformation with unexpected discovery (stopped counting), close mirrors hook with a layered second reversal. The density work is real. The delivery problem isn't structural.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The body, almost word for word. Sister Claire as discovery character, the water/oil mechanism payoff, the husband-touch identity beat, the "I stopped counting products" unexpected discovery — all of this is gold.
- The narrator-as-skeptic framing. Having the narrator already be at rock bottom (she quit entirely) is a powerful trust-builder. Worth keeping.
- The mechanism education. "You can't fix an oil problem with water" is the clearest belief shift in the library.

### Change
- **Rebuild the hook as a scene, not a thesis.** The November morning when she walked out without applying anything is a SCENE. Open with that scene — "I walked out of the bathroom that morning without putting anything on my face for the first time in twenty years" — instead of with the abstract "I gave up" claim. Same emotional content, dramatized.
- **Add a curiosity promise to the first 3 sentences.** The reader needs to know something INTERESTING is coming. "I gave up" promises nothing. "The morning I walked out of the bathroom with nothing on my face was the morning my husband finally noticed me again" promises both a problem and a resolution.
- **Don't kill the body.** When a B9/B10 ad rebuilds the hook, port this body forward intact.

### Test Next
- A B9 ad could open with the November-morning scene and keep the rest of B5C3's body. Direct test of "does a stronger hook fix the delivery problem on a body we already know is structurally sound?"
- The "narrator as skeptic" framing is a structural pattern worth porting to other ads. Future Product Graveyard (Sub-Avatar #2) ads especially.

---

## Decision

Killed via starvation. The body is the most over-engineered piece of writing in the project and almost none of it got read. This ad is a study in why hooks decide everything — the body can be perfect and still not matter if the first 3 sentences don't earn the click. The lesson is going into the next iteration: pair this body with a scene-based hook and re-test as a B9.
