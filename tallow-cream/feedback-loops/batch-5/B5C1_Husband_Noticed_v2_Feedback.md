## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What this ad proved

**1. The densified hook converts at full price (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

B1C2 (5-element version) generated 2 ATCs and 0 sales at $19.99 max. B5C1 (10-element version) generated 1 sale at FULL price $49.99. Same hook, structurally rebuilt. The density thesis did exactly what it was supposed to do: deepen the conversion mechanism so the reader buys at full price instead of bouncing or only converting on a discount. Even with limited data, the directional movement (from 0 sales to 1 full-price sale) is meaningful.

**2. Conversion funnel is clean: 3 clicks → 2 LPV → 1 ATC → 1 Checkout → 1 Purchase (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

This is a textbook funnel. No drop-off at any stage. 100% LPV-to-ATC, 100% ATC-to-Checkout, 100% Checkout-to-Purchase. The PEOPLE this ad attracts are buyers, not browsers. Sample is 3 — but the shape of the funnel is the right shape. Compare to B2C2 (4 LPV → 0 ATC, completely broken funnel).

**3. The hook is locked in (HIGH CONFIDENCE — across two runs)**

"My husband looked up from his laptop last Tuesday and said 'your skin looks different. What are you doing?' He never notices anything." This hook produced engagement in BOTH runs (B1C2 and B5C1) with completely different bodies underneath. The opening 3 sentences are the highest-leverage piece of copy in the entire project library.

**4. CBO funded it day 1 (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

$21.21 on day 1 = serious CBO commitment. The algorithm saw the engagement signal early and fed it. The density may not have lowered CPM but it did make the ad a CBO winner.

### What this ad did NOT prove

**1. The CPM thesis FAILED (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

B5C1 averaged $214 CPM. B1C2 averaged $166 CPM. The densified version is MORE expensive per impression than the thin version. Density does not reduce CPM. This was the headline hypothesis of the B5 batch and it's been falsified. The CPM problem is upstream — not addressable through creative density.

**2. One sale is not statistical proof of the funnel (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

3 LPV → 1 sale is 33% but it's three data points. Could be a fluke. Could be the one analytical buyer who would have converted on any halfway-decent ad. We need 10+ LPVs at this funnel rate before we can call it structurally validated.

**3. ROAS sustainability is uncertain (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Day 1 ROAS 2.36x is decent. Day 2 ROAS 0 (no sales on $1.71). Pattern across 5+ days is required before calling sustainable performance. 1 sale in 2 days at $22.92 spend = $0.05 per dollar spent on a sustained basis if Day 1 was an outlier.

**4. Density alone won't fix the underlying auction problem (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Even at 10/10 density, the ad still got starved on Day 2 ($1.71 spend, 14 imp). The auction is not respecting the structural quality of this creative. Whatever's keeping CPM high is upstream of element count.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The locked hook. Three runs in a row would be a stronger validation test — keep this hook in any future re-launch.
- The 10-element density framework. Even if it didn't lower CPM, it produced our first full-price sale, which is the conversion outcome that matters more than auction price.
- The progressive 4-week transformation arc (Week 1 melted in, Week 2 reach was gone, Week 3 foundation held, Week 4 husband touched her face). This is the conversion mechanism — it gives the reader a vivid sequence to project themselves into.
- The "absent-minded husband touch" as the identity-level payoff. Most powerful close-beat in the project.

### Change
- **Stop calling B5 "the CPM reduction test."** That hypothesis is dead. Rename or close out the batch officially.
- **Move B5C1 into the fresh-campaign test.** This ad earned the right to a clean campaign environment. Run it alongside B3C2 and B7C1 in the proposed isolated campaign.
- **Don't kill B5C1 prematurely.** The kill list has it on for kill but the funnel data argues against killing the only ad that's produced a full-price sale via the cleanest funnel in the project.

### Test Next
- The fresh-campaign duplicate (with B3C2 + B5C1 + B7C1) is THE test that should run next. If B5C1's CPM drops in a clean campaign, the upstream theory is confirmed. If CPM stays $200+, the problem is at the page/pixel/product level and needs a different intervention.
- A "Husband Noticed v3" densified test could swap the husband for a different external witness (adult daughter, longtime friend, hairdresser) to isolate whether the husband-character specifically drives the engagement or whether the external-noticing ACT is what carries.

---

## Decision

Reconsider the kill. This is the only ad that has produced a full-price sale on a clean conversion funnel. The CPM thesis failed, but the conversion thesis succeeded. This belongs in the fresh-campaign test, not in the graveyard. The right move is: kill it from the current campaign (it's getting starved anyway), then resurrect it in a clean campaign as one of the three converters.
