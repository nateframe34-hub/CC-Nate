## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why we have no real read on this ad

**1. 15 impressions across 2 days = total starvation (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

CBO is not feeding this ad. 1 click on 4 impressions = 25% CTR is statistical noise. 0 clicks on 11 impressions = 0% CTR is also noise at that sample size. The ad has not been tested.

**2. CPM data is contradictory because of tiny samples (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Day 1: $325 CPM on 4 impressions. Day 2: $59 CPM on 11 impressions. Neither number is reliable. A single ad-served event swings the CPM by $50-100 at this sample size. Treating either day's CPM as signal would be a regression-to-mean trap.

**3. The confession opener is a high-risk hook format (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"I've been trying to figure out how to say this for almost six weeks. I kept starting. I kept stopping." This is intimate inward-tone copy. It works in a Facebook post written by someone the reader already knows. It struggles in a paid placement to a stranger because the reader has no relationship-equity with the narrator yet. The confession lands harder once the reader is already in the story; leading with it asks the reader to invest emotion in someone they've never heard of.

**4. The hook may be exactly what CBO is bouncing off (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

CBO's first ~10 impressions of an ad determine whether it scales. If the first impressions go to readers who scroll past the slow confession opener, the algorithm reads "no engagement" and pulls budget. That could be why we're stuck at 15 impressions — the algorithm sampled, didn't see early clicks, and walked away.

**5. Element density is real (10/10) but doesn't help if the ad never delivers (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The body copy is structurally sound — multiple named characters, full trust ladder, progressive transformation, identity-level payoff. But density only helps the reader who reaches the body. If the hook can't earn the click, the body never matters.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The confession structure as a body-copy device. Embedded inside a stronger hook, the "I haven't told anyone" beat could be a powerful pattern interrupt at the 30-50% mark.
- The 10-element density framework. Body is well-built — just needs a different opening.

### Change
- **Replace the confession opener with a scene opener.** The confession can be the SECOND or THIRD beat, not the first. Open with an external moment (Rachel at Thanksgiving, the friend who notices, a specific scene) and have the confession land at the pattern-interrupt point.
- **Don't claim signal from sub-50-impression samples.** Same rule applied to B4C1.
- **Consider this ad a structural lesson, not a test result.** We didn't get to test the confession-opener hypothesis. We tested whether the algorithm bothers to deliver a slow opener — and the answer is "barely."

### Test Next
- A B9 or later variant could test the confession beat as a MID-AD pattern interrupt rather than as an opener. "Here's the part I haven't told anyone" appearing at the 40% mark, after a scene-based opener, would test whether the confession mechanism converts when the reader is already in the story.
- Don't re-run B5C2 as-is. The starvation pattern is conclusive enough to stop investing in this configuration.

### Reconsider
- The April 13 keep-list has B5C2 staying on. Given 15 impressions in 2 days, the practical effect of "keeping" it is identical to killing it — CBO won't fund it either way. Worth questioning whether "keep" status is just inertia.

---

## Decision

Effectively dead via starvation. Not technically killed but functionally dead. The confession-opener hypothesis is untested — not falsified, just untested. The 10-element body construction was solid; the hook may have been the wrong experiment for a stranger-cold paid placement. Lesson: hook format determines whether ANY of the body work matters, regardless of how dense the body is.
