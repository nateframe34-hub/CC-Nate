# Gap Analysis: June 2026 Review + B17C1 Angle Dissection

**Date:** 2026-07-02
**Scope:** everything run in the past month (B17 through B22), with the standing question: what are we not doing that would either lower delivery cost or open a new converting angle.

---

## Part 1: What B17C1's angle actually is (dissection)

Everyone shorthand-labels B17C1 "the counter ad" or "object discovery," but the label undersells what is doing the work. Read as a desire mechanism, the ad is a **peer-secret envy angle**. The precise shape:

1. **A peer, not a brand and not an expert, has visibly solved the problem the reader still has.** Elaine's counter went from seven products to one. Nobody sold her anything on camera. The reader is watching one woman notice another woman's quiet win.
2. **The evidence is an artifact, not a claim.** The one jar on the counter is proof that persuades before a single product word appears. This is why the ad opens on an object and not a face or a promise.
3. **The secret is withheld, then earned.** She doesn't ask at book club. She waits three weeks. The reader waits with her. By the time Winnie's explanation arrives, the reader has begged for it internally, which converts the mechanism from "marketing claim" to "answer to a question I asked."
4. **Authority is laundered through two degrees of separation.** Winnie (76, bees, Vermont, 1987, farmer's market) is unGoogleable, uncommercial, and specific enough to feel real. The brand never speaks. The chain is friend -> friend's aunt -> physics.
5. **The close is the actual core desire: being seen again.** The husband at the candle table. The product is a means; the purchased outcome is "the person across the table looking up."

So the angle stack is: **envy of a peer's quiet result -> withheld secret -> laundered authority -> mechanism -> being noticed again.** Its 7 purchases are not "a good story converts." They are this specific ladder converting. B21's image swap keeps the whole ladder and only changes the thumbnail; that is why it was the right first test.

**The dissection's practical implication:** the account has ONE proven desire ladder and has been re-rolling it (B17C1 = B9C3; B21 = same copy again). Every other converter (B7C1 Pond's nostalgia, B5C1 husband-notice, B3C2 mechanism metaphor) is a partial expression of the same ladder. The untested adjacent angles below inherit pieces of it deliberately.

## Part 2: Gaps, ranked by expected impact

### GAP 1 (CPM lever, biggest and cheapest): post ID reuse, social proof accumulation
Every new batch has launched as fresh ads with zero likes, zero comments, zero shares. Meta prices unproven, engagement-free ad posts higher, and users trust them less (both CPM and CVR suffer). The fix is standard practice we have never used: run every ad as an **existing post** (use the same post ID across ad sets/campaigns) so all engagement accumulates on one object. B17C1 has burned $210 this month building engagement that got thrown away every time the ad was recreated. Related unused lever: nobody is replying to comments as Julie, and comment sections on a camouflage ad are where the format either survives (real-feeling conversation) or dies (silence, or worse, unanswered "is this an ad?"). Cost: zero. Applies to: every ad from tomorrow onward, including B21/B22.

### GAP 2 (CPM lever): everything we run is a static image, video inventory is systematically cheaper
All 22 batches are statics. Meta's auction has more video inventory than static (Reels especially) and typically prices it 30-50% cheaper per impression. This does NOT require abandoning the native strategy: the lowest-effort test is the same B9C3 hook image as a 10-15 second slow-zoom/pan "slideshow" with the first lines of the copy as native-feeling captions, exported as video. It keeps the camouflage (real photo, no production) but buys entry into cheaper auctions. This is the single largest untouched delivery-cost lever after post ID reuse, and it composes with the B21 problem/outcome imagery already made (trash can render as frame 1, counter render as frame 2, jar as frame 3 = a 3-shot story slideshow using assets that already exist).

### GAP 3 (CPM lever): placement breakdown has never been read
No analysis this month (or ever, per the tracker) has split CPM by placement. If Advantage+ placements are dumping B17C1 into Facebook Feed desktop or right-column inventory for the 45-65 demo, part of the $200 CPM is placement mix, not creative. Free diagnostic in Ads Manager (Breakdown -> Placement). If Reels/Stories impressions are materially cheaper for the same ad, a Reels-first version of native (vertical 9:16 crop of the same images) is a cheap follow-up. Zero cost to look.

### GAP 4 (CVR lever for B22 and any produced format): clicks land raw on the PDP with no bridge
The whole Origins playbook this repo is built on runs produced/curiosity formats through a **presell/advertorial page**, and `origins-training/swipe-file/Presell swipe/` has 10 reference captures sitting unused. Native works direct-to-PDP because the ad IS the presell (1,500 words of it). B22's news-chyron format has no story at all; a click lands on a product page having read one headline. That gap is very likely where "the news format gets solid CPMs but is mid at converting" lives. A single advertorial page (the B9C3 story lightly reformatted as an article, "A 76-year-old beekeeper's routine is going viral," CTA to PDP) would give every produced-format test a bridge, and it reuses copy that already exists. This is the biggest gap in the B22 experiment design as it stands.

### GAP 5 (portfolio risk): converter concentration and abandoned proven ads
Month spend was ~$400, of which ~$280 went to B17C1 + its B21 re-roll (same copy). Meanwhile B7C1 (2 purchases) and B5C1 (1 purchase) are proven converters that have had zero spend since April/May and were never re-run post-PDP, post-USD-switch, or with the post-ID trick. Before writing brand-new copy for future batches, re-running proven converters cheaply is lower-risk than net-new creative. If B17C1's copy ever fatigues (frequency is fine today, but one copy carrying the account is fragile), there is no warmed-up second string.

### GAP 6 (angle gap): the "reads like food" trust angle is signaled but never run as native
B20C3 (Label Test whiteboard: long unreadable ingredient list vs 4 readable words) quietly produced the best whiteboard engagement after C2 and multiple ATCs at a $58-75 CPM, and Amazon-review research shows buyers repeatedly citing "I can pronounce everything in it." That is a real, distinct angle (trust through legibility, "your grandmother could read this label") which has never been given the native narrative treatment. It inherits the mechanism rung of the B17C1 ladder but swaps envy for suspicion-relief, a different emotional door into the same purchase. Candidate for the next net-new native batch.

### GAP 7 (angle gap): nobody buys this as a gift yet, and the story already contains the gift-giver
Lily (the 27-year-old daughter) exists in the converting copy already. The daughter-buys-for-mother direction (younger woman sees the ad, buys for her mom; or mom sends it to daughters) is a second audience with dramatically cheaper CPMs (25-34 women are among the cheapest inventory on the platform) and it does not require abandoning the Invisible Woman: the ad speaks ABOUT her, to her daughter. This is the only identified path to structurally cheap CPMs that does not dilute the buyer signal, because the buyer is genuinely a different, cheaper demo. Worth one test cell when bandwidth allows.

### GAP 8 (seasonality, plan-ahead): dry-skin demand is counter-cyclical and it is July
The entire product story (tight by noon, cracking foundation, carrying lotion) peaks in cold months. Summer is structurally the weakest demand season for this product, meaning: (a) current CPMs and CVRs are being read at the seasonal trough, so decisions made now are conservative-biased; (b) October-January is when a proven ad + cheap delivery combination would actually scale. The work being done now (finding the cheap-delivery format for the proven ladder) is exactly the right summer project, but the calendar deserves to be explicit: the goal is to walk into October with the winner + post-ID engagement + a video variant + an advertorial already built.
