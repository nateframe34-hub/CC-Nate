## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What this ad's data is telling us

**1. The day-1 CPM ($70) and CTR (15.38%) on 13 impressions was statistical noise — not signal (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

In retrospect, calling this "promising early signal" was a regression-to-mean trap. 2 clicks on 13 impressions = 15.38% CTR. 1 fewer click would have made it 7.7%. CPM at $70 on 13 impressions has a $20 swing per single ad-served event. We made the same mistake here we made on B1C1's "18.18% CTR" reading. The April 10 data confirmed the ad's true CPM is much higher — $686 on a slightly larger (still small) sample.

**2. CBO has stopped feeding this ad (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

April 10: $5.49 / 8 impressions. April 11: $0.01 / 3 impressions. The algorithm is starving it. Either the engagement signal vanished, or the calendar urgency framing tripped a delivery filter, or both.

**3. The "calm resolve" urgency angle is the cleanest of the three B4 urgency tests (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The hook — "I'm 49. It's 10:47pm on a Tuesday. I just made a decision." — passes compliance cleanly. No menopause, no symptom words, no doctor character, no hiding behaviors. The internal-decision-moment frame is well-constructed. This is one of the better-written ads in the project, and it still couldn't get CBO to fund it. That's diagnostic — when even clean compliance can't force delivery, the auction problem is upstream.

**4. The "compounding damage clock" mechanism is intellectually correct but emotionally cold (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"Every month you wait, you lose more ground" is a true statement and a real urgency lever — but it's a HEAD argument, not a HEART argument. The avatar's deepest desire is to be SEEN AGAIN by people she loves. "Ground lost" is abstract. The B5/B7 successful ads close on relational moments (husband touching her face, friend ordering one too) — those are concrete. B4C1's close is internal: "I made a decision." That doesn't reach into the avatar's social world.

**5. Element density estimated 7-8/10 (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Better than B1-B3 — written under the early version of the density rule. But still missing some pieces: secondary characters are thin (largely a solo internal monologue), trust ladder is built around the decision-moment beat rather than mechanism, no skeptic convert frame because the narrator IS the calm decider. The calm-resolve register mutes the density.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The hook structure ("I'm [age]. It's [specific time]. I just [action].") This is a strong scene-opener template — pulls the reader into a specific moment with a specific decision. Worth replicating.
- The compliance posture. This ad is one of the cleanest in the library on Meta's 2026 rules. Use it as a template for future urgency-themed ads.
- The 6th-grade reading level execution. Very clean.

### Change
- **Don't read CPM signal from sub-50-impression samples.** Hard rule going forward — no claiming a winner until 100+ impressions across at least 2 days.
- **Add 2-3 named external characters.** The decision-moment opener is internal; the body needs friends/sisters/spouses to externalize the validation. Right now the ad is a single voice in a single head for too long.
- **Move the close from internal decision to relational outcome.** "I made a decision" → "Three weeks later my sister called and asked what I'd done."
- **Cut the compounding-damage clock language.** It's intellectually right but emotionally cold and may be tripping subtle compliance filters around "your skin will get worse" implications.

### Test Next
- The hook template is the keeper. A B9 or B10 ad could re-use the "I'm [age]. It's [time]. I just [action]." pattern but with relational anchor characters and an external-validation close. That would test the template separately from the urgency mechanism.
- Don't re-run B4C1 as-is. The starvation pattern means CBO has decided.

---

## Decision

Effectively dead. Spent ~$6 across three days, no engagement, no conversions. This is a quiet kill — CBO did the killing for us. The ad's lesson is not that urgency doesn't work, it's that internal-decision urgency doesn't earn delivery. Externalize urgency through other characters or calendar events with people in them, not through abstract "ground being lost" framing.
