## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What the $1,000 CPM is telling us

**1. "Active harm" framing is a separate Meta penalty category (HIGH CONFIDENCE — DOCUMENTED LEARNING)**

The hook references a moment of physical revulsion ("I wanted to throw up") tied to an anti-competitor / anti-product reframe ("the moisturizer you're using is actively making your skin worse"). Even though the ad has no menopause, no doctor character, no symptom strike words, and no "your body stopped" diagnostic claim — Meta's scanner still hit it with a delivery penalty 10x worse than B2C2's $213 CPM. This is a NEW class of penalty we hadn't documented before. We now know: anti-competitor / "this product is actively harming you" framing is its own penalty bucket. This is a project-shaping insight worth $14 of spend.

**2. Visceral disgust language ("throw up") is itself a flag (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Meta's content scanner penalizes physical-disgust content separately from health claims. "I wanted to throw up" is the kind of language that triggers content moderation across the entire ad surface — even if the rest of the ad is compliant. The hook condemned the ad before any other line was read.

**3. The "humectants are pulling water from your skin" mechanism is true but reads as anti-establishment (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Glycerin / hyaluronic acid as humectants CAN pull water from deeper skin layers in dry environments — that's an established cosmetic chemistry fact. But "your cream is hurting you" framing puts the brand in adversarial posture against an entire category. Meta's algorithm has 2026-era heuristics for "wellness conspiracy" content and this trips them.

**4. The $1,000 CPM means the ad got 14 impressions because CBO had no choice (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Meta's auction priced this ad out of every desirable inventory slot. The 14 impressions it did serve were the cheapest available — likely Reels in low-quality placements. CBO didn't choose to starve it; the auction did. This is structurally different from the B1C1 / B1C3 starvation pattern (which was CBO declining to fund a low-engagement ad).

**5. We can't evaluate the actual angle because the ad never got tested (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The "humectant reversal" mechanism, the STOP command close, the anger register — none of these got tested. The hook killed the ad before any other element could be measured.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The anger register CONCEPT — anger is a documented avatar emotion. But anger has to be directed at the SYSTEM/category, not at a competitor product. B3C2's "I'm still angry about it" works. B4C2's "your cream is hurting you" doesn't.
- The humectant mechanism EDUCATION — this is a real differentiator for tallow vs water-based creams. But it has to be framed as "different category" (B5C3's approach), not as "your current cream is harming you."

### Change
- **Hard rule added:** never use disgust/illness language in a hook ("throw up," "sick," "gross," "disgusting"). Goes on the strike list.
- **Hard rule added:** never frame competitor categories as "actively harming." Differentiation is "different category" or "wrong category." Not "harmful."
- **Hard rule added:** no STOP command close. Imperative anti-competitor instructions trip multiple Meta heuristics simultaneously.
- These rules need to be formalized in `compliance-and-readability.md` as Rule 7: Active-Harm Framing Is A Separate Penalty Category.

### Test Next
- Don't re-run any version of B4C2. The active-harm framing is structurally banned now.
- The humectant mechanism is currently being delivered effectively in B5C3 ("Every cream I'd been buying was made of water... You can't fix an oil problem with water"). That's the right execution.

Killed correctly within hours. The $14 spent is the cheapest education we'll ever get on a brand-new compliance category. The ad's ONLY contribution is the lesson: active-harm framing is a $1,000 CPM mistake. That lesson is now embedded in the project's documented compliance posture and prevents this whole class of error in B5/B6/B7/B8 and beyond.
