## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What the data tells us (and doesn't)

**1. CBO won't fund this ad — 9 impressions across 2 days (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The starvation pattern is extreme. Even B1C1 got 11 impressions on day 1. B4C3 got 9 across two days. The algorithm is actively choosing not to deliver this creative. We can't tell if the auction is rejecting it (high CPM penalty) or if engagement signals from the first few impressions were so negative that CBO de-prioritized it.

**2. The $100 CPM is more reliable than B4C1's $686 readings (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

Both spread across single-digit impressions but the price stayed consistent at ~$100 across both days. That's directionally cleaner than other ads where CPM swung from $70 to $686 between days. Still too small a sample to call it the "true" CPM.

**3. The hook is the strongest of the three B4 urgency mechanisms (HIGH CONFIDENCE — qualitative)**

"In six weeks, I'm walking into a high school gym I haven't been in since 1994." External calendar urgency with a real specific event the avatar can imagine. No menopause, no doctor, no symptom words. Compliance is clean. The reunion mechanism is well-researched (the deep research identified milestone events as a real avatar trigger). This SHOULD have worked. The starvation suggests something else is upstream.

**4. The "30-year reunion" specificity may be too narrow for broad delivery (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"Six weeks from Saturday" + "high school reunion" is intensely specific. Meta's algorithm reads the text to estimate audience match — a hyper-specific scenario may have been classified as a low-relevance creative for the broad 40+ targeting. The narrative works for the narrow segment going to a reunion; for everyone else, the urgency doesn't translate.

**5. Element density estimated 7-8/10 (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

Same as B4C1 — written under the early density rule. Strong external calendar anchor (the reunion), some secondary characters (high school friends mentioned), but trust ladder is built around the deadline rather than the mechanism, and the skeptic convert frame is light.

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The external calendar anchor mechanism as a concept. Real upcoming events (reunions, weddings, milestone birthdays) are documented avatar urgency triggers and the structure passes compliance cleanly.
- The "time safety net" framing of the 60-day guarantee. "If it doesn't work in 6 weeks, return it" — this is the cleanest guarantee framing in the project. Worth porting to other ads.
- The 6th-grade reading level execution. Clean.

### Change
- **Broaden the calendar anchor.** Instead of one specific event (a reunion), use a category of events ("we have weddings, birthdays, and graduations on the calendar every spring"). This may help Meta classify the creative for broader delivery.
- **Add 2-3 named external characters.** Old high school friends, the friend planning the reunion, sister discussing what to wear — all could externalize the urgency and add density.
- **Replace the singular reunion deadline with a sequence of relational moments.** "I'll see Janet for the first time in 30 years" is one beat. Adding "and my niece's wedding in August. And my mother-in-law's 80th in November." spreads the deadline across multiple anchors that are more likely to resonate with non-reunion-attending readers.

### Test Next
- Don't re-run B4C3 as-is. The starvation pattern is conclusive enough to stop investing.
- A future "calendar anchor" ad could test the broader-event-category version. Lower priority than densifying the proven hooks (Husband Noticed, Pond's, Drawer).

---

## Decision

Effectively dead via starvation. Spent $0.91 across two days, no engagement, no conversions. The cleanest urgency mechanism in B4 still couldn't get delivery, which reinforces the campaign-level insight: even compliant, well-structured ads aren't escaping the auction problem. The B4 batch as a whole (B4C1 starved, B4C2 killed at $1k CPM, B4C3 starved) tells us the urgency hypothesis was right to test but the execution layer can't fix what's happening upstream.
