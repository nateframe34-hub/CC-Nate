## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why this ad got 0 clicks on 47 impressions

**1. The doctor/dermatologist authority figure violates Meta's 2026 health ad rules (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Meta penalizes ad copy that uses medical authority figures to endorse skincare/wellness products. "I asked my dermatologist..." in the hook is exactly the trigger pattern. This is now a documented Rule in `compliance-and-readability.md` (no doctor/dermatologist character endorsing the product). $175 CPM on a low-engagement creative is consistent with Meta penalizing the ad while still delivering it to a small inventory pool.

**2. The hook reads as a brand pitch, not a personal story (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

"I asked my dermatologist why my skin suddenly couldn't hold moisture. What she said made me rethink everything I was putting on my face." This is the structure of a clickbait health article, not a friend's confession on Facebook. Native ads succeed because they DON'T sound like ads. This sounds like an ad. The 0% CTR confirms it — 47 people saw it and not one felt compelled to investigate.

**3. The hook is question-based, not scene-based (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The opening describes a CONVERSATION the narrator had, summarized — not a SCENE the reader can see. Compare to B1C2's "He never notices anything" — that's a character beat the reader knows. B1C3 opens with a generic doctor-visit setup that could apply to anyone. No specificity, no character, no scene. Per `phase-2-story-elements.md`, opening with a scene is foundational. This ad opened with a thesis statement.

**4. CBO recognized the weakness immediately (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

$8.23 spend on 47 impressions = CBO gave it a small test budget, watched the engagement signals (0 clicks), and pulled back. This is exactly how CBO is supposed to work — punish creatives with no engagement signal. The algorithm did its job.

**5. The mechanism payoff (sebum decline) is buried in jargon (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

Even if the reader had clicked, the body copy explains skin oil decline using terms like "sebum production" and "skin barrier function" — vocabulary that fails the 6th-grade reading test in `compliance-and-readability.md`. The B5/B6 rewrites use "the oil your skin makes on its own" — same mechanism, accessible language.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The underlying mechanism — skin produces less oil after 40 — is the right belief shift for the avatar. It's the WHY behind every successful tallow ad.
- The instinct to use authority — but reframed. Sister, friend, older woman whose skin looks great — these are peer authorities that don't trip Meta's scanner.

### Change
- **Never use a doctor/dermatologist/medical professional as the discovery character.** This is now a hard compliance rule. Replaced with peer authorities (sister Claire in B5C3, friend Lisa in B5C1, neighbor Tina in B5C2).
- **Open with a scene, not a conversation summary.** Place the reader in a specific moment with specific characters at a specific time. Generic doctor-office settings are deadweight.
- **Translate every mechanism term to 6th-grade language.** "Sebum" → "the oil your skin makes." "Barrier function" → "what holds moisture in." "Lipid layer" → don't use it at all.

### Test Next
- The "older sister whose skin always looked better" character (used in B5C3) is the peer-authority replacement for the dermatologist. That's the live test now. If B5C3 produces better engagement than B1C3, the substitution is validated.
- Don't re-run the dermatologist angle. It's structurally penalized.

---

## Decision

Killed correctly. This ad was a compliance own-goal. The dermatologist character is a 2026 Meta penalty, the hook was generic, and the language was jargon-heavy. Nothing here is salvageable as-is — the LESSON is. Doctor/medical authority discovery is now formally banned in `compliance-and-readability.md` because of this ad.
