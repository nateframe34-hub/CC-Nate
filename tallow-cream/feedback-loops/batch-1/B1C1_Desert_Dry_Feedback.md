## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why this ad got starved and never produced data

**1. The 18.18% CTR is statistically meaningless at 11 impressions (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

2 clicks out of 11 impressions reads as a "great CTR" but it's noise. The math: 1 more click would have made it 27%, 1 fewer would have made it 9%. There is no signal in this number. Treating it as a winner is a regression-to-mean trap. We don't know how this ad performs because we never gave it impressions to perform on.

**2. CBO never voted for this creative (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

CBO distributed only $1.09 of the campaign's day-1 budget here — under 2% of total spend. Meta's auction algorithm saw it, looked at the creative quality / engagement / placement signals, and decided B2C2 ($38 spent) and B1C2 ($11.65 spent) were better bets. When CBO won't feed an ad, the ad isn't getting tested — it's getting avoided.

**3. The hook is openly menopause-targeted, which is exactly what Meta's 2026 scanner penalizes (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The ad opens with: "I went from oily skin my whole life to desert dry in one year. Nobody told me this was coming." Combined with the body copy referencing "menopause" / "your hormones" / "your body stopped" — this is the worst-case compliance profile. This is the exact reason we wrote the `compliance-and-readability.md` rules afterward. The $99 CPM on 11 impressions is consistent with an ad Meta is willing to deliver to a tiny inventory pool but won't scale.

**4. Element density is at 4-5 of 10 (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Predates the 8-of-10 element density rule. Missing: multiple secondary characters (only narrator + abstract "doctor friend"), trust ladder is shallow (no multi-rung observable→bigger claim escalation), no skeptic convert frame, no specific inciting incident scene (the "one year" is a summary, not a scene).

**5. The "desert dry" frame is functional and compelling, but buried (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

"Desert dry" is the strongest functional descriptor in the batch — it's specific, sensory, non-clinical, and avoids the symptom-word strike list. But it's wrapped in a hormonal-shift narrative that drowns the language out. The hook itself isn't the problem — the surrounding compliance violations are.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The "desert dry" descriptor itself. It's a clean functional metaphor that passes Meta's filter and lands emotionally. Pull it forward into future Invisible Woman ads.
- The oily-to-dry contrast structure (former state vs current state). It's a real lived experience for the avatar.

### Change
- **Strip the menopause/hormonal frame entirely.** The whiplash itself is a real and ownable story — but it has to be told without naming the cause. "Around 40, my skin just turned" works. "My hormones changed and my skin couldn't hold moisture" does not.
- **Add an external witness for the low point** instead of internal complaint. A friend, a daughter, a husband noticing — anything to externalize the change per `compliance-and-readability.md` Rule 2.
- **Densify to 8-of-10 elements minimum.** Add 3-4 named secondary characters, build a 4+ rung trust ladder, escalate stakes across 3+ beats, build the skeptic convert frame.
- **Insufficient data alone doesn't justify a re-run.** Even with the rewrites above, this hook competes against the proven "Husband Noticed" angle. Lower priority.

### Test Next
- Don't re-run B1C1 directly. The "desert dry" language should be ABSORBED into a future ad as a secondary descriptor inside a stronger primary structure (e.g., used by an external witness saying "your skin looks like the desert").

---

## Decision

Killed correctly. Sample size is too thin to draw creative conclusions, but the real lesson is structural: a compliance-flagged hook combined with starved delivery means we never even got to test the angle. Future B1-style ads must clear compliance pre-write and meet 8-of-10 density before launch.
