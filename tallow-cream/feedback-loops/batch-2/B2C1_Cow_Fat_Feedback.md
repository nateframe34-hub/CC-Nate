## Step 1: Hypothesis

### What the data is telling us

**1. The $65 CPM is the most interesting number in the entire B1/B2 launch (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

While B1C2 ran $166 CPM and B2C2 ran $213 CPM on the same day in the same campaign, B2C1 came in at $65 — half to a third of the others. That delta is real and it tells us the creative itself (image + first-line hook) cleared Meta's scanner more cleanly than the other ads in the batch. The "I have a drawer full of skincare products that didn't work. Then I tried cow fat" hook reads as a personal confession, not a health claim. No menopause, no symptom words, no doctor — just a real person doing a weird thing.

**2. CBO starved it anyway — only $2.71 spend (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

This is the painful contradiction. Lower CPM doesn't mean CBO will feed it. CBO optimizes for the conversion event, not for delivery efficiency. Because B2C2 had higher LPV count early in the day, CBO funneled budget there even though B2C2's CPM was 3.3x worse. The result: the ad with the best delivery economics never got tested at scale.

**3. The "cow fat" framing is a real differentiator (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

The visceral discovery — "I put cow fat on my face" — is unique to this product category. No other skincare ad can use it. It's a built-in pattern interrupt. The 2.38% CTR on 42 impressions is directionally OK for a tiny sample, suggesting the hook does generate curiosity. But 1 click + 1 LPV + 0 ATC = we have no real read on whether it converts.

**4. Element density is at 4-5/10 — predates the density rule (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

Same problem as the rest of B1/B2: missing multiple secondary characters, no real trust ladder, no skeptic convert frame, no progressive transformation timeline with external validation. The hook is good. The structure underneath the hook is thin.

**5. Insufficient data to know if it converts (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

42 impressions / 1 click / 1 LPV is statistical noise. We do not know if this ad converts. Killing it after one day means we never learned. The kill was correct given budget pressure and CBO's clear preference for B2C2 — but we should not write off this angle on this data.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The "cow fat" hook concept. It's a unique-to-category pattern interrupt that no competitor can use. It survives compliance better than most of our hooks.
- The "drawer of failed products" inciting incident. This is research-validated avatar behavior for The Product Graveyard sub-avatar.
- The image creative type that drove the lower CPM (need to revisit which exact creative pulled the $65). Worth replicating the visual style.

### Change
- **Densify to 8-of-10 elements.** Add 3-4 named secondary characters (a friend who recommended it, a daughter making a face, a partner asking what's on the bathroom counter). Build a trust ladder. Add a skeptic convert frame — narrator IS the skeptic ("I made the face you're making right now").
- **Bridge to the mechanism.** Why does cow fat work? "It's the closest thing to the oil my skin makes on its own." Without this bridge, the "cow fat" hook stays at the gross-out level and never converts.
- **Resolve the open loop opened by the hook.** "I tried cow fat" makes the reader want to know WHY it worked. The current copy doesn't pay that off hard enough.

### Test Next
- A densified "Cow Fat" rewrite is a high-priority next test, especially because the lower CPM signal suggests this hook may bypass some of the auction penalty other ads are catching. If the upstream pixel/category problem is real, this hook may be one of the few that delivers efficiently regardless.
- Test pairing the cow fat hook with the B5/B6 trust-ladder structure (Claire-style older-peer authority + 4-week transformation timeline + husband-touch identity-level payoff).

---

## Decision

Killed correctly given the day-1 budget triage, but this is the B1/B2 ad with the most untested upside. The CPM signal here is the only piece of B1/B2 data that suggests creative variables CAN move the auction price. Worth a densified rewrite in a future batch — flagged as a candidate for a B9 or Product Graveyard-focused batch.
