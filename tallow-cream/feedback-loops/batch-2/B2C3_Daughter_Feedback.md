## Step 1: Hypothesis

### Why this ad got 0 clicks on 32 impressions

**1. The hook is a punchline, not a hook (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

"My daughter asked where all my moisturizers went. I told her I only need one now." This is the END of a story compressed into the OPENING. The reader gets the resolution before they get the conflict. There's no curiosity gap, no open loop, no inciting incident, no "what happened?" pulling them forward. They read the line, get the joke, and scroll. That's why 32 people saw it and 0 clicked.

**2. The daughter character does no validation work (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

The whole purpose of the daughter as a secondary character should be EXTERNAL VALIDATION of the change. But here she's just the prop for a quip. She doesn't notice better skin, doesn't ask what happened, doesn't compare. She just notices the absence of bottles. That's a logistics observation, not an emotional one — and the avatar doesn't buy on logistics.

**3. CBO recognized the weakness immediately (HIGH CONFIDENCE)**

$2.50 spend, 32 impressions, 0 engagement. CBO ran a tiny test, got no signal, and pulled back. Same algorithmic behavior as B1C3. When the engagement data is zero, CBO won't fund.

**4. CPM at $78 is actually relatively clean for the campaign (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

Lower than B1C2 ($166), B1C3 ($175), B2C2 ($213). Same range as B2C1 ($65). This suggests the creative + image combination isn't tripping the worst of the auction penalty — but we never got to test it because the hook didn't earn engagement. Lower CPM doesn't matter if no one clicks.

**5. The "minimalism" angle is correct for a sub-segment of the avatar — but underbuilt (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)**

Going from many bottles to one is a real avatar desire (validated by deep research — "I have a drawer I don't open"). The angle isn't wrong. The execution doesn't earn the click because it skips the entire emotional journey of HOW she got from many bottles to one. B5C3 "I Gave Up" tells exactly this story properly — and converted at full price in a later batch.

---

## Step 2: Action — What Changes Going Forward

### Keep
- The minimalism angle. "I went from a drawer of products to one jar" is real avatar desire and worth keeping in the library.
- The daughter character as a secondary character — but reposition her. She should NOTICE the SKIN change, not the bottle absence. "Mom, your skin looks different" is the move.

### Change
- **Open with a scene, not a punchline.** The current first sentence IS the resolution. Move the resolution to the close. Open with the moment OF the failure — staring at 8 bottles, not knowing which to use.
- **Make the daughter validate the SKIN, not the inventory.** External witness to physical change beats external witness to bottle count.
- **Build the journey from many to one.** The reader needs to feel the resignation, the discovery, the test, the realization. The current ad skips all of that.

### Test Next
- B5C3 "I Gave Up" essentially tests the live-version of this concept with a sister character instead of a daughter, and it produced engagement (1 click on 14 impressions on April 11, but more importantly real conversion behavior in the broader B5 set).
- A future variant could specifically use the daughter character — adult daughter visiting from out of town, noticing the change. "What are you using? Whatever it is, can I have some?" That's the daughter angle done as external validation instead of as a punchline.

---

## Decision

Killed correctly. This ad never produced any data because the hook didn't earn the click. The lesson is structural: the FIRST SENTENCE of a native ad must open a loop, not close one. The daughter angle isn't dead — it just needs to be rebuilt as a discovery scene, not a punchline. Flagged for potential rework in a future Product Graveyard batch.
