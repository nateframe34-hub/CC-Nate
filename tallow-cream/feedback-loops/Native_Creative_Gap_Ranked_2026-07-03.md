# Native Creative Differences vs Swipe File, Ranked by Likely CPM Impact

**Date:** 2026-07-03. Follow-up to the hook-immediacy finding: same 8-ad comparison, this time the actual images, not the copy.

## What the swipe file images actually show (re-examined)

- **Ad #2** (BV/orgasm): a couple embracing at a lake, golden hour, both faces fully visible, genuine joyful expression.
- **Ad #3** (hip pain): a woman lying in bed, face visible, visibly grimacing in real pain.
- **Ad #5** (hormone/wild yam): a smiling selfie, face fully visible, genuine warm expression, plus a product-texture-on-arm inset.
- **Ad #1** (BV/menopause): not a photo at all, a vintage-style anatomical illustration.
- **Ad #4** (hip/gluteal tendinopathy): an x-ray image with a bold colored annotation (orange arrow, blue degree marker) layered directly onto it.
- **Ad #6** (knee sleeves): legs on a couch, bandage visible, no face, matches Tallow's own object/body-only approach.
- **Ad #7** (sleep pillow): an empty hotel bedroom, no people at all, already flagged as the visually weakest of the set.

## Ranked differences, most to least likely to move CPM

### 1. Visible human faces showing legible emotion (HIGHEST LIKELY IMPACT, never tested)
3 of 7 usable images (Ad #2, #3, #5) put a real face, showing a real emotion, at the visual center. This is the single most consistently documented lever for organic engagement and predicted-engagement scoring, independent of everything else about the photo. Every native image Tallow has ever run, baseline through B21, is deliberately faceless (hands, counters, trash cans, an arm). This was a real compliance/camouflage choice, not an oversight, but it is also the single biggest untested lever in this whole comparison, bigger than the lighting/crop/color swings already tried in B21. Already flagged once in the predicted-engagement audit as a candidate test; this comparison makes it the top-ranked one, not a minor add-on.

### 2. Bold graphic elements layered directly onto real photos, not just around them (LIKELY, partially tested via B22)
Ad #4 doesn't just show an x-ray, it draws a bright orange arrow and a blue angle measurement directly onto the photo. This is a bolder, more integrated version of what B22's badge/inset-circle template already does around the outside of a photo. Worth testing a native image with a similar direct-annotation treatment (a simple graphic arrow or circle drawn onto an otherwise real, candid-feeling photo) rather than confining bold graphics to the B22 format only.

### 3. Full non-photographic illustration as the primary image (LIKELY, a genuinely new lever)
Ad #1 abandons the photo premise entirely and runs a stylized illustration as the whole ad. Combined with B20C3's whiteboard label-test already showing cheap CPM, this reinforces that illustrated/non-photographic imagery has real cheap-delivery potential independent of the badge-graphic template B22 uses. A vintage botanical/apothecary-style illustration (which would also match the brand's own "Warm Evidence-Forward Apothecary" visual identity already locked for the PDP) is a candidate worth testing as its own native image type, distinct from both the candid-photo and the badge-graphic approaches already tried.

### 4. Faceless object/body photos still appear and still look authentic (CONFIRMS existing direction, not a new lever)
Ad #6 (legs on a couch, no face) is proof that faceless still works when the photo is genuinely gritty and unstaged, consistent with the B21 pivot already made. Ad #7 (empty hotel room) is proof that faceless fails when the photo looks produced/staged instead. This doesn't reveal something new, it re-confirms the existing problem/outcome direction is the right shape for faceless images specifically.

## What "riskier, not health-claim-risky" should mean here

The founder's framing is right: the risk lever available here is visual/identity risk (showing a face, breaking the strict hands-only rule), not compliance risk (no health claims get bolder). Recommended first test: one native cell built around a moment already IN the proven copy that implies a face without needing a posed hero shot, the husband's hand on her cheek at the restaurant table, shot so her face is genuinely visible and reacting, not hidden. This uses copy that's already proven, adds the single biggest untested visual lever from this comparison, and stays inside a real compliance-safe zone (a candid emotional photo of an anonymous stock-cast woman is not a testimonial claim).
