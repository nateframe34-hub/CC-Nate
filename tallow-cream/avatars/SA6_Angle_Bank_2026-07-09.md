# SA6 "The Sun-Lived": Angle Bank (post round-2 research), 2026-07-09

Built from `SA6_SunLived_Primary_Research_2026-07-07.md` (round 1) and the round-2 findings pasted back into chat 2026-07-09. Round 2's real shape: pride register holds but skews male and skews *seasonal tan-line* rather than decades of leathery texture; the third-person "admiring description of someone else's weathered skin" register is genuinely the richest, warmest material found across both rounds; and a new, distinct population showed up in round 1's kid-sports thread that round 2 didn't chase down, the sport parent, who isn't lifestyle-identity sun-exposed at all, just incidentally exposed for years running.

Three angles below. First two are refinements of what's already in `Brand_Mastery.md`. Third is new and is the one Nate specifically asked to develop: the sport parent.

---

## Angle A: Badge of Honor (pride, direct)
**Sub-avatar fit:** farmers, ranchers, landscapers, golfers, sailors, still mid-lifestyle. Male-skewing per round 2 (women's pride language attaches to farm/outdoor identity generally, not to the visible tan-line marker itself, see round 2 section 6).
**Awareness level:** Problem aware, solution unaware to solution aware, this population already knows their skin is weathered and mostly doesn't mind, so the ad isn't introducing a problem, it's introducing a small permission (comfort doesn't require undoing the tan).
**Sophistication response:** identity. The market's heard every "fix your skin" claim; this angle doesn't fix anything, it validates.
**Reason to buy:** she/he earned that outdoor life and isn't asking to look different, just wants the skin comfortable again.
**Research support:** 15 verbatim first-person pride quotes across both rounds ("the tan is free," "proud owner of a terrific farmer's tan," "these are my well-earned candidate tan lines"). Real caveat from round 2: most of this material is about a seasonal tan line, not lifelong texture change, so the hook should stay in tan-line/badge territory rather than reaching for "decades of leather skin," which isn't where the pride language actually lives.
**Hooks:**
- "Forty Summers Outside. Wears It Like A Badge."
- "The Tan Is Free. Everything After That Costs Extra."
- "Still Proud Of The Farmer's Tan. Skin's Asking For A Favor Back."

## Angle B: The Leather-Handed Tribute (third-person, warm, gift-coded)
**Sub-avatar fit:** this flips the buyer. Round 2 found the richest material is NOT self-description, it's someone else describing a father's, grandfather's, or spouse's weathered hands/skin with real warmth ("hands like leather, heart as big as a house," "my grandfather's hands were beautiful to me"). That means the strongest copy here may not be first-person ad-to-self-buyer at all, it may be a gift angle: an adult child or spouse buying it FOR the sun-lived person, prompted by noticing and admiring those hands.
**Awareness level:** solution aware (buyer already loves this person and has likely already tried something), the ad's job is standing out among generic hand-cream gifting, not creating desire from scratch.
**Sophistication response:** mechanism + identity. "This isn't a hand cream, it's tallow, it doesn't wash off with the first sink of dishes" pairs naturally with the emotional register since it's a gift that has to actually survive daily use to be worth giving.
**Reason to buy:** you've noticed those hands your whole life and admired them, this is a small way to say so.
**Research support:** 7 strong third-person quotes, all warm, all unprompted admiration ("my grandfather's hands were beautiful to me," "my father, hands like leather, heart as big as a house").
**Hooks:**
- "His Hands Have Looked Like That My Whole Life. I Finally Got Him Something For Them."
- "Hands Like Leather. Heart Like A House. He'd Never Buy This For Himself."
- "You Notice Whose Hands Did The Work. This Is For Those Hands."

## Angle C: The Bleacher Parent (new, native long-form, problem-unaware) - the one to prioritize
**Sub-avatar fit:** not a farmer, not lifestyle-identity outdoor. A parent (any gender, though tournament/travel-sport moms are the sharpest version) who's spent years at outdoor practices, games, and tournaments for a kid's sport (soccer, baseball, softball, track, swim, golf, lacrosse) and has accumulated real sun exposure as a side effect of showing up, not as a lifestyle choice. This is genuinely a different population than SA6's other two angles, no identity pride attached to it at all, if anything there's mild surprise/self-neglect ("I put sunscreen on my kid every single time and never once on myself").
**Explicit target clarification: the PARENT is the buyer and the subject, not the kid.** The kid's sport is the setting/cause of the sun exposure, the product is being sold for the parent's own skin. Parents aren't buying skincare treatments for their kids, they're the ones sitting in the sun for years and neglecting themselves while doing it. Every headline, hook, and image prompt for this angle keeps the parent as the "you"/"her" and "her skin" throughout, the kid never gets positioned as needing or receiving the product.
**Why this fits native format specifically:** this population is likely problem-UNAWARE, most haven't connected "ten years of bleachers" to their own skin change, they've just been busy being a parent. A punchy direct-response image ad has nothing to hook into yet, there's no felt problem to interrupt. This needs the Origins 5-phase native/camouflage structure: open in a relatable, non-branded moment (the bleachers, the cooler, the folding chair, sunscreen for everyone but her), let the reader recognize themselves, THEN surface the realization (her hands/arms look different than she remembers, always the last one out of the car because she's packing everyone else's gear and sunscreen), THEN introduce the product as the natural next beat, not a hard pivot. This is a full-funnel awareness journey inside a single ad, unaware to problem-aware to solution-aware, which is exactly what long-form native does well and a Pubity-style image ad can't.
**Awareness level:** unaware to problem-aware (starts unaware, the ad's job IS the awareness move).
**Sophistication response:** information first (the realization itself is the hook), mechanism second (once she's problem-aware, tallow-vs-lotion becomes the differentiator), identity last (the "of course I forgot me, I'm the mom" self-recognition, which is register-neutral warmth, not pride, not regret).
**Reason to buy:** not fixing sun damage, not a pride marker either, it's the much simpler, universally relatable beat of a parent who takes care of everyone else first and is allowed five minutes to take care of her own hands.
**Research support:** round 1 flagged this population from a single hook idea ("bleacher-seasons") without primary research behind it; round 2 didn't chase it down directly (it wasn't in the 7 search sections), so treat the emotional read here as directionally sound (matches the well-established "mom puts herself last" register that's already proven to convert in the account's validated Invisible Woman avatar) but not yet backed by verbatim sport-parent-specific quotes. **Before writing full ad copy, worth a targeted round-3 search** (r/Parenting, r/beyondthebump, travel-sports parent Facebook groups via Google site-search, r/xxfitness for sideline-mom threads) specifically for language like "sunscreen for everyone but me," "forgot my own hands again," "ten years of Saturday games." Flagging this honestly rather than writing the ad on an assumption.
**Draft hook (native open, not a direct ad headline):** "I have sunscreen for four kids in my bag at all times. I have never once remembered to use it on myself." → builds to the realization beat → product as small permission, not a fix.
**Format recommendation:** long-form native ad via the native-ad-writer skill (5-phase Origins structure), NOT a Pubity-style image ad like B25, this angle's whole value is the awareness-building narrative arc that a static image can't carry.

---

## Recommendation
Angle C (Bleacher Parent) is the one worth the most effort, it's the actual underserved gap Nate flagged, since it's a population the account has never targeted and it explains why prior native/publisher CPMs may have struggled: SA6's other angles (A, B) are talking to people who already know they have "sun-lived" skin, which is a narrower audience than the sheer number of sports parents in the country. But it needs either a fast round-3 language check or an honest "written from a well-established adjacent pattern, treat as a hypothesis to test" flag in the ad brief, since round 2 didn't validate it directly. Nate's call on whether to test-write it now or run one more quick research pass first.
