# B17C1 Isolation Test: 3 Angle Briefs (SA2, SA3, SA4 run standalone)

**Date:** 2026-07-14
**Why this exists:** `B17C1_Deep_Analysis_Why_It_Works.md` established that B17C1 (the account's best converter) is actually 3 sub-avatars stacked in one ad, SA2 The Product Graveyard as the lead hook, SA4 The Tight-by-Noon as the mid-beat, SA3 The Unnoticed as the close, never separated. This has never been isolated. These 3 briefs are the plan to run each desire standalone, as both hook AND close, to find out which one is actually load-bearing.

**Founder hypothesis, worth testing directly:** stacking 3 desires into one ad may be part of why B17C1's CPM is so expensive ($160-400+, see `B17C1_CPM_Diagnosis.md`). Meta's delivery system prices ads partly on how precisely it can predict who will act on them. A single-desire ad gives the algorithm a narrower, more legible signal to match against (one clear "who this is for"), where a 3-desire ad may be reading as a blurrier, harder-to-classify signal, forcing broader and more expensive delivery to find someone who responds to at least one of the three threads. This is a new candidate explanation, additive to the CPM diagnosis's existing #1 ranked cause (women 45-65 being expensive inventory generally) and #2 (personal-attribute classification risk from age-specific language) - a mixed-desire ad could be compounding both. **Adding this as a note to the CPM diagnosis file's ranked list is a fast follow after this test reads, not before.**

**Isolation discipline (non-negotiable, per the B17 lesson):** each of these 3 runs in its OWN ad set/campaign from day one. Never share a CBO with a proven converter (B17C1 itself, or with each other) until each has had a real, uncontested read.

**Locked across all 3 (do not vary, per Converter Pattern Synthesis):** Solution-Aware awareness level, Stage 3-4 sophistication response (tallow is a New Mechanism to a Stage-5-jaded buyer, needs the water-vs-oil reveal, not a bare benefit claim), accidental discovery (never sought out), peer-adjacent non-medical authority, water-vs-oil mechanism at 50-60% of body length, day-numbered progressive transformation (Day 1/4/9/14/21 shape), skeptic-convert beat, habit-cessation beat, 1,400-1,600 words, 10-element density, full compliance audit, zero em-dashes, 6th-grade readability. The ONLY thing that changes per batch is which desire is doing the hook and close work.

## Batch X - SA2 The Product Graveyard, standalone

**Sub-avatar:** SA2 The Product Graveyard
**Desire:** one thing that finally works; to stop the cycle of buying and being disappointed.
**Experience:** counted 10-14 products on the counter/in the drawer; can name what each cost; tried "everything" including other naturals.
**Emotion:** defeat wearing a mask of skepticism ("nothing works on me, prove it").
**Awareness:** Solution-Aware.
**Sophistication response:** she's heard "clean," "natural," "hydrating" a hundred times, the ad has to lead with the CONTRAST (fourteen things that failed vs. one that works) not a benefit claim, and let the water-vs-oil mechanism carry the "why this one's different" case.

**What changes vs B17C1:** the fourteen-things/one-jar contrast is currently only ever the OPENING hook, never the closing payoff too. In B17C1, the ad drifts away from Graveyard once the hook lands, spends its middle on Tight-by-Noon, and closes on Unnoticed. This batch keeps Graveyard as the spine start to finish: the close should return to the graveyard/inventory image, not a husband-notice beat. Candidate close direction: the narrator looking at her now near-empty counter, or telling someone else about the switch from fourteen to one, rather than an external-witness noticing moment. (Exact close scene to be drafted at copy stage, not locked yet.)

**Hook (already locked/proven as an opener):** "One jar. Fourteen down."

**What this test answers:** if SA2 standalone converts at or above B17C1's rate, the Graveyard desire alone is sufficient and load-bearing, the other two desires in B17C1 may have been additive noise rather than necessary. If it underperforms B17C1, Graveyard needs the other two threads to close, and the stacking is doing real work.

## Batch Y - SA3 The Unnoticed, standalone

**Sub-avatar:** SA3 The Unnoticed
**Desire:** for the person across the table to look up and SEE them again.
**Experience:** partner stopped commenting years ago; last unprompted compliment is a specific, remembered event.
**Emotion:** longing they don't say out loud; disbelief-then-warmth when it happens.
**Awareness:** Solution-Aware.
**Sophistication response:** this desire is emotional/relational, not mechanism-skeptical, so the Stage-5 filter applies less to the hook itself and more to making sure the mechanism section (required regardless of hook) doesn't read as a generic hydration claim once it arrives.

**Real risk, flagged directly in `Brand_Mastery.md`:** SA3 is documented as "a poor single-frame hook" and "the account's deepest CLOSE, proven" - meaning there's already a standing hypothesis that this desire doesn't work as an opener. This batch is the actual test of that hypothesis, not a given. If it fails as a lead, that's a real, useful, already-half-predicted result, not a surprise.

**What changes vs B17C1:** lead directly with the noticing/being-seen desire instead of the object-discovery scene. Candidate hook direction: open on the specific remembered moment of NOT being noticed (the "last time" she can name), rather than opening on someone else's bathroom counter. The husband-table close from B9C3/B17C1 can be reused near-verbatim here since it's already the proven SA3 close, the variable being tested is whether the SAME desire also works as the opening hook, not just the finale.

**Hook (needs drafting, not locked):** something in the register of "He stopped mid-sentence. 'You look different.'" but reframed to open a story rather than close one, e.g. built around the LAST time before that (a remembered non-noticing moment) as the inciting scene.

**What this test answers:** whether SA3 can carry an entire ad, or whether `Brand_Mastery.md`'s existing "poor hook, strong close" read is correct. If it fails to earn clicks (low CTR, starved fast), that confirms the standing hypothesis and closes this line of testing. If it converts, SA3 is more versatile than currently documented and worth reconsidering as a lead angle generally, not just a close.

## Batch Z - SA4 The Tight-by-Noon, standalone

**Sub-avatar:** SA4 The Tight-by-Noon
**Desire:** skin that just feels normal through a day; stop managing it.
**Experience:** reapplies at their desk/car/purse; foundation cracks by 2pm; skin audibly "pulls" by lunch.
**Emotion:** low-grade frustration, maintenance-fatigue (a "mass technological problem," per `Brand_Mastery.md`'s own framing: the burden is upkeep, not appearance).
**Awareness:** Solution-Aware.
**Sophistication response:** this is the most FUNCTIONAL of the three desires (closest to a mechanism-buyer mindset), so it can lead a little more directly with the water-vs-oil reveal without feeling like a bare claim, the reader's own daily-repetition experience does most of the persuasion work before the mechanism even arrives.

**Validation note, worth knowing before writing:** `Brand_Mastery.md` flags SA4 as having "weakest PUBLIC verbatim evidence of the seven, no recurring clock-time idiom found on the open web," but also notes it already has its OWN independent conversion evidence (B19C2 converted on this alone, not through B9C3/B17C1). That's a genuinely different data point from SA2/SA3, this desire has already proven it can convert on its own in a different ad, this batch is really asking whether it can do so at B17C1's scale/reliability, not whether it can convert at all.

**What changes vs B17C1:** lead directly with the daily-repetition problem (tight by ten, cracked by two, reapply, repeat) instead of the object-discovery scene, and let the habit-cessation beat (stopped carrying backup lotion) double as BOTH the emotional turn and a natural closing image, rather than burying it mid-ad under an unrelated hook.

**Hook (already partially proven, per B19C2 and B17C2's anchor text):** "Apply at 7am. Still working at 2pm." or the daily-grind framing already drafted in B17C2's urgency anchor ("It was the same losing morning, over and over"), repurposed here as an OPENING scene rather than a mid-ad urgency insert.

**What this test answers:** whether the Tight-by-Noon desire, which has already independently converted once (B19C2), can be built into a full B9C3-caliber ad and hold up at B17C1's spend level and consistency, or whether B19C2's single conversion was itself a fluke that needs the other two desires to be reliable.
