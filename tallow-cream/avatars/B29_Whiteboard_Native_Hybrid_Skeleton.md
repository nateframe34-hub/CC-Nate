# B29 Test Skeleton: Whiteboard-Native Hybrid (TOF Discovery Format)

**Date:** 2026-07-17
**Status:** Skeleton/plan only. No ad copy drafted yet.
**Origin:** founder observation, B19C2 (whiteboard mechanism diagram) converted at $11.35 CPA / 4.40x ROAS with CPM in the $19-97 range (per `Ad_Performance_Tracker.csv`), far cheaper than any native cell, but the format's own feedback loop found a single static image can't reliably carry enough persuasion to convert without a story behind it. ## The core structural change: Problem-Aware, not Solution-Aware

Every native converter to date opens on a Solution-Aware narrator: she's already tried fourteen things, already jaded, the ad's job is just reframing why they all failed. That posture assumes the reader is at the same place. A whiteboard-style hook like "Did you know your moisturizer is evaporating throughout the day?" is aimed at someone who has NEVER considered this, she's Problem-Aware at best (she knows her skin gets tight, she doesn't know why). Running a Solution-Aware body under a Problem-Aware hook is a mismatch, the reader hasn't caught up to the story's assumed starting point.

**The fix, per founder's direction:** front-load the mechanism discovery earlier and slower than native currently does (native reveals it at ~50-60% of the body, this format should land it earlier, roughly 25-35%), and narrate it as a live, step-by-step realization rather than something the narrator already knows and is recounting. The founder's example line captures this exactly: "I looked at the ingredients of my moisturizer after a day of having to constantly reapply, and it made sense. It was mostly water, so it was evaporating." That's the reader's own logic being handed to her one step at a time, not a conclusion being announced at her. Every beat should produce an "oh, yeah, that makes sense" reaction, never a "wait, really?" that invites skepticism.

## Skeleton structure

1. **Image:** bold hand-drawn whiteboard-style diagram, same visual family as B19C2 (colored markers, simple line-art, mechanism made visible), NOT a photo, NOT a face, NOT bold/produced-style (that's the disproven B23 direction). The diagram should visually echo the hook line directly, e.g. water droplets rising off a surface with a clock reading a losing time, paired with a hook like "Did you know your moisturizer is evaporating throughout the day?"
2. **Hook (first 3-5 lines, above the fold):** the aggressive curiosity question, native's "must open on a named scene" rule gets a slight adjustment here since whiteboard-style hooks are proven cheap-CPM openers on this exact account, but the SCENE still needs to arrive within the next beat, not be delayed indefinitely, to avoid the "opens on a bare claim" killed-batch pattern.
3. **The walk-through discovery (roughly 15-35% of body):** Julie describing the exact moment she looked at her own ingredients after a day of reapplying, thinking it through in real time, arriving at "it was mostly water, so it was evaporating" as her own deduction, not a fact relayed to her by a peer authority. This is a structural departure from every prior converter (which use a peer-relay chain, Elaine/Winnie, Dana, Priya), worth being explicit about: **this cell tests self-discovery instead of peer-relay discovery.** That's a second real variable riding along with the format change, flagged here so it isn't missed if results are ambiguous, self-discovery vs peer-relay could independently explain any performance difference from B17C1/B28.
4. **Mechanism, compressed and confirmed (35-50%):** once she's arrived at her own hypothesis, a brief confirming beat, this can still use a peer or a simple further-reading moment to validate what she'd already worked out, rather than being the FIRST time the information appears.
5. **Skeptic-convert + product intro (50-60%):** same proven beat, "cow fat, on my face," kept as-is, it's cross-cutting proven material.
6. **Day-numbered transformation (60-85%):** same proven shape, SA4-native content (tight-by-noon resolving, per B28C3's already-written version of this beat if reusable).
7. **Close (85-100%):** SA4-native, absence-of-maintenance payoff, same as B28C3's close direction.

## Success criteria

1. **Primary: CPM vs B19C2's $19-97 range and vs native's $210-408 range.** If this format lands closer to whiteboard's numbers while still converting, the hybrid thesis is confirmed.
2. **Secondary: CPA/ROAS vs B19C2's $11.35/4.40x and vs B17C1's $31.30/1.60x lifetime.**
3. **CTR-to-purchase ratio,** watching for the same curiosity-clicks-that-don't-convert pattern flagged in the B17 urgency test and B23, a bold image with high CTR and zero purchases would mean the image is doing its job but the copy transition isn't landing.
4. **Compliance/tone read specifically on the "oh yeah that makes sense" requirement:** if early comments or any signal suggest readers feel talked-down-to or like the ad is over-explaining, that's a real failure mode for this format specifically since it's more overtly educational than anything the account has run.
