## Research Base

~35 verbatim quotes, self-sorted by the founder into: reasons people bought, objections/reasons they didn't, positives, negatives, and "interesting." Sources appear to be Reddit/forum threads and review comments across **multiple competing tallow brands** (Hearth & Homestead, Kind Tallow, Vibe & Thrive, Amallow, Dalton & Co, Frandora, Magnum Source, plus butcher/food-grade DIY).

**What this research is good for:** it is category-buyer research, not our-customer research. That makes it unusually strong for finding what makes someone buy *a tallow product at all*, and for finding the objections that kill the sale before brand ever enters the picture.

**Where it is thin — read before trusting any of the below:**
- **Behaviors are underrepresented.** People describe experiences and outcomes; they rarely describe recurring habits. Several behavior fields below are inferred from a described action, and are flagged.
- **No price-sensitivity data at our price point.** Price complaints reference $2 butcher tallow and "a bit pricey" Amazon jars. Nobody in this set is reacting to $49.99.
- **No pre-purchase desire language.** Almost every quote is retrospective (post-purchase). The founder has already identified this and is collecting problem-side language tomorrow — that is the correct next step and the single biggest gap here.
- **Age skew is visible** (19, 38, 40s, 45, 46, 55+, 62, 69) but per Origins this is deliberately not used as an avatar axis. Noted only because it confirms the desires span four decades, which is itself an argument against demographic targeting.

## Product & Sophistication Gate

- **Functional product:** makes skin stop feeling tight, dry, raw, or flaky, using a fat close in structure to the skin's own oil, from a label short enough to read in full.
- **Mechanism:** fat-first, zero-water formulation. Water-based creams evaporate; a skin-similar fat absorbs and stays.
- **Sophistication:** the tallow *niche* has moved to **Stage 4 (mechanism competition)** since the last assessment. The evidence is direct — buyers in this research are no longer asking "does tallow work," they are comparing *formulations against each other* and arguing about which additive causes which outcome ("it's the beeswax," "the olive oil in the product you were using," "jojoba doesn't clog"). That is textbook Stage 4 behavior.
- **Required response:** New Information dominant (reframe *why the last tallow product failed*), New Mechanism secondary. **Do not run Stage 3 "here's how tallow works" education as a lead** — this audience already knows. That would be a sophistication mismatch.

### ⚠️ Two product-truth conflicts this research surfaces

These are the most commercially important findings in the document and they are not avatar findings, so they are stated up front.

**1. Our formula contains two of the three most-blamed clogging ingredients.**
Across 5 independent quotes, buyers who broke out attributed it to an *additive*, not to tallow. The named culprits, in order of frequency: **beeswax**, **olive oil**, **coconut oil**, and **essential oils**. Direct quotes:
- *"Jojoba oil doesn't cause clogged pores which I think the olive oil in the product you were using may have been."*
- *"in my experience beeswax causes clogged pores and breakouts. that might be the culprit, and also, essential oils. any sensitivity to it could cause a breakout."*
- *"You didn't really just use tallow, though, beeswax and other ingredients that were in it."*

The reformulated product contains **olive oil and essential oils**. The old formula contained olive oil. This is a live objection that an informed buyer will raise against our exact ingredient list, and we currently have no answer prepared for it. This does not necessarily mean reformulate — it means the objection needs a decided response before we write ads into this audience.

**2. "Whipped" is a purchase driver in this category, and we just removed it.**
Six independent quotes treat whipped texture as the reason the product was tolerable on the face, explicitly contrasted with heavy/thick alternatives: *"it is whipped and light; it does not feel heavy as expected"*, *"I'm using Whipped beef tallow (probably makes it a bit lighter on the skin?)"*, *"it is really thick"* (as a complaint), *"It is not heavy, sticky, or greasy feeling."* Several buyers specifically searched for whipped.

The reformulated product is **denser, not whipped**, and we removed "Whipped" from the product name. Flagging because this was a supplier-driven change whose marketing cost had not been assessed. Worth an explicit founder decision.

## Core Avatar (recommended)

**Desire:** *"I want my skin to stop stinging, peeling, and feeling raw — without giving up the thing that's actually working."*

**Core desire underneath:** Health, with a strong Comfort component.

**Why this one:** it wins on urgency by a wide margin and holds its own on the other two dimensions. The language in this cluster is the most emotionally loaded in the entire document — *"I was desperate,"* *"leather like feel,"* *"nothing worked,"* *"my skin started to withdrawal,"* *"feeling like it might split."* Nobody in the routine-simplification cluster talks like that. It also renews continuously, because these people are not stopping their tretinoin or their acids — the problem regenerates nightly.

Critically, it is **untested by this account**. SA2 (Product Graveyard), SA3 (The Unnoticed), and SA4 (Tight-by-Noon) are all validated but all sit on the same general dryness/aging desire. This is a genuinely different desire with a different urgency profile.

## Sub-Avatars (ranked)

### SA-A — The Actives Casualty ⭐ STRONGEST

- **Desire:** "I want my skin to stop peeling and burning without quitting the treatment that's working."
- **Experience (product-based):** on tretinoin, accutane, high-percentage acids, or a topical steroid. The active is doing its job on the original problem, and has left the skin raw, flaking, and tight as the cost. Several have tried and failed with conventional barrier creams first.
- **Emotion:** desperation. *Primary emotion underneath: fear* — specifically fear that they must choose between clear skin and comfortable skin, and that there is no third option. One quote reaches genuine distress ("my skin started to withdrawal").
- **Behavior:** layers the tallow *over* the active, at night, deliberately separated in time. Two independent quotes describe engineering the sequencing themselves (*"I do not use tretinoin and tallow at the same time"*, *"I put on my retinol or lactic acid, let it dry down and then put the tallow and honey mixture over it"*). **This is the strongest behavior in the whole document** and it is not inferred — they describe the exact protocol unprompted.
- **Demographic:** none needed. Spans 19 to 55+ in this research alone.
- **Verbatim language:**
  - *"I've never had a product cause my Irritated skin from Tret / High % Acids just completely erase the irritation overnight like Beef tallow."*
  - *"I suffer from dry skin especially from using tretinoin... I no longer have dry, flaky, peeling skin."*
  - *"Tightness, excessive flakiness, leather like feel, bumpy and just terrible. I used laroshe posay healing balm, ketaconazole cream and even diaper rash cream to try and get it under control. Nothing worked."*
  - *"Bit pricey but I was desperate. I love this shit man."*
  - *"a form of slugging so to speak but without the gross petroleum product slime."*
- **Evidence strength:** 6 independent sources. Highest-conviction cluster in the research.

> ⚠️ **Compliance constraint on this sub-avatar.** This is the most claim-dangerous avatar we have ever built. Everything here must stay in comfort/feel language — *stops feeling tight, stops flaking, comfortable again* — and must never touch heal, repair, treat, barrier repair, or any named condition. Do not name tretinoin/accutane as products we complement; let the reader supply that from their own life. The customer's own framing ("the thing you use at night") is safer and reads more native anyway.

#### Angles

**Angle 1: You don't have to choose between the treatment and comfortable skin**
Reason to buy: the flaking and tightness is coming from what the treatment strips out, not from your skin being broken — put the fat back on top afterward and you keep the results without the peeling.
Hooks:
1. "You don't have to stop using it. You have to put something back."
2. "The treatment works. The peeling is the part nobody fixed."
3. "Keep the routine. Lose the flaking."

**Angle 2: It goes on after, not instead**
Reason to buy: this isn't a replacement for what's working — it's the layer that goes over the top once it dries down, so nothing about your existing routine changes.
Hooks:
1. "Goes on after your retinol. Not instead of it."
2. "Let it dry down. Then put this over it."
3. "One extra step. Nothing removed."

**Angle 3: Slugging without the petroleum**
Reason to buy: you already know sealing your skin overnight works — this does the same job with a fat instead of a petroleum product.
Hooks:
1. "Slugging, without the slime."
2. "Same idea as slugging. Four ingredients instead of petroleum."
3. "Seal it overnight. Skip the Vaseline."

### SA-B — The Next-Morning Checker ⭐ SECOND STRONGEST

- **Desire:** "I want to know whether this is working by tomorrow, not in three months."
- **Experience:** has burned months on products that were supposed to take 6-12 weeks and then didn't work. One describes *"the same routine for three years with diminishing returns."*
- **Emotion:** relieved surprise — the dominant emotional note across the entire research file. *Primary emotion underneath: surprise*, specifically at the speed. The all-caps and profanity in these quotes is the tell.
- **Behavior:** checks and judges in the mirror the next morning. Multiple sources independently frame their verdict around waking up. (Partially inferred — the *checking* is described, the *habitual* nature of it is my inference.)
- **Demographic:** none needed.
- **Verbatim language:**
  - *"HOLY EFFING SHIT I have woken up with the smoothest, least puffy skin in like forever I cannot freakin believe how good my skin is?!?"*
  - *"It's now day 3 and I havnt felt my skin like this in months. The stubborn texture and dry patches are gone. Like that."*
  - *"On day one when I first put Tallow on my face, the first effect that I noticed was that it immediately softened my skin."*
  - *"my skin looks & feels wonderful in the morning!"*
  - *"I picked at spot and ruined a patch of my skin. Rubbed some tallow on it and it healed in 6 hours."*
- **Evidence strength:** 8 independent sources — the single most-repeated pattern in the document. Nearly every positive quote anchors its verdict to day 1, overnight, or day 3.

**Strategic note:** speed is the most under-used asset the account has. Every ad we have written asks the reader to imagine a 2-3 week arc (the day-marker structure: Day 1/4/9/14/21). This research says the category's actual emotional payload fires on **night one**, and our day-marker structure may be burying our strongest claim behind a three-week wait. That is a testable structural change, not just a new angle.

#### Angles

**Angle 1: You'll know in the morning**
Reason to buy: you don't have to commit to a 90-day trial to find out — the difference shows up the first time you wake up after using it.
Hooks:
1. "You'll know by morning."
2. "One night. Then decide."
3. "Most products need three months. This one needs one night."

**Angle 2: Stop paying for the 12-week maybe**
Reason to buy: every product you've bought asked for months before it would admit it wasn't working. This one tells you immediately.
Hooks:
1. "Twelve weeks to find out it didn't work. Again."
2. "No 90-day trial. You'll know tomorrow."
3. "The last one took three months to fail."

**Angle 3: The overnight difference is the whole point**
Reason to buy: it absorbs overnight and the change is visible when you wake up, which is exactly when you look at your face anyway.
Hooks:
1. "Put it on at night. Look in the mirror. That's the test."
2. "Soaked in by morning."
3. "The first morning is the one that convinces people."

### SA-C — The Label Counter ⭐ THIRD

- **Desire:** "I want fewer things on my face."
- **Experience:** has accumulated and then deliberately dismantled a multi-step routine. Several describe arriving at a 2-4 ingredient product after trying many.
- **Emotion:** distrust of the category, edging into disgust at the volume of product they were sold. *Primary emotion underneath: disgust* — *"all the traditional products on the market make your skin sooo much worse."*
- **Behavior:** counts ingredients on the label before buying, and treats a short list as the buying criterion rather than a nice-to-have. Well-evidenced: *"the ingredient list was like, maybe 4 or 5 things??"*, *"just tallow and jojoba oil, nothing else"*, *"2 ingredients."*
- **Demographic:** none needed.
- **Verbatim language:**
  - *"I wanted to minimize my routine as much as possible... Many testimonies and reviews have stated that Tallow replaced their entire skincare routine."*
  - *"I completely simplified my skincare routine, I feel like the more you use and all the traditional products on the market make your skin sooo much worse."*
  - *"it kept coming up in threads and the ingredient list was like, maybe 4 or 5 things??"*
  - *"I use a very simple formulation with just tallow and jojoba oil, nothing else. I have tried others that clogged my pores but the simple tallow/jojoba is fantastic."*
- **Evidence strength:** 6 independent sources. Directly supports our 4-ingredient story.

**Caution:** this avatar reads labels, which means this is the avatar most likely to find our olive oil and essential oils and raise conflict #1 above. Do not target this sub-avatar until that objection has a decided answer. Targeting label-readers with a label that contains two of the category's blamed ingredients is an own goal.

#### Angles

**Angle 1: It replaced the whole shelf**
Reason to buy: the products stacked on your counter are each solving a problem the previous one caused — one fat-based jar removes the whole chain.
Hooks:
1. "One jar replaced six."
2. "The shelf was the problem."
3. "Four ingredients. Nothing else on the counter."

**Angle 2: You can read the entire label**
Reason to buy: you can pronounce and identify every single thing in it, which is not true of anything else on your counter.
Hooks:
1. "Four ingredients. You can read all of them."
2. "Read the whole label. It takes four seconds."
3. "Nothing in it you'd have to look up."

**Angle 3: More products made it worse**
Reason to buy: adding products was making your skin worse, not better — the fix was subtraction.
Hooks:
1. "The more I used, the worse it got."
2. "Adding products was the problem."
3. "I fixed my skin by removing things."

### SA-D — The Shine Fighter ⭐ FOURTH (highest New-Information value)

- **Desire:** "I want to stop being shiny."
- **Experience:** has oily or shiny skin and has spent years *avoiding* oils and heavy creams on principle, assuming they would make it worse.
- **Emotion:** frustration, and something close to disbelief when an oil-based product reduced oil. *Primary emotion underneath: surprise*, with an anger component at having avoided the fix.
- **Behavior:** avoids anything oil-based on principle; buys for "oily skin" specifically. (Inferred from stated avoidance, not from a described habit.)
- **Demographic:** none needed.
- **Verbatim language:**
  - *"Another crazy thing is that it actually stopped my oily skin. I've never had a moisturizer that did that before."*
  - *"lifelong oily skin (rare acne,, just annoyingly shiny)... Shiny shows off wrinkles...banish the shine!"*
  - *"It moisturizes while also keeping skin supple and non-oily."*
  - Mechanism, from a licensed esthetician in-thread: *"Our skin over produces oil when our skin is dehydrated, which is why OP's oily skin no longer produced oil."*
- **Evidence strength:** 4 sources for the outcome, plus one professional-source mechanism explanation.

**Why this ranks high despite fewer sources:** it is the only cluster in the research carrying a genuine **New Information** payload — a counterintuitive causal explanation the reader has not heard, that reframes why everything they've tried failed. Per the sophistication gate, New Information is exactly the required response at Stage 4. This is the closest thing in this research to a new mechanism reveal on the scale of the water-evaporates finding.

⚠️ The same esthetician quote *also* warns that dense products clog oily/acne-prone skin. The angle must stay on **shine**, never on acne.

#### Angles

**Angle 1: Your skin is oily because it's dry**
Reason to buy: skin overproduces oil when it's dehydrated — give it the fat it's missing and it stops overcompensating.
Hooks:
1. "Oily skin is thirsty skin."
2. "Your skin makes oil because you keep stripping it."
3. "The reason you're shiny by lunch isn't what you think."

**Angle 2: Stop stripping and it stops overproducing**
Reason to buy: every mattifying product you use strips your skin, which tells it to make more oil — breaking that loop is what actually stops the shine.
Hooks:
1. "Mattifying products are why you're still shiny."
2. "Strip it, and it makes more. Every time."
3. "The oil is a response. Stop causing it."

**Angle 3: Shine is what shows the lines**
Reason to buy: it isn't just the shine that bothers you — shine is what makes every line catch the light.
Hooks:
1. "Shine shows off wrinkles."
2. "It's not the lines. It's the light hitting them."
3. "Kill the shine, lose ten years of lines."

## Testing Priority

**Test SA-A (The Actives Casualty), Angle 1, hook 1 first: "You don't have to stop using it. You have to put something back."**

Rationale: highest urgency in the research, the most distinctive language, a genuinely untested desire for this account, and a behavior (layering over the active at night) so specific that calling it out should produce the "that's exactly me" reaction the whole method is aimed at. It also sidesteps the two product-truth conflicts above — it doesn't depend on whipped texture, and it isn't aimed at label-counters.

**You do not need to test each angle separately.** One concept can carry the strongest hook and layer supporting angles into the body copy. The natural build is SA-A's Angle 1 as the lead, with Angle 2 ("goes on after, not instead") as the mechanism beat and Angle 3 ("slugging without the petroleum") as a comparison beat — that's one ad, not three.

**Second priority: SA-B's speed angle applied as a structural change rather than a new batch.** Test a version of an existing converter where the payoff lands on night one instead of day 21. Cheaper than a new batch and tests the highest-frequency pattern in the research.

**Do not test SA-C or SA-E** until the olive oil / essential oils question has a decided answer.

## Gaps & Next Research

The founder's stated plan for tomorrow — finding people *currently having* these problems and capturing their language and desperation level — is exactly the right next move, and this extraction sharpens what to look for:

1. **Problem-side language for SA-A.** This research has post-purchase relief language. What's missing is how these people describe the peeling *while they're in it* and before they've found anything. That is the hook language.
2. **Desperation calibration.** Two people used the literal word "desperate." Find out whether that's the register of this problem or two outliers — it determines how hard the copy can push.
3. **The additive objection, answered.** Go find whether people blaming olive oil are a real cluster or a few loud voices, and whether anyone defends it. This decides SA-C and SA-E, and possibly the formula.
4. **Whipped vs dense.** Find out whether "whipped" is a genuine buying criterion or just descriptive language people echo from listings. This has a direct product decision attached.
5. **Behavior data generally.** The weakest category across every sub-avatar here. Look specifically for recurring habits — what they do every night, what they check, what they carry, what they've stopped doing.
