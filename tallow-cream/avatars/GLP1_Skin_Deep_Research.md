## THE HEADLINE FINDING: the unclaimed middle ground is trade-honoring tone + claim discipline

Every brand currently talking to this audience does one of two things: denies the problem ("it's not the drug, it's just weight loss," the community's own gatekeeping script) or oversells a fix ("reverses Ozempic face," instantly executed as snake oil). The community itself talks about the problem a third way that no brand has adopted: **as a trade they made on purpose and would make again.** The single most common rhetorical move in every thread: acknowledge the loss is real, then "but I'd do it again in a heartbeat" / "wrinkles are a privilege" / "I'll take the wrinkles over the weight." The emotional center is not "fix my flaw," it's **"help me tend a wound I'm proud of."**

This maps almost exactly onto the register this account already validated for SA6 Sun-Lived (earned-it pride, comfort not correction, no repair claims). We already know how to write this voice. That's the strategic unlock: B27 is the Sun-Lived playbook ported to a bigger, hotter, more urgent population.

## The problem-description spine (5 most repeated phrases, ranked)
1. **"Crepe-y / crepey"** - universal, face/neck/body, so standard Gold Bond named a product after it
2. **"Turkey neck"** and neck language generally (waddle, wattle) - the #1 complaint ZONE, rivals all face language combined. (Good news: PDP is Face & Body, neck explicitly supported)
3. **"Gaunt / aged / tired-looking"** - almost always reported as OTHER people's words reflected back (family, husband, mother: "you have ozempic face"), the compliment-that-stings cluster
4. **The community rebuttal script: "it's just weight loss, there's no such thing as Ozempic face"** - the dominant reply to any complaint post; copy must not collide with this
5. **Sunburn-that-won't-heal / burning-dryness** ("sunburnt raisin," "crusty," "felt like I had a sunburn while on Zepbound") - least universal but most vivid and most sensation-forward, and it's a DRYNESS sensation, i.e. the one zone where our product claim is fully honest

## Sensory + timeline + social texture
- **Sensory:** heat/tightness complex, "sunburn" reached for independently by multiple posters; "kneaded like dough," "melty," "rippled," "deflated." Distinct phenomenon to avoid: allodynia (facial nerve pain, "migraine in my cheeks"), medically separate from dryness, do NOT fold into our copy.
- **Timeline:** physically gradual, narrated as a sudden-mirror moment ("caught my reflection in sunlight"), clustering at the TAIL END of loss / near goal weight ("worst right at the end of losing"). B27 arc: gradual in mechanism copy, sudden in the emotional hook.
- **Social:** husband saying "turkey neck" to her face, mother saying "you have ozempic face," partner who "turns off lights and stops talking to me." Camera-avoidance implied constantly, rarely stated.
- **Identity:** overwhelming "100% worth it" verdict; regret when present is about SPEED of loss, not the drug. A vocal subset rejects the term "Ozempic face" itself as fat-shaming, another reason our copy should describe the experience, not use the term as a label.

## Failed-solutions graveyard (summary; full table in the research transcript)
- Water/electrolytes: necessary-not-sufficient, body improved, face didn't
- Collagen powder: "haven't noticed [it] doing anything over the years," sticky habit anyway
- Hyaluronic acid: plumps "just on the surface"
- Tretinoin: most-endorsed active BUT real irritation on GLP-1-thinned skin ("retinol burn," months-long recovery when combined with red light)
- CeraVe/drugstore: the trusted, zero-backlash floor; manages dryness, does nothing for laxity
- Luxury "targeted to GLP-1 users": suspicion triggered by the TARGETING, not the product ("investing in insecurities")
- Procedures (Sculptra/Radiesse/lasers): the only things that "work" on laxity, thousands of dollars, lifetime maintenance trap ("it is life long if you want to maintain the look")
- Gua sha/microcurrent/red light: the "worked for two hours" category, community consensus is temporary
- Community maxims: **"Anything except surgery is either temporary or snake oil"** and "no topical can tighten skin, don't let the industry take your money"
- Off-label oddity worth knowing (not ours to touch, prescription drug): topical estrogen cream applied to face, spreading peer-to-peer

## The claim boundary (the rules B27 lives or dies by)
1. **"Fixes/reverses Ozempic face" = executed.** Bright line, no exceptions.
2. **"Firming/tightening/collagen-boosting" = skeptical tolerance ONLY if cheap + not GLP-1-targeted** (the Gold Bond exception). At our price with targeting, this lane is closed.
3. **"Hydration/barrier" = fully accepted, never challenged, dermatologist-endorsed in-thread.** Our lane, exclusively.
4. **The targeting itself is a trigger:** even legitimate brands (SkinCeuticals) drew suspicion purely for being "targeted to GLP-1 users." Implication: B27 must not be packaged as "for GLP-1 skin." The native ad tells a first-person story someone in this population recognizes; the product stays a general 4-ingredient balm that happens to be exactly what the story needs. The Andromeda logic applies to claims, not just creative: target with the story, never with the label.
5. Ad-shaped comments get silently frozen out (zero engagement) even without callouts. Native discipline matters more here than anywhere we've run.

## Who's speaking (sub-avatar texture)
- **Midlife women, 35-58, concentrated 45-55**, self-tagged flairs (45F/57F/63F) dominate face/skin threads. Many explicitly name perimenopause as a compounding double-hit (hormonal collagen loss + weight loss), same demographic as our entire existing customer base.
- Complaint onset: wide range (15 lbs to 100+ lbs lost), truest trigger is **proximity to goal weight**; typically 5-14 months on the drug.
- **On-drug (active loss) population is much larger and louder** than graduates (150-350 comment threads vs 40-90), and speaks in present-tense crisis ("what do I do about this right now") vs the graduates' calm retrospect. B27 speaks to the on-drug maintenance audience.
- Men: rare, joking register. New moms: not a distinct population this round.

## B27 implications (decision list)
1. **Register:** trade-honoring, never corrective. Acknowledge the win first, the skin as its cost second, the product as maintenance/tenderness, not repair. "Your body earned this. Your face didn't ask for it" survives as the wedge, reframed as honoring the trade.
2. **Zone:** neck + face lead (turkey neck is the #1 zone and our PDP covers it).
3. **Claims:** hydration/comfort/barrier for drug-dried skin, full stop. The ad can say out loud that nothing in a jar tightens skin, that sentence IS the trust-builder in this community.
4. **Structure:** sudden-mirror hook, gradual mechanism, failed-solutions beat drawn from the graveyard above (water helped the body not the face, the $10 crepe cream coin-flip, the two-hour gua sha effect), close on the trade-honoring register.
