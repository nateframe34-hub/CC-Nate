# Tallow Ad Creative Principles — READ THIS BEFORE WRITING ANY AD

> **MANDATORY:** Every session that involves writing, briefing, or reviewing a tallow cream ad MUST read this file first. These principles supersede general copywriting advice and are derived from analysis of converting vs non-converting ads on this account.

---

## PRINCIPLE 1: Targeting happens at the CREATIVE level, not the audience level

Post-Andromeda (Meta's 2024 ad delivery system overhaul), interest targeting barely matters. Meta reads the AD ITSELF — the text, the image, the engagement patterns — and decides who to show it to. The audience setting is broad. The CREATIVE is the targeting.

**What this means in practice:**
- Stop writing ads for "women 45-60 with skin changes." That audience is millions of people. The auction is brutal.
- Start writing ads for **specific shared experiences within that group** — women who remember their mother's blue Pond's lid, women who reapply foundation in the work bathroom at 2pm, women who stopped being in family photos.
- The more specific the creative, the more precise Meta's lookalike targeting becomes automatically.
- The creative IS the audience filter. If only the right women feel "that's me" recognition, only the right women will engage. Meta sees who engages and finds more like them.

**Wrong:** "If you're a woman over 40 and your skin has changed, this is for you."
**Right:** "I found my mother's Pond's jar in the medicine cabinet on Saturday. Same blue lid I remembered from childhood. Her skin at 79 is still better than mine at 53."

## PRINCIPLE 2: The IMAGE is the scroll-stop, not the text

On mobile, the image loads first. The reader decides in <1 second whether to stop or scroll past. If the image doesn't trigger instant recognition or curiosity for the SPECIFIC sub-sub-avatar, the body copy never gets read.

**The image prompt deserves equal craft to the copy.**

For each sub-sub-avatar, the image must trigger instant "that's my life" recognition:

| Sub-Sub-Avatar | Image That Triggers Recognition |
|---|---|
| Pond's Daughter | Blue lid in an older medicine cabinet |
| Retinol Burnout | Bathroom counter with 4 expensive serum bottles + 1 jar |
| Foundation Cracker | Compact mirror in a fluorescent work bathroom at 2pm |
| Photo Avoider | Phone showing a family photo with one person cropped/missing |
| Sister Comparison | Two women's hands at a coffee table, one's skin different |

**Image rules:**
- iPhone snapshot quality, NOT professional photography
- POV is first-person (looking down, looking at, looking through)
- Composition is slightly off-center, slightly tilted, taken one-handed
- Lighting is REAL (kitchen overhead, bathroom fluorescent, salon lighting) not flattering or staged
- **UPDATED 2026-07-29, HARDENED WITH TEST DATA:** **Real branding on real products in frame is GOOD and should be left alone.** Founder has tested this directly: **branding on the product has zero measurable impact on CPM**, and a visibly branded object reads as *real* where a blank or de-labelled bottle reads as staged. A blank bottle is a tell. Do not crop, blur, or angle-away third-party branding in a photo, and do not write "unbranded," "no logos," "no readable text," or "no brand marks" into an image prompt for real-world props. **Any instruction anywhere in this repo that says otherwise is superseded by this line.** (Note: this is about *real objects in a photo*. It does not license adding graphic overlays, badges, or designed brand furniture to a creative, which is a separate failure.)
- **UPDATED 2026-07-03:** legible product labels/logos are fine. Outside reference ads analyzed in `tallow-cream/feedback-loops/Native_Swipe_Comparison_2026-07-03.md` run literal product shots as native ads and still convert at scale; a readable label does not break camouflage on its own, staged/hero-lit composition is what breaks it (see `feedback-loops/H1B_Bold_Native_Close_Crop` style QC-gate notes). Keep the label from being the visual point of the shot (don't center/hero-light the product like a commercial), but stop treating a legible label as disqualifying.
- **UPDATED 2026-07-03:** faces are no longer banned outright. Every native image run to date has been faceless by convention, not because a face breaks compliance, and `feedback-loops/Native_Creative_Gap_Ranked_2026-07-03.md` ranks visible human faces showing legible emotion as the single biggest untested scroll-stop/CPM lever, based on the same outside reference set. Faceless object/body POV shots remain a valid, proven style (keep using it), but a genuine candid face, or a bolder produced-lifestyle face per the B23C3/C4 test, is now an approved variant to test, not a rule violation. First live tests: B23C1-C4.
- No text overlays unless the ad is specifically a text-overlay format (warmup/engagement campaigns only)
- 4:5 aspect ratio (1080 × 1350) for feed
- The image should look like something the narrator would text her sister, not something a brand would post

## PRINCIPLE 3: Sub-Sub-Avatars beat broad avatars

"Invisible Woman" is too broad. Every converter on this account has been an Invisible Woman defined by a SPECIFIC shared life experience.

**Validated sub-sub-avatars (each has converted at least once):**
- B5C1 Husband Noticed v2 → Invisible Women whose husbands have stopped noticing them (1 purchase, 2.36x ROAS)
- B7C1 She Used Pond's → Invisible Women whose mothers used Pond's (2 purchases on old BM)
- B9C3 One Jar → Invisible Women who go to book clubs / friend gatherings (1 purchase 4.62x old BM, 1 purchase 1.88x new BM)

**The framework for picking the next sub-sub-avatar to write:**
1. How many women share this exact experience? (bigger pool = better Meta delivery)
2. How emotionally loaded is the recognition moment? (stronger = higher CTR)
3. Does it naturally lead to the tallow mechanism? (smoother = better conversion)
4. Is it different enough from existing converters? (no overlap = fresh audience pool)
5. What IMAGE triggers the recognition? (if you can't visualize it in <1 second, it's too vague)

**Candidate sub-sub-avatars to test:**
- The Pond's Daughter (B12C2 covers this)
- The Retinol Burnout — spent $100+ on retinol that used to work, now it burns
- The Foundation Cracker — foundation cracks by noon, daily reapplies
- The Photo Avoider — stopped being in family photos
- The Sister Comparison — sister close in age whose skin didn't change the same way
- The Olay Lifer — used Olay since her 20s, stopped working at 45
- The FaceTime Angler — adjusts camera angle to hide jawline/neck on every video call
- The Primer Dependent — entire morning routine built around hiding texture
- The Dermatologist Dismissed — derm told her "that's normal," left with nothing
- The Wedding Dreader — specific event approaching, skin is the #1 anxiety
- My Daughter Sent It (B12C3 covers this)

## PRINCIPLE 4: The 10-Element Converter Skeleton (LOCKED)

Every ad that has converted on this account contains ALL 10 of these elements. Non-converters are missing at least 3. This is the structural skeleton — DO NOT SKIP ANY.

1. **External noticing as hook** — someone else notices the change first, NOT the narrator looking in the mirror
2. **Skin-specific noticing language** — "your skin looks different/softer," not generic "you look great"
3. **Named peer-adjacent authority** — credibility from character traits or relationship length, NOT credentials. Name the person. Specify their relationship to the narrator.
4. **Mechanism explained BY the discovery character** — the "why it works" comes from a real person, NOT narrator-as-expert. Narrator never lectures.
5. **Progressive day-marker transformation** — Day 1, Day 4-5, Day 8-10, Day 14, Day 21. Specific day numbers. Specific moments at each.
6. **Unexpected habit-cessation beat** — "I didn't notice I stopped..." Functional habit extinction, NOT vanity loss. Examples: stopped checking phone front camera at stoplights, stopped packing toiletry bag for trips, stopped applying primer before foundation.
7. **Close mirrors hook** — structural loop back to the opening scene with an inversion. The external witness from the hook gets echoed by a different external witness at the close.
8. **Identity-level payoff** — closes on what the skin change MEANS relationally ("being seen again," "not having to check"), NEVER on "soft skin."
9. **Solution-aware + accidental discovery** — narrator already knows skin is the problem. Has tried things. Discovery happens accidentally, not because she was looking for the product.
10. **Zero medical authority, zero shame framing** — no dermatologist recommendations. Doctor mentioned only as the dismissing authority. Low points are FUNCTIONAL ("foundation cracked by noon"), NOT EMOTIONAL ("I couldn't leave the house").

## PRINCIPLE 5: Voice Rules

- **Reading level:** 6th grade
- **Sentence length:** Average ~10 words. Maximum 20.
- **Voice:** Active throughout. No passive voice.
- **Pronouns:** "I" and "she" — first person narrator with named secondary characters
- **No second-person preaching:** Don't write "you should" or "imagine if you." The narrator is talking about her own experience.
- **Concrete nouns over abstractions:** "kitchen counter at 8:47pm" not "in the evening at home"
- **Specific numbers over vague:** "twelve years" not "many years," "$140 jar" not "expensive cream"
- **Pattern interrupts every 3-5 paragraphs:** Short standalone lines that break the rhythm and re-engage the reader

## PRINCIPLE 6: Compliance — Banned Phrases (Non-Negotiable)

These phrases trigger Meta restrictions, raise CPMs, or read as ad copy:

**Medical/Hormonal:**
- "menopause," "perimenopause," "hormones," "estrogen," "hormonal"
- "eczema" — use "dry," "tight," "papery," "cracked"

**Anti-aging language:**
- "anti-aging," "wrinkles," "fine lines," "aging skin," "youthful"

**Skincare industry language for the product:**
- DON'T say: "moisturizer," "serum," "skincare routine," "regimen"
- DO say: "jar," "stuff," "balm"

**Influencer/spa language:**
- "self-care," "nourishing," "luxurious," "spa," "your skin deserves"
- "revolutionary," "game-changing," "obsessed," "holy grail"
- "clean beauty," "toxin-free," "all-natural," "chemical-free"

**Medical claims:**
- "cures," "treats," "heals" — use "works," "absorbs," "feels different"

## PRINCIPLE 7: Body Copy Structure (Standard Beats)

~1,500 words. Scene-break with `========` between major beats:

1. **Hook scene (50-100 words)** — external noticing moment, specific scene, named witness
2. **Narrator context (150-200 words)** — name, age, location, occupation, family, brief skin history
3. **Failed products list (150-200 words)** — what she's tried, why each failed, doctor's dismissive response
4. **Discovery scene (200-250 words)** — discovery character introduces the product, specific physical setting
5. **Mechanism explanation (150-200 words)** — discovery character explains WHY (water vs oil)
6. **First use + day markers (250-300 words)** — Day 1, Day 4, Day 9, Day 14, Day 21
7. **Habit-cessation beat (100-150 words)** — the unexpected thing she stopped doing without noticing
8. **Household/social expansion (100-150 words)** — others using it, social proof
9. **Close (200-250 words)** — mirrors hook + identity payoff + product line ($49.99, 60-day guarantee)

## PRINCIPLE 8: Differentiation Requirement

Every new ad MUST have a Differentiation Table comparing it against existing converters across these dimensions:

- External witness (who/where)
- Setting
- Authority's proof
- Habit cessation specifics
- Mechanism deliverer
- Lighting motif
- **Unique element** (the ONE thing this ad does that no other converter does)

If the new ad has NO unique element, it shouldn't be written. We're not making variations — we're making distinct sub-sub-avatar tests.

---

## PRINCIPLE 9: The Image Prompt Format

Every ad's image prompt should:
- Specify the POV (first-person, looking at what)
- Specify the EXACT scene (not "a kitchen" — "a kitchen counter at 8:47pm with a half-empty wine glass and the daughter's college acceptance letter")
- Specify the lighting (real, not flattering)
- Specify what's IN frame and what's BLURRED in background
- End with: "Looks like a photo a woman would text her sister: '[specific phrase]'"
- Aspect ratio: 4:5 (1080 × 1350)

The image should make a member of the sub-sub-avatar feel "that's MY counter" / "that's MY mother's medicine cabinet" / "that's MY Saturday morning" in <1 second.
