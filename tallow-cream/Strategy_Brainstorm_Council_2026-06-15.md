# Strategy Brainstorm Council, 2026-06-15

**Method:** 5 agents (Origins fidelity, market gap, core-desire gap, high-leverage prioritizer, challenger) answering the founder's four questions. Deliberately kept to a short decision log, not a new strategy doc, per the challenger's rule (see bottom).

---

## Q1: Are we actually sticking to Origins?

**Mostly, in substance not just vocabulary.** Genuine application: desire-first avatar (not a demo in desire clothing), mechanism deployed Origins-style (water-vs-oil delivered by a discovery character at ~50-60% depth, tied to a transformation payoff, not a lecture), and disciplined feedback loops with emotion removed (the June 10 analysis is textbook Rule 2, and it is the thing that found the converter).

**The one real drift:** we treat the AD as the funnel, but Origins says the ad is the FILTER and the advertorial is the FUNNEL. Our 1,500-word native ad does heavy Problem/Solution-Aware education, then dumps the reader on a Most-Aware PDP. The LPV-to-ATC seam we keep dying at is structurally an awareness-stage gap, and the advertorial is the tool built for exactly that. We have been iterating the filter to fix a funnel problem. (See the advertorial-sequencing decision below.)

## Q2: Are we targeting a real market gap?

**Real but thin and undefended.** The gap: tallow stripped of ancestral ideology (Primally Pure, Toups) and stripped of eczema/treatment claims (Vintage Tradition), positioned as relief and simplicity for the post-40 woman whose skin changed. The PDP swipe confirms no competitor targets a named woman 45-65 or sells tallow-as-relief. So the position is currently unoccupied.

**But there is no structural moat.** Tallow + honey is a commodity (a $12 Etsy jar undercuts us). The only moat is operational: the narrative-ad craft and the avatar lock, and it is unproven at 9 sales. The biggest danger is the woman not distinguishing us from a cheap jar, which means the differentiation lives entirely in the creative.

**The ownable position:** "The One Jar / the last thing you'll buy." Replacement, not addition. An Etsy jar is *another* jar; we become the jar that ends the jars. An ideology brand or a volume brand cannot adopt this without abandoning their own model. This is already latent in B9C3 ("threw out 8, kept one") and is the cheapest gap-widener available.

## Q3: Other gaps within our core desire?

**Yes, one genuinely fresh and well-fitted: COGNITIVE RELIEF.** "I stopped thinking about my face." It is B9C3's exact proven mechanism with the relief relocated from the bathroom counter (physical not-carrying) into her head (mental not-carrying). Zero structural change to the skeleton, the habit-cessation beat becomes its strongest element ("somewhere in week three I noticed I'd stopped narrating my own face"), and it has no gravitational pull toward the recognition/being-seen lane that earns cheap clicks and zero carts. **This is the next angle test after B17.**

Runner-up: **PREDICTABILITY** ("I stopped getting ambushed, I never knew which skin I'd get"). Fits the v2 urgency anchor (volatility as the daily grind), fully internal payoff.

**The trap (logged so we avoid it):** any payoff that requires a WITNESS is the recognition lane in disguise. "Proving the doubters wrong," "ready in five minutes for the door," "even my dermatologist was surprised" all drift back to being-seen. The tell: if the payoff needs an observer, kill it.

## Q4: High-leverage moves, or is it just testing now?

**Honestly: mostly testing now.** The June 10 data relocated the bottleneck from the page to the ANGLE (B9C3 converts ~17% of LPVs; B5C1 converts 0% on the same page, same offer). So the thing that decides whether there is a business is whether a relief-angle ad sustains positive ROAS at a protected budget, which has literally never been tested.

The only pre-launch moves that outrank testing are the ones that protect the test's legibility (all founder-time, no budget):
1. Pixel verified firing Purchase-with-value (one test purchase). Non-negotiable, open since April.
2. Guarantee-language match: the ad says "money-back," the PDP says "keep-it." Match them so the decision point does not leak. ~20 min.
3. Replace the fabricated/mismatched PDP reviews with real ones from the 9 customers (FTC/platform risk on live paid traffic).
4. Offer held constant across all B17 cells.
5. Julie page kept lightly warm (camouflage infra).

Everything else (the advertorial now, more ads, more offer engineering, re-solving CPM, Klaviyo) is diminishing returns. **Single highest-leverage action: get B17 launch-ready and launch it the moment warmup gates clear.**

---

## The one council DISAGREEMENT, adjudicated: the advertorial

- Origins auditor: BUILD the advertorial, it is the methodology's funnel tool for the awareness seam.
- High-leverage prioritizer: REJECT it now, the June data moved the bottleneck to angle and the advertorial confounds the clean angle test.

**Resolution:** both are right on different timelines. The advertorial is the structurally-correct funnel fix (Origins auditor is right it is missing and it matters), BUT building it now would confound B17's angle isolation and would be built before we know which angle wins (prioritizer is right on timing). So it is NOT "someday Test E" and NOT "build it now." It is **the first thing built once B17 locks a winning angle**, applied to that winner. Carry the auditor's deeper warning: even the best ad angle may not fully close the LPV-to-ATC seam, because that seam is an awareness-stage problem an advertorial is built for, so do not expect the ad test alone to fix conversion.

## The discipline rule (adopted from the challenger)

**No new strategy doc until a test it depends on has spent.** Three prior councils flagged this brand's productive-procrastination pattern. Q1-Q3 are now spend-data-bound: B17 answers them better than any further analysis. This was the sixth council in a week on a test that has been proofed three times and is still unlaunched. The next round of analysis is the market. This log is the last strategy artifact before B17 spends.
