**Date:** 2026-07-29

---

## ⚠️ First: this is a different KIND of batch, and that's deliberate

B28 and B30 were **isolation tests** — single-angle discipline, floors protecting each cell, the goal being a clean read on one variable. B31 is not that. Per the 7/28 decisions (no forced floors, more creatives), **B31 is a winner-hunt.**

The tradeoff should be stated up front rather than discovered later:

| | Isolation test (B28, B30) | Winner-hunt (B31) |
|---|---|---|
| Floors | Yes, protecting each cell | **None** |
| Cells | 3 | **6** |
| Budget behavior | Forced even-ish spread | Meta concentrates on early signal |
| What you get | A clean read on every cell | A winner, fast — or six cheap deaths |
| What you give up | Speed | **Clean attribution on the losers** |

**The thing to be clear-eyed about:** with no floors and six cells at $48/day, Meta will pick one or two within days and starve the rest. That is the intended behavior, not a malfunction — it's the whole point of removing floors. But it means **most B31 cells will die without a readable verdict**, and we must not then write feedback loops claiming "SA11 doesn't work" when SA11's cell got $6 total. B30's own feedback loop already warns about exactly this failure of attribution.

If a clean read on a specific angle matters more than finding a winner, that's a different batch and it needs floors. Founder's call — but not both at once.

## The six cells

All native long-form primary text, direct to PDP, no advertorial. All on the reformulated product (60g, four ingredients, no "whipped," raw honey not manuka).

### B31C1 — SA9 × Camouflage Ad #3 ⭐ HIGHEST PRIORITY
**The strongest format-to-avatar match in the whole document.** The swipe file's dismissed-then-vindicated structure and our own buyer research independently produced the same ad.

- **Sub-avatar:** SA9 The Actives Casualty
- **Angle:** You don't have to choose between the treatment and comfortable skin
- **Hook:** "You don't have to stop using it. You have to put something back."
- **Awareness:** Problem-Aware entry → Most-Aware close
- **Sophistication response:** New Information (why the peeling happens and why moisturizers didn't fix it)
- **Structure:** opens on an authority who dismissed them — *"my dermatologist was very clear: DO NOT PUT ANYTHING ON YOUR SKIN THAT WE DIDN'T DISCUSS"* — then the vindication. Nearly-threw-the-sample-away beat. Closes on the first unsolicited compliment.
- **Must include:** the word **"sandwiching"** (their term); the three named zones (**nose creases, lip corners, eye corners**); damp-skin application; an explicit "I didn't stop using it" line so nobody reads this as an alternative to the treatment.
- **Image:** ordinary candid, single ambiguous object on a quiet surface (the account's only validated cheap-CTR visual trope — the jar on a bathroom counter next to an unbranded prescription tube, tube unreadable).

### B31C2 — SA9 × "Goes on after, not instead"
Second door into the same avatar. Single-angle depth, per the discipline that produced B29.

- **Angle:** It's the layer that goes over the top — nothing about the existing routine changes
- **Hook:** "Goes on after. Not instead."
- **Structure:** peer-relay. A friend who's been on the same active longer explains the sequencing.
- **Must include:** the "let it dry down first" instruction (verbatim-sourced), and the trade-pride register — *she is not being rescued from the treatment, she's surviving it.*
- **Image:** object-discovery trope, different scene from C1.

### B31C3 — SA4, hook rebuilt from the 7/29 null result
- **Sub-avatar:** SA4 The Tight-by-Noon
- **Angle:** Still tight even after moisturizing (REBUILT — supersedes the clock framing as lead)
- **Hook:** "Still tight. Even after moisturizer."
- **Why this hook:** it states the problem *and* sets up the mechanism reveal in one line. "Why isn't it working?" → "because it's mostly water." The 2pm version never had that built in.
- **Must include:** the verbatim *"like plastic over a flame"* sensation; the sixth-moisturizer targeting (she has already failed with the cheap ones — name the category, not the brands); the water-first label check.
- ⚠️ **Do NOT use the "stop carrying backup" angle.** Zero external evidence across two research rounds. It's our invention, same as the clock language.
- **Image:** object-discovery trope.

### B31C4 — B17C1 re-cut to a night-one payoff ⭐ HIGHEST ODDS
**Cheapest high-value test on the account.** Not a new ad — a variant of the proven converter.

- **What changes:** the Day 1/4/9/14/21 marker structure compresses so the emotional payoff lands on **the first morning** instead of day 21.
- **Why:** 8 independent sources anchor their verdict to night one or day three. Every ad we've written asks the reader to imagine a three-week arc. We may be burying our strongest claim behind a three-week wait.
- **What stays identical:** everything else. Same avatar, same characters, same mechanism, same close. This is a single-variable test riding inside a winner-hunt, and it's the one cell that *will* produce a clean read because it has B17C1's own history as the control.
- **Hook:** "You'll know by morning."

### B31C5 — BOF: Classy Offer ⭐ FILLS A TOTAL GAP
**We have never run a BOF ad. Not one.** Every ad this account has produced is TOF or MOF, despite having a free gift, a 60-day guarantee, and free shipping sitting unused.

- **Format:** Classy Offer (swipe file) — elevated, non-salesy offer communication. Closest BOF format to our brand voice.
- **Angle:** Trying it isn't really a decision (60-day guarantee + free gift)
- **Hook direction:** "Sixty days to decide. The lip mask is yours either way."
- **Copy length:** SHORT. This is the one cell that isn't long-form — a BOF creative closes someone already warmed by the others, and length would be friction.
- **Why it works without a prelander:** it isn't teaching anything. No persuasion gap to bridge.
- **Note:** per the 7/28 decision, this rides in the same CBO. Meta finds the already-warm viewers; we do not build an audience for it.

### B31C6 — SA11 × the oily-skin paradox
- **Sub-avatar:** SA11 The Shine Fighter
- **Angle:** Your skin is oily because it's dry
- **Hook:** "Oily skin is thirsty skin."
- **Sophistication response:** New Information — the only genuine one we have found since fat-vs-water
- **Structure:** peer-relay, mechanism front-loaded (~30%). The reveal is counterintuitive enough to carry the whole ad.
- 🚨 **Stay on SHINE. Never acne.** The same esthetician who supplied the mechanism also warns dense products clog acne-prone skin. That boundary is what makes this cell safe to run.
- **Image:** object-discovery trope. ❌ Not a whiteboard diagram — the format library pairs SA11 with whiteboard, but whiteboard is a TOF static needing a prelander, and we don't have a working one.

## Structure & budget

- **One CBO**, all six cells, **no minimum-spend floors** (7/28 decision — forcing spend has never once produced a sale here).
- **Budget:** hold at $48/day. Do not raise it to "support" six cells — that's floor-thinking in a different costume. The point is that Meta concentrates.
- **One ad set per creative**, identical audiences, broad.
- **B17C1 and B28C1 keep running** in their existing structure. C4 is a *variant* of B17C1, not a replacement — if C4 wins, retire the original then.

### On the CPC problem

Current CPC is $4.46-4.75 against a sub-$2 target, driven by $259-295 CPMs. **More creatives will help partially, not completely.** More creative variety gives Meta more matching options, which can lower delivery cost. But our CPM is also carrying an expensive-audience premium and a conversion-objective premium that creative volume does not touch.

**Judge B31 on whether ANY single cell gets CPC under ~$2.50 — not on whether the batch average improves.** The average is the wrong metric for a winner-hunt.

## Kill rules (unchanged, Regime A)

Every B31 cell is unproven → **flat $40 spend with no purchase = kill.** No exceptions, no "it's still learning."

Two guardrails specific to this batch:

1. **Do not read CTR under 50 impressions or CPM under 100 impressions** (`Killed_Batch_Failure_Analysis.md` Pattern A). With no floors, several cells will never clear those thresholds. That means "no verdict," not "it failed."
2. **A starved cell is not a tested cell.** If a cell dies at $6 of spend, it goes back in the queue — it does not get a feedback loop concluding its angle doesn't work.

**Breakeven CPA is ~$30.16.** Kill line $40.

## Compliance

SA9 (C1, C2) is the most claim-dangerous avatar in the document.

- Comfort and feel language only. **Never** heal, repair, restore, barrier repair, treat, or any named condition.
- **Do not name the prescription products.** Let the reader supply that from their own life — *"the thing I use at night"* is safer and reads more native than anything we'd write.
- No menopause, no anti-aging, no medical authority, no stated ages, no em-dashes.
- C6 stays on shine, never acne.
- All cells: reformulated product facts only.

## What success looks like

1. **Primary:** any cell banks a purchase at ≤$30 CPA.
2. **Secondary:** any cell gets CPC under ~$2.50 — the first real movement on the account's actual current problem.
3. **Structural:** C4 answers the night-one question, which applies to every ad we write from here regardless of what else happens.
4. **Gap-closing:** C5 tells us whether BOF creative works at all on this account. Even a clean failure is worth knowing after never having tried.

## Open question for founder

**Six cells at $48/day means most cells die unread.** That's the intended mechanic of a winner-hunt, but it's worth confirming you want it, because the alternative is a real option: **run four cells instead of six** (C1, C3, C4, C5 — dropping the second SA9 door and SA11), which gives each cell enough room to actually produce a verdict while still being double the creative variety of B28/B30.

**My recommendation: six.** Finding a winner matters more right now than clean attribution on cells we can re-run later, and the two cells I'd cut are the two I'd most want a real read on eventually — which is an argument for running them when they can get one, not for dropping them permanently.
