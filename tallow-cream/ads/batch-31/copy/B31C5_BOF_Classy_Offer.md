# B31C5 — "Sixty Days To Decide." (BOF, Classy Offer)

**Format:** Classy Offer (Origins swipe file) — offer communication delivered in an elevated, non-salesy register.
**Funnel role:** BOF. **This is the first BOF ad this account has ever run.**
**Sub-avatar:** none, deliberately. A BOF creative closes someone the other cells already warmed up; narrowing it to one avatar throws away most of that warmed audience.
**Awareness level:** Most-Aware entry. She knows the product, she's read something of ours, she hasn't bought.
**Sophistication response:** none required — the persuasion already happened elsewhere. This ad's only job is removing the last reason not to.
**Length:** SHORT, on purpose. Length is friction at this stage.
**No prelander** — nothing is being taught, so there's no persuasion gap to bridge.

> **Why this exists:** every ad this account has produced is TOF or MOF, despite having a free gift, a 60-day guarantee, and free shipping sitting completely unused in the offer. Even a clean failure here is worth knowing after never having tried.

> **Why the swipe file's "Classy Offer" and not "Get X For Free":** the teardown's note is that it "allows you to show sales ads but with a higher class and less salesy approach." That's the closest BOF register to this brand's voice, which is dry and understated. A loud four-free-gifts stack would break voice, and voice is most of what this brand has.

## Primary Text

Most people who buy this have already tried five or six other things.

So here's how it works, in case that's you.

One jar. Four ingredients: tallow, raw honey, olive oil, essential oils. That's the whole label.

$49.99, and shipping is free.

You get sixty days. Not sixty days to return an unopened jar. Sixty days to actually use it and decide. If you don't want it, you email us and we send the money back. You don't have to post anything back to us.

The overnight lip mask comes with it. That's yours either way, including if you ask for a refund.

We'd rather you use it and tell us it didn't work than not try it because you've been burned before.

Link's below.

## Why this is written the way it is

**Opens by naming who it's for, not what it is.** *"Most people who buy this have already tried five or six other things."* One line, and it does three jobs: it self-selects the exact person the other cells have been warming, it reframes the price against money already spent, and it signals we know she's sceptical before she has to say so.

**The guarantee is written as a mechanic, not a badge.** "60-day money-back guarantee" is a phrase people's eyes slide off — it's on every DTC page in existence. Spelling out the actual operation (*"Not sixty days to return an unopened jar. Sixty days to actually use it and decide"*) converts a dead trust-marker into a live one, and the "you don't have to post anything back" line removes the friction people silently assume is there.

**The gift is placed after the guarantee and framed as unconditional.** *"That's yours either way, including if you ask for a refund."* Putting it before the guarantee would make it read as a sweetener designed to close. Putting it after, and explicitly surviving a refund, makes it read as confidence. Same offer, opposite implication.

**The last line is the whole ad.** *"We'd rather you use it and tell us it didn't work than not try it because you've been burned before."* It's a damaging admission at the brand level — we openly allow the possibility that it won't work for her — and it's the only sentence in the ad doing emotional work. Per the Origins swipe file's Classy Offer note, the elevation comes from lowering the sales pressure, not from raising the polish.

**What's deliberately absent:** no urgency, no scarcity, no countdown, no "only X left." This audience has already seen us at least once. Manufactured urgency on a warm viewer reads as a downgrade in brand quality, and this account's own history with urgency testing (B17's urgency isolation) never produced a lift.

## Compliance Audit

- No health claims of any kind. This cell makes zero claims about what the product does to skin, which is the cleanest compliance position of any ad in the batch.
- Product facts verified against the reformulation: four ingredients (tallow, raw honey, olive oil, essential oils), $49.99, free shipping, 60-day guarantee, overnight lip mask as the free gift. No "whipped," no manuka, no calendula.
- ⚠️ **Two things to confirm against the live store before this ships:** (1) the guarantee genuinely requires no physical return, and (2) the free lip mask is genuinely retained on a refund. **If either is not true, cut that line.** The entire ad rests on those two claims being literally accurate, and a BOF ad that overstates its own terms is worse than no BOF ad.
- No em-dashes. No urgency or scarcity language.

## Word count

~140 words. Deliberately the shortest ad this account has run.

## What a result here means

- **If it converts:** we have been leaving the offer unused for the entire life of the account, and BOF creative becomes a permanent slot in every batch.
- **If it gets clicks but no purchases:** the offer isn't the barrier — belief is. That redirects effort back to mechanism and proof, and it's a genuinely useful negative.
- **If it gets no clicks:** short-copy BOF doesn't work on this account's audience without a prelander, which would be consistent with the advertorial history and would close the question cheaply.
