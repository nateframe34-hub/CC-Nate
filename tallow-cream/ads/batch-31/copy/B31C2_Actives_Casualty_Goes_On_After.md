## Body Copy

I was doing all four steps. In the wrong order. For nine months.

Nobody had ever told me the order was a thing. It isn't written on anything. The pharmacist doesn't mention it. The little folded paper in the box doesn't mention it.

I found out from a woman I sat next to at a work thing.

========

I'm 46. I've been on a prescription for my skin since last autumn.

I was careful with it. I read what you're supposed to read. I bought the cream people recommend, and when that didn't do much I bought a thicker one, and when that didn't do much either I bought an ointment.

Four steps every night. Wash, prescription, cream, ointment.

And every morning my face felt like it had been through something.

Not raw. Just taut, in a way where I'd catch myself moving my mouth around to test it before I got out of bed. Like checking a bruise.

========

The corners of my nose flaked constantly. I got good at not touching them.

I kept a small tube in my desk drawer at work and another one in my bag, and I'd reapply somewhere around eleven and again after lunch, in the bathroom, quickly, so nobody would clock me doing it.

That's the part nobody talks about. Not the dryness. The managing of it. The low background hum of a thing you have to keep dealing with all day, that you've stopped consciously noticing you're dealing with.

========

In March I got seated next to Priya at one of those all-day training sessions. We'd met maybe twice.

Somewhere in the afternoon she pulled a small amber jar out of her bag and put a bit on the back of her hand and offered me some, the way you'd offer gum.

I said I was on a prescription and probably shouldn't put anything random on my face.

She said, "So am I. Four years."

I looked at her properly then. No flaking. Nothing at the corners of her nose.

I asked what she used and she said the name of it, and then she said the thing that actually mattered, which was: "But it only works if you put it on after. And you have to let the prescription dry down first, properly dry, not thirty seconds."

========

I said I already put things on after.

She said, "On damp skin, straight away?"

I said yes. Because that's what you do, isn't it. That's what everybody tells you. Damp skin, lock it in.

She said, "Not with a prescription. If your face is still damp the prescription travels. It gets where you didn't put it. That's usually why the corners of your nose and your mouth go first, it's pooling in the creases."

I hadn't known that. I want to be honest that I didn't fully believe her either, sitting there.

========

Then she explained the second part, which was the part that actually changed something.

She said the reason the creams weren't holding was that they were mostly water. A prescription like this turns your skin over faster, so there's less of your own oil sitting at the surface holding water in. Put more water on top and it evaporates, because that's the only thing water does.

"You're topping up a bucket with a hole in it," she said. "Four times a night."

She told me to flip my products over and look at the first ingredient.

I did it in my hotel room that night. Cream: water. Second cream: water. Ointment: not water, but it's a petroleum seal, so it sits on top and does nothing except stop what's already there from leaving.

Three products. Not one of them was putting anything back.

========

Priya's is tallow. Four ingredients. She said her order is: wash, let it dry completely, prescription, wait, then the balm.

And on the nights she's not using the prescription, the balm goes on while her face is still damp, because then there's nothing to travel and the damp actually helps it absorb.

I said, "So the same rule reverses depending on the night."

She said, "Yes. That's why nobody explains it. It sounds fiddly. It takes about four extra minutes."

========

I ordered one on the train home. $49.99, free shipping, and there was a 60-day guarantee, which is mostly what got me over the line, because at that point I'd already wasted more than that on the three things that weren't working.

========

Night 1, I did it her way. Waited until my face was completely dry after the prescription. Properly waited, checked the clock, about ten minutes. Then the balm.

Morning 2, nothing dramatic. My face felt normal instead of taut. I remember thinking that was probably in my head.

Day 5, the corners of my nose were still flaking, and I was irritated about it, and I nearly went back to the ointment.

Day 8, the flaking stopped. Not improved. Stopped.

Day 15, I was getting ready in the morning and realized I hadn't done the mouth-moving thing when I woke up. I'd stopped checking.

Day 24, I found the tube in my desk drawer while looking for a stapler. It had been there untouched long enough that I couldn't remember the last time I'd used it.

========

Here's the part I didn't expect.

I never changed the prescription. Same tube, same nights, same everything. I didn't reduce it, I didn't skip nights, I didn't do any of the things I'd been quietly considering in February.

The only thing that changed was what happened in the ten minutes after.

========

I told Priya at the next session. She wasn't surprised, which was somehow the most convincing part of the whole thing.

She said, "Everyone's doing the right things. Almost nobody's doing them in the right order. There's no reason you'd know."

========

If you're on one of these, I'd guess you already own two or three things you bought specifically to fix this, and you're still using them out of some combination of hope and sunk cost.

And I'd guess you've got one somewhere that isn't at home. A drawer at work, a bag, a car.

You didn't put it there on purpose. It accumulated. That's what this problem does.

========

I'm not telling you a jar of cow fat fixes a prescription. That sounds like an ad. This isn't an ad. I'm telling you that I did four steps in the wrong sequence for nine months and a woman I'd met twice fixed it in a conversation.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day guarantee, so if it does nothing you send it back.

Link is below if you want it.

Priya is on year four. I'm still on the prescription. The ointment is in a drawer and I've stopped meaning to throw it out.

What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the ten minutes at the end of the night doing something that actually holds.

I thought I was going to have to pick.

Turns out I just had the order wrong.
