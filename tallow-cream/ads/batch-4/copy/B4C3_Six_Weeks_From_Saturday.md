# B4C3 — Six Weeks From Saturday

**Sub-Avatar:** The Invisible Woman
**Angle:** External deadline urgency — a specific calendar event (30-year high school reunion) creates a hard countdown. The 60-day guarantee gets reframed from trust reassurance into a TIME SAFETY NET.
**Character type:** Peer (woman 48, determined, slightly nervous, running the clock)
**Hook format:** Scene opener + direct deadline statement
**Awareness level:** Solution Aware

## Body Copy

In six weeks, I'm walking into a high school gym I haven't been in since 1994.

Three hundred people from my senior class are going to be there. My 30-year reunion. The one I told myself I wasn't going to care about.

I care about it.

========

I didn't know I cared until Tuesday night.

I was in bed, lamp on, scrolling the reunion Facebook group on my phone. Someone had posted a picture from senior prom. There were maybe eight of us in the photo. We were standing in somebody's backyard before the limo came.

I was on the left end. Seventeen years old. Long hair. A blue dress my mom made me.

I wasn't looking at how young I was. I was looking at my face.

My face in that picture was just a face. It wasn't doing anything. I wasn't thinking about it.

I hadn't felt that way about my own face in about two years.

I put my phone down on the nightstand. I lay there in the dark for a while.

========

Marcia called me on Wednesday.

Marcia and I have been friends since the 7th grade. She's going to the reunion too. She called to say she was driving up. She asked if I wanted to come with her and stay at her aunt's the night before.

I said yes. Then I said "Marcia. Does skin stuff feel like a second job now?"

She paused for a second and said "Every single day. I started using something my sister-in-law told me about six months ago. I was going to wait until the reunion to tell you. I didn't want you to think I was being ridiculous."

I said "Tell me now."

========

She told me about a balm. Whipped tallow and honey. Four ingredients. She'd been using it for six months.

I said "Tallow like beef tallow?"

She said "Yes. And I know how it sounds. I made the same face you're making right now. The reason it works is simple. Tallow is almost the same fat skin makes on its own. So when you put it on, it doesn't sit on top like a regular cream. It absorbs in. The body recognizes it."

"Six months?"

"Six months."

"And your skin..."

"Feels like it did when I was 38."

========

I did the math after she hung up.

Six weeks. Forty-two days.

I've spent two years buying creams that didn't work. CeraVe. A $90 night cream. Retinol. A vitamin C serum. A hand cream I started using on my face when I ran out of the others. Six bottles in my drawer right now.

Six bottles. Two years. And I still feel like skincare is harder than it used to be.

Forty-two days is not two years. Forty-two days is barely enough time for skin to renew one full cycle. Skin cells turn over about every 28 days. I'd have one and a half rounds before the reunion.

Not a lot of time. But enough, if I started immediately.

And I couldn't waste another two weeks on something that wasn't going to work.

========

Here is the part that surprised me.

The balm Marcia recommended comes with a 60-day money-back guarantee.

I know that sounds like a normal return policy. It's not, in this case. Let me explain.

If I order it tonight, it'll be here in four days. I'll have 38 days before the reunion. If nothing is changing by day 20, I can send it back. I'd have 18 days to try something else. Or nothing else. Or just walk into the reunion with the face I have.

If it does work, I walk into the reunion feeling different.

I'm not going to lie to you. I did this math at 10pm on Wednesday and I ordered it at 10:04pm.

========

What I want to tell you is the part that wasn't about the reunion.

It was the picture.

Seventeen-year-old me wasn't thinking about her face. That's what I want back. Not the face. Not the hair. Not the blue dress my mom made me. Just the part where I wasn't thinking about my face all the time.

That's the real gap between 17 and 48. It's not the lines. It's the awareness. It's thinking about my face when I didn't use to.

I want to walk into that gym and not be thinking about it.

========

Marcia and I are staying at her aunt's house the night before the reunion. She told me her aunt has a big bathroom with good lighting. She said "We can get ready together like it's 1994."

I told her that was the best thing anybody had said to me all month.

========

I'm telling you this because some of you are about to do the same math.

Maybe it's not a reunion for you. Maybe it's a wedding. Maybe it's a summer vacation. Maybe it's your daughter's graduation. Maybe it's your own 50th birthday. Maybe the weather is about to change. You're going to be in more photos than you've been in all year.

Whatever yours is, it's probably closer than you think.

If you have 42 days, here's the math. Waste two weeks trying one more thing that won't work. Then two more weeks trying the thing after that. You're at the door of your event with no time left.

If you order the balm Marcia told me about tonight, you'll have it by Monday. You'll have 38 days. If it's not working by day 20, you have 18 days to recover. And if it is working, you walk into your thing feeling different.

That's the only math I cared about when I placed the order.

========

Link is below if you want it.

It's six weeks from Saturday for me. I hope your thing is just as close.

## Brief Self-Audit Notes

**Compliance pass:**
- Zero "menopause" / "perimenopause" — used "since 1994," "two years," "at 48"
- Zero "your body" / "your skin" diagnostic claims — mechanism explained in third-person general ("tallow is almost the same fat skin makes on its own") and first-person discovery ("I did this math")
- Zero symptom words — functional descriptions only ("skincare is harder than it used to be," "skin stuff feel like a second job")
- Zero hiding/avoiding behaviors — the inciting incident is a Facebook post, not a mirror avoidance. The prom photo scene describes what 17-year-old her WASN'T doing without describing current shame behaviors ("it wasn't doing anything. I wasn't thinking about it")
- Zero "if you're 45, 50, 55+" close — replaced with event-based conditional ("If you have 42 days") which is about TIME, not personal attributes
- Zero specific health stats with percentages — used absolute counts (six bottles, 42 days, 38 days, 28-day cycle)
- Zero medical authority endorsing the product — Marcia (peer) recommends; sister-in-law (mentioned peer) was her source
- Zero anti-medical framing

**Readability pass:**
- Average sentence length ~9 words
- Longest sentences: ~15 words (at cap, not over)
- Zero strike-list adverbs ("actually," "basically," "literally," "really," "completely," "very")
- Active voice throughout
- Concrete nouns (gym, lamp, duvet, phone, prom, blue dress, limo, nightstand)
- Most paragraphs 1–3 sentences
- Jargon swaps: zero jargon in this ad. "Skin cell cycle" is the most technical phrase and it's immediately framed as "Skin cells turn over about every 28 days."

**Urgency mechanism (B4 test variable #1) — different from B4C1 and B4C2:**
- B4C1 used: compounding damage + 30-day mirror (passive loss, internal deadline)
- B4C2 used: active harm via humectant reversal (the cream is making it worse NOW)
- B4C3 uses: **external calendar deadline** — a specific hard date (30-year reunion in 42 days) creates time pressure from outside the narrator
- The urgency physics: "Forty-two days is barely enough time for skin to renew one full cycle" — time is finite and measurable
- The guarantee gets reframed: it's not trust reassurance, it's a **time safety net** — "If nothing is changing by day 20, I can send it back. I'd have 18 days to try something else"
- The reader is forced to name their own upcoming event and do the math

**Buying mood mechanism (B4 test variable #2) — different from B4C1 and B4C2:**
- B4C1 close: "Decide what you want to see in 30 days" (calm resolve, future-oriented)
- B4C2 close: "Stop tonight" (anger, STOP command, present-oriented)
- B4C3 close: **"Whatever yours is, it's probably closer than you think"** — determination + slight panic, calendar-oriented
- The emotional engine is nostalgia + agency (the prom photo moment) rather than anger or resolve
- Forces the reader into their OWN deadline math by listing possible events (wedding, summer, graduation, birthday, photos)
- Time insurance framing lowers the perceived risk of acting now

**Why the reunion specifically:** A 30-year high school reunion is a near-universal event for the Invisible Woman demographic (women 45-55).
