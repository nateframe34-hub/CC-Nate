# B4C2 — I Wanted To Throw Up

**Sub-Avatar:** The Invisible Woman
**Angle:** Active harm reframe — the cream isn't failing neutral, it's actively making things worse. Humectant reversal as the urgency physics.
**Character type:** Peer (woman 50, normally measured, now angry)
**Hook format:** External dialogue — opens with the hairdresser's confession
**Awareness level:** Solution Aware

## Body Copy

"I went to a seminar last month and I wanted to throw up."

That's what my hairdresser said to me on Tuesday.

Her name is Jen. She's been doing my hair for 12 years. We talk about everything in that chair. Her divorce. My kids. The book her sister wrote. The trip she took to Iceland.

We've never talked about something like this.

========

I had been complaining. Same complaint I always have. My skin doesn't hold anything. My foundation cracks. I've tried six creams in the last year and none of them work.

Jen was rinsing my hair. She paused with her hands above the sink.

"Can I tell you something?" she said. "I went to a continuing-ed seminar last month. There was a cosmetic chemist there. She talked for an hour about what the things in our drugstore moisturizer actually do. I wanted to throw up."

I asked her what she meant.

She said "I'm going to wait until I'm not holding scissors."

========

We sat down at her station and she pulled up the seminar slides on her phone.

I want to tell you what she showed me. But I want to set it up first. Because if I just tell you what the chemist said, you'll dismiss it the way I almost did.

The thing the chemist explained — and Jen kept stopping to make sure I was following — was about a class of ingredients called humectants. That's a fancy word for "things that pull water." Glycerin is one. Hyaluronic acid is another. They're in almost every drugstore moisturizer.

The pitch on the bottle is that humectants pull water into the skin. And they do, when the air around you is humid.

But here's the thing nobody tells you.

When the air is dry, humectants reverse direction. Winter air is dry. Air conditioning is dry. A bedroom with the heat on is dry. Most of the rooms most of us live in are dry.

In dry air, humectants don't pull water from the air into the skin.

They pull water from the skin into the air.

I am not making this up. Jen showed me the slide. The chemist had pulled it from a published study.

Every morning I'd been putting on a cream that was pulling moisture out instead of in. That's what humectants do in dry air. They take more than they give.

========

I sat in that salon chair for a full minute without saying anything.

I'd been doing this to myself.

Twice a day. For two years.

I had been thinking the whole time that the cream wasn't working. I'd been buying new ones because the old ones "failed." I'd been adding more products on top because nothing was holding.

The cream wasn't failing. It was working. It was working at the wrong thing.

Every single application had been a small loss. Not zero. A loss. And I'd been doing it 700 times a year and I had no idea.

========

Jen was watching me in the mirror.

She said "I'm sorry. I should have told you sooner. I figured it out a year ago. I switched. I didn't say anything because I felt weird about it."

I asked her what she switched to.

========

She told me she'd switched to a tallow balm. Whipped tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula. Four ingredients on the back of the jar.

I said "Cow fat?"

She said "Yes. And before you make a face — the reason it works is simple. Tallow is almost the same fat skin makes on its own. It doesn't pull anything out. It absorbs in. The body recognizes it."

I sat there with foil in my hair and looked at her in the mirror. Her face is a year older than the last time I looked at her closely. It looks softer than it did a year ago. I noticed it the second she said it.

She caught me looking.

She said "Yeah. That's why I felt weird telling you."

========

I drove home that afternoon doing the math in my head.

Two years times 365 days times two applications a day. That's 1,460 small losses.

Almost 1,500 times. I'd been pulling water out instead of putting it in. I had no idea.

I want to tell you the part that made me angry. It wasn't the chemist. It wasn't the bottle. It was the idea that I'd done it to myself for so long without knowing.

I want to make sure you don't do that for one more day.

========

I got home. I walked straight to my bathroom. I lined up all six bottles on the counter.

CeraVe. The $90 Sephora night cream. The vitamin C serum. The retinol that burned. The hyaluronic acid serum that everyone on TikTok was using last summer. A hand cream I'd been putting on my face when I ran out of the others.

Six bottles. Twenty-something ingredients each. Almost all of them were water and humectants and chemicals I couldn't pronounce.

I threw them in a plastic bag. I tied the bag. I put the bag in the trash.

Then I sat down at my kitchen table and ordered the balm Jen told me about. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If it doesn't work for me I send it back.

That was Tuesday. It's Friday morning now.

========

Here is what I want you to do. I'm not going to soften this.

Walk to your bathroom right now. Pick up whatever moisturizer is on your counter. Read the first three ingredients on the back.

Look at the first ingredient. If it says "water" or "aqua," that's a humectant cream. Not a moisturizer. And it almost always does say water.

It's a thing that moves water around. In dry indoor air, it moves water out faster than in.

I'm not asking you to believe me. I'm asking you to read the ingredient list. Then ask yourself if you've been doing what I was doing.

Then ask yourself how many more applications you want to do.

Stop tonight. Don't put anything on tomorrow morning until you've decided. Keep doing what isn't working. Or try something different.

========

I called my friend Lauren after I placed the order. She's 48.

She'd switched to tallow eight months ago. I'd never asked her about it. I assumed it was a phase.

I told her what Jen told me at the salon.

She was quiet for a second and said "Oh. Yeah. That's the part nobody knows. I figured it out from a Reddit thread last year. I almost called you about it but I didn't think you'd believe me."

Two of my friends knew. Neither of them told me. Not because they didn't care. Because they thought I'd think they were on a kick.

I want to be the friend that tells you.

========

Link is below if you want it. Same balm Jen showed me. Four ingredients. $49.99. 60-day money-back guarantee. Free shipping. If it doesn't work for you, send it back and you get every dollar back.

But before you click anything — go look at the bottle on your counter. I want you to see it for yourself.

If the first ingredient is water, you have until tonight to decide. Put it on one more time tomorrow morning. Or stop.

I made my decision Tuesday. I'm telling you on Friday. I should have known a year ago. Nobody told me.

I'm telling you now.
