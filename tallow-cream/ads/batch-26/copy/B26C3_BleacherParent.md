## Body Copy

I have sunscreen in my bag at every single game. Spray for my son, stick for my daughter, the roll-on she likes because it doesn't sting her eyes.

I have never once put any of it on myself.

My friend Kara pointed it out at a tournament in June, watching me dig through the bag reapplying everyone but me for the third time that day. "You know you're going to be the last one standing here with sunscreen still in your bag, right?" She wasn't wrong. I laughed because it's true, and because every travel-ball mom I know does the exact same thing.

We joke about it constantly in the group chat. Someone posts a picture from a tournament and it's always the same caption. Tan lines from three states this summer. Nobody's surprised. It's just part of the deal, like the folding chairs and the cooler that never has enough ice.

Here's the part I don't joke about as much. I've been doing this for nine years. Rec league, then travel ball, tournaments most weekends from April through August. Nine years of full sun on my face while I'm busy making sure four kids don't get burned.

I knew. That's the thing. It's not like I never thought about it. I just always figured I'd deal with it eventually, the way you figure you'll get to the garage someday. Eventually turned into nine years.

My husband said something at a tournament last summer that stuck with me more than I let on. "You look wiped in every single photo from these things." I brushed it off in the moment. Later that night I actually looked back at three summers of tournament photos on my phone. He wasn't wrong either.

If I'm honest, I'd made peace with looking a little worn in every sideline photo. Told myself that's just what nine years of tournament weekends does to you, same as the sunburn on the back of my neck that never fully evens out anymore. Not a problem. Just the cost of showing up.

That's the low point, if I'm being straight with you. Not the tan lines. The part where I stopped expecting anything different for myself while still expecting it for my kids.

I'd tried things, on and off. A drugstore moisturizer that lived in my glove box for exactly this reason and did nothing by the second inning. A fancier one my sister gave me that I used maybe four times before it disappeared into a bathroom drawer.

My teammate on the team-mom group chat, Priya, said almost the same thing when I brought it up. "I've got sunscreen for three kids and myself as an afterthought maybe twice a season," she said. "Whatever I put on my own face is gone before the second game starts."

That's when I found out something I didn't know. Regular lotion is mostly water. Water doesn't survive four hours in the sun with you reapplying everyone else's sunscreen and forgetting your own. It was never going to hold, no matter how many times I bought a new bottle hoping this one would be different.

Skin needs actual fat to hold onto moisture through a day like that. Not water. Fat.

That's how I found tallow. Rendered fat and honey, four ingredients, nothing else.

I said the same thing you're probably thinking right now, out loud, in my kitchen, with my son laughing at me from the counter. Cow fat, on my face, after nine years of buying every skincare thing that promised more than it gave. I felt ridiculous ordering it.

But it's the same kind of fat skin is already made of, which is the actual reason it stays through a whole tournament instead of giving up by lunch.

First tournament weekend with it, nothing dramatic, but I noticed I wasn't digging through my bag looking for something to reapply for myself, because I didn't need to.

Second weekend, Kara asked what I was using because my face looked less tired in her photos than it usually does by day two of a weekend tournament.

Third weekend, my husband looked at a picture I sent him from the hotel and said, "You don't look wrecked in this one." I hadn't told him anything had changed.

I didn't set out to fix nine years of tournament sun. I just got tired of packing sunscreen for everyone in my family except the person driving them there. What I got was one less thing on the sideline that I'd made peace with not fixing.

You deserve to be on that list of people you take care of. Not last. Not eventually. Now.

A lot of the moms in my group chat say some version of this once I bring it up. Sunscreen for everyone but me, for years, and always meaning to get around to it.

There's a 60-day guarantee on it, so if a season of tournaments doesn't feel any different, you haven't lost anything finding out.
