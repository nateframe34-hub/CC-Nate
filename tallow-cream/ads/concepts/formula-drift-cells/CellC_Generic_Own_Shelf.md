## Body Copy

I counted what was on my bathroom counter last February.

Eleven things. A cleanser, a toner, a vitamin C serum, a hyaluronic acid, a retinol I had to stop using because it burned, a day cream, a night cream, an eye cream, a face oil, a neck cream, and a brightening serum for dark spots I bought off an Instagram ad eight months ago and had used maybe four times.

My skin was tight by noon every day. Foundation cracked around my mouth by two.

I was spending more than I ever had and getting less than I ever had. I'd been doing some version of this for fifteen years.

========

I didn't think there was anything wrong with the products. I thought there was something wrong with my face.

I'd tried cheaper things and they didn't work. I'd tried more expensive things and they didn't work either. I'd had a derm tell me my skin was "just dry, that's normal at your age" and send me home with nothing new. I went back to drugstore basics twice and both times my skin was still tight by noon.

At 53, I had decided that this was just how my face was going to be from here on.

========

My friend Carol came over for coffee one Saturday in March. We've known each other since our daughters were in the same kindergarten class. Carol worked a department store cosmetics counter for almost eight years in the late eighties and nineties, before she went back to school. She knows more about what actually goes in these products than anyone I know who isn't paid to say so.

I wasn't planning to bring it up. I just mentioned, while we were sitting there, that I'd spent a hundred and twelve dollars on a new face serum two weeks ago and it wasn't doing anything.

She said: "What's the first ingredient?"

I went and got it.

Water.

She said: "What about the day cream?"

Water.

She said: "The toner?"

Water.

She put her coffee down.

She said: "Every single skincare product you own probably has water as the first ingredient. I would bet money on it. And that's why nothing is working."

========

I said: "Water is bad?"

She said: "Water evaporates. That's what water does. You put it on at seven in the morning and it's gone by noon. That's why you're tight by noon. That's not your skin. That's physics."

She told me what she'd seen from the counter. Decades ago, the base in most face creams was oil or fat. Heavy. Dense. Stayed on. Then the whole category moved to water, because water is cheaper to manufacture and lighter to wear. Consumers liked the feel. Nobody told them the feel wasn't the same as the function.

She said: "The serum you just spent a hundred dollars on has the same problem as the two-dollar thing from the drugstore. They're both water. Neither of them can hold."

She said: "Your skin doesn't need water. Your skin makes its own oil. After about forty it makes a lot less of it. What you actually need is something fat-based. Something close to what skin makes. That's what used to be in these creams before the whole category changed."

I sat there looking at my eleven products.

I said: "So all of this is wrong."

She said: "All of it."

========

She told me what she'd switched to about four months ago. A whipped balm. Four ingredients: tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula.

I made a face. Tallow. Beef fat.

She laughed. "I know. Just hear me out. Skin makes a fat. Tallow is close to that fat in structure. Close enough that your skin absorbs it instead of sitting on top. It treats it like its own. It doesn't evaporate."

She said: "You have eleven products. None of them are fat-based. That's the whole problem."

I didn't have anything to say to that.

========

I ordered one that night. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. I told myself that if one more thing didn't work I was done trying.

Day 1 — I pressed it into my cheeks and jaw and forehead. About thirty seconds. It melted into my hands like warm honey instead of sitting in a layer on top.

Day 4 — I woke up and touched my face before I got out of bed. Soft. A different kind of soft than what moisturizer does in the first hour. Underneath soft.

Day 9 — My coworker Diane leaned over at her desk. She said: "Your skin looks good. Are you sleeping better?" I had not changed anything about my sleep.

Day 14 — I put on foundation at seven for a meeting. I checked at two in the work bathroom. It sat flat. It had not cracked around my mouth. That had been happening every day for two years.

Day 21 — Carol came over again. She looked at me at the door. She said: "There it is." That was all she said.

========

Here is the thing I didn't expect.

For two years I had been carrying small lotion packets in my purse. The foil ones from the drugstore. When my skin got tight mid-morning at work I would go to the bathroom and use one. I kept some at my desk too. And in my car.

About a month in, my daughter came over and asked if I had any lotion in my purse.

I looked. Nothing.

I checked my desk Monday. Nothing there either. My car Tuesday morning. Nothing.

I hadn't bought any. I hadn't thought to.

She said: "Mom. You always have those."

I said I'd run out.

I hadn't run out. Somewhere in the last four weeks, my skin had stopped getting tight during the day. I hadn't noticed because there was nothing to notice. The problem had just quietly stopped.

========

Eleven products. All of them water. My skin tight every day anyway.

One jar. Four ingredients. Fat instead of water.

The eleven things are gone. I kept one.

My face is not past help. It was never past help. I was just using the wrong kind of thing for a very long time, and nobody selling the wrong kind of thing has any interest in telling you what it actually is.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. Six weeks. If nothing changes you email them and get every dollar back.

Link is below.

Carol is still on hers. Diane ordered one the week she asked me about my sleep. I've thought about how many years I spent buying water and calling it skincare. I don't love that number.
