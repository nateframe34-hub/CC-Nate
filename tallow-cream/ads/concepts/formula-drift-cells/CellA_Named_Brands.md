## Body Copy

I went back to Pond's last spring.

I'd been using the same ten things on my face for two years. Cleanser, toner, vitamin C serum, retinol I stopped being able to use because it burned, hyaluronic acid, two different moisturizers, eye cream, face oil, neck cream. My skin was tight by noon every day. Foundation cracked around my mouth by two. I was spending more than I ever had and getting less than I ever had.

I remembered my mother's bathroom. One jar. The white glass one with the blue lid. Pond's Cold Cream, same jar her whole adult life. Her skin at 55 was softer than mine at 53.

So I went back to Pond's. Bought a jar at the drugstore. Figured I'd been overthinking it.

========

I used it for three weeks.

My skin was still tight by noon.

I sat at my bathroom mirror on a Tuesday morning and thought: this is just it. My face is past the point of help and I have to accept that.

========

My friend Carol came over for coffee that Saturday. We've known each other since our daughters were in the same kindergarten class. Carol worked the Clinique counter at the Bay for almost eight years in the late eighties and nineties, before she went back to school. She knows more about what goes into creams than anyone I know who isn't paid to say so.

I told her what I'd been trying. The ten products that weren't working. Going back to Pond's. Three weeks of nothing.

She put her mug down.

She said: "Flip the Pond's over. Read me the first ingredient."

I got up and got it from the bathroom.

Water. First ingredient.

She said: "Now flip over one of the serums."

Water.

"The moisturizer."

Water.

She said: "That's what happened. I watched it happen on the counter. Late eighties, early nineties, everything went to water and glycerin. Cheaper to make. Lighter feel. Easier to pump out of a tube. Pond's, Olay, all of them. Your mother's Pond's had mineral oil as the base. The one you just bought has water as the base. It's not the same product. It just has the same name."

========

I said: "But it's still Pond's."

She said: "The label is still Pond's. The base isn't what it was."

She wasn't saying it was dangerous. She wasn't saying they did something wrong. She was just telling me what she'd seen: the category moved. Oil and fat out, water in. And once water is the first ingredient, the whole product changes. Water evaporates. You put it on at seven in the morning and by noon it's gone. That's why nothing was holding. Not because my skin was past help. Because every single thing I was putting on it was gone before lunch.

I sat with that for a minute.

I said: "So what actually works?"

She said: "Something oil-based. Something fat-based. The way they used to be."

========

She told me what she'd been using for about four months. A whipped balm. Four ingredients: tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula. That's it.

I made a face. Tallow. Beef fat. On a face.

She laughed. "I know. I said the same thing. But think about it. Your mom's Pond's worked because it was mineral oil. This is closer to your skin than mineral oil is. Skin makes its own fat. After about forty it makes a lot less of it. This is close enough in structure that your skin absorbs it instead of sitting on top."

She said: "Your mother wasn't doing anything special. She was just using the right kind of thing. So was your grandmother. This is just the right kind of thing with better ingredients."

I didn't have a good answer for that.

========

I ordered it that night. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money back guarantee.

Day 1 — I pressed it into my cheeks the way my mother used to press in her Pond's. Two fingers. Fast. It melted into my palm like warm honey.

Day 4 — I woke up and touched my face before I got out of bed. Soft. Not the kind that disappears in the shower. Underneath soft.

Day 9 — My coworker Diane stopped at my desk. She said: "Your skin looks good. Are you sleeping better?" I hadn't changed how I was sleeping.

Day 14 — I put on foundation at seven for a meeting. Checked at two in the bathroom at work. It sat flat. It hadn't cracked around my mouth. That had been happening every day for two years.

Day 21 — Carol came back for coffee. She looked at me when I opened the door and smiled without saying anything. She didn't have to.

========

Here is the thing I didn't expect.

I'd been buying travel-size lotion packets for years. The foil ones from the drugstore endcap. I kept them in my purse, my desk drawer, my car. Every time my skin got tight I dug one out.

About four weeks in my daughter asked me for one out of my purse. I looked. Nothing.

I checked my desk Monday. Nothing. The car the next morning. Nothing.

I hadn't bought them. I hadn't thought about it. I hadn't felt tight enough to go looking.

She said: "Mom, you always have those."

I said I'd run out.

I hadn't run out. I'd stopped needing them without noticing I'd stopped.

========

I keep the old Pond's jar on the bathroom counter now. I'm not using it. I just keep it there to look at.

It's not a bad product. Carol never said it was a bad product. It just isn't what it used to be. And what it used to be isn't what my skin actually needed anyway. My mother had the right instinct. One jar. Oil-based. Simple. She just happened to pick something that worked because of what it was, not because of the name on the label.

I know what my skin actually needs now. And I know why the thing I went back to didn't give it to me.

That's the part nobody told me. Not Pond's, not Olay, not the woman at the Sephora counter. None of them said: "The category changed. Water is cheaper. You've been putting water on an oil problem for thirty years."

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If it does nothing in six weeks you email them and get every dollar back.

Carol is still on hers. Diane ordered one the week after she asked me about my sleep. My mother would have found this funny. I find it mostly embarrassing. Thirty years and one conversation with the right person would have fixed it.
