## Body Copy

I went back to the simple cream last spring.

My mother had one jar on her bathroom counter her whole adult life. The same cream, the same size, the same drugstore. She didn't think about it. She just used it. Her skin at 55 was softer than mine at 53 with ten products.

So I went back to it. Bought the same one. Put it on every night the way she used to.

Three weeks later my skin was still tight by noon.

========

I sat at my bathroom mirror on a Tuesday morning and decided that was just it. My face had finally gone past the point where anything would help. I was 53 and I needed to accept that.

I had ten products on the counter. A cleanser, a toner, a vitamin C serum, a retinol I had to stop using because it burned, hyaluronic acid, two moisturizers, eye cream, face oil, neck cream. None of them were working. The simple thing from my mother's bathroom wasn't working either. I had run out of options to try.

========

My friend Carol came over for coffee that Saturday. We've known each other since our daughters were in the same kindergarten class. Carol worked a department store cosmetics counter for almost eight years in the late eighties and nineties, before she went back to school. She knows more about what actually goes into creams than anyone I know who isn't paid to say so.

I told her what I'd been trying. The expensive shelf that stopped working. Going back to the simple thing my mother used. Three weeks of nothing.

She put her mug down.

She said: "Go get it. Read me the first ingredient."

I got up and got it.

Water.

She said: "Now flip one of the serums."

Water.

"The day cream."

Water.

She said: "That's what happened. Every cream on your counter has water as the first ingredient. So does the one your mother used. That's not what she was putting on her face in 1978. Back then those creams were oil-based. Fat-based. Somewhere in the eighties and nineties the whole category moved to water. Cheaper to make. Lighter feel. Easier to pump out of a tube. The name on the jar is the same. The base isn't."

========

I said: "So the cream my mother used and the one I bought are different."

She said: "Same name, different formula. You didn't go back to what she was using. You went back to the label."

She wasn't saying anyone did anything wrong. She was just telling me what she'd watched happen from the counter: oil and fat out, water in, because water is cheaper and lighter and consumers stopped knowing what to look for.

And once water is the first ingredient, the whole product changes. Water evaporates. You put it on at seven in the morning and by noon it's gone. That's why nothing was holding. Not because my skin was past help. Because everything I was putting on it was gone before lunch.

I sat with that for a minute.

I said: "So what actually works?"

She said: "Something fat-based. The way they used to be."

========

She told me what she'd been using for about four months. A whipped balm. Four ingredients: tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula.

I made a face. Tallow. Beef fat. On my face.

She laughed. "I made the same face. But listen. Your mother's cream worked because it had an oil base. This is closer to your skin than that was. After about forty your skin makes a lot less of its own oil. The fat in this is close enough in structure that your skin absorbs it instead of sitting on top. It treats it like its own."

She said: "Your mother was doing the right thing without knowing exactly why. This is just the right thing with better ingredients."

I didn't have a good answer for that.

========

I ordered one that night. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

Day 1 — I pressed it into my cheeks the way my mother used to press in hers. Two fingers, both cheeks, forehead, jaw. Thirty seconds. It melted into my palm like warm honey.

Day 4 — I woke up and touched my face before I opened my eyes. Soft underneath. Not the kind that disappears when you wash it.

Day 9 — My coworker Diane stopped at my desk. She said: "Your skin looks really good. Are you sleeping better?" I hadn't changed anything about my sleep.

Day 14 — Foundation at seven, meeting at nine, restroom at two. It sat flat. It hadn't cracked around my mouth. That had been happening every single day for two years.

Day 21 — Carol came over again. She looked at me when I opened the door and smiled and didn't say a word. She knew what she was looking at.

========

Here's the thing I didn't expect.

I had been buying the little travel-size lotion packets for years. The foil ones from the drugstore. I kept them in three places: my purse, my desk drawer, my car. Every time my skin got tight during the day I would dig one out and use it in the bathroom at work.

About a month in, my daughter asked for one out of my purse.

I looked. Nothing.

I checked my desk. Nothing. My car the next morning. Nothing.

I hadn't bought any. I hadn't even thought about it. I hadn't felt tight enough to go looking.

She said: "Mom, you always have those."

I said I'd run out.

I hadn't run out. I just hadn't needed them.

========

I know what happened now. My mother was right, she just wasn't fully right for the reason she thought. The cream she used worked because of what it was made of, not because it was the brand she'd always bought. When that base changed, the thing she was trusting stopped being the thing that had worked. She might not have even noticed.

I noticed because I went back and it didn't work and I finally talked to the right person.

The thing that fixes it isn't on the expensive shelf. It isn't the cream from the drugstore either, not anymore. It's the kind of thing those creams used to be: fat-based, four ingredients, made to absorb instead of sit on top.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If you use it for six weeks and nothing changes you email them and get every dollar back.

Carol is still using hers. Diane ordered one the week after she asked me about my sleep. My mother would have laughed at all of it. I do a little, most mornings.
