## Why this structure fits SA9 exactly

The dismissed-then-vindicated shape needs a moment where a legitimate authority
tells the narrator her problem is not a problem. This community supplies that
moment constantly and unprompted: *"it's normal"*, *"push through"*, *"it settles
around week twelve."*

Critically, **the authority is not wrong and must not be written as a villain.**
It does settle for many people. The tension is that "it's normal" is not the same
as "there is nothing you can do", and that gap is where the ad lives. A villain
with a defensible position creates real narrative tension; a cartoonish one
breaks the ad and, here, would also insult a clinician the reader trusts.

## Body Copy

I asked her what I could put on it and she said don't put anything on it.

Push through. It settles down around week twelve for most people.

I was in week nineteen.

========

I want to be fair to her, because she was not being careless and she was not wrong.

It does settle for a lot of people. That is true. She has presumably watched hundreds of people come back at week fourteen and say it stopped and they are glad they stayed with it.

And she was right that I did not need to pile things on top of it. Most of what I would have piled on would have made it worse.

But "it's normal" and "there is nothing you can do" are not the same sentence, and I walked out of there having heard the second one.

========

Week nineteen looked like this.

The creases either side of my nose. Both corners of my mouth. I had a small split at one of them that reopened every time I ate anything.

I was fine everywhere else. That is what made me feel ridiculous bringing it up at all.

I had tried the tub cream everyone recommends. Three times, actually. Petroleum jelly. An oil. Something expensive my sister brought me from Paris that I resented buying.

Every one of them was gone by two in the morning.

========

Marguerite is 61 and works the front desk at my dentist. She has been on hers for nine years. She has never sold anything to anyone in her life.

I mentioned week nineteen while she was booking me in and she stopped typing.

She said: "Nobody ever tells you what to put on. Only what not to."

I said that was exactly it. That was the whole appointment in one line.

========

She said turn over whatever you are putting on top and read the first ingredient.

I did that night, at the sink, in my dressing gown. Water. Water. Water second on the oil. The Paris one, water.

Water evaporates. That is all it is doing. It is not a bad product. You put it on at eleven and by two those places are bare again, and bare is when they split.

She said the layer on top has to be a fat, because a fat has nothing in it to leave. And the one she uses is close enough to what skin makes on its own that it goes in instead of sitting there in a film.

Then she said the part that mattered most to me.

"It goes on after. Not instead. You do not change one thing you are already doing. You let it dry down and you put this over the top."

I was not being asked to stop. I had spent nineteen weeks braced for someone to tell me to stop.

========

Night 1, it did not sting on the mouth corners. I sat on the edge of the bath for a second because I had genuinely expected it to.

Week 2, I woke up and the creases beside my nose were smooth and I did not believe it, so I checked again the next day and the day after.

Week 4, the split at the corner of my mouth had closed and stayed closed. I ate a sandwich without thinking about it, which I had not done since February.

========

I saw her again in June.

She looked at my face for a moment longer than she needed to and asked if I had eased off it at all.

I said no. Still every night. Same as always.

She said, "Well. It settled, then."

I said yes. It settled.

I did not correct her, and I still do not think she was wrong exactly. I just think there was a sentence missing from that first appointment, and a woman at a dentist's front desk was the one who said it to me.

========

Link is below.

It is more than the tub from the pharmacy. I bought the tub three times, plus the jelly and the oil and the Paris one, and together they cost more than this and not one of them made it to morning.

If you are in week nineteen and you were told to push through: it might settle. It really might.

But nobody told you what to put on top, and that is a different question than the one you asked.
