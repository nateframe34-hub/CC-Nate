## Body Copy

My friend Rosa asked to borrow lotion at the gym and I handed her one of three tubes I had on me. She looked at my bag and said, "Why do you have three?"

I didn't have a real answer. I just always had.

========

Here's what my normal looked like. One tube in my gym bag, one in my car console, one in my desk drawer at work. Not because I planned it that way, I'd just slowly built a whole backup system without ever deciding to. If I felt tight anywhere, I had one within reach.

Three places. For years. I'd stopped thinking of it as excessive, it was just how I existed.

I forgot the gym one once, on a day I actually needed it, stuck in a work dinner right after with my skin pulling every time I smiled. I kept my mouth mostly closed through dessert. Nobody noticed. I noticed the entire time.

========

Rosa asked if I'd ever wondered why I needed backup at all. I said I figured everyone did.

She pulled a receipt out of her bag and flipped it over. "Can I show you something?"

She drew two shapes on the back of it. A little puddle with dotted lines rising off it, and next to it a solid blob that stayed put.

"This is what's in basically every lotion," she said, tapping the puddle. "Water. It evaporates, that's just what water does, whether it's a two dollar bottle or a forty dollar one. You put it on and a few hours later most of it's already left the building. That's why you need backup. Not because your skin's needy. Because the thing you're using leaves on its own."

She tapped the solid blob. "This is fat. Doesn't evaporate the same way, gets absorbed instead, because it's close to the same structure as the oil your skin already makes. So it just stays."

========

I asked if she was making this up. She said check any bottle I owned, water's always first on the label.

I went home and checked all three tubes. Water, first ingredient, every single one.

========

She'd switched about a year earlier. Four ingredients, she said. Tallow doing the actual work.

I said, "Cow fat. Are you serious."

She said, "I said the same thing. Try it two weeks before you decide anything."

I ordered one that night, still a little skeptical honestly, but for $49.99 with free shipping it felt worth the two weeks, especially with a 60-day guarantee sitting there if it didn't pan out.

========

Day 1, before bed, it melted into my palm like warm honey, no fighting it in.

Day 4, one application in the morning, and I caught myself reaching for the gym-bag tube around noon out of pure habit. My skin wasn't asking for it. I put it back.

Day 9, I checked the car console. Tube still capped, untouched.

Day 14, I opened my desk drawer for a pen and found the third tube exactly where I'd left it two weeks earlier.

Day 21, I realized I hadn't refilled or replaced any of the three. Not because I ran out and forgot. Because I'd stopped needing them and hadn't noticed the stopping.

========

Here's the part I didn't expect. I hadn't decided to stop carrying backup. I'd just quietly stopped needing it, and all three tubes sat untouched for weeks before I ever registered that on purpose.

That was the actual thing I wanted. Not softer skin by itself. The not-needing-backup.

========

Rosa asked me at the gym again a few weeks later if I still had all three tubes on me.

I checked my bag. Zero.

She just smiled like she already knew the answer.

========

If you've ever caught yourself doing a mental check before you leave the house, gym bag, car, desk, all accounted for, you already know the specific relief of confirming you're covered. You probably did it again this morning without noticing you did it.

========

I'm not telling you a jar of cow fat rewires your whole routine. That sounds like an ad. This isn't an ad. I'm telling you about a receipt with two shapes drawn on the back of it, and three tubes I stopped needing without ever deciding to.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. There's a 60-day guarantee too, so trying it isn't really a risk either way.

Link is below if you want it.

Rosa's still on it. My gym bag has one less thing in it now, and I didn't even notice when that happened.

What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the day you check your bag and realize you stopped preparing for a problem that isn't there anymore.

I'd stopped believing I'd ever travel light again.

Now I just do.
