## Body Copy

My sister Nora did the math on her phone at dinner one night. "You've reapplied lotion, what, three times today? That's maybe six minutes. Times three sixty five. That's over thirty five hours a year. That's basically a work week."

I told her that was a ridiculous way to think about it. She said maybe, but she wasn't wrong either.

========

Here's what my actual days looked like. Lotion on before I left the house. Reapply before lunch, my skin already pulling at the corners of my mouth. Reapply again mid-afternoon, always around the same time, like clockwork. A small, unpaid shift I never clocked in for on purpose, it just became part of the day.

I never thought of it as work. Nora's math made me think of it as work.

========

She asked if I'd ever actually looked into why it needed doing that often. I said I figured that was just what skin needed.

She grabbed a paper placemat and drew two shapes with the pen from her purse.

"This is water," she said, drawing a wavy line with little arrows pointing up off it. "It evaporates. That's the whole reason you're reapplying three times a day, whatever you're using probably lists water first, and water just leaves. It's not that your skin needs constant maintenance. It's that the thing you're putting on doesn't stay."

She drew a second, flatter shape next to it. "This is fat. Doesn't evaporate the same way. Gets absorbed instead, because it's close enough in structure to the oil skin already makes. It just stays put. No reapplying required, because there's nothing evaporating off in the first place."

========

I asked if that was really it. She said flip anything I owned over, water's always first.

I checked when I got home. Every bottle. Water first, every time.

========

Nora had switched about nine months earlier. Four ingredients, tallow doing the actual work, she said, like she was reading it off the label rather than convincing me of anything.

I said, "So I've been trading thirty five hours a year for a bottle of water with a nice label."

She said, "Basically. Try it two weeks before you decide."

I ordered one that night. Skeptical, if I'm honest, but for $49.99 with free shipping it felt like a fair trade to find out, and there was a 60-day guarantee if the two weeks didn't convince me.

========

Day 1, before bed, it melted into my palm like warm honey, no fighting it in.

Day 4, one application in the morning, and by early afternoon, out of pure habit, I reached for a tube that wasn't even in my bag anymore. My skin wasn't asking for it.

Day 9, I made it through a full workday, no reapplying, no clock-watching for when my skin would start pulling again.

Day 14, I realized at the end of the day I hadn't thought about my skin once, not a single check-in.

Day 21, I did Nora's math again in my head. Zero minutes spent reapplying that week. The shift I never clocked in for on purpose had just ended, and I hadn't noticed the last day I worked it.

========

Here's the part I didn't expect. I hadn't decided to stop. I'd just stopped needing to, and it took me until I did the math again to notice the job itself was gone, not just quieter.

That was the actual thing I wanted. Not softer skin on its own. Getting the thirty five hours back without ever asking for them.

========

Nora brought up her own math again at another dinner a few weeks later, half joking. "You haven't reapplied once tonight."

I hadn't noticed. That was sort of the point.

========

If you did your own version of Nora's math right now, three times a day, six minutes each, you'd land on the same number I did. You're probably due for a reapplication sometime in the next hour or two, whether or not you've clocked it yet.

========

I'm not telling you a jar of cow fat gives you back a work week. That sounds like an ad. This isn't an ad. I'm telling you about the math my sister did on her phone at dinner, and the shift I didn't notice I'd stopped working.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. There's a 60-day guarantee too, so there's not much to lose finding out.

Link is below if you want it.

Nora's still on it. I haven't done the reapplying math again because there's nothing left to add up.

What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the thirty five hours back, spent on literally anything else.

I'd stopped believing that time was mine to get back.

Now it just is.
