## Body Copy

My daughter woke up early last Saturday and found me in the bathroom.

Eleven bottles on the counter. Lined up in order. I was on step six.

She stood in the doorway in her college sweatshirt and said: "Mom, what is all of this?"

I didn't have a good answer.

I tried. "It's my morning routine." She looked at the counter. She looked at me. "That's a routine? That looks like a pharmacy."

She was home for the weekend. She'd brought a small clear bag with one thing in it. She washed, she put it on, she was done. Three minutes. She walked out of the bathroom and went downstairs to make eggs.

I stood there for another twenty minutes finishing my eleven steps.

She's 24. I'm 52. She uses one thing and never thinks about it. I use eleven things and think about it for forty-five minutes every morning. I spend more time managing this than I spend eating breakfast. More than I spend reading. More than I spend on the phone with my sister.

When did it get this complicated?

It got complicated around 49. Things changed. What used to work stopped working. So I added something. Then something else. Then the $90 thing from the beauty counter. Then the one my doctor suggested. Then the one from the article. Then the one my friend used. Each one solved one small piece and none of them solved the whole thing. And nobody ever told me to stop adding. They just kept telling me to add.

My sister told me to stop. Not add something different. Stop.

She'd been using one jar for about a month. Four things in it. Oil-based — not water-based like everything on my counter. She said the difference was simple: the water-based ones don't stay. They go on and evaporate. The oil-based one stays. One application. Done.

I didn't believe her. But I was tired of the forty-five minutes.

**Week 1:** I used it alongside everything else. Couldn't let go yet.

**Week 2:** I dropped the old routine. One jar. Morning and night.

**Week 3:** My daughter came home again. She walked past the bathroom. One jar on the counter. She stopped.

"Where did everything go?"

I held up the jar. "This is it now."

"That's it?"

That's it.

She'll never know what eleven bottles feels like. I hope she never does.

I don't know if this is the answer for every woman running a forty-five-minute morning operation. I know my counter has one thing on it now. I know my morning takes four minutes. I know I eat breakfast sitting down.

Individual results may vary.

→ Simple isn't settling. It's solving.
