# B8C3 — I Added It Up

**Sub-Avatar:** The Invisible Woman
**Angle:** The cost — time and money — of a routine that doesn't deliver
**Character type:** Peer

## Body Copy

I added up what I've spent in the last three years. $3,400. For something that wasn't working.

I was sitting at the kitchen table on a Tuesday night with the calculator open on my phone. My husband was doing dishes. I wasn't looking for this number. I was looking for something else — a receipt — and I started adding.

The $90 thing from the beauty counter. The $45 one my friend recommended. The $28 one from the drugstore I bought every six weeks. The $78 one I ordered online at midnight because a woman on a forum said it changed her life. The $65 one my doctor suggested. Two or three I bought twice because I forgot I already had them.

$3,400 over three years. Give or take.

Then I did the other math. The time math.

Forty-five minutes a day. Every day. Three years. That's 821 hours. Thirty-four full days of my life. Spent on something that didn't hold past lunch.

I sat there looking at those numbers and I didn't feel angry. I felt tired. Three years of trying. Three years of reading articles and watching videos and listening to recommendations and buying the next thing. Each one was going to be the one that worked. None of them were.

And here's the part that got me. They were all the same thing. I pulled them out one night and looked at the ingredients. Water. Glycerin. A long word. Another long word. Water again. Eleven different bottles. One category. Water-based. Every single one.

I was spending $100 a month and 45 minutes a day to put water on myself eleven different ways.

A woman in a group I'm in posted something one night. Not a product recommendation. A question: "Has anyone tried switching to something oil-based instead of water-based? Because nothing water-based stays."

Forty-seven comments. All saying the same thing.

Someone mentioned a balm. One jar. Four things in it. Oil-based. She said it stays because it's a different kind of thing — not water that evaporates, but oil that absorbs. Simple enough that I almost didn't believe it.

I ordered it that Tuesday night. Sitting at the same kitchen table. Same calculator still open on my phone.

**Week 2:** I stopped buying. Didn't need to.

**Week 4:** I threw out the eleven bottles. Put them in a bag. Tied the bag.

The math got simple. One jar. No reapplying at noon. Four minutes in the morning instead of forty-five.

My husband asked what I was doing with the calculator that night. I said: "I was figuring out what I've been spending." He said: "On what?" I said: "On something that wasn't working."

He looked at the one jar on the counter. "And now?"

Now the math is different. Individual results may vary.
