# B8C1 — One Less Thing

**Sub-Avatar:** The Invisible Woman
**Angle:** The skincare routine as mental load — one more thing she manages that nobody counts
**Character type:** Peer

## Body Copy

I was spending 45 minutes every morning on something that wasn't working.

That was my first task of the day. Every day. Before coffee. Before the calendar. Before anything that mattered.

Eleven steps. I had a whole system. Cleanser, toner, two different things with droppers, something for under my eyes, something for my neck, a cream, another cream, a mist, something with SPF. All of it in a specific order. All of it recommended by someone I trusted at some point.

And by lunch every single day — gone. Like I hadn't done any of it.

So I'd reapply. Midday. In the bathroom at work. A tube I carried in my bag like medication.

I was 52. I had been doing this for three years. Forty-five minutes a day, seven days a week, fifty-two weeks a year. That's 273 hours a year. Eleven full days. Spent managing something that never stayed managed.

Nobody counts that time. Not my husband. Not my calendar. Not me, until I sat down and did the math.

My friend Sarah did the math before I did. Same age. Same situation. She'd been running the same kind of routine. One night she said: "I stopped all of it. I'm using one thing now."

I thought she was giving up.

She said: "I was putting the wrong kind of thing on. Eleven times. Every day. All of it was the same category. Water-based. Water doesn't stay. I switched to something oil-based. One jar. Four things in it. It stays."

I almost didn't try it. Because the idea of something simple working felt like an insult to the three years I'd spent being complicated.

But I was tired. Not of how I looked. Of the routine. Of the managing. Of the forty-five minutes.

**Week 1:** I used it alongside my old routine. Couldn't commit yet.

**Week 2:** I stopped reaching for the second tube at noon. Didn't need it.

**Week 3:** My morning took four minutes. I stood in the kitchen with coffee and seven extra minutes I hadn't had in three years.

**Week 5:** I stopped thinking about it entirely. That was the thing. Not that something finally worked. That I stopped having to think about whether it was working.

My husband said something the morning of Week 3. Not about how I looked. He said: "You seem different this morning. Less rushed."

He was right. I wasn't rushed. For the first time in three years I had gotten dressed without managing a small medical operation first.

I don't know if this works for everyone. I know I have one jar on my counter now instead of eleven. I know my morning belongs to me again.

Individual results may vary.

→ One less thing. Everything else follows.
