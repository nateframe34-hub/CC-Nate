# B7C3 — Are You Keeping Busy?

**Sub-Avatar:** The Invisible Woman
**Angle:** Social invisibility at a dinner party — "are you keeping busy?" as the inciting incident that connects to the broader pattern of becoming background, including her skin
**Character type:** Peer
**Hook format:** External dialogue + social rage + pattern interrupt + open loop (4 mechanisms stacked)
**Awareness level:** Solution Aware

---

## Body Copy

Three weeks ago a man at a dinner party asked my husband about his work. His projects. His team. His plans for next quarter. Three questions. Real questions.

Then he turned to me and said: "And are you keeping busy?"

I have a full-time job. I run a household. I manage the calendar for four people. I coordinated this dinner reservation.

I said "mm-hmm" and reached for the wine.

I've been thinking about it every day since.

========

My husband didn't notice. He's not unkind. He just didn't hear it. He was already answering a question about his quarter two forecast by the time I picked up my glass.

On the drive home I sat in the passenger seat and replayed it.

Are you keeping busy. Like I'm retired. Like I'm filling time. Like my days are a hobby and his days are a career.

I didn't say any of this to my husband. He would've said "I'm sure he didn't mean it that way." And he'd be right. The man didn't mean it that way. That's what makes it worse. He wasn't being rude. He just didn't see me as someone with an answer worth asking for.

========

I got home that night and went to the bathroom to wash my face. I stood at the counter looking at the eight products lined up on the vanity. Cleanser. Toner. Two serums. Eye cream. Moisturizer. Night cream. Lip balm.

I thought: when did this become the other job nobody notices me doing?

Because that's what it had become. Twenty-five minutes every night. Thirty dollars a month, sometimes more. And my skin was still tight by noon. Still flat by 3pm. Still not what it was three years ago.

I was doing everything right and it wasn't working. My skin. My schedule. My presence at that table. Same pattern. Same result. Same silence.

========

Let me back up. The dinner party wasn't the start.

Three months before that, a woman at my office — younger, new to the team — presented an idea I'd suggested in a meeting the week before. She got the credit. I watched it happen. I didn't say anything because I did the math on what it would cost to raise it. The math didn't work.

Two months before that, my daughter came home from college and we were getting ready for dinner. She finished her four-product routine in three minutes. I was on step seven. She watched me for a second and said: "Why do you need all that?"

Because I'm 52, honey. Because something changed and nobody warned me.

One month before that, a waiter handed the check to my husband. I had made the reservation. I had chosen the restaurant. I had given my card when we walked in.

Each thing was small. Each thing was nothing. Together they were a drawer I was filling without knowing it. A drawer of moments where I became the background of a room I was standing in the middle of.

========

The Saturday after the dinner party I was cleaning the bathroom. I wasn't thinking about skincare. I was thinking about the man's face when he asked me that question — the way his eyes had already moved to the next person before I answered.

I picked up one of my serums. Flipped it over. Read the back.

First ingredient: water. Second: glycerin. Third: something I can't pronounce.

I picked up the next one. Water. Glycerin. A different chemical.

I picked up the moisturizer. Water.

I stood there with three bottles in my hands thinking: all of this is water.

========

My friend Natalie told me what nobody else had told me. Not my dermatologist. Not the woman at Sephora. Not a single product label in twenty years.

Natalie and I go back fifteen years. She's 54. She's the most skeptical person I know. She once spent forty-five minutes at a farmers market asking a soap maker about her ingredient sourcing before she bought one bar.

If Natalie tells me something works, it works.

She said: "Skin makes its own oil. After about 40, it makes a lot less. Every cream on your counter is mostly water. Water doesn't replace oil. You've been putting the wrong thing on your face."

She'd been using a whipped tallow and honey balm for about six weeks. Four ingredients. Tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula.

I said: "Tallow. Like cow fat."

She said: "I know. I said the same thing. Try it for two weeks. If you hate it I'll pay you back myself."

That's Natalie. I ordered it that night. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

========

Day 2 I woke up and pressed my hand to my cheek. Soft. Not the thirty-minute soft that washes off. A different kind of soft. Like something underneath had been filled in.

Day 6 I put on foundation at 7am. At 3pm — the time of day I'd stopped scheduling video calls because of how my skin looked under the office light — I went to the bathroom to check. It sat flat. It hadn't settled into the lines around my mouth. First time in over a year.

Day 9 Natalie texted me: "Well?"

I sent her a selfie. No filter. She wrote back: "Told you."

Day 12 my coworker Meg asked me what I was using. She'd been dealing with dry skin on her hands and forearms all winter. I showed her the jar. She ordered one that afternoon.

Day 14 my sister stopped by on a Sunday. She looked at me in the kitchen and said: "Something's different. Did you change your hair?"

I hadn't changed my hair. I told her about the balm. She read the label. "Four ingredients?"

I said: "Four."

She said: "I have nineteen products in my bathroom. What am I doing wrong?"

I said: "The same thing I was doing wrong. The wrong kind of thing."

========

Here's what I didn't expect.

I'd been waking up every morning and going straight to the bathroom mirror. Checking. Not checking my outfit. Checking my face. A habit I didn't remember starting. I was evaluating myself before the day began. Every single morning.

Around Day 10 I stopped. I didn't decide to stop. I just noticed one morning that I hadn't done it. I was making coffee before I realized I'd walked right past the bathroom.

That's the thing I wanted back. Not the soft skin. The not-checking.

The not-managing. The not-performing. The being present in a room without calculating whether the light is good.

========

I'm going to another dinner party next month. Someone's birthday. Same group. Same restaurant.

Someone will ask my husband about his work. Someone will turn to me. Maybe the same man. Maybe someone else.

If they ask me what I do, I'll tell them. If they ask me if I'm keeping busy, I'll say yes. But this time I won't be lying. And this time I won't reach for the wine first.

That's not about a jar. It's about a pattern I finally broke. One small piece at a time.

But it started with the jar. Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day guarantee. If it doesn't work, send it back.

Link is below.

I keep thinking about what Natalie said to me. "You've been putting the wrong thing on your face." She was talking about skincare. But she was also talking about something bigger. I'd been applying things that looked right and felt right and did nothing. For years. In more places than my bathroom.

One jar. The right thing. That's where it started.
