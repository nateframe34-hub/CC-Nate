## Body Copy

I asked my dermatologist why my skin suddenly couldn't hold moisture. What she said made me rethink everything I was putting on my face.

I'd been dealing with it for almost a year.

Dry by noon. Tight by 3pm. Flaky around my nose and jawline. Foundation cracking by the end of the day.

I was moisturizing twice a day. Sometimes three times. Layering serum under cream under oil. Drinking 80oz of water.

Nothing held.

========

I finally got an appointment with a new dermatologist. My old one had just said "it's aging" and handed me a retinol prescription that made everything worse.

This one was different.

She didn't look at my skin for two seconds and reach for a prescription pad.

She asked me one question:

"When did this start?"

I thought about it. "About a year ago. Maybe a little more."

She nodded. "And how old were you?"

"48."

She put her pen down.

========

"Your skin isn't damaged," she said. "It's depleted."

She explained that your body makes a natural oil called sebum. It's what keeps your skin soft, hydrated, and protected. You've been producing it your entire life without thinking about it.

Around your mid-40s, that production starts to drop. After menopause, it can fall by more than half.

"Think of it like this," she said. "Your skin had a built-in moisturizer your whole life. And it's running out."

I stared at her.

"So all the creams I've been using..."

"They're adding water. Your skin needs oil. Specifically, it needs fats that match what your skin is made of."

========

She asked me what I'd been using.

I listed everything. CeraVe. A vitamin C serum. Hyaluronic acid. A $90 night cream. Retinol before that.

She pulled up the ingredient lists on her computer.

"These are all water based," she said. "Water, glycerin, dimethicone. They feel hydrating when you apply them. But they evaporate. Your skin isn't holding them because it doesn't have the oil layer to seal them in."

It was like someone turned a light on.

That's why I was reapplying three times a day. The moisture wasn't leaving because I wasn't using enough. It was leaving because there was nothing to hold it there.

========

I asked her what I should use instead.

She hesitated for a second. Then she said something I wasn't expecting.

"There's a growing body of evidence that animal-based fats, specifically tallow, are the closest match to the oils your skin produces naturally. It's about 87% similar."

"Tallow? Like... beef fat?"

"I know how it sounds. But the fatty acid profile is almost identical to human sebum. That's why it absorbs instead of sitting on top. Your skin recognizes it."

She told me she couldn't officially prescribe it. It's not a pharmaceutical product. But she said she'd been hearing more and more from patients who tried it and came back with dramatically better skin.

========

I went home and fell down a rabbit hole.

I found women on forums saying the exact same thing I'd been feeling.

"My skin eats moisturizer and it's dry again in an hour."

"I'm 55 and menopausal. This is the only thing that changed my skin."

"Post menopause, my once dry skin has never felt better."

One woman, 67 years old, wrote: "Can I really have glass skin at this age? It feels that way."

I ordered a jar that night. Whipped tallow and honey balm. Four ingredients. Tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula.

========

I didn't expect much. I'd been disappointed too many times.

But the first morning, I touched my face and it was still soft. Not tight. Not flaky. Just soft.

By the end of the first week, I stopped layering products. Just the balm.

By week two, the dry patches around my nose were gone.

By week three, I looked in the mirror and my skin didn't look tired anymore. It looked like mine again.

My sister noticed at Sunday dinner. "Your skin is glowing. What did you change?"

One jar. That's what I changed.

========

I went back to my dermatologist for a follow up.

She looked at my skin and smiled.

"You tried the tallow."

I told her it was the only thing that worked. That I'd thrown away five half-used products in the past month.

She said she wasn't surprised.

"Your skin knows what it needs. Most of the time, we're just giving it the wrong thing."

========

That sentence stuck with me.

Because I spent over $300 last year on products my skin didn't recognize. Products that evaporated in 30 minutes. Products that were essentially expensive water.

And the thing that actually worked cost $50 and has four ingredients I can read.
