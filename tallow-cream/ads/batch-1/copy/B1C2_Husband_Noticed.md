## Body Copy

My husband looked at me last week and said "your skin looks different. What are you doing?"

He never notices anything.

New haircut? Nothing. New dress? Silence. I rearranged the entire living room last year and it took him four days.

But last Tuesday, unprompted, across the kitchen table, he said that.

And I almost cried.

========

Because six months ago, I couldn't look at myself.

Not dramatically. Not like a breakdown. Just this quiet thing where I'd avoid the bathroom mirror in the morning. Check my phone camera instead of the rearview mirror. Angle away from the window in restaurants.

My skin looked tired.

Not old. Tired.

Like no amount of sleep or water or expensive cream could wake it up.

I'm 52. And sometime around 49, my skin just became someone else's.

The dryness came first. Then the texture. Then the way my foundation would settle into lines I didn't have a year ago.

I'd sit across from women my age and think "why does her skin look like that and mine looks like this?"

========

I didn't tell anyone how much it bothered me. Because it sounds vain. It sounds like something you're not supposed to care about at this age.

But I cared.

I cared when I stopped wanting to FaceTime my daughter at college.

I cared when I started wearing more makeup to cover what less makeup used to hide.

I cared when I looked in the mirror after a full skincare routine and thought "that did nothing."

========

I tried everything.

And I mean everything.

A $120 night cream that my friend swore by. Used it for a month. Couldn't tell the difference.

Collagen supplements. More water. A humidifier in the bedroom.

I wasn't even mad anymore. I was just tired of trying.
