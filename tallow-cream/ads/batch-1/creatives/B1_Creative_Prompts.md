# Batch 1 Creative Prompts — The Invisible Woman (TRUE CAMOUFLAGE FORMAT)

> **CRITICAL — FORMAT NOTES:**
>
> Camouflage ad images show the PAIN POINT or the IDEAL OUTCOME. Nothing else.
>
> For us, that means **extreme close-ups of skin** — either:
> - **The pain:** normal everyday dryness — tight, dull, slightly flaky, dehydrated. The kind of dryness a real woman in her late 40s notices in the bathroom mirror. NOT eczema, NOT cracked, NOT a medical condition, NOT bleeding, NOT inflamed.
> - **The outcome:** smooth, plump, glowing, hydrated, healthy skin
>
> NOT faces. NOT couples. NOT bathrooms. NOT product. NOT lifestyle.
> Just skin. The body part where the problem lives.
>
> **The dryness rule:** The skin should look like the avatar's own skin on a normal day — not a worst-case medical photo. If she sees the image and thinks "that's not me, my skin isn't THAT bad," we've over-shot. The goal is recognition: "yeah that looks like my skin in winter."
>
> **Reference:** Origins swipe file shows BV ads use anatomy diagrams of the affected area, hip pain ads use x-rays of hips, knee surgery ads use photos of knees on couches. The image is always the literal body location of the problem.
>
> **The body copy is what sells. The skin close-up is what stops the scroll.**

## B1C1 — "I went from oily skin my whole life to desert dry in one year"

**The pain visual:** Normal everyday dryness — the kind a real woman notices in the mirror.

**Primary Image — Pain Point (Normal Dry Skin Macro):**
```
Extreme macro close-up photograph of a woman's cheek skin in cool flat natural light. The woman is in her early 50s — the skin clearly shows mature, dehydrated, thirsty skin (NOT young skin, NOT a medical condition).

Specific visual cues that MUST be present:
- Matte, dull, lifeless surface — no glow, no sheen, no dewy quality
- Visibly papery, crepey texture across the entire patch
- A network of fine surface dehydration lines distributed across the skin
- Several SUBTLE fine wrinkles — soft creases that would be present on a real woman in her early 50s (mostly hairline depth, a couple slightly deeper around the cheek area)
- Slightly sunken/flattened look (skin doesn't look plump)
- Washed-out, slightly ashy skin tone — not pink, not warm, not glowing
- Visible natural pore texture, but the skin around the pores looks tight and thirsty

What it should NOT show:
- NO cracks
- NO flaking
- NO redness or inflammation
- NO scabs, sores, or medical-looking patches
- NO deep dramatic wrinkles (not 70-year-old skin)
- NO glow, no warm light, no peach-fuzz catching light

The image should make the avatar (a woman 45-55) think "that looks like my skin in the mirror." It should look like skin at the end of a dry winter, not skin in a magazine.

Cropped extremely tight on a patch of cheek/upper jaw — no eye, no mouth, no nose, no full face, no hair, no jewelry. Cool flat indoor lighting (NOT golden hour, NOT warm window light). Real photograph, not retouched, photorealistic, true-to-life skin texture. Documentary reference style — looks like a candid photo someone took of their own skin in a bathroom mirror.
```

**Alternate — Outcome Visual (Plump Hydrated Skin):**
```
Extreme macro close-up photograph of healthy, hydrated, glowing facial skin in soft natural light. The skin looks plump, smooth, with a natural healthy sheen — not greasy, not retouched, not dewy fake. You can see the natural skin texture but it looks well-fed and resilient. Cropped tight on a patch of cheek skin — no eye, no mouth, no identifying features. Warm soft light. Looks like a "before/after" reference photo from a skincare article showing what hydrated skin actually looks like. Macro lens, real skin, no makeup, no product visible.
```

## B1C2 — "My husband looked at me and said 'your skin looks different'"

**The outcome visual:** The "different" skin he noticed. Glowing, alive.

**Primary Image — Outcome (Glowing Cheek Macro):**
```
Extreme macro close-up of a woman's cheek and jawline in soft golden hour light from a window. The skin has a natural healthy glow — plump, hydrated, slight luminosity, like skin that's been well-moisturized. You can see real skin texture, fine lines, and natural pores — not airbrushed. Slight peach-fuzz catching the light. NO makeup. NO product visible. Just bare skin in natural light. Cropped from cheekbone to jawline only — no eye, no mouth, no full face. Looks like a candid photo someone took of themselves in a window. Real, intimate, almost macro level zoom. iPhone or DSLR macro quality, warm tones.
```

**Alternate — Pain Point (Tired Skin Under Eye):**
```
Extreme macro close-up of the under-eye and cheek area showing normal everyday tiredness — slight dullness, mild dehydration lines, the "tired" look that comes from skin not holding moisture. NOT puffy. NOT dark circles from illness. NOT a medical condition. Just normal late-40s skin that looks slightly tired. Soft natural light. Cropped tight — no full eye, no nose, no identifying features. Just the area where everyday tiredness shows. Looks like a real woman's skin on a normal Tuesday morning, not a worst-case photo. Real, unretouched.
```

## B1C3 — "I asked my dermatologist why my skin couldn't hold moisture"

**The educational visual:** Either the pain or a medical/textbook visual that pairs with "asking the dermatologist."

**Primary Image — Pain Point (Normal Tight Skin):**
```
Extreme macro close-up photograph of normal, slightly dry skin on a woman's neck or jawline. The skin shows mild dehydration — surface tightness, subtle texture changes, the everyday tightness that comes from skin not holding moisture well. NOT cracked. NOT flaking. NOT a medical condition. NOT red or inflamed. Just normal late-40s skin that needs better hydration. Soft natural lighting like a bathroom mirror, not clinical exam-room lighting. Cropped extremely tight — only skin visible, no other features. Looks like a real reference photo of normal everyday dryness, not a worst-case dermatology image. Real skin, no makeup, true-to-life texture.
```

**Alternate — Medical Cross-Section Diagram:**
```
A clean, minimal medical textbook illustration showing two side-by-side cross-sections of human skin in the style of a dermatology educational handout. Soft muted pastel colors (pale pink dermis, soft yellow subcutaneous fat, light blue background). Each cross-section shows the layered structure of skin: epidermis at top, dermis in the middle (with collagen fibers shown as thin curved lines), subcutaneous fat at the bottom, and a single hair follicle with a sebaceous (oil) gland attached.

LEFT cross-section: dense, plump dermis layer with thick collagen fiber network. The sebaceous gland is LARGE, visibly full, with small yellow droplets indicating active oil production flowing up around the hair follicle.

RIGHT cross-section: same skin layers but the dermis is noticeably THINNER and flatter, the collagen fibers are sparse and broken, and the sebaceous gland is significantly SMALLER and almost empty — barely any oil droplets visible.

The two cross-sections sit side by side with a thin vertical divider between them. Equal size, identical framing, identical illustration style — only the internal anatomy differs.

TEXT (this is critical — keep text minimal and clean):
- Single bold title at the top: "How Skin Changes With Age"
- Above the LEFT diagram: "AGE 30"
- Above the RIGHT diagram: "AGE 50"
- One small clean label below the LEFT sebaceous gland: "Active Oil Production"
- One small clean label below the RIGHT sebaceous gland: "Reduced Oil Production"
- NO other text. NO extra labels. NO arrows. NO subtitles. NO disclaimer. NO watermark.

Use a single clean sans-serif font (like Helvetica or Arial) for all text, all the same color (dark gray, not black). Text must be perfectly legible and grammatically correct. Soft minimal background, flat illustration style (no shading, no gradients). Looks like a screenshot from a Healthline article or a Mayo Clinic infographic. Not photorealistic. 4:5 aspect ratio.
```

**Critical text-rendering tips for this prompt:**
- AI image generators struggle with text. Generate 5-10 variations.
- Keep ONLY the variations where ALL 5 text elements are spelled correctly and legible.
- If the AI keeps mangling text, generate the diagram with ZERO text and add the labels in Canva afterward — the illustration itself is what matters.

## GENERATION NOTES

**Style anchor words to include:**
- "extreme macro close-up"
- "normal everyday dryness" (NOT "severe" or "extreme")
- "true-to-life skin"
- "real reference photo"
- "documentary"
- "no product"
- "no face"
- "just skin"
- "medical illustration" (for diagram options)

**Words to AVOID:**
- "beauty photography"
- "editorial"
- "studio lighting"
- "8K"
- "f/2.8"
- "cracked"
- "flaking heavily"
- "severe dryness"
- "medical condition"
- "inflamed"
- Anything that implies a posed photoshoot OR a worst-case dermatology image

**Aspect ratio:** 4:5 (1080 × 1350) for Meta feed

**Mental check before generating:**
> "Would the avatar see this and think 'that looks like my skin' — or would she think 'thank god my skin isn't that bad'?"
>
> If it's the second one, the dryness is over-exaggerated. Regenerate with softer/more normal skin.
