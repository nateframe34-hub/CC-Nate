# Batch 1 Creative Prompts — The Invisible Woman (TRUE CAMOUFLAGE FORMAT)

> **CRITICAL — FORMAT NOTES:**
>
> These are NOT beauty product photos. These are NOT lifestyle shots. These are camouflage ads.
>
> **Rules from the Origins swipe file (origins-native-swipe folder):**
> - Image looks like something you'd see embedded in a Reddit post, a news article, or a personal Facebook share
> - NOT polished. NOT staged. NOT a product shot.
> - The image relates to the body copy but doesn't sell the product directly
> - Goal: scroll-stopper that doesn't look like an ad
> - Image types that work: medical/anatomy diagrams, x-rays, candid POV photos, grainy phone photos, screenshots that look like article images, photos that look "found" not "shot"
>
> **What to AVOID:**
> - Beauty photography lighting
> - Polished skincare-ad aesthetic
> - Smiling models holding products
> - Editorial composition
> - Studio quality
> - Anything that screams "I am a Facebook ad"
>
> **The body copy is what sells. The image is what STOPS the scroll.**

---

## B1C1 — "I went from oily skin my whole life to desert dry in one year"

**Concept:** A "found" reference image that visualizes the oily-to-dry skin shift. This needs to look like something pulled from a women's health article or a dermatology blog — NOT a beauty ad.

**Primary Image — Medical/Educational Style:**
```
Medical illustration in the style of a women's health article diagram. Two side-by-side cross-sections of human skin showing the difference between healthy hydrated skin (left) and dry depleted skin (right). The left cross-section shows plump skin with visible oil glands producing sebum, the right shows the same skin layers but flattened, dry, with reduced sebum production. Soft pastel colors, illustrated style (not photorealistic), labels and arrows like a textbook diagram. Clean white background. Looks like a screenshot from a medical journal or a Healthline article — not a product ad. Educational, informational tone.
```

**Alternate — Candid Phone Photo Style:**
```
Authentic phone photograph of a woman's bathroom counter, taken from above at a slight angle by someone holding their phone. Multiple half-used skincare bottles and creams scattered on a marble counter — moisturizers, serums, an eye cream, a face oil. Some have their caps off. The lighting is real bathroom lighting, slightly yellow, not professional. The photo is slightly out of focus in places. There's a hand visible at the edge of the frame. Looks exactly like a photo someone took to text to a friend captioned "look at all this stuff that doesn't work." iPhone photo quality, not staged, not styled, not beauty photography. Authentic, raw, real.
```

## B1C2 — "My husband looked at me and said 'your skin looks different'"

**Concept:** A candid, almost paparazzi-style photo of a couple in a real moment. NOT posed. The story is the relationship — that's what's at stake.

**Primary Image — Candid Couple at Kitchen Table:**
```
Candid documentary-style photograph of a man and woman in their early 50s sitting across from each other at a kitchen table, both holding coffee mugs, mid-conversation. Real morning light through a window. The wife is laughing slightly, the husband is looking at her with a soft surprised expression — like he just noticed something. They are NOT looking at the camera. Casual home clothes, messy hair, real kitchen with normal kitchen clutter (a banana on the counter, an open laptop, a newspaper). The photo is taken from the side, slightly off-center, like it was snapped quickly by their adult child visiting for the weekend. iPhone photo quality, not professional, not styled. Looks like it could be a real photo from a real morning. Warm tones, candid, intimate.
```

**Alternate — Just Her, Caught Mid-Smile:**
```
Candid photograph of a woman in her early 50s sitting at a kitchen table holding a coffee mug, mid-smile, looking off to the side toward someone who just spoke to her. Natural gray streaks in her hair. No makeup. Soft window light from behind. The photo is taken at eye level from across the table, slightly off-center. Looks like it was taken on a phone by her husband across the table mid-conversation. Real kitchen background, slightly out of focus. Not a posed photo. Not editorial. Authentic candid moment, slight motion blur, iPhone quality.
```

## B1C3 — "I asked my dermatologist why my skin couldn't hold moisture"

**Concept:** Looks like a photo from a doctor's office or a screenshot from a medical/wellness article. The "authority figure visit" visual.

**Primary Image — Doctor's Office Wide Shot:**
```
Authentic photograph of a woman in her late 40s sitting in a dermatologist's exam room, taken from behind/side angle. She's sitting on the exam table chair (not lying on the table) listening to a female doctor in a white coat explaining something while pointing at a tablet or chart. The doctor's face is mostly turned away. The room has typical dermatology office details: a wall poster about skin layers, a counter with cotton swabs and supplies, neutral lighting. The patient is NOT facing the camera — we see her from a 3/4 angle, listening. Real medical office, not glamorous, slightly fluorescent lighting. Looks like a stock photo from a women's health article or a screenshot from a dermatology blog. Documentary, informational, NOT a beauty ad.
```

**Alternate — Anatomy Diagram of Skin Sebum:**
```
Educational illustration showing the structure of human skin in cross-section, with the sebaceous glands highlighted. The illustration uses a soft beige and pink color palette typical of medical textbook diagrams. There are subtle labels pointing to "sebaceous gland," "epidermis," "dermis." The illustration looks like it could be from a Mayo Clinic article or a medical textbook. Clean white background, illustrated style (not photorealistic), professional medical illustration style. The kind of image that would appear next to a women's health article about why skin changes after 40.
```
