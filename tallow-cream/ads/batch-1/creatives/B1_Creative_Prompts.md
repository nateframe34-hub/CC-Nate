# Batch 1 Creative Prompts — The Invisible Woman (TRUE CAMOUFLAGE FORMAT)

> **CRITICAL — FORMAT NOTES:**
>
> Camouflage ad images show the PAIN POINT or the IDEAL OUTCOME. Nothing else.
>
> For us, that means **extreme close-ups of skin** — either:
> - **The pain:** dry, cracked, dehydrated, dull, flaking, tight skin
> - **The outcome:** smooth, plump, glowing, hydrated, healthy skin
>
> NOT faces. NOT couples. NOT bathrooms. NOT product. NOT lifestyle.
> Just skin. The body part where the problem lives.
>
> **Reference:** Origins swipe file shows BV ads use anatomy diagrams of the affected area, hip pain ads use x-rays of hips, knee surgery ads use photos of knees on couches. The image is always the literal body location of the problem.
>
> **The body copy is what sells. The skin close-up is what stops the scroll.**

---

## B1C1 — "I went from oily skin my whole life to desert dry in one year"

**The pain visual:** Dry, dehydrated, depleted skin texture.

**Primary Image — Pain Point (Dry Skin Macro):**
```
Extreme macro close-up photograph of dry, dehydrated facial skin in late afternoon natural light. The skin shows visible dehydration: fine lines from dryness, slight flaking, dull texture, that "crepey" thin look. Tight surface with subtle cracks at the corners. The image is cropped so tight you only see skin — no eye, no mouth, no identifying features, just a patch of skin from a woman's cheek or temple. Cool natural light, no flattering retouching, slightly desaturated. Looks like a clinical or medical reference photo of dehydrated skin in a dermatology article. Real, raw, uncomfortable to look at. Macro lens, true-to-life skin texture. NO product. NO hands. NO face. Just the skin showing the problem.
```

**Alternate — Outcome Visual (Plump Hydrated Skin):**
```
Extreme macro close-up photograph of healthy, hydrated, glowing facial skin in soft natural light. The skin looks plump, smooth, with a natural healthy sheen — not greasy, not retouched. You can see the natural skin texture but it looks well-fed and resilient. Cropped tight on a patch of cheek skin — no eye, no mouth, no identifying features. Warm soft light. Looks like a "before/after" reference photo from a skincare article showing what hydrated skin actually looks like. Macro lens, real skin, no makeup, no product visible.
```

## B1C2 — "My husband looked at me and said 'your skin looks different'"

**The outcome visual:** The "different" skin he noticed. Glowing, alive.

**Primary Image — Outcome (Glowing Cheek Macro):**
```
Extreme macro close-up of a woman's cheek and jawline in soft golden hour light from a window. The skin has a natural healthy glow — plump, hydrated, slight luminosity, like skin that's been well-moisturized. You can see real skin texture, fine lines, and natural pores — not airbrushed. Slight peach-fuzz catching the light. NO makeup. NO product visible. Just bare skin in natural light. Cropped from cheekbone to jawline only — no eye, no mouth, no full face. Looks like a candid photo someone took of themselves in a window. Real, intimate, almost macro level zoom. iPhone or DSLR macro quality, warm tones.
```

**Alternate — Pain Point (Tired Skin Under Eye):**
```
Extreme macro close-up of the under-eye and cheek area showing tired, depleted skin. Visible dehydration lines, dullness, slight darkness, the "tired" look that has nothing to do with sleep. Soft cool natural light. Cropped tight — no full eye, no nose, no identifying features. Just the area where exhaustion shows. Looks like a reference photo from a women's health article about why skin looks tired after 40. Real, unretouched, shows the actual visual of "tired skin."
```

## B1C3 — "I asked my dermatologist why my skin couldn't hold moisture"

**The educational visual:** Either the pain or a medical/textbook visual that pairs with "asking the dermatologist."

**Primary Image — Pain Point (Tight Cracked Skin):**
```
Extreme macro close-up photograph of dry, tight skin on a woman's neck or jawline. The skin shows the classic signs of moisture loss: hairline cracks, dehydration lines, surface tightness, slight rough texture. Cool clinical lighting like an exam room. Cropped extremely tight — only skin visible, no other features. Looks like a dermatological reference image you'd see in a clinical study or a women's health article about dehydrated skin. Real skin, no makeup, slightly desaturated, documentary style.
```

**Alternate — Medical Cross-Section Diagram:**
```
Educational medical illustration showing a side-by-side cross-section of human skin at age 30 versus age 50. The age 30 cross-section shows healthy plump dermis with active oil glands producing sebum. The age 50 cross-section shows the same skin layers but flattened, dry, with clearly reduced sebum production marked with subtle arrows. Soft pastel colors, medical textbook illustration style (not photorealistic). Clean white background. Looks like a screenshot from a Healthline article or a Mayo Clinic page about skin aging. Educational, informational, not promotional.
```

## GENERATION NOTES

**Style anchor words to include:**
- "extreme macro close-up"
- "dehydrated skin texture"
- "true-to-life skin"
- "clinical reference photo"
- "dermatology article"
- "documentary"
- "no product"
- "no face"
- "just skin"
- "medical illustration" (for diagram options)

**Words to AVOID:**
- "beauty photography"
- "editorial"
- "studio lighting"
- "8K"
- "f/2.8"
- Anything that implies a posed photoshoot

**Aspect ratio:** 4:5 (1080 × 1350) for Meta feed

**Mental check before generating:**
> "Is this image showing me the EXACT problem (dry skin) or the EXACT outcome (hydrated skin)?"
>
> If it shows anything else — a face, a product, a couple, a bathroom — regenerate.
