## C1 → the office whiteboard (3rd image)

Closed red circle, four labels running clockwise — STRIP IT → SKIN PANICS → MAKES MORE OIL → SHINY BY LUNCH — with "no exit" underlined dead centre. One thick green arrow breaks out to the lower right to "PUT THE FAT BACK / nothing left to replace ✓". Summary line beneath: **"Your skin isn't oily. It's compensating."** Black and red markers in the tray, our jar sitting on the tray at the right.

**Everything load-bearing is there.** The circle reads as genuinely closed, which is the persuasive job — you can see there's no way off it before reading a word. The green arrow is the only line in the image that goes anywhere, and it's visibly thicker than the red. "no exit" in the middle does the work of the whole ad in two words.

The jar on the marker tray is incidental rather than displayed, which is right.

## C2 → the fridge whiteboard (1st image)

Two columns split by a dashed line. Left: "WHAT YOU'RE PAYING FOR / – removes oil ✓" and then **conspicuously empty**. Right: "WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS" with the red cascade — "oil removed" ↓ "skin loses water" ↓ "skin makes MORE oil" (double-underlined) ↓ **"YOU BUY MORE"** circled in red. Summary across the bottom: **"$410 a year to make it worse."** with a green "← put the fat back ✓" squeezed in beside it. Our jar on the counter below-left, marker in the tray.

**The asymmetry is the argument and it survived intact** — one short line on the left against a four-step cascade on the right. That's visible before any reading happens.

Two details that add real credibility: the half-erased **"Shopping list"** ghosting at the top, and the takeaway menu magnet at the right. Both say *this is a fridge someone actually uses*, which is exactly the register.

## C3 → the outdoor noticeboard (2nd image)

Two identical face profiles either side of a dashed line. Left labelled **SHINY**: sun, one red arrow in, one red arrow bouncing sharply off, solid black shadow triangle, "line reads deep". Right labelled **MATTE**: same sun, same red arrow in, but green arrows scattering, a lighter hatched shadow, "same line. barely reads." Summary beneath: **"The line didn't change. The light did."** Jar on the wooden ledge, marker beside it.

**This is the most demonstrable of the three** and the least claim-exposed, because the viewer can verify it against their own experience of light immediately. It's a fact about surfaces, not a claim about skin. The scattered-versus-single arrow contrast reads instantly.

The car park, brick wall, and grimy board are all correct — this is the community centre noticeboard the copy describes, not a studio prop.
