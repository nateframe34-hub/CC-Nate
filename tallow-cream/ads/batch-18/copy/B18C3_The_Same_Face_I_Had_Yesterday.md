## Body Copy

I stood at the bathroom mirror on a Wednesday morning and stared at my own face for too long.

I was waiting for it to look different than yesterday.

It didn't.

It looked exactly like Tuesday. And Monday. And the Sunday before that.

I have not had that experience since 2019.

========

I'm 54. For about eight years I have been getting ambushed by my own face.

Tuesday I would wake up and it would be fine. Wednesday I would wake up and my cheeks would be burning by 9am for no reason I could trace. Friday the patch under my chin would flake. Sunday I would break out along my jaw the way I had not broken out since I was sixteen.

I never knew which face I was going to get. So I had built a system around the not-knowing.

I had three routines. One for tight days. One for flaky days. One for the days my skin felt fine but I knew it was about to turn. The products lived in a drawer above the sink that I had divided into three compartments. Every morning I would open the drawer, decide which face I had, and pick the routine.

I had been doing that for about eight years. I had been making that decision before coffee for about eight years.

Then I stood at the mirror on a Wednesday and the face I saw was the same face I had at bedtime. Which was the same face I had Tuesday morning. Which was the same face I had Monday.

I did not open the drawer.

========

When I sat down with my coffee I realized I had not opened the drawer in about three weeks.

I had not picked a routine. I had not braced for the wrong face. I had not stood there at 7am deciding whether today was a "fine but turning" day or a "going to flake" day.

I had been waking up. Splashing water. Putting on the same thing. Walking out of the bathroom.

I did not know exactly when it had started.

========

Then I remembered.

About four weeks earlier I had been at my friend Elaine's house for book club. I went upstairs to use her guest bathroom. There was one thing on her counter. One jar. Amber glass. Simple label.

I had been in that bathroom a hundred times. Eighteen months ago it had a toner and two moisturizers and a face oil and a spot treatment and two perfumes and a retinol.

Now it had one jar.

I came downstairs and didn't bring it up.

========

The next book club was at my house three weeks later. Elaine came early to help me set out food. I waited until she was pouring herself wine in the kitchen. Then I asked her about the jar.

She laughed and put the bottle down. She said: "I wondered if you'd notice."

She said she had thrown everything out about nine months earlier. The toner. The serums. The retinol. She kept one jar.

She said she had asked her Aunt Winnie about it at her daughter Jess's wedding last June.

Winnie is 76. She lives in Vermont. She keeps bees and reads more than anyone Elaine knows.

Elaine cornered her in the kitchen during the reception and asked: "What do you do to your face?"

Winnie said: "I've been using the same thing since 1987. Four ingredients. I bought my first one by accident at a farmer's market in Brattleboro."

Winnie is 76 and her skin looks like a woman in her early 60s.

Turns out it was one jar.

========

Here is what Elaine told me Winnie had told her. Read this part slowly.

Skin makes its own oil. It always has. It's the reason you didn't think about moisturizer when you were twenty-five. The oil was just there.

After about forty, skin makes a lot less of it. The drop is real. Most women don't connect what's happening to their face to what changed underneath.

Every cream in Elaine's bathroom, every single one, had water as the first ingredient. She flipped each bottle over one afternoon and checked. Water. Glycerin second. Something unpronounceable third.

Water evaporates in an hour. That's what water does. You put it on at 7am and by noon it's gone. That's why nothing was holding. That's why Elaine's skin was tight some mornings and burning others. That's why mine was.

Winnie told Elaine the fat in the balm is almost the same structure as the fat skin makes on its own. So when you put it on, it absorbs in instead of sitting on top. Skin treats it like its own oil. Because, roughly, it is.

Elaine ordered one the week after the wedding.

========

I said: "Tallow. Like cow fat."

She said: "I know. I made the same face. Just try it."

I ordered one that night after everyone left. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

========

Day 1, I put it on before bed. Melted into my palm like warm honey. Thirty seconds.

Day 4, I woke up and my face looked the same way it had looked when I went to sleep. I had not had that experience in eight years.

Day 9, I realized I had not checked the weather to predict what my skin was going to do that day. I have checked the weather every morning since 2018 to brace for it.

Day 14, I put on foundation at 7am for an offsite. It went on the way it had the previous Wednesday. And the Wednesday before that. I have never in my life had foundation go on the same way two Wednesdays in a row.

Day 21, I looked in the drawer above the sink and realized I had not touched the "flake routine" compartment in three weeks. The eye cream had a thin film of dust on the cap.

========

Here is the part I didn't expect.

My sister Karen came to stay for a weekend in April. She has been using my guest bathroom for thirty years. She came down for breakfast on the second morning and asked me where I had moved the "skincare drawer."

I said I had not moved it. It was where it had always been.

She said: "Then why is it empty?"

I had given the toners away. I had given the retinol away. I had given two of the moisturizers away. I had not really noticed that I had done it. The compartments I had built for the three routines were sitting empty above the sink.

That was the thing I wanted back. Not the soft skin. The boring.

I have never in my life been so grateful for boring.

========

I told my coworker Sylvia about it the next week. We've worked two desks apart for eleven years. I told her Elaine, Winnie, the jar, the four ingredients. I told her about the drawer.

She listened all the way through. Then she said: "My aunt is 79 and she's always had beautiful skin. Like she never fussed. I wonder if she's been doing something like this her whole life and never told anyone."

She ordered one that afternoon.

========

My husband and I were having dinner outside at our usual place on a Saturday in May. Candle on the table. He was finishing his wine.

He looked up at me across the table and said: "You stopped doing that thing."

I said: "What thing?"

He said: "You used to come downstairs in the morning like you were waiting for something to go wrong. You haven't been doing that lately."

I sat there for a second. I had not noticed. He had.

========

I'm not telling you a jar of cow fat will fix your life. That sounds like an ad. This isn't an ad. I'm telling you what Elaine had in her bathroom and what Winnie told her at her daughter's wedding and what my sister Karen said when she came down for breakfast.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If it doesn't do anything in six weeks you email them and you get every dollar back.

Link is below if you want it.

Elaine is still on it. Sylvia ordered one after I told her. Lily asked me for the name last Sunday.

What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the next Wednesday morning when the face in the mirror is the same one you had Tuesday.

I'd stopped believing that was coming back.

Now it's back.
