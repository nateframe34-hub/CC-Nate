## Body Copy

I was walking back from lunch on a Tuesday and caught my reflection in the window of the dry cleaner on Elm Street.

And I kept walking.

I didn't stop. I didn't lean in. I didn't tilt my chin to check the light.

I have not done that without checking in about eight years.

========

I'm 54. I have been monitoring my own face the way you monitor a stove you might have left on. Background process. Every day. No time off.

The side mirror getting out of the car. The bathroom every time I wash my hands. The front camera on my phone before sending a text. The conference room window when I walk past it on my way to the printer.

I did not used to do this. I started about eight years ago, the same year my skin started feeling tight by noon.

For eight years I have had a running commentary in my head about my own face. How it looked. How long until something cracked. Whether the foundation had moved. Whether the dry patch under my chin was visible in this light. Whether anyone could tell.

It ran underneath everything else. Work calls. Talking to my husband. Reading to my daughter Lily when she still let me read to her. The commentary was always there.

On a Tuesday, walking past a dry cleaner, I noticed it had gone quiet.

========

I went home that night and sat at the kitchen table and tried to remember the last time I had not checked.

I couldn't.

Not the morning. Not the afternoon. Not the bedroom mirror getting dressed. Not the bathroom of any restaurant since 2017.

What had not been in my head for about three weeks was the voice that ran the math.

It had stopped and I did not know I had stopped it.

========

Then I remembered.

Three weeks earlier I had been at my friend Elaine's house for book club. I went upstairs to use her guest bathroom. There was one thing on her counter. One jar. Amber glass. Simple label.

I had been in that bathroom a hundred times. Eighteen months ago it had a toner and two moisturizers and a face oil and a spot treatment and two perfumes and a retinol.

Now it had one jar.

I came downstairs and didn't bring it up.

========

The next book club was at my house three weeks later. Elaine came early to help me set out food. I waited until she was pouring wine in the kitchen and then I asked her about the jar.

She laughed and put the bottle down. She said: "I wondered if you'd notice."

She said she had thrown everything out about nine months earlier. The toner. The serums. The retinol. She kept one jar.

She said she had asked her Aunt Winnie about it at her daughter Jess's wedding last June.

Winnie is 76. She lives in Vermont. She keeps bees and reads more than anyone Elaine knows.

Elaine cornered her in the kitchen during the reception and asked: "What do you do to your face?"

Winnie said: "I've been using the same thing since 1987. Four ingredients. I bought my first one by accident at a farmer's market in Brattleboro."

Winnie is 76 and her skin looks like a woman in her early 60s.

Turns out it was one jar.

========

Here is what Elaine told me Winnie had told her. Read this part slowly.

Skin makes its own oil. It always has. It's the reason you didn't think about moisturizer when you were twenty-five. The oil was just there.

After about forty, skin makes a lot less of it. The drop is real. Most women don't connect what's happening to their face to what changed underneath.

Every cream in Elaine's bathroom, every single one, had water as the first ingredient. She flipped each bottle over one afternoon and checked. Water. Glycerin second. Something unpronounceable third.

Water evaporates in an hour. That's what water does. You put it on at 7am and by noon it's gone. That's why nothing was holding. That's why Elaine's skin was tight. That's why mine was.

Winnie told Elaine the fat in the balm is almost the same structure as the fat skin makes on its own. So when you put it on, it absorbs in instead of sitting on top. Skin treats it like its own oil. Because, roughly, it is.

Elaine ordered one the week after the wedding.

========

I said: "Tallow. Like cow fat."

She said: "I know. I made the same face. Just try it."

I ordered one that night after everyone left. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

========

Day 1, I put it on before bed. Melted into my palm like warm honey. Thirty seconds. I did not think about it again until morning.

Day 4, I woke up and got out of bed without going to the mirror first. I noticed two hours later in the office bathroom.

Day 9, I sat through a budget call without thinking once about how my face looked on the camera. I have done budget calls for fifteen years. I had never done one without checking.

Day 14, I put on foundation at 7am for an offsite. I did not check it at the stoplight on the way there. I did not check it walking in. I did not check it at any point during the meeting. At 2pm I went to the restroom and saw it was still flat.

Day 21, I left my phone face-down on the kitchen counter all evening while I made dinner. I noticed it at 9pm. Three weeks earlier I had been picking it up to use the front camera maybe six times an hour without thinking about it.

========

Here is the part I didn't expect.

Lily had her 27th birthday in March. I drove out to her apartment with the cake. I stood in her kitchen for two hours while her friends talked about their jobs.

When I got back in the car at 9:30pm I realized I had not thought about my own face for the entire afternoon. Not in her bathroom. Not in her hallway mirror. Not in the reflection of her oven door. Not once.

I had been present at my own daughter's birthday in a way I had not been present for anything in eight years.

I sat in the driver's seat and didn't turn the key for a while.

That was the thing I wanted back. Not the soft skin. The quiet.

========

I told my coworker Sylvia about it the next week. We've worked two desks apart for eleven years. I told her Elaine, Winnie, the jar, the four ingredients. I told her the part about Lily's birthday.

She listened all the way through. Then she said: "My aunt is 79 and she's always had beautiful skin. Like she never fussed. I wonder if she's been doing something like this her whole life and never told anyone."

She ordered one that afternoon.

========

My husband and I were having dinner outside at our usual place on a Saturday in May. Candle on the table. He was finishing his wine.

He looked up at me across the table and said: "You seem different lately."

I said: "Different how?"

He said: "I don't know. Like you're actually here."

I sat there for a second. Then I said: "I am."

He didn't know what I meant. He had not noticed the running commentary. Only I had. Now there was nothing to notice.

========

I'm not telling you a jar of cow fat will fix your life. That sounds like an ad. This isn't an ad. I'm telling you what Elaine had in her bathroom and what Winnie told her at her daughter's wedding and what happened in my daughter's kitchen on her 27th birthday.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If it doesn't do anything in six weeks you email them and you get every dollar back.

Link is below if you want it.

Elaine is still on it. Sylvia ordered one after I told her. Lily asked me for the name last Sunday.

What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the next time you walk past a window and don't stop.

I'd stopped believing that was coming back.

Now it's back.
