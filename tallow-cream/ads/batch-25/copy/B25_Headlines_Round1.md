## Angle 1: Confrontation
**Sub-avatar:** SA2 Graveyard + SA5 Label Reader (these run as one audience per `Brand_Mastery.md`). **Awareness:** Solution Aware (she's tried products, is now evaluating a specific claim). **Sophistication response:** New Mechanism + blunt New Information, Stage 5 market means a soft claim dies, this angle works because it's confrontational, not because it's clever.
**Desire being amplified:** stop wasting money on products that don't deliver; the anger under "$860 in six months" and "nothing works, prove it."

1. "WHY DOES YOUR $80 MOISTURIZER FEEL LIKE IT'S DOING NOTHING? THIS IS THE INGREDIENT MOST CREAMS SKIP..."
2. "YOUR MOISTURIZER'S FIRST INGREDIENT IS WATER. HERE'S WHY THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE PRICE TAG..."
3. "$80 CREAM. 70% WATER. THIS $50 JAR HAS NEITHER PROBLEM..."
4. "STOP BUYING MOISTURIZER THAT'S GONE BY NOON. HERE'S WHAT ACTUALLY STAYS..."
5. "THE REASON YOUR $300-A-YEAR SKINCARE ROUTINE STILL ISN'T WORKING..."

## Angle 2: 43 Ingredients vs 4
**Sub-avatar:** SA5 Label Reader (SA2 overlap). **Awareness:** Problem/Solution Aware crossover, she knows something's off with her routine but hasn't located the cause. **Sophistication response:** Radical Legibility mechanism, "anti-ingredient-list" contrarian positioning, this is new to the niche even though transparency itself isn't new to Stage 5 skincare broadly.
**Desire being amplified:** trust what goes on her skin; relief from suspicion, not more decisions.

1. "HER MOISTURIZER HAS 43 INGREDIENTS. THIS ONE HAS 4. GUESS WHICH ONE HER SKIN ACTUALLY ABSORBS..."
2. "COUNT THE INGREDIENTS ON YOUR MOISTURIZER. NOW COUNT THESE..."
3. "43 INGREDIENTS SHE CAN'T PRONOUNCE. 4 SHE ALREADY KNOWS. SHE SWITCHED..."
4. "SHE TRIED READING HER MOISTURIZER'S LABEL OUT LOUD. SHE DIDN'T FINISH THE SENTENCE..."
5. "ONE OF THESE INGREDIENT LISTS FITS ON A STICKY NOTE. THE OTHER DOESN'T FIT ON THE BOTTLE..."

## Angle 3: Radical Legibility (read the label out loud)
**Sub-avatar:** SA5 Label Reader. **Awareness:** Problem Aware leaning Solution Aware. **Sophistication response:** same Radical Legibility mechanism as Angle 2, but leads with the trust/transparency payoff itself rather than the comparison stat, a softer entry point into the same cluster.
**Desire being amplified:** to trust what she's putting on her skin without having to research it herself.

1. "FLIP OVER YOUR MOISTURIZER. IF YOU CAN'T READ INGREDIENT #3, KEEP READING..."
2. "THIS MOISTURIZER HAS 4 INGREDIENTS. YOU COULD READ THE WHOLE LABEL AND STILL KNOW EVERYTHING IN IT..."

4. "MOST MOISTURIZERS NEED A CHEMISTRY DEGREE TO READ THE BACK. THIS ONE DOESN'T..."
5. "SHE SWITCHED TO A 4-INGREDIENT MOISTURIZER. HERE'S WHAT SHE NOTICED FIRST..."

## The listicle these route to

All three angles are variations on ONE cluster (ingredient distrust + wasted money + a legibility payoff), so per the B25 concept plan, they route to a single shared listicle rather than three separate destinations, that keeps the "which ad wins" comparison clean since only the ad changes, not the offer or the landing experience.

**Working title:** *"5 Signs Your Moisturizer Is Working Against You (Not For You)"*

**Why this frame fits all three ads:** every one of the three headline sets above makes a claim (waste, ingredient count, illegibility) and ends on an ellipsis, an open loop. A listicle titled around "signs" is the exact shape of payoff a reader expects after a confrontational or comparison-based hook: not a story, a checklist that lets her self-diagnose against something concrete. That match between what the ad promises and what the page delivers is the same lesson B24 already proved out (reveal-forward ad -> reveal-forward destination, restating instead of building costs conversions).

**How it walks her to a converting mindset, sign by sign:**

1. **Sign 1, water-first ingredient lists** (serves Angle 1/Confrontation directly): reader flips her own moisturizer mentally, this is the "prove it" moment, doesn't ask her to trust the brand yet, just her own bottle.
2. **Sign 2, the label she can't finish reading** (serves Angle 2/3): reframes illegibility from "normal" to "a red flag," this is the New Information beat, most listicles like this stop at "here's a problem," this one names WHY it's a problem (absorption, not aesthetics).
3. **Sign 3, the evaporation-by-noon tell** (mechanism, cross-cutting): this is the account's single most-proven beat (B19C2, B9C3's spine), water evaporates, fat absorbs, gives her the actual physical reason behind signs 1 and 2 instead of leaving it as a vague feeling.
4. **Sign 4, the shelf-full-of-half-used-products tell** (serves SA2 Graveyard underneath all three angles): this is where the piece stops being diagnostic and starts being personal, most readers self-select into this one, it's the account's best-converting emotional beat.
5. **Sign 5, the cost tell** (closes the loop back to Angle 1's dollar-figure confrontation): adds up what she's likely spent, then pivots directly into the product as the one-time fix, this is the hinge from information to offer.

**Then the actual conversion mechanics:** inline blue-link CTAs woven into signs 3 and 4 (same Javvy-style pattern proven on the advertorial, not a hard-sell button until the very end), a real customer review after sign 4 for social proof at the exact moment she's most emotionally open, then the close: the 4-ingredient label restated as the payoff (ties back to whichever ad she clicked), the guarantee as risk reversal (the account's proven strongest lever per the June offer research, not price), and one final CTA button.

**Why this converts rather than just informs:** the whole arc amplifies desire before ever mentioning the product. By the time she reaches sign 5, she's diagnosed her OWN moisturizer as failing on 4-5 concrete counts, not been told a story about someone else's. The product then arrives as the answer to a problem she just built for herself in real time, that's a stronger purchase trigger than either pure information (she learns something and leaves) or pure story (she's moved but not implicated). This mirrors exactly why SA2's "fourteen products failed for one shared reason" beat already converts, specificity turns a vague dissatisfaction into a named, countable problem, and a named problem is the thing money actually gets spent solving.
