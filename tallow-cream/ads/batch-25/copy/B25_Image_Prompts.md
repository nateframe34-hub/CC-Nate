# B25 Image Prompts: 3 Locked Creatives, Pubity-Style Format

**Date:** 2026-07-07, REVISED same day after founder compared the first-round generations directly against the reference ad and flagged they "just don't hit the same."
**Format spec (from the reference image + `How To Create Ads.docx`):** one full-bleed image per ad. Bottom portion is a sharp, produced product photo with a bold white all-caps headline overlaid on a SOLID near-black base block (not a soft gradient over a busy background) for maximum contrast (two lines: the direct claim, then a shorter curiosity/authority trailing line ending in an ellipsis). Top-right carries a small circular inset photo, a genuinely charged "diagnostic" or before-moment beat that visually earns the headline's claim. No badge tag on these 3, the reference swipe itself doesn't use one.

**What the round-1 generations got wrong, fixed in this revision:**
1. **Too much clutter.** Round 1 had 5-8 background bottles competing with the hero jar. The reference has ONE product, tight and dominant, everything else is negative space. Fixed: at most 1-2 blurred background items, way more empty space around the jar.
2. **Flat, even, warm lighting.** Round 1 read as generic golden-hour lifestyle stock. The reference is moodier, higher-contrast, cooler-toned, almost editorial. Fixed: added explicit lighting/contrast direction below.
3. **Inset wasn't dramatic enough.** Round 1's insets were mild squints/frowns, a facial expression, not a gut-punch. The reference's inset (hospital bed, oxygen tube) creates real dread. Fixed: sharper, more visibly upset reactions specified per angle below.
4. **Text sat on a busy gradient.** Fixed: headline block is now a solid near-black rectangle, not a gradient over the wood counter.
5. **Too wide/lifestyle, not tight/graphic.** Fixed: tighter crop, more negative space, less "full bathroom scene."

**Round 3 fix, 2026-07-07: the inset faces read as AI-generated.** Founder flagged the close-up woman-reacting insets specifically, over-smooth skin, too-symmetric features, that uncanny "generated" look faces are still the hardest thing for AI image tools to nail, especially in a tight emotional close-up. Fix applied below: rewrote the inset language to intentionally target a raw, unposed PHONE-SNAPSHOT look instead of a polished portrait, imperfect focus, visible skin texture and pores, natural asymmetry, slight grain, off-center framing. This does double duty: it hides the generation artifacts (imperfection reads as authentic rather than uncanny), and it's a better fit for the concept anyway, the inset is supposed to be someone else's raw candid moment, not a produced photo. If faces still don't clear the bar after this, the fallback is sourcing real stock photography for just the insets and keeping AI generation for the product/jar shots, which have been working well throughout.

**Shared technical spec across all 3:** 4:5 aspect ratio (1080x1350), Meta US delivery, sharp/in-focus produced photography, moody/higher-contrast lighting (not soft golden-hour warmth), amber glass jar, real hands, minimal/tight composition with generous negative space, not a busy full-scene lifestyle shot.

## Angle 1: Confrontation
**Headline:** "WHY DOES YOUR $80 MOISTURIZER FEEL LIKE IT'S DOING NOTHING? THIS IS THE INGREDIENT MOST CREAMS SKIP..."
**What the inset needs to earn:** the price/value confrontation, this is about money spent for nothing, not about ingredients yet (that's Angles 2/3's job).

**Full image prompt (revised):**
```
A tight, high-contrast, moody product-ad photograph, full bleed edge to edge, 4:5 aspect ratio, 1080x1350, shot like a real commercial/editorial campaign, not a warm lifestyle stock photo. Main image: a close, tightly cropped shot of a woman's hand and forearm holding an open amber glass jar of whipped balm directly toward the camera, filling most of the frame, shallow depth of field with the jar in crisp sharp focus. Background is a plain, softly darkened bathroom wall or blurred neutral surface with generous negative space, NOT a counter full of other products, at most one other bottle barely visible and heavily out of focus in a back corner. Cooler, higher-contrast lighting, slightly moody and directional rather than soft golden warmth, deep shadows, crisp highlights on the glass jar. In the upper right corner, a small circular inset photo (thin white ring border, subtle drop shadow), NO PEOPLE, NO FACES in this inset. A raw, unposed phone-snapshot-style close-up of a small handful of crumpled paper receipts fanned out on a counter or nightstand, visible line-item text implied but not legible at thumbnail size, next to a couple of empty or near-empty generic skincare bottles tipped on their sides, suggesting money spent and nothing to show for it. Ordinary indoor lighting, slight grain, off-center amateur framing, like a quick phone photo, not a produced still life. Across the bottom third, a solid near-black rectangular block (not a gradient) for maximum text contrast, with large bold white sans-serif all-caps text: "WHY DOES YOUR $80 MOISTURIZER FEEL LIKE IT'S DOING NOTHING?" with the phrase "$80 MOISTURIZER" in a warm accent amber/gold color to highlight it, rest in white. Below that, smaller white text: "THIS IS THE INGREDIENT MOST CREAMS SKIP..." A thin horizontal divider line and small logomark could sit above the headline block, similar to a real produced commercial ad. No badge, no news graphic elements, no fake outlet branding.
```

## Angle 3: Radical Legibility
**Headline:** "MOST MOISTURIZERS NEED A CHEMISTRY DEGREE TO READ THE BACK. THIS ONE DOESN'T..."
**What the inset needs to earn:** the same reading-the-label struggle as Angle 2, but the main image needs to pay off the RESOLUTION (a legible label), not just restate the problem, since this ad's whole claim is about the alternative being simple.

**Full image prompt (revised):**
```
A tight, high-contrast, moody product-ad photograph, full bleed edge to edge, 4:5 aspect ratio, 1080x1350, shot like a real commercial/editorial campaign, not a warm lifestyle stock photo. Main image: two hands (a giving/receiving gesture) holding an open amber glass jar of whipped balm between them at a slight angle so its simple kraft-paper label is clearly legible and dominant in frame, filling most of the composition. Background is plain and darkened with generous negative space, no cluttered counter, no other products visible at all. Cooler, higher-contrast lighting, slightly moody and directional, deep shadows, crisp highlight on the glass and label. In the upper right corner, a small circular inset photo (thin white ring border, subtle drop shadow), NO PEOPLE, NO FACES in this inset. A raw, unposed phone-snapshot-style close-up of 3-4 generic skincare bottles laid on their sides so their back labels face the camera, dense small-print ingredient text visible as a texture across all of them (not individually legible at thumbnail size), suggesting an unreadable wall of text. Ordinary indoor lighting, slight grain, off-center amateur framing, like a quick phone photo, not a produced still life. Across the bottom third, a solid near-black rectangular block (not a gradient) for maximum text contrast, with large bold white sans-serif all-caps text: "MOST MOISTURIZERS NEED A CHEMISTRY DEGREE TO READ THE BACK." with the phrase "CHEMISTRY DEGREE" in a warm accent amber/gold color, rest in white. Below that, smaller white text: "THIS ONE DOESN'T..." A thin horizontal divider line and small logomark could sit above the headline block, similar to a real produced commercial ad. No badge, no news graphic elements, no fake outlet branding.
```

## Notes for whoever generates/reviews these

1. **The two women in each inset should read as different people from the main jar-holding woman** (per the reference ad, the inset is a separate "someone else's" diagnostic moment, not the same person mid-story), reinforces this as a relatable pattern, not one individual's anecdote.
2. **No fake outlet names, logos, or press-style badges anywhere in any of the 3.** This is a harder line than B22 walked (B22 used a generic "BREAKING NEWS" badge and got compliance sign-off on it being aesthetic-only), B25 skips the badge question entirely by not using one, cleanest posture given the explicit warning in the founder's own process doc.
3. **"Click To Read Body Copy" is Meta primary text, not image copy.** Whoever builds the actual ad in Ads Manager should set the primary text field to a short line like "Click to read body copy" (or the account's usual short primary-text convention) rather than trying to bake it into the creative.
4. **Face visibility flag, worth watching per B22/B23's documented history:** B22C1 (a produced photo with a visible face) delivered at the account's cheapest CPM ever, while B23's quote-card faces died. These 3 are produced lifestyle photos like B22C1, not quote cards, so the face-inclusion decision here follows the B22C1 precedent, not B23's. Worth flagging as a variable to watch in the data once these run, same open question noted back in the B25 concept plan.

# Listicle Hero Images (3, one per page, currently blank)

**Different job than the ad creatives above.** Each listicle (`tallow-listicle-confrontation.liquid`, `tallow-listicle-labelcount.liquid`, `tallow-listicle-readaloud.liquid`) has exactly one image slot, `hero_image`, rendered at the top of the article right below the byline, full-width, height auto (no fixed crop enforced by the CSS, so a landscape/wide image reads best, not a square ad crop). This should feel like a calm editorial photo accompanying a personal write-up, "Julie B. wrote this and took a picture of her counter," not a bold scroll-stop ad. No headline text, no badges, no overlays, just a real, quiet photo.

**Shared technical spec:** landscape/wide crop, roughly 3:2 (1200x800 works well at the 1200px render width), sharp and well-lit but calm, natural window light, no dramatic gradients or bold typography, consistent with the account's established real-counter product photography.

## Hero 1: Confrontation page
**What it needs to support:** the water/price theme, without literally showing price tags (keep it visual, not text-based).
```
A calm, editorial-quality photograph, landscape orientation, roughly 3:2 aspect ratio, 1200x800. A bright, realistic bathroom counter shot from a slightly elevated angle, soft natural window light, warm neutral tones. One open amber glass jar of whipped balm sits alone on the counter, a small amount visible on the lid, simple and uncluttered. To the side, slightly out of focus, a single generic clear plastic pump bottle of lotion for scale and contrast, no visible logo, positioned so the two containers are being implicitly compared without any text or price signage in frame. Marble or light wood counter surface, a folded white towel in the corner, natural and lived-in, not styled like a product ad. No text, no overlays, no badges.
```

## Hero 2: Label-count page
**What it needs to support:** the 43-vs-4 counting theme, visual density contrast.
```
A calm, editorial-quality photograph, landscape orientation, roughly 3:2 aspect ratio, 1200x800. A bright, realistic bathroom counter shot from a slightly elevated angle, soft natural window light, warm neutral tones. One open amber glass jar of whipped balm in sharp focus in the foreground, its simple kraft-paper label facing the camera. Behind it, deliberately out of focus, a small cluster of 4-5 other generic skincare bottles and tubes crowded together, no visible logos, suggesting a shelf of products versus the one jar. Marble or light wood counter surface, natural and lived-in. No text, no overlays, no badges.
```

## Hero 3: Read-aloud page
**What it needs to support:** the legibility/trust theme, the label itself should be the visual focus.
```
A calm, editorial-quality photograph, landscape orientation, roughly 3:2 aspect ratio, 1200x800. A bright, realistic bathroom counter, soft natural window light, warm neutral tones. A woman's hand holding an open amber glass jar of whipped balm at a gentle angle so its simple kraft-paper label is clearly readable in frame, evoking a short plain ingredient list without needing to be perfectly legible at thumbnail size. Soft-focus bathroom background, marble or light wood counter surface, natural and lived-in, not styled like a product ad. No text, no overlays, no badges.
```

## Note on reuse
These 3 hero photos are close enough in setup (same counter, same jar, same lighting) that they could plausibly be shot as one photo session with 3 crops/compositions rather than 3 fully separate generations, worth keeping in mind if generating them takes meaningful time or credits.
