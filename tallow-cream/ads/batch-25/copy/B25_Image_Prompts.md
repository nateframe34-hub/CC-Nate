# B25 Image Prompts: 3 Locked Creatives, Pubity-Style Format

**Date:** 2026-07-07
**Format spec (from the reference image + `How To Create Ads.docx`):** one full-bleed image per ad. Bottom two-thirds is a sharp, produced product/lifestyle photo with a bold white all-caps headline overlaid on a dark gradient at the base (two lines: the direct claim, then a shorter curiosity/authority trailing line ending in an ellipsis). Top-right carries a small circular inset photo, a "diagnostic" or before-moment beat that visually earns the headline's claim without narrating it. No badge tag on these 3 (unlike B22's "BREAKING NEWS" badge), the reference swipe itself doesn't use one, and skipping it keeps clear distance from the "fake press release" compliance line flagged in `How To Create Ads.docx`, these read as native/organic posts, not news graphics. "Click To Read Body Copy" is NOT baked into the image, it's Meta primary text/ad copy sitting above the image in the actual post, matching how the reference ad is actually structured.

**Shared technical spec across all 3:** 4:5 aspect ratio (1080x1350), Meta US delivery, sharp/well-lit/in-focus produced photography (not a grainy candid or screenshot), consistent with the account's established product-photography house style (amber glass jar, warm neutral tones, real hands/real bathroom counters, no stock-photo gloss).

---

## Angle 1: Confrontation
**Headline:** "WHY DOES YOUR $80 MOISTURIZER FEEL LIKE IT'S DOING NOTHING? THIS IS THE INGREDIENT MOST CREAMS SKIP..."
**What the inset needs to earn:** the price/value confrontation, this is about money spent for nothing, not about ingredients yet (that's Angles 2/3's job).

**Full image prompt:**
```
A sharp, well-lit, in-focus product-ad photograph, full bleed edge to edge, 4:5 aspect ratio, 1080x1350. Main image (bottom two-thirds of frame): a woman in her mid-40s to mid-50s standing at a bright, realistic bathroom counter, holding an open amber glass jar of whipped balm toward the camera with one hand, her expression calm and slightly knowing, not posed-happy. Visible on the counter behind her, softly out of focus, several other half-empty skincare bottles and jars pushed to one side, suggesting a shelf she's stopped reaching for, without any visible logos or brand names on those blurred bottles. Natural window light, warm neutral tones, no harsh flash, genuine editorial-lifestyle quality, not glossy stock photography. In the upper right corner of the frame, a small circular inset photo (soft white ring border, subtle drop shadow) showing a candid, close, slightly unflattering moment: a different woman standing at her own bathroom mirror, squinting with a mild frown while holding a different, generic-looking cream bottle a few inches from her face, clearly checking something on it with visible skepticism, realistic and relatable, not exaggerated. Across the bottom of the main image, a dark gradient overlay for legibility, with large bold white sans-serif all-caps text: "WHY DOES YOUR $80 MOISTURIZER FEEL LIKE IT'S DOING NOTHING?" with the phrase "$80 MOISTURIZER" in a warm accent amber/gold color to highlight it, rest in white. Below that, smaller white text: "THIS IS THE INGREDIENT MOST CREAMS SKIP..." No badge, no news graphic elements, no fake outlet branding. Overall polished but native-feeling, like a real produced social post, not a screenshot or ad template.
```

## Angle 2: 43 vs 4
**Headline:** "HER MOISTURIZER HAS 43 INGREDIENTS. THIS ONE HAS 4. GUESS WHICH ONE HER SKIN ACTUALLY ABSORBS..."
**What the inset needs to earn:** the specific ingredient-count struggle, a literal moment of trying and failing to parse a long ingredient list.

**Full image prompt:**
```
A sharp, well-lit, in-focus product-ad photograph, full bleed edge to edge, 4:5 aspect ratio, 1080x1350. Main image (bottom two-thirds of frame): close, shallow-depth-of-field product shot, an open amber glass jar of whipped balm in sharp focus on a warm wood or marble bathroom counter, a small amount of the balm visible on a woman's fingertip reaching into frame from one side. Behind the jar, deliberately out of focus, a small cluster of other skincare bottles and tubes crowded together, suggesting a routine of many products versus this one jar, no visible logos. Natural daylight, warm and clean, editorial-lifestyle quality, not glossy stock photography. In the upper right corner of the frame, a small circular inset photo (soft white ring border, subtle drop shadow) showing a candid moment: a woman holding a different generic skincare bottle very close to her face, squinting hard while trying to read small text on the back label, genuinely straining, realistic and relatable, not exaggerated or cartoonish. Across the bottom of the main image, a dark gradient overlay for legibility, with large bold white sans-serif all-caps text: "HER MOISTURIZER HAS 43 INGREDIENTS. THIS ONE HAS 4." with the numbers "43" and "4" in a warm accent amber/gold color to highlight the contrast, rest in white. Below that, smaller white text: "GUESS WHICH ONE HER SKIN ACTUALLY ABSORBS..." No badge, no news graphic elements, no fake outlet branding. Overall polished but native-feeling, like a real produced social post, not a screenshot or ad template.
```

## Angle 3: Radical Legibility
**Headline:** "MOST MOISTURIZERS NEED A CHEMISTRY DEGREE TO READ THE BACK. THIS ONE DOESN'T..."
**What the inset needs to earn:** the same reading-the-label struggle as Angle 2, but the main image needs to pay off the RESOLUTION (a legible label), not just restate the problem, since this ad's whole claim is about the alternative being simple.

**Full image prompt:**
```
A sharp, well-lit, in-focus product-ad photograph, full bleed edge to edge, 4:5 aspect ratio, 1080x1350. Main image (bottom two-thirds of frame): a woman's hands holding an open amber glass jar of whipped balm at a slight angle so its simple kraft-paper label is clearly legible in frame, standing at a bright, realistic bathroom counter. Soft natural window light, warm neutral tones, no harsh flash, editorial-lifestyle quality, not glossy stock photography. The label itself should read simply, evoking a short, plain ingredient list (tallow, honey, olive oil, calendula) without needing to be perfectly legible at thumbnail size, the visual impression of simplicity matters more than exact text rendering. In the upper right corner of the frame, a small circular inset photo (soft white ring border, subtle drop shadow) showing a candid, slightly frustrated moment: a woman holding a different generic skincare bottle at arm's length, brow furrowed, mouth slightly open as if sounding out a hard-to-pronounce word on the label, realistic and relatable, not exaggerated. Across the bottom of the main image, a dark gradient overlay for legibility, with large bold white sans-serif all-caps text: "MOST MOISTURIZERS NEED A CHEMISTRY DEGREE TO READ THE BACK." with the phrase "CHEMISTRY DEGREE" in a warm accent amber/gold color to highlight it, rest in white. Below that, smaller white text: "THIS ONE DOESN'T..." No badge, no news graphic elements, no fake outlet branding. Overall polished but native-feeling, like a real produced social post, not a screenshot or ad template.
```

## Notes for whoever generates/reviews these

1. **The two women in each inset should read as different people from the main jar-holding woman** (per the reference ad, the inset is a separate "someone else's" diagnostic moment, not the same person mid-story), reinforces this as a relatable pattern, not one individual's anecdote.
2. **No fake outlet names, logos, or press-style badges anywhere in any of the 3.** This is a harder line than B22 walked (B22 used a generic "BREAKING NEWS" badge and got compliance sign-off on it being aesthetic-only), B25 skips the badge question entirely by not using one, cleanest posture given the explicit warning in the founder's own process doc.
3. **"Click To Read Body Copy" is Meta primary text, not image copy.** Whoever builds the actual ad in Ads Manager should set the primary text field to a short line like "Click to read body copy" (or the account's usual short primary-text convention) rather than trying to bake it into the creative.
4. **Face visibility flag, worth watching per B22/B23's documented history:** B22C1 (a produced photo with a visible face) delivered at the account's cheapest CPM ever, while B23's quote-card faces died. These 3 are produced lifestyle photos like B22C1, not quote cards, so the face-inclusion decision here follows the B22C1 precedent, not B23's. Worth flagging as a variable to watch in the data once these run, same open question noted back in the B25 concept plan.
