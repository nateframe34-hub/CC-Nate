**Claims are more hedged than the compliance floor requires.** Checked against `Tallow_Ad_Creative_Principles.md`'s actual banned-phrase list (below), B9C3 avoids all banned terms correctly, but it also avoids several categories of claim that AREN'T banned: it never states the mechanism as a hard, confident fact ("this isn't a theory, it's chemistry"), never makes a direct numbered comparison claim (ingredient counts, days-to-notice-a-difference stats), and keeps every claim wrapped in personal narrative distance even where a flat factual statement would be equally compliant and more persuasive. ## Part 2: Claims philosophy for this batch (addresses founder's direct note)

**The founder's point, restated so the line is explicit:** avoiding claims "like the plague" isn't the same thing as compliance. The account has real things it can say with full confidence, and has been under-claiming relative to what's actually safe.

**What we ARE allowed to state with full confidence, no hedging, because it's true, verifiable, and doesn't touch a banned phrase:**
- The mechanism, as flat fact, not a theory floated by a peer character: water evaporates within roughly an hour of application, that's chemistry, not a formulation flaw specific to any brand. Fat-based products don't carry that evaporation problem. This can be stated more directly than B9C3's soft "she flipped the bottle and checked" framing.
- Exact ingredient counts, stated as direct comparison, not vague "clean beauty" language (which IS banned): "4 ingredients" vs. a named or implied competitor count is a factual, verifiable claim, not a compliance risk.
- Price, guarantee terms, shipping: state these as flat fact, they're not marketing claims, they're terms.
- First-person, narrator-owned specific outcomes ("by day 14 my foundation didn't crack") are always safer AND can be stated more confidently than second-person promises ("yours won't crack either"), because they're reporting an experience, not diagnosing the reader.
- Comparative ingredient-list claims (43 ingredients vs. 4, water-as-ingredient-#1 vs. not) are factual and neutral, not active-harm framing, as long as they don't say a competitor's product is dangerous or harmful, only that it's diluted/temporary.

**What stays hard-banned regardless of this instruction, per `Tallow_Ad_Creative_Principles.md` Principle 6 (unchanged, non-negotiable):**
- Medical/hormonal: menopause, perimenopause, hormones, estrogen, hormonal, eczema (use dry/tight/papery/cracked instead).
- Anti-aging language: anti-aging, wrinkles, fine lines, aging skin, youthful.
- Skincare-industry words for the product itself: moisturizer, serum, skincare routine, regimen (use jar/stuff/balm).
- Influencer/spa language: self-care, nourishing, luxurious, spa, revolutionary, game-changing, clean beauty, toxin-free, all-natural, chemical-free.
- Medical claims: cures, treats, heals (use works/absorbs/feels different).
- Second-person diagnostic claims about the reader's body ("your skin is...") stay off-limits, first-person narrator claims about her OWN experience do not.

**Net instruction for this batch's copywriting:** state the mechanism and the comparison facts as confident, flat, verifiable claims. Keep every claim about a specific PERSON'S experience in first person. Never diagnose the reader's body directly. That combination lets the copy claim harder without moving the compliance line at all.

**Separate action item, not part of the ad copy itself:** the "Diana L., verified buyer" review quote used as the DEFAULT fallback text across the PDP, advertorial, and all 3 listicle sections is a placeholder, not a confirmed real review (the schema's own field label says "REAL customer review quote, never fabricate"). If no real quote has ever been entered in the theme customizer for these settings, that placeholder text may be live on the site right now, which is a real compliance/legal exposure independent of this batch. Recommend checking Judge.me for a real quote to replace it with before this batch launches, this affects every page in the funnel, not just B30.
