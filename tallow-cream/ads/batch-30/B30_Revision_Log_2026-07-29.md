## The finding that drove every change

**I read B17C1's live prompt and copy before touching anything, and the gap was immediate.**

| | B17C1 (converting, 5-11% CTR) | B30 (0-2.83% CTR) |
|---|---|---|
| Image subject | **One jar on an almost-empty counter. No people.** | C1: two faces in a FaceTime UI · C2: a social scene · C3: a woman at a mirror |
| Faces in frame | Zero | All three cells |
| Reference image needed | No | C1 required one, and fought bleed bugs across three rounds |

The CTRs track this exactly: C3 (woman + mirror) **0% on 418 impressions**, C1 (FaceTime faces) **0.82%**, C2 (social scene, at least a visual puzzle) **2.83%**. B17C1 with the object shot sustains 5-11%.

**Object-discovery — a single ambiguous object in an almost-empty quiet scene, no faces — is the only visual formula this account has ever validated for cheap CTR** (B17C1, B9C3, B28C1). B30 used it in zero of three cells. A 0% CTR on 418 impressions means the copy was never read; that is an image failure, not a copy failure.

## Changes

### All three cells: image prompts rebuilt as object-discovery
Modeled directly on B17C1's live prompt (the "one jar, almost-empty counter, faint ring-mark where a bottle used to sit" construction).

- **C1** — phone face-down beside one jar on a dark kitchen counter. The FaceTime concept stays in the copy where it works. **This also deletes the reference-image bug class entirely** — no face reference is needed, so it cannot bleed.
- **C2** — two coffee cups (one finished), a chair pushed back, one jar slid toward the empty seat. Keeps the "locate the tension" puzzle that made C2 the batch's best performer.
- **C3** — the old work badge and one jar on a dresser, badge photo angled away from the light so no face is legible. Keeps the diegetic object, removes the person.

⚠️ **C2 is the lowest-confidence revision** — we're changing the batch's *best* image. If preferred, hold C2's original and let it run as the in-batch control against two object shots. That's a legitimate call and arguably the more rigorous one.

### C3: hook rewritten (Pattern C violation)
Was: `"Not younger. Just like myself again."` — an abstract identity claim standing alone before any scene. That is the "opens on a bare thesis" pattern from `Killed_Batch_Failure_Analysis.md`, despite the cell file claiming it avoided it.

Now opens on the physical scene and lets the identity line land at the close, where B17C1 puts its own payoff. B17C1's first line is pure scene with zero claim: *"I was at Elaine's house for book club last month and I went upstairs to use the guest bathroom."*

### C3: age numbers restored — a rule we had been over-applying
Every B30 cell was written to satisfy "zero stated age numbers." **B17C1 states ages constantly** — *"I'm 54. Elaine is 56."* / *"Winnie is 76."* — and it's the proven converter.

The real policy risk is calling out **the reader's** age ("Are you over 50?"), not a narrator describing herself inside a story. Character ages are a specificity device the winner leans on heavily, and B30 had been denied it for no compliance benefit. C3 now opens with *"I'm 51. The badge photo is me at 42."*

**This applies account-wide, not just to B30.**

## Correction to a recommendation I made on 7/29

I told the founder that SA4's "stop carrying backup" angle had **zero evidence** and that one more empty search should retire it.

**Reading B17C1 shows that was wrong.** The backup-carrying behavior is the habit-cessation beat of the account's best-performing ad:

> *"I'd been buying those little travel-size lotion packets... keeping them in three places. My purse. My car console. My bottom desk drawer at work."*
> *"That was the thing I wanted back. Not the soft skin. The not-carrying."*

It has no *external* validation, but it is load-bearing in a converter. **It cannot be retired.** Correct status: same as the clock language — real and working in our copy, but ours rather than customer language, so don't cite it as voice-of-customer. `Brand_Mastery.md` and the B29C2 hold notice are updated accordingly.
