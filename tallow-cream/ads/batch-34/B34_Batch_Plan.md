## The decision this batch encodes

Two findings from 2026-08-04 drive every choice here.

**1. Attribution is the conversion lever, and it lives in the COPY.**
The same mechanism sentence appears in four ads. Twice attributed to a named
76-year-old beekeeper, relayed by a friend (B17C1 / B28C1) — 12 purchases.
Twice asserted by the advertiser in short-form (B20C3 507 impressions,
B22C1 495 impressions) — 0 purchases across ~1,002 impressions. Same claim,
different author, opposite result.

**2. The image and the copy are INDEPENDENT variables, and we have been
changing them together.** B17C1's image is a jar on a counter — an authorless
object with no text and no person — and it is the best-converting ad in
account history. The whiteboard and chyron ads did not fail because their
images lacked an author. They failed because their COPY was 60-100 words of
brand assertion. Every previous format test adopted a cheap visual and then
shrank the copy to match it. That is the mistake this batch does not repeat.

> **The image's job is to stop the scroll. The copy's job is to carry the
> attribution. Never trade one for the other.**

So: B17C1's copy architecture is held essentially fixed across all three
cells. Only the image class changes, to an authored surface with far higher
scroll-stop than a jar on a counter.

## Delivery context — read before judging results

B33 produced no usable creative read. 68-89% of its impressions landed in
**Facebook right column**, a placement that does not render primary text at
all, so CTR there was structurally impossible rather than low. Account-level
placement exclusions (right column, Audience Network, Marketplace) were set
2026-08-04, and ad-set placements narrowed to Facebook Feed, Facebook profile
feed, Instagram feed, Instagram profile feed.

**Expect headline CPM to RISE to $150-400 once cheap filler inventory is gone.
That is not a regression.** It is the price we were always paying for legible
impressions, finally shown undiluted.

**Judge on CPC at 300+ cumulative impressions or 3 days, whichever is later.**
Not CPM, not CTR, not first-day anything.

C1 and C2 share a format deliberately — but they are different doors. C1 enters

discovery vehicle. It is the cell most likely to fail and the only one that
teaches us anything new about doors.

## What is held constant (the converter spine)

Every cell carries all nine, in this order. These are not stylistic — they are
the elements both converters share and no volume non-converter has.

1. Named cast with continuity (Elaine, Winnie, Sylvia) — never "a friend"
2. **Mechanism quoted from a named person with nothing to gain** — the lever
3. Dated timeline (Day 1 / 4 / 9 / 14 / 21)
4. Doubt voiced before each result
5. Third parties noticing unsolicited
6. The objection in the reader's own words, answered by a peer
7. Permission shift placed BEFORE the mechanism
8. Habit cessation as the payoff
9. Product never named — no reveal, no tonal break

## Hard constraints

- **No offer terms in any image.** No price, guarantee, return window, shipping,
  discount or CTA — not as overlay, not as diegetic text. Per the rule added
  2026-08-04 after B33C3. Offer terms belong in the primary text only.
- **Real branding stays** on any real-world product in frame. Never write
  "unbranded" or "no logos" for real props.
- No added badges, graphic overlays or designed furniture.
- Compliance: no menopause, no anti-aging, no medical claims.
- Target 1,300-1,500 words, matched across all three cells so length is not an
  uncontrolled variable.
