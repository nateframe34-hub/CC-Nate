## Body Copy

If O'Keeffe's worked, I wouldn't be writing this.

I've been through the list. Every man who works with his hands has the same list. O'Keeffe's Working Hands. Aquaphor. Bag Balm. Vaseline. Whatever my wife left on the bathroom counter that I grabbed one night because I was out of ideas.

None of them held past lunch.

========

I'm a carpenter. Forty-six years old. Been building things since I was nineteen. Framing, finish work, decks, remodels. My hands are in sawdust, caulk, wood stain, and cold air for ten hours a day, five or six days a week. I wash them with whatever soap is on the job site, which is never the gentle kind.

Every winter since my early thirties my knuckles crack open. The splits happen at the joints — right where the skin bends when you grip a hammer. They open up in November and they don't close until April. Sometimes they bleed into my gloves. The cold water at the job-site wash station is the worst part. Feels like someone running a razor across the split.

I stopped complaining about it years ago. Just part of the job.

========

O'Keeffe's was the best of the bad options. It held maybe two hours on a good day. I'd put it on in the truck before I went on-site. By lunch it was gone. By three o'clock my knuckles were cracking again.

Aquaphor was worse. Greasy. Got on my tape measure, got on my pencils, got on the steering wheel. Felt like I'd dipped my hands in cooking oil. I used it for a week and threw it out.

Bag Balm — I respect Bag Balm. My dad used it. His dad used it on cattle. But it's the same problem. Too heavy. Sits on top. Doesn't go in. By the time you put your gloves on it's all over the inside of the glove and none of it is in your skin.

I tried my wife's Eucerin one night when I couldn't sleep because my right index finger had a split that kept catching on the sheets. It smelled like a department store and did nothing.

That was my rotation for about twelve years. Pain, cream, two hours, pain again.

========

A guy I know from church — Ron — does HVAC. His hands used to be as bad as mine. Worse, actually. The sheet metal tears your fingertips up on top of everything else.

I saw him one Sunday morning in March and his hands looked different. Not perfect. Just not cracked. In March. That's not normal for a guy who works with sheet metal.

I said, "What are you using?"

He reached into his coat pocket and pulled out a small jar.

He said, "My wife found this. Don't ask me what's in it. It works."

========

I looked at the jar later that afternoon. Four ingredients on the back. Tallow. Honey. Olive oil. Something called calendula.

Tallow. Beef fat.

My first thought was: you're kidding me.

My second thought was: my grandfather would not have been surprised by this at all.

========

I put it on that night before bed.

It didn't feel greasy. That was the first thing I noticed. It went in. Rubbed in like it was supposed to be there instead of sitting on top waiting to get on everything I touched.

Day 2 I put it on before work. I did a full morning of framing — saw, hammer, tape, chalk line, wash hands twice — and at lunch my knuckles were still closed.

That had not happened in twelve years.

Day 5 I stopped carrying the O'Keeffe's tube in my truck. Didn't need it.

Day 10 my wife asked me what happened to my hands. I held them out. She looked at them like she was seeing someone else's hands. She said, "Are those your hands?"

I told her about the jar. She took it upstairs. I had to order another one.

========

Here's the short version of why it works better than everything else I tried: the fat in this stuff is close to what your skin already makes. So it absorbs instead of coating. O'Keeffe's and Aquaphor sit on top. This goes in. That's why it holds through a shift.

I didn't need the science. I just needed my knuckles to stop splitting.

========

My wife uses it on her face now. My daughter puts it on her hands after volleyball practice — her palms get torn up from the floor. The jar that Ron's wife found for his HVAC hands is now the only thing in our house that everyone uses.

I bought it for cracked knuckles. Now it's the family jar.

========

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day guarantee — if your hands aren't better in two weeks, send it back.

Ron still uses it. Two other guys on our job site are on it now. One of them is a plumber whose fingertips used to crack so bad he'd wrap them in tape. He stopped wrapping them in January.

I don't care about skincare. I've never cared about skincare. I care about my hands not hurting at work. This fixed that.

If O'Keeffe's worked, you wouldn't be reading this either.
