## Body Copy

Beef fat. Four ingredients. Costs less than a case of beer. I know how that sounds.

========

My name's not important. I'm a rancher. Fifty-one. I run cattle on 600 acres in the panhandle with my brother and my oldest son. We're out in weather that would crack a rock — wind, cold, dry air, fence wire, feed buckets, everything your hands touch is either frozen or covered in something you don't want on a cut.

My hands have looked like a road map since I was about thirty. Splits across the knuckles. Cracks at the fingertips. The skin between my thumb and index finger goes raw every December and stays raw until March.

I've used Bag Balm since I was a kid. My dad kept it in the barn for the cattle and I'd put it on my hands when they got bad. It helped some. But it's thick and it's greasy and by the time you pull a glove on it's all in the glove and none of it's in your skin.

O'Keeffe's I tried for a couple of winters. Better than Bag Balm for grease. Worse for lasting. Two hours on a good day.

That was about it. I figured cracked hands were just what you got for working outside.

========

My wife's aunt — a woman named Doris, eighty-one years old, been on a ranch her whole life — came for Thanksgiving last year. She's the kind of woman who doesn't say much and when she does you listen.

She saw my hands when I was carving the turkey. She didn't say anything at dinner. After the dishes she came into the kitchen where I was drying my hands on a towel and she set a small jar on the counter.

She said, "Your grandfather used something like this. You should too."

I said, "What is it?"

She said, "Beef tallow. Honey. Olive oil. That's about it."

========

I almost laughed. Not at Doris — you don't laugh at Doris — but at the idea. Beef fat on my hands. Like I don't handle enough beef as it is.

But she was right about one thing. My grandfather did use something like that. He kept a tin of rendered fat in the mud room and he'd rub it into his hands after he washed up in the evenings. His hands at seventy looked better than mine do at fifty-one. I'd forgotten about that until she said it.

========

I used it that night. Thanksgiving night. Put it on after the dishes were done and everybody had gone to bed.

It didn't feel like Bag Balm. It didn't feel like anything I'd used before. It rubbed in. Not greasy, not heavy. Just went into the skin like the skin wanted it there.

Day 3 the cracks at my fingertips started closing. Those are the ones that catch on everything — rope, wire, the dog's collar. They'd been open since October.

Day 7 I did a full day of moving hay in twenty-degree wind. Gloves on, gloves off, hands in and out of cold water at the trough. End of the day I looked at my knuckles. Still closed.

I called Doris. I said, "Where'd you get this?"

She said, "I've been making my own for forty years. But the one I gave you is from a company. Four ingredients. Same idea, just done right."

Day 14 my brother noticed. He said, "Your hands look different."

I showed him the jar. He ordered one that night.

========

Here's the thing my grandfather knew that I'd forgotten: the fat that cattle carry is close to what human skin makes on its own. That's why it goes in instead of sitting on top. That's why Bag Balm half-works — it's got lanolin, which is animal fat from sheep. This is the same idea but cleaner, simpler, and it absorbs in a way Bag Balm never did.

My grandfather didn't know the science. He just knew it worked. Doris knows it works. Now I know it works.

========

My wife started using it on her face about a week after Thanksgiving. She said her skin felt better than anything she'd bought at a store. My son — he's twenty-three, works cattle with me — puts it on his hands after the evening wash-up. Same jar. Same four ingredients.

The jar Doris gave me lasted about six weeks. I ordered two more. One for the mud room. One for the truck.

========

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day guarantee.

Beef fat. Honey. Olive oil. A flower extract. That's the whole list. Your grandfather would've recognized every one of them.

My hands don't crack anymore. My brother's hands don't crack anymore. My son's hands look better at twenty-three than mine did at thirty.

Doris is eighty-one. Her hands look like she's sixty. She's been doing this her whole life and she finally told the rest of us.

I'm not selling you skincare. I'm telling you what works. If it doesn't work for you, send it back.
