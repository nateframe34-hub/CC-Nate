# B11C1 — Cracked

**Sub-Avatar:** The Working Hands Man (Sub-Avatar 11)
**Angle:** Everything Failed — hands that crack every winter, coworker hands him a jar, it works
**Character type:** Pragmatic Skeptic Convert — he didn't care about skincare, he cared about hands that hurt
**Hook format:** Direct statement + specific damage + duration + open loop (4 mechanisms)

## Body Copy

My knuckles have been splitting open every winter since I was twenty-five.

That's seventeen winters.

========

I'm a welder. Forty-two years old. I've worked in a shop for most of my adult life — metal, heat, chemical flux, grinding dust. My hands get punished. That's part of the job and I've never lost sleep over it.

But every November my knuckles crack open and they don't close back up until March. The cold makes it worse. Washing my hands every couple of hours makes it worse. Gripping a tool with open knuckles makes it worse. You stop noticing the pain eventually. You just know not to make a fist when it's below forty outside.

I've tried everything with a label on it.

O'Keeffe's Working Hands. Maybe two hours of relief, then it's gone. Aquaphor. Too greasy — got on my phone, got on my tools, got on everything. Bag Balm. My grandfather used it on his cows' udders in the winter. I tried it for three weeks. Same grease problem. Vaseline. A joke. My wife's hand cream from the bathroom — I don't even remember the name, it smelled like something that shouldn't be on a man's hands, and it lasted twenty minutes.

I'd accepted that cracked knuckles were just a winter tax for what I do.

========

There's a guy on our crew named Dave. Carpenter, works on the same job site I've been on since September. Dave's hands were worse than mine. Worse than anyone's I'd seen. He had splits on three knuckles that kept opening up every time he bent his fingers all the way.

One morning in January I noticed his knuckles looked different.

They were closed up. Not scarred over — just healed. Like it was June.

I asked him what happened.

He reached into his truck and pulled out a small amber glass jar. He handed it to me. He said, "Try this tonight."

That was the whole pitch. No explanation. Just: try this tonight.

========

I looked at the jar.

Four ingredients. One of them was beef tallow.

Beef fat. On my hands.

I looked at Dave.

He said, "I know."

========

I'm not a person who researches things. I either try something or I don't. I tried it.

That night I put it on before bed. It's not greasy the way Aquaphor is — it goes in. Rubs in like you'd expect something to absorb instead of just sitting on top. That was the first difference.

Day 3, I noticed I hadn't woken up with knuckles splitting in the morning. That's what happens in winter — you sleep with your hands curled up and you unfold them and they crack open again. Three days in, no morning split.

Day 7 I did a full shift — six hours of grinding and flux and washing my hands probably fifteen times — and my knuckles were still closed at the end of it. That hadn't happened in November, December, January, or any January before that I could remember.

Day 14 my wife grabbed my hand at the dinner table and looked at it. She said, "What did you do?"

I told her. She gave me a look I recognized from other times I've come home with something she thinks is weird. Then she grabbed the jar.

========

Here's the part I didn't think about beforehand: the reason it works longer than O'Keeffe's or Aquaphor is that those things sit on top of your skin. They don't go in. The fat in this stuff is close to what your skin makes on its own — that's why it absorbs instead of coating. I didn't need a science lesson. I just needed it to work.

It worked. That's the whole story.

========

Two weeks after that, my wife started putting it on her face at night. She has dry skin in the winter too — different problem than mine, but the same product fixed it. My daughter has these dry patches on her elbows every year. My wife put a little on them before bed for a few nights. They cleared up.

I bought it for my cracked hands. Now it's the jar in our house that everybody uses.

I had to order a second one because my wife claimed the first.

========

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. If it doesn't fix your hands, send it back — they refund you, no questions.

My buddy Dave fixed his hands. I fixed mine.

If yours are cracking, try it.

Individual results may vary. But Dave's results didn't vary much, and mine didn't vary much, and I've sent the link to two other guys on the site who had the same problem.

Four ingredients. Costs less than a case of beer.

Link is below.
