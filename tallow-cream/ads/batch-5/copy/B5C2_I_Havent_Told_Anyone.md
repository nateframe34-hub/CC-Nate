## Body Copy

I've been trying to figure out how to say this for almost six weeks.

I kept starting. I kept stopping. I kept thinking I'd sound ridiculous if I said it out loud.

So I'm just going to say it.

At Thanksgiving, my sister-in-law Rachel told me something I haven't been able to stop thinking about. And what I'm about to tell you is a little embarrassing. But I think it matters.

========

We were in the kitchen doing dishes. Just the two of us. Everyone else was in the living room watching football.

Rachel is married to my brother. She lives in Denver. I see her twice a year. She's kind. She doesn't say things she doesn't mean.

She was drying a plate and she looked over at me and said "Hey. Are you taking care of yourself?"

I said "What do you mean?"

She said "I don't know. You just look like you could use a rest."

I'd been sleeping fine. I'd had a normal week. I wasn't stressed. I told her I was okay.

She nodded and went back to drying plates.

And I stood there with my hands in the sink thinking about that sentence for the rest of the night.

========

Here's why it stuck.

Rachel wasn't the first person to say something like that. She was the third.

Three weeks before Thanksgiving, a woman at my office had leaned over my desk and said "long week?" I'd had a normal week.

Two days before that, my mother-in-law Margaret had called about Christmas plans and in the middle of the conversation said "you sound tired, honey." I wasn't tired.

Three people in three weeks. And none of them were being mean. That's the part that made it worse. They were concerned. They meant it.

Something was showing on my face that I couldn't see from the inside.

========

I'm 51. And sometime around 48, my morning routine stopped doing what it used to do.

I'd put on moisturizer and feel my face go tight again before I got to the car. I'd do my makeup at 7am and check it at 11am and it would be settling into places I didn't want it to settle. I'd reapply lotion at lunch. Then again at 3pm. Then give up.

I wasn't mad about it. I'd gotten past mad a long time ago. I was just quiet about it. I stopped bringing it up to Kate.

Kate is my best friend. Twenty years. We tell each other everything. But I hadn't told her about this because I didn't know how to say "my face is bothering me" without sounding vain.

That's the embarrassing part. It's not supposed to matter this much.

But it does.

========

The week after Thanksgiving I pulled out photos from my college reunion ten years ago. I looked good in those photos. Not young. Just — like myself. Like the outside matched the inside.

I put the photos back in the drawer and sat at the kitchen table for a while.

I had a book club meeting at Tina's house that Thursday.

Tina is the woman in our group who brings a spreadsheet to everything. She fact-checks recipes. She doesn't fall for anything. If Tina told me the sky was blue I'd still look up to make sure.

We were cleaning up after everyone left and she said something I almost missed.

"I switched what I use on my face about two months ago. Have you ever heard of tallow?"

I said "Like beef tallow?"

She said "I know. I made the same face. But it works and I can't explain it any other way."

========

I went home that night and looked it up.

I want you to read this part slowly because it changed the way I think about everything I've been putting on my face for ten years.

Skin makes its own oil. It always has. It's the reason a 25-year-old doesn't think about moisturizer. It's the reason your grandma used a bar of Dove and never worried about it.

After about 40, skin makes a lot less of that oil. The drop is fast. Most women don't connect what's happening on their face to what changed underneath.

And here's the part that made me close my laptop and stare at the ceiling.

Almost every cream I'd been buying was made of water. Water and glycerin and a list of things I can't pronounce. Water doesn't replace oil. It sits on top for an hour and then it's gone.

I'd been using the wrong thing for ten years. Nobody told me. Not my dermatologist. Not the woman at Sephora. Not a single label on a single bottle.

========

Now here's the part where I tell you what Tina was using.

A whipped tallow and honey balm. Four ingredients on the back of the jar. Tallow from grass-fed cows. Manuka honey. Olive oil. A flower extract called calendula.

That's the whole list.

The reason it works — and I made the same face you're making — is that tallow is almost the same fat skin makes on its own. Same kind of oil. Same structure.

So when you put it on, it doesn't sit on top like a cream. It absorbs in. The body recognizes it the way a lock recognizes a key.

I ordered it that night. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

========

Week 1 after it arrived, I didn't notice much. I put it on at night. It melted into my hand like warm butter. It smelled like honey.

Week 2, something shifted. I couldn't name it yet. My face just felt different under my hand in the morning. Less tight. Less like something I had to fix before I left the house.

Week 3, I was holding my coffee mug close to my face the way I do on cold mornings. My hand brushed my cheek. And I stopped. Because my cheek felt like a cheek. Not like something stretched over a frame.

I hadn't felt that in three years.

========

Here's what I wasn't expecting.

I'd been putting on makeup before I answered the front door. Every time. The UPS guy. The neighbor dropping off mail. Girl scouts selling cookies. I'd check myself in the hall mirror and add something before I opened the door.

One morning around week 3 the doorbell rang and I walked straight to it. I opened the door in a t-shirt and bare face and handed the UPS driver a signature and closed the door and then stopped.

I hadn't checked. I hadn't thought about it. I just opened the door.

That was the moment I knew something had changed. Not on my face. In my head.

========

I FaceTimed Kate last Sunday.

We were twenty minutes into talking about her daughter's wedding plans and she stopped mid-sentence.

"Okay. What are you doing?"

"What do you mean?"

"Your face. Something is different. Tell me right now."

I told her everything. Tina. The balm. The oil thing. The four ingredients. Rachel's comment at Thanksgiving that started the whole thing.

She was quiet for a second. Then she said "Why didn't you tell me sooner?"

I said "Because I didn't know how to say it without sounding ridiculous."

She said "You sound ridiculous for NOT telling me. Order me one right now."

I ordered it for her while we were still on the phone.

========

Rachel is coming to visit in February. I keep thinking about what she's going to say when she sees me.

Tina told three women at book club. Margaret asked me what I was using after Christmas dinner. My neighbor saw the jar on my counter when she came over for coffee and asked about it.

I didn't plan any of that. I didn't tell most of them. They asked.

========

This is the thing I've been trying to figure out how to say.

It's not about the jar. It's not about four ingredients or the money I've saved or the ten bottles I threw away.

It's about the fact that for three years I was quietly carrying something that I thought was too small to talk about. And it wasn't small. It was the first thing I thought about every morning and the last thing I checked every night.

And now I don't check.

That's what I wanted to tell you. That's what took me six weeks.

========

Four ingredients. $49.99. 60-day money-back guarantee. Free shipping. Link is below if you want it.

If it doesn't work for you, send it back. You get every dollar back.

But if you're the person I was thinking about when I started writing this — the one who's been carrying something quiet and hasn't told her Kate yet — this is me telling you.

You're not ridiculous for caring about this. And you don't have to figure it out alone.

I didn't.
