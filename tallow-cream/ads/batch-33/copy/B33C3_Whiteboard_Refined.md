## Where this concept came from

**Our own performance data.** B19C2 ran **$74.83 CPM** — against $318-531 for the jar-on-a-surface natives — and converted at **$11.35 CPA / 4.40x ROAS**, still the best unit economics in the account's history. The whiteboard format is the single cheapest delivery asset we own, and B32C1 posted **$39.26 CPM** on 7/30 running the same family.

**What actually killed B19C2 was not the board.** Its entire primary text is 58 words:

> *"Most creams stop working by noon because water evaporates. That's not the skin. That's physics. Tallow is close enough to what skin makes that your skin lets it in instead of sitting on top. It doesn't evaporate. It stays. Four ingredients. $49.99. 60-day money-back guarantee. Free shipping."*

It stopped the scroll at 2.04% CTR and then gave nobody a reason to act. **Every cheap-CPM cell in the account's history was also a short-copy cell.** The format has never once been tested with long-form copy underneath it.

## Founder direction on the competitor's version

> *"Don't love this for the format... It seems like there would be too much going on in it at once. The consumer should be able to take a quick glance and understand the creative, a refined version of B19C2 with text would probably be more likely to."*

Correct, and the reason is structural: the competitor's brick-wall board is carrying a **body oil with five separate benefits** (firms, nourishes, smooths, hydrates four body zones, supports elasticity, adds glow). A benefit stack needs a list. **Our mechanism is one idea** — fat absorbs, water leaves — and stacking a cost tally and testimonial quotes on top of it buys clutter, not comprehension.

So this cell goes the other way. B19C2's original prompt specified **fourteen distinct drawn elements per column** (H₂O circles, five evaporation arrows, a sun icon, a clock, "TIGHT" in red, a frowning face, two sub-lines with X marks, and more). That is already too much at feed size. **This version cuts it to five per side and doubles the stroke weight.**

## What it uniquely tests

**Was B19C2's 2.04% CTR the board, or the 58 words?** This is the cleanest question in the batch and the one with the highest expected value, because the format's cheap delivery is already proven and only the reason-to-act is missing.

## Note on the product

**Deliberately not in frame.** The governing "if the story says she bought it, SHOW it" rule in `PRODUCT_IMAGE_REFERENCE.md` applies to native story scenes. This is a mechanism explainer, and B19C2 earned its $74 CPM and $11.35 CPA with no product visible. Adding a hand holding a jar is exactly the clutter the founder flagged.

## Hook candidates (NOT YET PICKED — founder selects at step 3)

A board needs a reason to have been written on. All four keep the peer-relay chain that appears in 4 of 4 converters.

1. *"My sister-in-law drew this on the board in her kitchen because I would not stop arguing with her about it."* ⭐
2. *"She drew two columns and asked me which one I thought I had been buying for eleven years."*
3. *"I made her explain it twice. The second time she got up and drew it."*
4. *"It took her about forty seconds and a marker to explain what nobody had explained to me in a decade."*

> #1 and #3 both put the reader in the skeptic's seat before the explanation lands, which is the "I made the face you're making" beat present in 4 of 4 converters.
