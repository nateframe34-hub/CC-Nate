## Where this concept came from

Two independent sources:

1. **Founder-supplied competitor swipe, ad 4 of 5** ("40 Plus & Fabulous") — a flat vector illustration of a woman touching her neck, flat teal background, a large white speech bubble containing bold black capitals and a red call-to-read line beneath, with a product tube drawn into the frame. **Running 185 days.**
2. **Origins native swipe, Camouflage Ad #1** (`origins-native-swipe/42.png`) — a vintage medical illustration used as the image of a long-form native. Drawn, not photographed.

## Why this is the only cell that can carry Angle 2

Angle 2 is a **loop**: you buy something, it causes a problem, you buy something for that problem, which causes another. **A loop cannot be photographed.** A photo captures one moment; the whole argument here is accumulation over time. Drawing is the only medium in this batch that can put a process in a single frame.

The angle and the format were assigned together for that reason, not by coincidence. This is also why this cell is the batch's only departure from Angle 1.

## Why it should earn cheap delivery

Flat vector is instantly legible at thumbnail — hard edges, flat fills, no depth-of-field mush, no lighting to resolve. It is also unmistakably not a product photograph, which is the classifier signal we want.

It carries a compliance advantage the photographic cells cannot: **a drawing of dry, tired-looking skin is not a before/after photograph of a real person's results.**

## The execution requirement — ugly is mandatory, not tolerated

The competitor's illustration works *because* it is crude: flat unmodulated colour, an unglamorous face, hand-lettered-looking caps, no gradients, no polish. **The moment this looks professionally illustrated it becomes an ad again and the cheap delivery evaporates.** Reject any generation that looks like premium editorial illustration, Corporate Memphis, or a designed brand asset.

## What it uniquely tests

**Does a non-photographic medium change delivery cost?** Nothing in the account's history has ever run as an illustration.

```
A flat vector illustration in a simple, slightly crude infographic style, filling the entire frame. Solid unmodulated background colour: a muted dusty teal (#5E9A9E), completely flat with no gradient, no texture, no vignette. The whole image uses a limited flat palette and hard edges throughout — no soft shading, no blur, no depth of field, no drop shadows, no glossy highlights, no 3D rendering.

THE FIGURE, positioned in the lower-left two thirds of the frame, drawn at roughly three-quarter length from the waist up, facing the viewer and turned very slightly to her right:
A woman who reads as late forties. Simple flat-shaded rendering: skin in a flat warm beige with ONE single darker beige tone used sparingly for the shadow under the jaw and along one side of the nose, nothing more. Hair is medium-length, mid-brown with a few flat grey strands at the temple, drawn as simple solid shapes with no strand detail. She wears a plain dusty-rose top rendered as a single flat colour with one darker fold line.

Her expression is the important part: tired and faintly exasperated, NOT smiling, NOT distressed, NOT glamorous. Eyebrows slightly drawn together, mouth a flat neutral line turned marginally down at one corner, eyes drawn simply and looking directly out at the viewer. Simple flat line-drawn detail under her eyes and two short lines at the outer corner of each eye, drawn in the single darker beige tone — enough to read as tired, not enough to read as a rendering of a condition. Her skin is drawn with a few small flat pale-beige irregular patches on the cheek indicating dryness and flaking.

One of her hands is raised to her cheek, fingers flat and simply drawn, resting against the side of her face — the universal gesture of someone assessing their own skin in a mirror.

NOT photorealistic. NOT 3D rendered. NOT a polished editorial illustration. NOT Corporate Memphis style with oversized limbs or abstract shapes. NO arrows, badges, starbursts, price flashes, star ratings or percentages. NO wounds, lesions, redness patches or anything reading as a medical condition — the skin is DRY and TIRED only. NO cheerful or smiling expression. NO beauty-campaign styling. NO watermark. NO additional text anywhere outside the speech bubble.
```

## QC gate — reject and regenerate if any of these are true

- [ ] It looks professionally illustrated, premium, or like a brand asset
- [ ] Any gradient, soft shading, texture or drop shadow appears
- [ ] Fewer than twelve containers on the counter, or they are neatly spaced
- [ ] Any text is misspelled, badly kerned, or too small to read on a phone
- [ ] The red line is larger than or equal to the black block
- [ ] She is smiling, or looks distressed rather than tired and exasperated
- [ ] Her skin reads as a medical condition rather than dry and tired
- [ ] Any legible brand name appears on a container

## Hook candidates (NOT YET PICKED — founder selects at step 3)

Angle 2 hooks. The speech bubble already carries the chain idea, so the copy's opening line should not simply restate it — it should give the chain a scene.

1. *"I worked out that four of the things on my counter existed only to fix what the other things were doing."* ⭐
2. *"The more I used, the worse it got. I assumed that meant I hadn't found the right thing to add yet."*
3. *"I fixed my skin by taking things away. It took me four years to try that."*
4. *"I was using eleven things and my skin had never been worse, and nobody ever suggested those two facts were connected."*

> #1 gives the loop a concrete count and keeps the narrator working it out herself, which is the accidental-discovery move present in 4 of 4 converters.
