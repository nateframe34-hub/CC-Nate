## Where this concept came from

**Founder concept**, refining an earlier order-history-screenshot idea I proposed that had no swipe behind it:

> *"What we could do is something like the image I sent, have sticky notes on every product listing the price, then we can talk in the copy about how we spend sooooo much but then we found something that did it all for way cheaper... we can put like a sticky note under each product listing it's price it would have to be a much different scene then the one I provided but you get what I am saying."*

Founder-supplied reference: products on a white fluffy surface with sticky-note labels beside each.

**Why this is better than what I proposed.** My version was a screenshot of one number. This repeats the number across the frame so the total *accumulates as the eye travels*, and it needs no fabricated document. It is diegetic text in its cleanest form — handwriting on a real object inside the scene, not an overlay.

It also pulls in SA2's most concrete verbatim, which we have never once put into an image: *"$860 in 6 months," "$165 + tax down the drain."* The dollar language is the most specific thing in the entire research corpus for this avatar.

## Truthfulness framing — matters for the copy

The prices on the notes are **the narrator's own record of what she personally paid.** That is a first-person account, not a market survey or a competitor price comparison. The copy must carry it that way. Do not write copy that presents these as current retail prices for named brands.

Per the branding principle in `Tallow_Ad_Creative_Principles.md` (real branding on real props stays and is never stripped), this prompt does not instruct the generator to remove or hide labels. It simply does not specify any brand, and the shooting distance means package text is present but not the focus.

## Why it should earn cheap delivery

An overhead phone snap of a cluttered table has no ad signature — no hero product, no negative space, no lighting design. The sticky notes are the scroll-stop: a viewer's eye lands on a handwritten dollar figure, then finds another, then another, and the arithmetic starts running before a single word of copy is read.

## What it uniquely tests

**Can a number out-stop a scene?** It is also the only cell in the batch where our product appears, which makes it the batch's test of whether product presence hurts a native at all.

## HOOK — SELECTED (founder, 2026-07-31), REWRITTEN to solve the jar problem

> **"My daughter thought I was exaggerating.**
> **So I put all of it on the table and wrote what I paid on each one.**
> **Then I put the only thing I still use down at the other end."**

**Founder picked candidate 3** (*"My daughter did not believe me, so I got the sticky notes out"*) and correctly flagged the problem with it:

> *"gives a explanation but I think we need to improve it a bit, it doesn't explain why the tallow is there we don't want to go in depth about it but if we don't address it it'll instantly read ad and people will scroll."*

**This is the sharpest catch in the batch.** Our jar is sitting in the frame with a price on it. The original hook explains the pile and the sticky notes but leaves the jar unaccounted for, so the reader resolves the image as an ad before reaching line two. Every other cell can leave its image partly unexplained; this one cannot, because the unexplained element is the product.

**How the rewrite fixes it.** The jar's presence becomes the *reason the count happened*. The daughter's disbelief, the audit, and the lone jar are one causal chain, so nothing in the frame is unaccounted for. Three short sentences, one job each: the pile, the notes, the jar.

**What it deliberately does not do.** It never names the jar, never says what it is, and never claims anything about it. "The only thing I still use" is the whole reveal, and it plants the loop the body copy resolves: what is it, and why is it the only one? Per the hook standard, the reader must not be able to work out what is being sold from line one.

Silent-reading structure: three sentences, none over 16 words, each on its own line. No verbal tics.

### Variants considered and rejected

- *"I did not set out to prove anything. My daughter said I was exaggerating about what I had spent, and forty minutes later there was a table covered in sticky notes and one jar sitting off on its own."* — good accidental-discovery framing, but one long sentence fights the silent-reading rhythm.
- *"My daughter did not believe me, so I got the sticky notes out. The one down at the bottom on its own is what replaced all the rest of it."* — explains the jar too completely. Hands over the answer in line one and closes the loop before the ad starts.

## All candidates considered

The image already delivers the number, so the copy should not open by restating it flatly.

1. *"I only got the sticky notes out because my daughter did not believe me."* ⭐
2. *"I pulled everything out onto the kitchen table to throw it away and then I started writing the prices down instead."*
3. *"My husband walked past the table, stopped, walked back, and said the number out loud."* ⭐
4. *"Eight hundred and sixty dollars. That is what was sitting on my bathroom shelf not working."*

> #1 and #3 both put the count in someone else's mouth, keeping the peer-relay pattern. #4 is the verbatim figure but opens on a claim, which is the weaker shape.
