# B10C1 — Two Lives

**Sub-Avatar:** The Homesteader Mom (Sub-Avatar 6)
**Angle:** Kitchen/Bathroom Gap — the coherence-dissonance confession. She reads the ingredient list on her night cream the same way she reads the label on everything she eats, and realizes she's been living two lives.
**Character type:** Peer + Skeptic Convert (she's a partial skeptic of face-application, not of tallow)
**Hook format:** Confession + scene + ingredient-count stakes + open loop (4 mechanisms)
**Awareness level:** Solution Aware (she's already philosophically bought in on tallow — she needs brand legitimacy, not mechanism education)

## Body Copy

I was sitting at my kitchen table on a Sunday night in November. The sourdough starter was bubbling on the counter. The slow cooker had a chuck roast going for the next day. And I was holding my night cream under the kitchen light, reading the ingredient list the same way I read labels on everything we eat.

I got to ingredient twenty-three and stopped. I couldn't pronounce seven of them.

The starter bubbled. The roast simmered. I put the cream back on the counter.

Something was off about this.

========

I'm 34. My husband and I have been on our farm for six years. Two kids. Three hens. A slow homestead cycle we've been tightening every year. We got a chest freezer two winters ago. A quarter-share cow from a neighbor last spring. I've been rendering my own tallow for biscuits since February.

I read every ingredient on everything we eat. I've been doing that for about four years. I put things back on the shelf at Walmart because of what's in them. I drove forty-five minutes to the farm we buy eggs from because I wanted to see the pasture.

And my night cream had thirty-one ingredients. Seven of them I couldn't pronounce.

I had been cleaning up one side of my house and ignoring the other.

========

My sister Grace called me that Tuesday.

She has a small farm three hours south of us — goats, a few beef cattle, a cutting garden she sells from. She's four years older than me. She had switched her entire bathroom over to a tallow balm nine months earlier and she'd been meaning to tell me.

I told her what had happened at the kitchen table Sunday night.

She said, "I know you've been rendering tallow for biscuits. Did you know you can use it on your face?"

I said I hadn't really thought about it.

She said, "That's the whole problem. Think about it."

========

The next weekend I drove down to help her with her fall tomato canning. I stayed Saturday night.

On the counter in her guest bathroom was one amber glass jar with a simple label. Just one. Nothing else.

I picked it up and turned it over. Four ingredients on the back. Tallow. Manuka honey. Olive oil. Calendula.

She was brushing her teeth behind me. She saw me looking at it. She said, "I know. Just try it. The whole jar. If it doesn't do anything for you you send it back and they refund you."

I said, "Beef fat. On my face."

She laughed. "I made the same face. It's not greasy. It doesn't smell."

========

I was worried about the smell. She had read my mind.

She said, "They render it slowly, over two days, on low heat. That's what takes the beef smell out. What you smell in that jar is the olive oil and the honey. Nothing else."

I smelled it. She was right.

She also said something that stayed with me longer than I thought it would. She said, "The oil your skin makes on its own is almost the same structure as this fat. That's why it absorbs in instead of sitting on top. It's not a cream. It's an oil going back where oil is supposed to be."

I understood that about fats in my cooking already. I just hadn't made the jump to skincare.

I ordered one from her driveway before I left the next afternoon. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

========

Day 1 I put it on before bed. It melted in my palm like warmed beeswax. I pressed it into my cheeks, my jaw, my forehead. Thirty seconds total.

Day 4 I noticed I hadn't reached for my hand cream once. My hands are in water and flour and dirt all day. They had been raw through the whole winter — knuckles splitting, the skin around my nails cracking every morning. By Day 4 I'd been using the tallow balm on my face at night, and the little extra I had on my fingers I'd rubbed into my knuckles before bed. My knuckles healed in four days. I'd been fighting them since October.

Day 9 my friend Tess from our homeschool co-op stopped me at the library on a Wednesday. Our pod meets there once a week for the shared science lesson. She sat down next to me and said, "Hey. You look different. Are you sleeping more?"

I wasn't sleeping more. I told her no.

She said, "Something then. You look rested."

I didn't tell her what it was. I wasn't sure yet.

Day 14 I put on foundation at 8am for a church potluck. Three and a half hours later — when the potluck ended — I looked in the visor mirror on the drive home. It hadn't settled into the creases around my mouth. It hadn't cracked around my nose. My makeup looked the way it did when I was twenty-six.

Day 21 I was packing away the Christmas tins in the pantry and walked past my bathroom counter. I stopped. I used to have eight things on it. I was down to two. Cleanser. The jar.

Everything else had disappeared over three weeks. I hadn't thrown them out on a specific day. I'd just stopped reaching for them and forgotten they were there.

========

Here's the part I didn't see coming.

My husband's hands split open the coldest weekend of January. He had been outside moving hay bales and the wind off the pond cut his knuckles apart. He asked me if I had anything that worked. I gave him my jar.

Four days later his knuckles were closed up. He asked me where I got it.

My daughter is six. Every January she gets dry patches on her cheeks that go red and rough. The pediatrician gave us a steroid cream for them last year that I used for three nights and then stopped using. This January I put a tiny dot of the tallow balm on each patch before bed for five nights. By the sixth morning they were gone. I didn't get the prescription cream out of the drawer at all.

========

Now it's the only skincare in our house. One jar. Four ingredients. My husband's hands. My daughter's cheeks. My face.

Everyone is using the same thing. The jar is on the counter next to the cleanser and that's the whole bathroom.

========

I think about that Sunday night at the kitchen table a lot. The sourdough starter bubbling. The chuck roast going. The ingredient list on the cream.

My kitchen had been doing one job for four years. My bathroom is finally doing the same one now.

I feel like I live in one house instead of two.

========

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If it doesn't do what I've told you it does, you email them and they refund you. No questions.

Link is below if you want it.

Grace is still on it. Tess from co-op ordered one after the library Wednesday. Three other moms in our church have asked me for the name in the last month. It's moving through my community the way these things move — one jar in one hand at a time.

It's one jar. It's four ingredients. It's the same standard I've been holding everything else in my house to for four years.

Now everything finally matches.
