# B10C2 — For The Kids First

## Body Copy

Every January since Claire was four years old, the skin on her forearms goes rough and red.

She's eight now. Five winters of the same thing. The inside creases of her elbows and the skin along her forearms — dry patches that go scratchy and raw no matter what we do. This January was the worst yet. By the second week of January the inner crooks of both elbows had cracked open. At night she'd scratch in her sleep. She'd come to breakfast with red marks on her arms where she'd been at it.

We'd seen the pediatrician twice. Steroid cream for eleven days. It wasn't holding.

I was out of ideas.

========

I drove Claire to homeschool co-op on a Tuesday morning in late January. Her co-op meets in a church fellowship hall two miles from our house. Twenty-three kids. Eleven families.

Rachel was in the parking lot when I pulled in. She homesteads on five acres two miles from the church. Four kids. Her oldest is fourteen. We've been doing co-op together for three years.

She asked how I was doing while I was getting Claire out of her car seat. I started to say fine, and then I didn't.

I told her about Claire's arms.

We ended up sitting in my front seat with the door open. Claire had run in. I was telling Rachel about the pediatrician, the steroid cream, the night-scratching. Rachel listened. She didn't interrupt.

Then she said, "Let me show you something."

She walked to her truck and came back with an amber glass jar.

========

She handed it to me and I turned it over. Four ingredients on the back. Tallow. Manuka honey. Olive oil. Calendula.

She said, "Start with this. Before bed, a little dab on the rough spots. Do it for four nights."

I looked at the jar. I looked at her.

I said, "Beef fat. On my face."

She smiled. "I made the same face. It's not greasy. It doesn't smell."

The smell question was already forming. She answered it before I asked.

She said, "They render it slowly, over two days. What you smell in the jar is the honey and the olive oil. Nothing beefy."

I smelled it. She was right.

========

She said one more thing before I went inside.

She said, "The oil your skin makes on its own is almost the same structure as this fat. That's why it absorbs in instead of sitting on top. It's not putting something foreign on the skin. It's putting back something familiar."

I understood that. I've rendered tallow myself, for cooking. I knew what the fat was. I just hadn't made the jump.

I thanked her. I took the jar home.

========

That night I put a small amount on Claire's worst inner-elbow patches instead of the steroid cream.

She was sitting on the edge of the tub while I put it on. She said, "That feels different. It's not sticky."

I told her we were trying something new for four nights.

Saturday morning — four days later — the redness on both forearms was visibly pale. Not gone, but pale. The cracking inside her right elbow, which had been the worst, had started to close.

Tuesday she came downstairs in a short-sleeved shirt for the first time since October. She grabbed a banana off the counter and went outside to do her morning chores without saying anything about her arms.

I stood in the kitchen for a minute after she went out.

========

I ordered my own jar that same morning.

I didn't think about it for very long. Four ingredients. I could read all four. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

I ordered a second jar for the bathroom when the first one arrived.

========

Day 3 I noticed something. My face felt softer underneath in the morning — not from a cream sitting on top but from something the skin was doing on its own. That unfussed softness you can't get from a night product. The kind that's there when you wake up, not when you apply something.

Day 7 the second jar arrived. It went in the bathroom cabinet.

Day 11 I was at the feed store getting oats. A neighbor of mine, woman named Diane, stopped me in the aisle. Diane is in her mid-sixties. She's been farming longer than I've been alive. She said, "Did you do something? You look less tired. You look more rested."

I hadn't done anything except this.

Day 16 I went to reach for my usual night cream before bed. I stopped. I'd had the same amount in the tube for three weeks. I had just stopped reaching for it. I put it back and used the jar instead.

Week 4 I looked at the bathroom shelf. Cleanser. The jar. That was it. I'd thrown out five things. Two in the trash, three to the church donation bin. I hadn't done it on purpose. I'd just stopped using them and then one morning moved them out.

========

Here's what I didn't see coming.

My husband fenced in the rain for a weekend in March. Two days outside in wet cold, pulling wire, resetting posts. His knuckles split open the way they do — that deep cracking at the joints that takes weeks to close in a normal year.

I gave him my jar.

Four nights later his knuckles had closed up. He came in from the barn Saturday morning and held his hands out. He said, "Where'd you get this?"

I told him. Rachel. The co-op parking lot. January.

He nodded like that made sense.

========

Owen is two. When he comes in from the car seat and his cheeks are pink from the cold, I put a small dot under his jaw before his afternoon nap. There's a diaper-bag-sized jar now that travels with us.

Claire, eight, hasn't pulled her sleeves down over her hands since the second week of February. Her arms look like a regular eight-year-old who plays outside. Small bruise from the barn door last week. Tiny scrape from her bike. Nothing like January.

She asked recently if she could use the jar on her feet. I ordered her her own. It sits on her dresser.

Four jars in the house now. Bathroom counter. Claire's dresser. Mudroom sink. Diaper bag.

========

I almost didn't tell Rachel what was happening with Claire.

I had errands that morning. I had three other things I was supposed to do before noon. When I pulled into the co-op parking lot and saw her I almost just said we're fine and kept moving.

Everything that has happened in our bathroom over the last three months happened because I stopped for three minutes in a parking lot and told the truth to a friend. She had the thing I needed. She gave it to me without making me feel like I was being sold to.

Now I carry a jar in my glove compartment in case I get to have the same conversation with somebody else.

========

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. Link is below.

Rachel is still on it. Three more women from our co-op started after Rachel told them. Two moms from Claire's Sunday school group texted me this month asking for the name.

It is the only skincare in our house now. One jar. Four ingredients. My eight-year-old's arms. My two-year-old's cheeks. My husband's hands. My face. Four of us. Four ingredients.

It turns out we didn't need more than that.
