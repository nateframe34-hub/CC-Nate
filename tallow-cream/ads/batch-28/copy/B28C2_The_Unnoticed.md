## Body Copy

I can tell you the last time my husband said something about how I looked. Not guess. Tell you.

It was a Tuesday in October, almost two years ago. I'd gotten a haircut at lunch. Mark looked up when I walked in the door and said, "Oh, that's nice," and went back to his phone.

That's the last one. I checked my own memory for a long time before I let myself believe that was really it.

-------------

Mark and I have been married nineteen years. We're not having problems, if that's the question. He still reaches for my hand in the car. He still tells me about his day.

He just stopped noticing anything about how I look, somewhere around that October, and I stopped expecting him to.

I didn't decide to stop expecting it. That's not how it works. You just quietly lower the number of times a day you think someone might look up. And then one day you realize the number is zero, and you can't remember when it got there.

-------------

I caught myself doing the thing where you brace before you catch your reflection in a store window. Not dramatic, just a small flinch, expecting to see something disappointing before you've actually looked.

My friend Renee noticed me do it once, at the mall, and asked what was wrong. I said nothing was wrong. I didn't have a way to explain that I was bracing for a compliment that was never going to come, and flinching anyway, out of habit.

That's the actual low point. Not any particular thing about my face. The bracing. The habit of expecting to be disappointing before anyone's even looked.

-------------

I mentioned it to my sister-in-law Dana at a birthday dinner, half joking, the way you say a true thing while pretending it's a bit. "I don't think Mark's actually looked at my face in two years."

She didn't laugh. She said, "Have you looked at yours?"

I hadn't, not really. Not past the automatic once-over for lipstick on my teeth.

-------------

Dana was the one who explained the actual mechanism to me, a few weeks later, over coffee. She'd gone down the same rabbit hole herself the year before.

Skin makes its own oil. It always has. Most people don't connect what's changed on their face with what's changed underneath, they just assume it's the face itself getting worse.

Every moisturizer she'd owned, she said, had water as the first ingredient. Flip the bottle, it's right there. Water evaporates in about an hour. You put it on in the morning and by the time anyone's actually looking at you across a table, it's already gone.

The fat in tallow, she said, is close to the same structure as the fat skin makes on its own, so it absorbs in instead of sitting on top and disappearing.

She'd been using it for eight months. Four ingredients.

-------------

I said: "Cow fat. On my face."

She said: "I know how it sounds. Just try it."

I ordered one that night. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

-------------

Day 1, I put it on before bed. It melted into my palm like warm honey.

Day 4, I woke up and touched my face before I opened my eyes. Soft in a way that felt underneath, not just on top.

Day 9, I caught myself walking past the hallway mirror without the flinch. I didn't notice I hadn't braced until I was already past it.

Day 14, at the grocery store, I looked at my reflection in the freezer case door and didn't look away first.

Day 21, I realized I'd stopped rehearsing the deflection I used to keep loaded, the "oh, I know, I look tired" line I had ready in case anyone commented. Nobody had commented. But I noticed the line wasn't loaded anymore.

-------------

That was the thing I didn't expect. Not the skin itself. The unloading of a defense I didn't know I was carrying.

-------------

Six weeks in, Mark and I had dinner outside at our usual place on a Saturday. There was a candle on the table. He was finishing his wine.

He looked up at me across the table and stopped mid-sentence.

He said: "You look different."

I said: "Different how?"

He said: "I don't know. Lighter. Something."

He reached across and put his hand on the side of my face for a second. His thumb near my ear.

He said: "Whatever you're doing, I can see it."

I sat there with his hand on my face and the candle between us and I didn't move.

-------------

That was six weeks ago. He's said something two more times since. Once walking the dog at dusk. Once when I was fixing an earring and he came up behind me.

I'm not telling you a jar of cow fat fixes a marriage. That sounds like an ad. This isn't an ad. I'm telling you about a Tuesday in October I can still name, and a Saturday in May I can also name, and the two years in between where I stopped expecting anyone to look up.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If nothing changes in six weeks, you get every dollar back.

Link is below if you want it.

What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the Saturday. The person across the table looking up mid-sentence and saying it out loud, with his hand on your face and a candle between you.

I'd stopped believing that was coming back.

Now it's back.
