## Body Copy

I was at Elaine's house for book club last month and went upstairs to use the guest bathroom.

There was one thing on the counter.

One jar. Amber glass. Simple label.

I stood there with my hands wet and stared at it for too long.

-------------

Elaine and I have been in the same book club since our daughters were in preschool together. I've been in that bathroom a hundred times.

The last time I was up there, maybe eighteen months ago, the counter had a toner and two moisturizers and a face oil and a spot treatment and two perfumes and a retinol.

Now it had one jar.

I dried my hands and came downstairs and didn't bring it up.

-------------

When I got home that night I went into my own bathroom and counted what was on my counter.

Fourteen things.

Cleanser. Toner. Vitamin C serum. Hyaluronic acid. A retinol I could barely use anymore because it burned. A day cream. A night cream. An eye cream. A lip treatment. A face oil I'd bought on Instagram. A brightening serum for dark spots. A neck cream. Two different lip balms.

Fourteen. And somewhere in the last few years I'd quietly stopped expecting any of them to do anything. I just kept buying the next one anyway, the way you keep paying a subscription you forgot to cancel.

That's the real low point. Not the fourteen things. The part where I'd stopped believing the fifteenth would be any different, and bought it anyway.

-------------

The next book club was at my house three weeks later. Elaine came early to help me set out food.

I waited until she was pouring herself wine in the kitchen. Then I asked.

I said: "What's the one jar in your bathroom?"

She laughed and put the bottle down.

She said: "I wondered if you'd notice."

-------------

She said she'd thrown everything out about nine months earlier. Everything. The toner, the serums, the retinol. She kept one jar.

She said she'd asked her Aunt Winnie about it at her daughter Jess's wedding last June.

Winnie lives in Vermont. She keeps bees and reads more than anyone Elaine knows.

Elaine cornered her in the kitchen during the reception and asked: "What do you do to your face?"

Winnie said: "I've been using the same thing since 1987. Four ingredients. I bought my first one by accident at a farmer's market in Brattleboro."

Elaine had been watching Winnie's skin at family things for a decade trying to figure out what she was doing.

Turns out it was one jar.

-------------

Here's what Elaine told me Winnie had told her. Read this part slowly.

Skin makes its own oil. It always has. Most people don't connect what's happening to their face with what changed underneath, they just buy the next bottle instead.

Every cream in Elaine's bathroom, every single one, had water as the first ingredient. She flipped each bottle over one afternoon and checked. Water. Glycerin second. Something unpronounceable third.

Water evaporates in an hour. That's what water does. You put it on at 7am and by noon it's gone. That's why nothing was holding, for her or for me.

Winnie told Elaine the fat in the balm is almost the same structure as the fat skin makes on its own. So it absorbs in instead of sitting on top. Skin treats it like its own oil. Because, roughly, it is.

Elaine ordered one the week after the wedding. She'd been on it nine months by the time I asked her in my kitchen.

-------------

I said: "Tallow. Like cow fat."

She said: "I know. I made the same face. Just try it."

I went upstairs after everyone left and ordered one. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

-------------

Day 1, I put it on before bed. It melted into my palm like warm honey. Thirty seconds total.

Day 4, I woke up and touched my face before I opened my eyes. Soft. Not the thirty-minute fake soft that disappears in the shower. Underneath soft.

Day 9, my coworker Sylvia asked me at lunch what I was testing this month. We've worked two desks apart for eleven years and I'm the one with a review for everything. I told her nothing new. She didn't believe me.

Day 14, I filled a grocery bag with six of the fourteen and put it in the garage. Not brave enough for the trash yet. The garage first.

Day 21, I stood in my own bathroom and realized I hadn't bought anything new in three weeks. Not a serum, not a "just to try" jar from the drugstore checkout line. Nothing.

-------------

Here's the part I didn't expect.

I used to check for new product launches the way some people check sports scores. A new serum, a "breakthrough" cream, whatever was trending that week, I'd add it to a mental list. I didn't buy everything on the list. But I kept the list.

About a month in, I noticed I hadn't opened that tab in a while. I hadn't scrolled past a skincare ad and felt the little pull to screenshot it. I went looking for the list on my phone, the notes app where I used to track "things to try," and the last entry was from before I'd ordered the jar.

I hadn't added anything since. I hadn't even noticed I'd stopped.

That was the thing I wanted back. Not the skin. The quiet.

-------------

Sylvia brought it up again at the end of week five. She stopped at my desk on the way back from a meeting.

She said: "Okay, I need to know. My cabinet looks like yours used to. What did you switch to?"

I told her. Elaine. Winnie. The jar. The four ingredients.

She listened all the way through. Then she said: "My aunt's always had beautiful skin. Like she never fussed. I wonder if she's been doing something like this her whole life and never told anyone."

She ordered one that afternoon.

-------------

Elaine came over a few weeks ago to help me clean out the hall closet before her daughter visits. She opened my bathroom cabinet looking for a hand towel and just stood there for a second.

She said: "Where did everything go?"

I said: "I threw it out in April. All of it."

She looked at the one jar sitting where fourteen things used to be and didn't say anything else. She didn't need to. She'd already done this exact thing nine months before me.

-------------

I'm not telling you a jar of cow fat will fix your life. That sounds like an ad. This isn't an ad. I'm telling you what Elaine had in her bathroom, and what Winnie told her at her daughter's wedding, and what my own counter looks like now that I stopped buying the fifteenth thing.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If it doesn't do anything in six weeks you email them and you get every dollar back.

Link is below if you want it.

Elaine is still on it. Sylvia ordered one after I told her. My own list of "things to try" hasn't had a new entry in months.

What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the empty shelf. The stopping. The part where you're not standing in the drugstore aisle anymore, reading a new label, hoping this is the one that finally works.

I'd stopped believing that stopping was possible.

Now it has.
