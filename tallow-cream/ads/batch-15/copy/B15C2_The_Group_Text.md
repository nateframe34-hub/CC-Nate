# B15C2 — The Group Text

**Sub-Avatar:** The Invisible Woman
**Angle:** Peer cohort self-exclusion with peer-self-investigation mechanism, photo-avoidance cessation, cumulative husband notice
**Character type:** Peer cohort (3 college friends, same age)
**Hook format:** Digital self-exclusion in a long-running group text
**Awareness level:** Solution Aware

---

## Body Copy

Three of us turned fifty-two this spring. Diane, Karen, and me. We've had a group text since the four years we lived together at Penn State.

Sunday morning Diane sent a selfie from her back porch. Karen sent one back from her kitchen. I didn't send mine. An hour later Karen wrote, "Where's yours?"

I read the message and put my phone face-down on the counter.

========

I'm Pat. Fifty-two, school librarian at Madison West, twenty-two years. My husband Ron is fifty-four, manages a Toyota dealership service department. Our son is twenty-six in Minneapolis, our daughter is twenty-three in Chicago, both out and on their own.

Diane and Karen and I have been in a group text since we got smartphones. Before that we had a chain email. Before that we just called. Three women who lived together at twenty and stayed close at twenty-five and at thirty and at forty and now at fifty-two. We send pictures. We send articles. We send the things we read at midnight that we'd be embarrassed to read in front of anyone but each other.

I'd stopped sending pictures around the time I turned fifty.

========

My skin changed at forty-eight. The usual. Tight by nine in the morning. Foundation cracking by lunch. My doctor looked at my face during a physical and said, "That's just aging. Some women get this." She offered nothing else.

I tried CeraVe for a year. Then a retinol from a dermatologist I saw once and never went back to. Then an eighty-five-dollar serum at Sephora I bought because a woman my age was standing at the counter and said it had changed her life. Then a vitamin C serum I ordered at midnight after reading a Reddit thread.

By fifty I'd stopped buying things. Not because I'd accepted it. Because I was tired of throwing money at a problem nothing was solving.

I'd also stopped sending selfies in the group text. I told myself it was a phase. It wasn't a phase.

========

Tuesday after the Sunday I didn't send mine, Diane called me.

"I saw what you didn't do," she said. "We're talking about this. Wednesday morning. I'm coming over."

She drove from Stoughton in the rain. Sat at my kitchen island while I poured coffee. Diane is the most matter-of-fact person I know. She was a nurse for fifteen years before she quit to run her own bookkeeping practice. She doesn't waste words.

She said, "Karen and I have both been using something for the past few months. I should have told you. I didn't know how to bring it up. Karen didn't either. So we just kept not bringing it up. That was stupid. We're talking about it now."

I asked how long. She'd been on it six months. Karen had been on it three. Karen had told Diane in April. Diane had told her three weeks later. They'd been texting each other about it without me on a separate thread.

I didn't know what to say. I wasn't mad. I was sitting there with my coffee not knowing what to say.

========

"Here's what's happening to your face," Diane said. "I figured this out at one in the morning last September. I was at the bathroom sink and I'd just bought another cream that didn't work. I flipped every bottle I had on the counter over to the back label. Eight bottles. The first ingredient on every one was water. Second was glycerin. Third was something I couldn't pronounce."

She drank her coffee.

"Then I read something that made it click. Skin makes its own oil. It always has. That's why you didn't think about moisturizer at thirty. After forty you make a lot less of that oil. Way less. Water sits on a counter for an hour and evaporates. It does the same thing on your face. That's why none of those eight bottles ever held past lunch. They were never going to. Water can't replace oil. That's not the bottle failing. That's just what water does."

I let her keep talking.

"What I bought after that night is fat. Same kind of fat your skin used to make. It absorbs in. Doesn't sit on top. Four ingredients on the back. Tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula. The honey is manuka, which is a different thing from regular honey, that one matters in this jar. Not a marketing thing. Different chemistry. The reason I actually pulled the trigger is they give you sixty days to send it back. Most places don't even give you thirty. Karen and I talked about it before she ordered hers. The sixty days was what got both of us over the line. Worst case we lose nothing."

She didn't push. She just kept drinking her coffee.

I ordered one from my phone before she left.

========

Day 1 was Tuesday night. It went on like warm honey and went in faster than I expected. No film. I went to sleep and thought, well, we'll see.

Day 4 was Saturday morning. I touched my face before I opened my eyes. Soft. Not the temporary kind. The underneath kind. I lay there for a minute.

Day 9 was a Sunday. My daughter came home from Chicago for the weekend and we went out for coffee on Sunday afternoon. Halfway through she set her cup down and said, "What are you doing differently."

I told her nothing. She said, "Something then." I let it go.

Day 14 was the next Sunday morning. The group text. Karen sent a selfie from her front porch. Diane sent one back from her car at the lake. I held my phone for a minute, then took one at my kitchen table and sent it. No comment. Just the photo. Karen wrote back, "There she is."

Day 21 was a Wednesday. Ron was reading the paper at the kitchen table while I was making eggs. He looked up and said, "You look rested." I told him I'd slept the same as always. He said, "Then I don't know."

========

Here's the thing I didn't see coming.

For about eighteen months I'd been the one who offered to take the picture at any family event. Easter, my niece's college graduation, my parents' anniversary dinner. Someone would say "we should get a photo" and I'd say "I'll take it" before anyone else moved. I'd gotten very smooth at it. Nobody had to ask me. I'd find a better angle. I'd get out of the frame.

My niece graduated from Marquette in May. After the ceremony, the whole family gathered on the steps of the chapel for a picture. The photographer was the husband of one of my cousins. He waved everyone tighter and said, "Pat, get in here." I walked over and stood next to my brother-in-law. The picture happened. I didn't think to step out. I didn't think to volunteer to take it. I was just there.

I saw it on Facebook a week later. I'm in the front row, smiling, between my brother-in-law and my mother. I'm wearing the navy blue dress I almost didn't buy because the dressing-room lighting made me look bad.

I'd been in the picture and I hadn't thought about it.

========

That same week Ron said it again at dinner. "You look rested. Whatever you're doing, keep doing it." Then on Saturday at the gas station while I was filling the car he came out of the convenience store with coffee and said it a third time. Three times in seven days. Ron does not say things like that in a vacuum.

I told Karen at Easter. She said, "This is the version of you I forgot existed." My mom asked at the same dinner what I was using. I told her about the sixty days. She ordered a jar that night. She's seventy-three. The sixty days was the whole reason.

========

Six months after that Sunday I didn't send my selfie, it was Sunday morning again. The group text.

Karen sent a selfie. Diane sent a selfie. I sent a selfie.

Karen wrote back: "There you are."

Three blue thumbs up appeared under the photo within ten minutes.

========

What I want you to have isn't the jar.

It's the Sunday morning group text with your two college roommates where you send the selfie without thinking about whether you should and your friends from thirty-three years ago send you a thumbs up because they can see you again.

It's the front row of the family graduation photo because you didn't step out of the frame this time.

It's the husband saying you look rested three times in a week because he can't think of anything else to call it.

Four ingredients. Tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula. Forty-nine ninety-nine. Free shipping. Sixty days to send it back if you hate it and they refund every dollar. The sixty days is the reason to try it.

Link is below.

I'm sending the selfie this week.
