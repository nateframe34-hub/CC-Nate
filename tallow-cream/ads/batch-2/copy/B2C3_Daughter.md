# B2C3 — "Daughter" (Product Graveyard)

## Body Copy

My daughter asked where all my moisturizers went. I told her I only need one now.

She looked at my bathroom shelf. One jar.

"That's it?"

That's it.

========

Three months ago that shelf had seven products on it.

A morning moisturizer. A night cream. A serum that went under the moisturizer. An eye cream. A face oil that went on top of everything. A lip balm. And a "barrier repair" something that I bought at 1am after reading a skincare article that scared me.

Seven products. Every morning and every night.

I had a routine down to the minute. Cleanser, serum, eye cream, moisturizer, oil, SPF. In that order. Don't mix them up. Wait 60 seconds between layers. Let each one absorb before the next.

It was a part time job.

And my skin was still dry.

========

Not a little dry. The kind of dry where you put on foundation at 8am and it's cracking by noon. Where your cheeks feel tight after you wash your face and don't ease up until you pile three things on. Where you carry a tube of something in your purse because you know you'll need it by 3pm.

I was doing everything right. Seven products. Twice a day. Consistent for months.

And it still wasn't enough.

========

My daughter came home from college for Thanksgiving.

She walked into my bathroom and laughed.

"Mom, you have more skincare than a Sephora."

She picked up the eye cream. "Does this work?"

I thought about it. "I don't know."

She picked up the serum. "What about this?"

"I think so? Maybe?"

She picked up the barrier repair cream. "And this?"

"That one I'm not sure about either."

She put it down and looked at me.

"Mom. You're spending all this money and you don't even know if any of it works?"

That hit different coming from a 21 year old.

========

She was right.

I was layering product on top of product because each one did a little but none of them did enough. So I just kept adding.

More products. More steps. More money.

And the end result was the same. Dry by lunch. Reapply by afternoon. Repeat.

I wasn't building a routine. I was compensating for products that didn't work by piling more products that didn't work on top of them.

========

After Thanksgiving, I started simplifying.

I cut the routine down to three products. Then two.

My skin didn't get worse. If anything, it felt about the same. Which told me something.

Half of what I was using wasn't doing anything.

========

Then my neighbor mentioned she'd switched to a tallow balm.

I made a face. "Like beef tallow?"

She laughed. "I know. I made the same face. Just try it."

She said she'd been using it for about six weeks. Said it was the only moisturizer that actually lasted all day. Said she threw out everything else.

I looked it up.

Whipped beef tallow. Manuka honey. Olive oil. Calendula.

4 ingredients.

No water. No glycerin. No dimethicone. No fragrance. Nothing I couldn't pronounce.

I ordered it mostly because I was curious.

========

First night. Washed my face. Scooped a tiny amount. Rubbed it between my palms. Pressed it into my skin.

It melted in. Like actually disappeared into my skin within a couple minutes.

I put nothing else on. No serum. No oil. No night cream on top.

Just the balm.

I woke up and my skin was soft. Actually soft. Not the fake soft that disappears when you wash your face. Real, lasting soft.

========

By the end of the first week, I stopped carrying moisturizer in my purse. Didn't need it.

By week two, my foundation went on smooth in the morning and stayed that way until I washed it off at night.

By week three, I took everything else off the bathroom shelf. Serum. Eye cream. Night cream. Face oil. Barrier repair thing. All of it.

One jar left.

========

My daughter came home for Christmas break.

Walked into the bathroom. Stopped.

"Where did everything go?"

I pointed at the jar.

"That's all you use now?"

"That's all I need."

She picked it up. Opened the lid. Smelled it.

"It smells like honey."

"It is honey. And tallow. And olive oil. And one flower extract. That's it."

She looked at my skin. Then she looked at me.

"Mom, your skin looks really good."

Four ingredients. One jar. The best my skin has looked in years.

And the best part? My bathroom shelf is clean. My mornings take 2 minutes instead of 15. And I'm not spending $200 a month hoping the next product will be the one that works.

This one works.
