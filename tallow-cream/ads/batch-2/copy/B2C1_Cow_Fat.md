## Body Copy

I have a drawer full of skincare products that didn't work. Then I tried cow fat.

I know. Stay with me.

========

That drawer has about 15 products in it right now. I counted.

Three moisturizers. Two serums. A vitamin C something. A retinol I used twice before it made me peel. A jar of ceramide cream that cost $80 and did absolutely nothing. Two bottles of hyaluronic acid. Something with snail mucin that my coworker swore by.

And a $45 face oil that smelled amazing and absorbed like I'd rubbed olive oil on a plastic bag.

Every single one was supposed to be The One.

Every single one worked for maybe a week. Then nothing.

I'd wake up with tight, dry skin. Layer on product. Feel okay for an hour. Then dry again by lunch. Reapply. Dry by dinner. Go to bed hoping tomorrow would be different.

It never was.

========

I stopped counting how much I'd spent. But if I had to guess? Over $500 in the last year alone.

On products that are sitting in a drawer.

The worst part wasn't the money though.

It was the hope.

Every time I bought something new, I'd feel this little spark. Maybe this one. Maybe this is the one that actually works.

Then a week later, same dry skin, same tight feeling, same reaching for the moisturizer at 2pm.

After a while you stop being disappointed. You just get tired.

I started to think maybe this is just what my skin is now. Maybe I need to accept it.

========

My friend Tammy texted me one morning. No context. Just a link to a product page and the words "trust me."

I clicked it.

Whipped beef tallow and honey balm.

I texted back: "You're putting cow fat on your face?"

She sent back a selfie. Her skin was glowing. Like actually glowing. Not filter glowing.

"Been using it for three weeks," she said. "I threw out everything else."

Tammy is the most skeptical person I know. She returns things. She reads every review. She once drove back to Target to return a $6 candle because it didn't smell right.

If Tammy says trust me, you trust her.

========

So I looked it up.

4 ingredients. Beef tallow from grass fed cows. Manuka honey. Olive oil. Calendula.

That's the whole list.

I flipped over my CeraVe. 23 ingredients. Half of them I couldn't pronounce.

I flipped over the $80 ceramide cream. 31 ingredients.

I looked back at the tallow balm. 4.

Something about that felt right. Like maybe the problem wasn't that I wasn't using enough products. Maybe I was using too many of the wrong ones.

========

I ordered it. $50. At this point, what's one more jar?

When it arrived, I opened the lid and it looked like whipped golden butter. It smelled like warm honey.

I was expecting it to feel heavy. Greasy. Like Vaseline.

It didn't.

I rubbed a small amount between my fingers and pressed it into my cheek. It melted in. Like actually absorbed into my skin. Not on top. Into.

That had never happened with any of the 15 products in my drawer.

========

I woke up the next morning and touched my face.

Soft.

Not tight. Not flaky around my nose. Not that weird film that some creams leave.

Just soft.

I didn't reach for a moisturizer. For the first time in I don't know how long, I didn't need to.

========

By the end of the first week, I stopped layering. No serum. No oil. No cream on top. Just the balm.

By week two, the dry patches on my jawline were gone. My makeup went on smooth. No caking.

By week three, I opened that drawer, looked at all 15 products, and felt something I hadn't felt in a long time.

Angry.

Not at myself. At every brand that sold me water and glycerin and synthetic fillers and told me it would fix my skin.

They weren't fixing anything. They were evaporating off my face in an hour.

========

Not because it's magic. Because it's what my skin was actually asking for.
