## Body Copy

I lined up every moisturizer I've ever bought. Then I looked at the ingredients. They were basically all the same thing in different bottles.

I'm not exaggerating.

========

It started because I was cleaning out under my bathroom sink.

I pulled out everything. All of it. Creams, serums, oils, jars, tubes. Some half used. Some barely opened. One still in the box.

I put them on the counter in a row. Eleven products.

And then, for some reason, I started flipping them over and reading the ingredient lists.

CeraVe: Water, glycerin, cetearyl alcohol, dimethicone...

Neutrogena: Water, glycerin, cetyl alcohol, dimethicone...

That $90 cream from Sephora: Aqua, glycerin, cetearyl alcohol, dimethicone...

I kept going.

Water. Glycerin. Dimethicone. Some variation of alcohol. Then a long list of things I can't pronounce.

Different brands. Different prices. Different promises.

Same base. Same fillers. Same formula dressed up in different packaging.

I felt sick.

========

I thought about how much money was sitting on that counter.

The CeraVe was $16. Fine. But the serum was $55. The night cream was $90. That "clean beauty" moisturizer was $72.

And they all had the same first four ingredients.

I'd been buying the same thing over and over for years. Paying $16 for it. Paying $90 for it. Thinking the expensive one would finally work.

It never did. Because it was the same thing.

========

No wonder my skin was still dry.

I wasn't trying different solutions. I was trying different bottles of the same solution.

Water based. Glycerin based. A thin layer of something that felt nice for 30 minutes and then evaporated.

Every single one.

My skin would drink it in, feel okay for an hour, then go right back to tight and flaky. I'd been blaming myself. Blaming my skin. Thinking I wasn't using enough or wasn't consistent enough.

But it wasn't me. It was the products. They were all built to feel good on application and then disappear.

========

That night, still annoyed, I went down a rabbit hole.

I searched "why doesn't moisturizer work" and found a thread where a woman explained something that hit me like a brick.

She said most moisturizers are water based. Water evaporates. That's just physics.

But your skin's own moisture system runs on oil. Specifically a fat called sebum.

As you get older, your body makes less of it. Way less. So your skin can't hold moisture on its own anymore.

And adding water on top of skin that can't hold moisture is like pouring water into a cup with no bottom.

It goes right through.

========

She said the only thing that finally worked for her was switching from water based products to something fat based. Something her skin could actually use.

She'd been using a tallow and honey balm.

Beef tallow.

Look. I get it. I had the same reaction.

But she said tallow is almost identical to the oil your skin naturally makes. So instead of evaporating off the surface, it absorbs. Your skin pulls it in because it recognizes it.

I looked at the ingredient list.

Beef tallow. Manuka honey. Olive oil. Calendula.

Four ingredients.

I looked back at the eleven products on my bathroom counter with their 20-30 ingredients each.

========

I ordered a jar.

When it arrived, the first thing I did was flip it over and read the back.

Four ingredients. Same four she listed.

No water. No glycerin. No dimethicone. No fragrance. No fillers.

I put a small amount on my cheek before bed. It melted in. Like actually sank into my skin instead of sitting on top.

I woke up the next morning and my skin was still soft. Not from reapplying. From the night before.

That had literally never happened.

========

One week in, I stopped layering. No serum underneath. No oil on top. Just the balm.

Two weeks in, the dryness that I'd accepted as permanent was gone.

Three weeks in, I took those eleven products off the counter. Put them in a bag. Donated the unopened ones. Threw out the rest.

My bathroom shelf has one jar on it now.

And for the first time in years, I'm not thinking about my skin. I'm not reapplying at lunch. I'm not wondering if the new product I ordered will finally work.

Because this one did.

========
