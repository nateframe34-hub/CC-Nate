# B9C3 — The Counter With One Thing

**Sub-Avatar:** The Invisible Woman

## Body Copy

I was at Elaine's house for book club last month and I went upstairs to use the guest bathroom.

There was one thing on the counter.

One jar. Amber glass. Simple label.

I stood there with my hands wet and stared at it for too long.

========

I'm 54. Elaine is 56. We've been in the same book club since our daughters were in preschool together in 1998. I've been in that bathroom a hundred times.

The last time I was up there — maybe eighteen months ago — the counter had a toner and two moisturizers and a face oil and a spot treatment and two perfumes and a retinol.

Now it had one jar.

I dried my hands and came downstairs and didn't bring it up.

========

When I got home that night I went into my own bathroom and counted what was on my counter.

Fourteen things.

Cleanser. Toner. Vitamin C serum. Hyaluronic acid. A retinol I could barely use anymore because it burned. A day cream. A night cream. An eye cream. A lip treatment. A face oil I'd bought on Instagram. A brightening serum for dark spots. A neck cream. Two different lip balms.

Fourteen. My skin was tight by noon every day. My foundation cracked by two.

I sat on the edge of the tub and thought about Elaine's counter for a long time.

========

The next book club was at my house three weeks later. Elaine came early to help me set out food.

I waited until she was pouring herself wine in the kitchen. Then I asked.

I said: "What's the one jar in your bathroom?"

She laughed and put the bottle down.

She said: "I wondered if you'd notice."

========

She said she'd thrown everything out about nine months earlier. Everything. The toner, the serums, the retinol. She kept one jar.

She said she'd asked her Aunt Winnie about it at her daughter Jess's wedding last June.

Winnie is 76. She lives in Vermont. She keeps bees and reads more than anyone Elaine knows.

Elaine cornered her in the kitchen during the reception and asked: "What do you do to your face?"

Winnie said: "I've been using the same thing since 1987. Four ingredients. I bought my first one by accident at a farmer's market in Brattleboro."

Winnie is 76 and her skin looks like a woman in her early 60s. Elaine had been watching her at family things for a decade trying to figure out what she was doing.

Turns out it was one jar.

========

Here's what Elaine told me Winnie had told her. Read this part slowly.

Skin makes its own oil. It always has. It's the reason you didn't think about moisturizer when you were twenty-five. The oil was just there.

After about forty, skin makes a lot less of it. The drop is real. Most women don't connect what's happening to their face to what changed underneath.

Every cream in Elaine's bathroom — every single one — had water as the first ingredient. She flipped each bottle over one afternoon and checked. Water. Glycerin second. Something unpronounceable third.

Water evaporates in an hour. That's what water does. You put it on at 7am and by noon it's gone. That's why nothing was holding. That's why Elaine's skin was tight. That's why mine was.

Winnie told Elaine the fat in the balm is almost the same structure as the fat skin makes on its own. So when you put it on, it absorbs in instead of sitting on top. Skin treats it like its own oil. Because, roughly, it is.

Elaine ordered one the week after the wedding. She'd been on it nine months by the time I asked her in my kitchen.

========

I said: "Tallow. Like cow fat."

She said: "I know. I made the same face. Just try it."

I went upstairs after everyone left and ordered one. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee.

========

Day 1 — I put it on before bed. It melted into my palm like warm honey. I pressed it into my cheeks, my jaw, my forehead. Thirty seconds total.

Day 4 — I woke up and touched my face before I opened my eyes. Soft. Not the thirty-minute fake soft that disappears in the shower. Underneath soft.

Day 9 — My coworker Sylvia asked me over lunch what I was doing different. We've worked two desks apart for eleven years. She never asks that kind of question. I told her nothing. She said "something then." I let it go.

Day 14 — I put on foundation at 7am for an offsite. At 2pm I went to the restroom. It sat flat. It hadn't cracked around my mouth. That hadn't happened in over a year.

Day 21 — Elaine hugged me at book club and smiled and didn't say anything. She didn't need to.

========

Here's the part I didn't expect.

I'd been buying those little travel-size lotion packets — the foil envelopes you get at the drugstore — and keeping them in three places. My purse. My car console. My bottom desk drawer at work. I'd been doing it for two years. When my skin got tight I'd dig one out.

My daughter Lily came over for dinner about four weeks in. She's 27 and she'd been on her feet all day at a trade show. She asked me for one out of my purse.

I couldn't find any.

I checked my car the next morning. None there either. I checked my desk drawer on Monday. None.

I hadn't bought them in weeks. I hadn't thought about it. I hadn't felt tight enough to go digging.

Lily laughed and said: "Mom. You always have those. Are you out?"

I said I was out. I didn't tell her why.

That was the thing I wanted back. Not the soft skin. The not-carrying.

========

Sylvia brought it up again at the end of week five. She stopped at my desk on the way back from a meeting.

She said: "Okay. What are you doing? Is it a facial? Something new?"

I told her. Elaine. Winnie. The jar. The four ingredients.

She listened all the way through. Then she said: "My aunt is 79 and she's always had beautiful skin. Like she never fussed. I wonder if she's been doing something like this her whole life and never told anyone."

She ordered one that afternoon.

========

My husband and I had dinner outside at our usual place on a Saturday in May. There was a candle on the table. He was finishing his wine.

He looked up at me across the table and stopped mid-sentence.

He said: "You look different."

I said: "Different how?"

He said: "I don't know. Lighter. Something."

He reached across and put his hand on the side of my face for a second. His thumb near my ear.

He said: "Whatever you're doing, I can see it."

I sat there with his hand on my face and the candle on the table and a waiter three feet away and I didn't move.

He pulled his hand back and picked up his wine.

I finished mine slowly.

========

That was six weeks ago. He's said something two more times since. Once walking the dog at dusk. Once when I was fixing an earring and he came up behind me.

I'm not telling you a jar of cow fat will fix your life. That sounds like an ad. This isn't an ad. I'm telling you what Elaine had in her bathroom and what Winnie told her at her daughter's wedding and what my husband said at the table with the candle.

Four ingredients. $49.99. Free shipping. 60-day money-back guarantee. If it doesn't do anything in six weeks you email them and you get every dollar back.

Link is below if you want it.

Elaine is still on it. Sylvia ordered one after I told her. Lily asked me for the name last Sunday.

What I want you to have isn't the jar. It's the Saturday night. The person across the table from you looking up and telling you he can see it. Out loud. With his hand on your face and a candle between you.

I'd stopped believing that was coming back.

Now it's back.
