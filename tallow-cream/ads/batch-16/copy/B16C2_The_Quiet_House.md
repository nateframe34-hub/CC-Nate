## Body Copy

The first dinner after we dropped Emma at school, it was just me and Dave at the table.

Twenty-four years of four chairs. Then two.

He looked at me across it for a second longer than usual. Then he said, "When did you start looking so tired?"

He didn't mean it to land the way it did. He was worried. That was the worst part.

========

I'm Carol. Fifty-two. I do the front desk at a pediatric dental office in Dayton, twenty years this spring. Dave installs HVAC. We've been married twenty-four years. Our son moved out four years ago. Our daughter Emma left for Ohio State in August.

For twenty-four years there was always something between me and the mirror. Lunches to pack. Practices to drive to. A kitchen that was never quiet. I was Mom. I did not have time to think about my own face, and honestly that was a kind of mercy.

Then the house went quiet.

My skin changed around forty-eight. Tight by mid-morning. Foundation cracking along my mouth by lunch. I tried CeraVe for most of a year. Then a retinol the dermatologist handed me in a six-minute visit. It burned for two weeks and made things worse. Then an eighty-dollar serum a younger woman at work swore by. It ran out in a month and changed nothing.

The doctor looked at my face for about a minute and said, "That's normal at your age." She didn't offer anything else. So I stopped asking.

========

Here is what the quiet house did.

For twenty-four years I never had to look at myself for very long. There was always a kid calling my name from another room. Now it was a Tuesday in September and the house was so quiet I could hear the refrigerator, and there was nothing between me and my own reflection anymore.

I set the table for four that first week. Out of habit. Then I stood there holding two extra plates and felt stupid and put them back.

That night I caught myself in the dark kitchen window while I was doing the dishes. The woman looking back looked exhausted. Not sad. Just worn down. Like she had been fighting something for years and losing.

And I realized I had nowhere left to hide from her. No lunches to pack. No practices. Just me, and Dave, and a quiet house, and a face I had been avoiding for four years without admitting it.

I could not keep not-looking. There was nothing left to look at but me.

========

My friend Brenda had me over for coffee that Saturday. Her kids left three years before mine. She knows the quiet house. She knows what it does to you.

She took one look at me and said, "Sit down. You look like I looked three years ago."

Brenda is not a fussy woman. She wore the same haircut for two decades. She is the last person who would fall for something. So when she got up, went to her bathroom, and came back with a small amber jar and set it on the kitchen table, I paid attention.

"Your skin makes its own oil," she said. "Always has. That's why you never thought about any of this at thirty. After forty you make a lot less of it. Mine basically quit. Every cream you've been buying is water and glycerin and something you can't pronounce. Water sits on your face for an hour and then it's gone. That's not the cream failing. That's just what water does. You've been pouring water on skin that needs oil."

I told her I'd tried everything.

"You tried more water," she said. "This is different. It's fat. The same kind of fat your skin used to make. It goes in instead of sitting on top. Four ingredients. Tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula. The honey is manuka, which actually matters here, it's not the regular kind. I've been using it a year. The reason I finally tried it is they give you sixty days to send it back. Most places don't even give you thirty. I figured I had nothing to lose but two months."

I ordered it in Brenda's driveway before I started the car.

========

Day 1, I put it on after Dave went to bed. It went on like warm honey and soaked in faster than I expected. No film. I lay there waiting to feel something and didn't.

Day 4, I touched my cheek before I opened my eyes. Not tight. I went to the bathroom and pressed my finger to my cheekbone and watched the color come back the way it used to. I stood there longer than I needed to.

Day 9, Dave looked up from his plate at our table for two. He didn't say anything for a second. Then he said, "You look different. Not tired. Just different." He has been looking at my face for twenty-four years. He noticed.

Day 14, I wore foundation to a long shift, eight hours, the whole waiting room cycling through. At four in the afternoon I checked in the bathroom mirror. It hadn't cracked. It hadn't moved. I hadn't seen that since I was forty-seven.

Day 21, I was doing the dishes at night and I caught myself in the dark kitchen window again. Same window. Same woman. Except she didn't look like she was losing anymore.

========

Here is the thing I didn't see coming.

For about a year I had been sitting at the side of the dinner table instead of across from Dave. I told myself it was so I could reach the stove. That was not why. It was so I'd be at an angle, not straight across from the person who has looked at me longest, under the kitchen light.

One night in October I sat straight across from him without thinking about it. We talked about Emma's roommate. I cleared the plates. It was not until I was rinsing them that I realized I had sat down across from my husband under a direct light and hadn't thought about my face once.

That was the thing I wanted back. Not the compliments. The not-managing. The sitting down across from him without doing math first.

========

I gave Brenda's name to a woman at work who had been quiet about something for months. She ordered one. My sister asked me at Thanksgiving what I was doing and I told her about the sixty days and she ordered one before the pie.

========

It's January now. Emma came home for break and went back. The house is quiet again. Last night it was just me and Dave at the table for two.

He looked at me across it. Then he went back to his food.

He didn't ask when I started looking so tired.

He didn't have to ask anything.

I was sitting straight across from him under the kitchen light, and I wasn't thinking about it.

========

What I want you to have isn't the jar.

It's the quiet house stops being a mirror you're afraid of.

It's sitting across from the person who has looked at you longest, under a direct light, and not doing the math first.

It's the dark kitchen window at night showing you a woman who isn't losing anymore.

Four ingredients. Tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula. Forty-nine ninety-nine. A jar lasts about three months. Free shipping. Sixty days to send it back if you hate it and they refund every dollar. The sixty days is the reason to try it at all.

Link is below.

The house is still quiet. I just don't mind the reflection in it anymore.
