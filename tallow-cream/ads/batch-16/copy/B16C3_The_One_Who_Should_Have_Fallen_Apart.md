## Body Copy

My friend Sandra got divorced last spring. Everyone expected her to fall apart.

We brought casseroles. We braced for the version of her that shows up to book club looking like she hasn't slept.

Instead she walked in this February looking ten years younger than the rest of us.

I cornered her in the kitchen and asked what she was doing. I figured she'd say Botox, or a younger boyfriend, or some procedure.

She said one word. Tallow.

========

I'm Joan. Fifty-four. I manage the office for a heating and plumbing company outside Minneapolis. My husband Tom and I have been married twenty-eight years. Two kids, both grown and gone. A stable life. A good one.

Sandra and I have been friends for twenty years. Her divorce was not quiet. Her husband left the way they sometimes do at our age, and the whole book club watched her go through it. We expected the fallout. We expected her to look like the thing had taken a piece of her.

My skin changed around forty-nine. Tight by mid-morning. Foundation cracking by lunch. I tried CeraVe, then a retinol that burned, then a vitamin C serum I bought at midnight after a Reddit thread. My doctor looked at my face and said, "That's just aging." Then she moved on to my blood pressure.

I stopped buying things. Not because I'd accepted it. Because I was tired of throwing money at a problem nothing was touching.

========

Here is what the night of that book club did to me.

I drove home in February and pulled into the garage and didn't get out of the car right away. I sat there with the engine off.

Because here is the math I couldn't stop doing. Sandra's whole life blew up. Her marriage, her house, her plans, all of it, gone in a year. And she walked into book club looking like she'd been on vacation.

My life is fine. Tom is good. The kids are good. Nothing has happened to me. Nothing has gone wrong.

And I'm the one who looks like she's been through something.

I caught myself in the rearview mirror, sitting in the dark garage, and I thought: her life fell apart and she looks like that, and mine is fine and I look like this. How is that the math.

It wasn't sadness. It was something sharper. I had been disappearing for two or three years, quietly, while everything in my life was going fine, and a woman whose life had detonated was sitting at book club blooming. And I could not stand it for one more season.

========

I texted Sandra that night. She came over Sunday.

She is not someone who falls for things. She returned a couch once because the cushions were an inch too deep. So when she reached into her bag and put a single small amber jar on my kitchen counter, I leaned in.

"There's one thing on my bathroom counter now," she said. "This. I threw everything else out in May. Here's why it works."

She tapped the lid.

"Your skin makes its own oil. Always did. That's why you didn't think about any of this at thirty. After forty you make a lot less. Mine had basically stopped. Every cream you and I have ever bought is water, then glycerin, then a thickener. Water sits on your face an hour and evaporates. That's the whole reason nothing held. It was never going to. You can't replace oil with water."

I said it sounded too simple.

"My life got very complicated last year," she said. "I was done with complicated. This is fat. The same kind of fat your skin used to make. It goes in instead of sitting on top. Four ingredients. Tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula. The honey's manuka, which actually matters, it's not the grocery kind. They give you sixty days to send it back. That was the only reason I tried it. I had lost enough that year. I wasn't going to lose fifty dollars too."

I ordered one before she pulled out of the driveway.

========

Day 1, I put it on before bed. Warm honey, soaked in fast, no film. I went to sleep figuring I'd see.

Day 4, I woke up and touched my face before I opened my eyes. Soft. Not the kind that washes off. The underneath kind. I lay there a minute.

Day 9, Tom was reading next to me and he looked over and said, "Your skin looks good. Did you do something?" Twenty-eight years. He does not say that into the air.

Day 14, I wore makeup to a long day at the office and checked at four o'clock. It had held. Hadn't cracked. Hadn't moved. I hadn't seen that in years.

Day 21, I pulled into the garage at night and turned the car off, and before I got out I looked in the rearview mirror. Same dark garage. Same woman. Except she wasn't disappearing anymore.

========

Here is the part I didn't expect.

For two or three years I had been tilting the rearview mirror up when I parked, so it pointed at the ceiling of the car. So I wouldn't catch myself in it at the end of the day, under that light, looking how I looked. I did it without thinking. A small adjustment every time I parked.

One night in spring I got in the car after work and the mirror was already where it belonged, pointed back at the road, and I realized I hadn't tilted it up in weeks. I had been getting in and out of my car like a person who didn't mind catching her own reflection.

That was the thing I got back. Not Sandra's secret. The not-flinching at myself in the mirror at the end of the day.

========

I told two women at the office. Both ordered. My sister noticed at Easter and I told her about the sixty days and she ordered before we cut the ham.

========

Book club was at my house in June.

A newer member, a woman who joined in the winter, cornered me in the kitchen while everyone else was finding seats. She lowered her voice and asked what I was doing. She said she'd noticed over the last few months. She figured it was a procedure.

I laughed, because I knew exactly where she was standing. I'd stood there in February with Sandra.

I told her one word.

Then I reached into the cabinet and showed her the one jar.

========

What I want you to have isn't the jar.

It's the end of the math you do in the dark garage, comparing yourself to the woman whose life fell apart while yours is fine.

It's the rearview mirror pointed back at the road, because you stopped needing it pointed at the ceiling.

It's catching your own reflection at the end of a long day and not flinching.

Four ingredients. Tallow, manuka honey, olive oil, calendula. Forty-nine ninety-nine. A jar lasts about three months. Free shipping. Sixty days to send it back if you hate it and they refund every dollar. The sixty days is the reason to try it.

Link is below.

My life is still fine. I just look like it now.
