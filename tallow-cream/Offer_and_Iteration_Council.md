# Offer + Iteration Council: BOGO Compellingness and the B9C3 Iteration Plan

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Method:** 6 in-house agents, two questions, three agents each, with a mandatory challenger on BOTH questions. Question 1: is BOGO 50% actually compelling enough? (Offer Architect / Buyer Skeptic / Offer Killer+Alternatives). Question 2: how do we iterate B9C3 to find the winning angle? (Element Isolator / Iteration Designer / Iteration Objector). The council's job is to surface disagreement, not consensus. It did.

---

## QUESTION 1: Is BOGO 50% Compelling Enough?

### The vote

| Agent | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Offer Architect | Conditional NO as the headline. Clears the bundle bar, fails the acquisition bar for THIS buyer. Launch single-jar hero, A/B offers later. |
| Buyer Skeptic (challenger) | KILL at first purchase. BOGO is a loyalty mechanic aimed at a pre-trust trial buyer. Move it post-purchase. |
| Offer Killer | Ship BOGO at launch but ONLY to hold the offer constant through B16, then test the $5-ship nudge at day 22. |

### The argument that decided it

The Buyer Skeptic produced the single sharpest insight of the council, and the Architect independently landed on the same point:

> **B9C3's conversion engine is "the not-carrying." One jar on a near-empty counter. The ad sells her the identity of the woman who finally owns ONE thing. Then the cart says: buy two.** The offer asks her to violate the exact identity the ad just sold her. She came in to be the woman with one jar. BOGO makes her the woman with two, from a brand she has not validated, at $74.99 when the ad calibrated her to $49.99.

Supporting evidence from all three agents:
- The validated buyer threw out 5-12 products. She is a burn-me-once trial buyer, not a stockpiler. Relief buyers buy one. Planners buy two. Our only converter converts on relief.
- BOGO is a LOYALTY mechanic in DTC. Native Deodorant's BOGO works because the buyer already knows Native works. None of the comparable tallow brands (Primally Pure, Toups, Vintage Tradition) run a standing first-purchase BOGO.
- The gift framing is a trap: it asks her to recommend an unvalidated $50 Facebook-ad cream to her sister BEFORE knowing it works, spending real social credibility. She won't.
- Margin math: 2-pack margin is $42.94 ($21.47/jar). At a $30-37 CPA that leaves $5-12 net per order. Single jar at $49.99 has $14 of CPA cushion; BOGO has $5. BOGO scaled on thin attach-rate data is fragile.
- The 60-day guarantee says "try risk-free." BOGO says "commit to two." The two anchors fight each other.

### Where the Offer Killer was right anyway

His contamination logic stands even though his ship-BOGO conclusion loses: **whatever the offer is during B16, it must not change mid-test, and it should match the configuration that set the 17% bar.** B9C3's 17% LPV-to-purchase was earned with a single jar at $49.99, free shipping, no BOGO. Launching B16 with BOGO as the headline would change the offer AND the ad at the same time, making the race unreadable against the benchmark.

### DECISION (adjudicated)

1. **B16 launches in the PROVEN configuration: single jar $49.99 hero, free shipping, 60-day guarantee moved up next to the CTA. BOGO is NOT the headline.** This is both the psychologically correct offer for the validated buyer AND the only configuration that keeps the B16-vs-17%-bar comparison clean.
2. **BOGO stays built. It moves to the post-purchase / reorder slot** (thank-you page, the abandoned-cart email, the day-30 reorder email: "your second jar half off"). That is where the loyalty mechanic belongs and it starts collecting attach-rate data at zero risk to the cold funnel.
3. **Phase 2 offer test (after B16 locks a winner, ~day 22+):** hold the winning ad constant, A/B the offer. The two candidates, from the Architect and the Killer:
   - **B1G1-FREE at $69.99** (not BOGO 50% at $74.99): charges $5 less, "free" frame beats "50% off" frame in the brand's own competitive research, near-identical margin.
   - **Nate's $5-ship nudge** ($49.99 + $5 ship single, free ship on the 2-pack): raises single-jar margin to $36.16, makes free shipping the visible reward on the bundle. Risk flagged: surprise shipping at checkout is the #1 DTC abandonment trigger, so the $5 must be visible on the PDP, never a checkout surprise.
4. **Risk-reversal pool for Phase 2/3** (from the Skeptic): extended 90/120-day guarantee, double-money-back, $15 trial mini. Cheap, untested, matched to a burned-8-times buyer. Park until the ad winner is locked.

## QUESTION 2: How Do We Iterate B9C3?

### The vote

| Agent | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Element Isolator | Iterate via single-variable isolation. Chain-depth test first (least pre-answered, most consequential). |
| Iteration Designer | 6 swings designed (B17C1-C6). Ship Quiet Brag + Reunion first. B16C3 accepted as written. B11 must run in parallel as the pocket-falsification test. |
| Iteration Objector (challenger) | DO NOT iterate yet. n=2 is not signal (95% CI on the 17% rate is roughly 2-48%). Validate B9C3 solo at protected budget FIRST, then ONE iteration head-to-head, never six. |

### The objector's case, taken seriously

- **The 17% LPV-to-purchase bar is built on 2 conversions from 12 LPVs.** Calling it "the winning angle" is the same regression-to-mean trap the account already documented at the CTR level (Pattern A in the killed-batch analysis). Equivalent purchase-level rule: no winner claims under 10 purchases.
- **The pocket hypothesis is live.** Every B9C3 sale came inside CBO, which concentrated spend to find buyers. If Meta found ~200 women who would buy any competent tallow ad, six iterations all land in the same pocket and return indistinguishable noise. Partial counter-evidence: B9C3 converted on TWO different BM environments with different audience graphs, which a pure pocket would not survive. Partial. Not proof.
- **Six cells at $50/day is $8.33/cell/day.** Nothing reads. The objector and the deep data analysis agree: 2 cells at $25/day.

### Where the three agents actually converge (the synthesis)

The objector demanded "validate B9C3 solo at protected budget, then ONE iteration head-to-head." **The B16 race as already designed IS exactly that:** C1 (B9C3 verbatim, own ABO ad set, $25/day) is the solo protected-budget validation that was specced in April and never ran, and C3 (the divorced-friend iteration, own ad set, $25/day) is the one iteration head-to-head. The Designer independently reviewed B16C3 and accepted it as written. So the objector's prescription, the designer's first ship, and the existing plan are the same launch. No change needed to B16. That convergence is the strongest finding on Question 2.

### The iteration queue (adjudicated between Isolator and Designer)

The Isolator wants chain-depth first (Test A: remove Aunt Winnie) because it is the least pre-answered variable (5%) and the most consequential: if the decades-proof elder is load-bearing, every future ad needs a Winnie figure. The Designer wants the close-swap first (B17C5 Quiet Brag). Adjudication: **B16 already partially answers the close question** (C3 carries the v2 self-orientation close vs C1's husband close), so running the close-swap next would buy mostly-duplicate information. **Chain depth is untouched by B16. It goes next.**

**The queue:**
1. **NOW: B16 C1 vs C3, ABO, $25/day each** (validation + iteration #1 in one launch)
2. **Write now, ship after B16 reads: B17C3 The Aunt Winnie Standalone** (chain depth 2 vs 3: narrator gets it directly from Winnie at Thanksgiving, no Elaine hop). Runs head-to-head vs the B16 winner.
3. **Then: B17C1 The Reunion One-Jar** (urgency-intensity ceiling: 35th reunion in 4 months as the narrative anchor) OR **B17C5 The Quiet Brag** (clean close isolation), chosen based on what B16 shows. If C3 beat C1, urgency is live and the Reunion tests its ceiling. If C1 beat C3, the close/payoff questions reopen and Quiet Brag runs.
4. **Bench (written specs exist, do not write copy yet):** B17C2 Work Mentor (narrator drops to 42), B17C6 Beekeeper Granddaughter, B17C4 Travel Cessation hook.
5. **The pocket-falsification card: B11 Working Hands Man.** The Designer is right that it is the only way to distinguish "the skeleton converts" from "the pocket converts on anything." But at $50/day there is no third cell. B11 enters when the founder's +20% scaling rule funds a third cell (~$75/day), or immediately if B17 iterations start flattening into identical noise.

### Locked rules carried out of this council

- **One iteration in market at a time, head-to-head vs the current best, ABO, $25/day per cell.** Never six at once.
- **10 combined purchases minimum before any "winner" language.** The 17% bar is a benchmark, not a fact, until then.
- **The do-not-write list stands:** no recognition-payoff hooks, no skeleton changes, no grief-coded narrators, no discovery characters without decades-of-proof somewhere in the chain.

---

## The Two-Line Answer

**BOGO: not compelling enough as a first-purchase headline for a buyer whose entire conversion psychology is "I am the woman with ONE jar." Launch B16 in the proven single-jar configuration, move BOGO to post-purchase, and A/B B1G1-free vs the $5-ship nudge after a winner locks.**

**Iteration: B16 C1-vs-C3 already is the correct first iteration test (and the long-overdue solo validation of B9C3). Next up is the chain-depth question (Aunt Winnie Standalone), one iteration at a time, with B11 held as the pocket-falsification card for when budget scales.**

*6-agent council, 2026-06-13. The challengers changed the outcome on both questions: the offer headline flipped from BOGO to single-jar, and the iteration plan compressed from six parallel swings to one-at-a-time head-to-heads. Council advises, founder decides.*
