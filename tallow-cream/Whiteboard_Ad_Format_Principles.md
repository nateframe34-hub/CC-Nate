# Whiteboard Ad Format Principles — READ BEFORE BUILDING ANY WHITEBOARD AD

> **MANDATORY** for any whiteboard-format ad (B19, B20, and forward). This format operates on a DIFFERENT psychological mechanism than the native long-form ads. The native principles (`Tallow_Ad_Creative_Principles.md`) still govern native ads; THIS file governs whiteboard ads. Do not apply the 10-element native skeleton to whiteboards — it's the wrong tool.

---

## Why This File Exists

The whiteboard format proved itself on our account: **B19C2 (Water vs Tallow mechanism diagram) delivered at $16-76 CPM and converted at 4.40x ROAS, while native B9C3 runs at $180-370 CPM on the same account, same day.** The format escapes the saturated native auction bucket — but NOT automatically. B19C3 (timeline) ran at $165-186 CPM, near native. The cheap inventory comes from the specific creative, not the format label.

Source evidence: a media buyer (IG @ignelmes) posted the format winning across FOUR unrelated markets — nootropics (Neuro-Ignite), GLP-1 muscle (PowerPeak), GLP-1 digestion (Javvy), kids' orthodontics. Caption: "format I'm seeing win across many ad accounts." Cross-niche wins = structural format, not a fluke. This file is the breakdown of why, turned into rules.

---

## PRINCIPLE 1: The mechanism of persuasion is CURIOSITY/LEARNING, not RECOGNITION

Native ads work by recognition — "that's my life, that's my counter." Whiteboards work by **curiosity and teaching** — "I'll learn why this happens." The reader stops because the diagram promises an explanation, then stays because following the diagram rewards them with understanding.

**Consequence:** the diagram must actually TEACH something. A whiteboard that just states a benefit ("soft skin!") fails — there's nothing to learn. A whiteboard that explains a *mechanism* (water evaporates, fat absorbs) or makes a *comparison that resolves a question* (14 products vs 1) succeeds. The reader's reward is the "oh, I get it now" click.

---

## PRINCIPLE 2: It must NOT look like an ad — it must look like organic educational content

The entire CPM advantage comes from Meta's classifier (and the user's eye) reading the creative as native educational content, not a polished ad. The moment it looks like a brand asset, the advantage collapses and you're back in the native/product auction.

**Rules:**
- Real physical whiteboard, visible aluminum frame, glossy surface, overhead light reflection, faint old-marker ghosting. It must look photographed, not designed.
- Hand-drawn marker imperfection. No clean vector lines, no brand fonts.
- No logos, no brand color palette, no polished product hero shots. (A real product jar physically sitting on the desk is fine — see Principle 6 — because that reads as "the thing being explained," not "a product ad.")
- No text-overlay ad conventions (no "SALE," no price slapped in a starburst).

---

## PRINCIPLE 3: The VS / two-column comparison is the core structure

Every winning example uses a side-by-side: bad column vs good column. Traditional Diet vs PowerPeak. Ozempic-alone vs Ozempic+Javvy. Foggy brain vs sharp brain. Other Balms vs Tallow Balm.

**Why it works:** the comparison forces the reader to pick a side. Two columns = "which one do you want to be?" The reader self-selects into the good column before any explicit ask. It also structures the mechanism automatically — left = the problem state and why, right = the solution state and why.

**Rule:** default to two columns divided by a vertical center line. Left = problem (the reader's current state). Right = solution (with our product as the bridge). Reserve single-flow diagrams (timelines) for when a comparison genuinely doesn't fit — and know they tend to run more expensive (B19C3 evidence).

---

## PRINCIPLE 4: Draw a BODY or organ — put the transformation on something physical

The strongest examples don't draw abstractions — they draw a **human figure or body part** and show the change ON it. Pot-belly → abs. Foggy brain → bright brain. This is the single biggest gap in our B19 ads, which used molecules, arrows, and surface lines (abstract) instead of a body.

**For tallow:** draw a **face** (or a skin cross-section, or both). Show the visible state on it:
- Problem side: a face/skin drawn dull, tight, flat-toned, with a downturned mouth or a "tight by noon" label
- Solution side: the same face/skin drawn soft, calm, healthy-toned, relaxed
- OR a skin cross-section: product sitting on top (problem) vs absorbed into the layers (solution)

The transformation needs a physical thing to happen TO. Abstract arrows are weaker than a visible before/after on a face.

---

## PRINCIPLE 5: Bold color fills + one emotional icon pair

The winners FILL shapes with color and use emotional iconography:
- Sickly/muted color for the bad state (yellow fat, gray fog), vibrant for the good state (blue, bright muscle)
- An emotional icon pair: sad toilet / happy toilet, frown / smile, X / checkmark

Our B19 ads used color but timidly (thin outlines). **Fill boldly.** And include exactly one emotional icon pair per board — a sad-face/happy-face or dull-skin/glowing-skin pairing the eye reads in <1 second, before any words.

**Tallow color convention (locked across whiteboard cells):**
- RED / ORANGE = problem side (sitting on top, shine, tight, $$$)
- GREEN = solution side (absorbs, soft, calm)
- BLUE = neutral mechanism/educational notes ("skin recognizes it")
- BLACK = structure (headers, surface lines, arrows, bottom line)

---

## PRINCIPLE 6: Composite the real product jar into the scene

Every winning example has a REAL product photo physically present — a jar on the desk, a coffee bag in frame. Not drawn. This grounds the diagram in a real object and bridges "explanation" to "thing you can buy" without breaking the educational frame.

**Rule:** place the actual tallow jar (real product photo) sitting on the desk/tray below the whiteboard, or on the marker rail. In-scene, slightly off to one side, naturally lit. Never a floating hero shot.

---

## PRINCIPLE 7: Name the product inside the diagram as the BRIDGE

The winners write the product name (in a distinct marker color) at the pivot point between the problem and solution columns — NEURO-IGNITE, JAVVY in purple, PowerPeak. The product is literally what moves you from the bad column to the good column.

**Rule:** the product/mechanism name appears at the bridge — e.g. on the arrow from problem to solution, or as the header of the solution column ("Tallow Balm"). It's the hinge, not a footnote.

---

## PRINCIPLE 8: One bold bottom-line payoff in large script

Each winner closes with a single big-script line: "Build Muscle, Burn Fat, See the Difference." "Clear Your Mind, Unlock Your Potential." It's the second-largest text on the board after the headers.

**Rule:** end every whiteboard with one bottom-line in large BLACK script — the takeaway the reader leaves with. Identity/relief framed, not "soft skin." Examples: "Fourteen down to one. The tight feeling went with them." / "Water sits on top. Skin lets fat in. That's it."

---

## PRINCIPLE 9: Dwell time is the CPM lever — make the board reward reading

The cheap CPM is bought with **dwell time.** A diagram people stop and read fully signals quality content to Meta → cheaper inventory. A board scanned in one second does not. This is the measurable difference between B19C2 ($16-76) and B19C3 ($165).

**Rules that increase dwell:**
- Enough content to read for 3-5 seconds (a mechanism with 2-3 steps, or a comparison with 3-4 contrast points per side) — but never so dense it's unreadable on mobile
- A reading PATH the eye follows (arrows, left-to-right, top-to-bottom) so the reader is pulled through
- A small "aha" payoff at the end of the path (the bottom line) that rewards finishing
- Legibility at mobile feed size is non-negotiable — if it can't be read at thumb distance, dwell drops to zero

---

## PRINCIPLE 10: Compliance still applies — same banned phrases as native

The whiteboard format does NOT relax compliance. Same banned list as `Tallow_Ad_Creative_Principles.md` Principle 6:
- No "menopause," "anti-aging," "wrinkles," "moisturizer/serum/regimen" for our product
- No medical claims ("cures," "heals," "repairs from within")
- Functional low points only ("tight by noon"), never shame/medical
- Category words (serum, retinol) may appear ONLY as labels on the discarded problem-side products, never describing our balm

---

## THE FULLEST EXPRESSION: video > static

The winning examples are all **talking-head UGC video with the whiteboard behind the presenter.** The person supplies trust + narration; the whiteboard is the visual aid. Static whiteboard (our current B19/B20) works and is cheaper to produce — but the format's ceiling is video. When we have a face to put on camera (Julie, or a UGC creator), a video-in-front-of-whiteboard version is the highest-upside test. Park as the next format evolution after static whiteboard conversion is proven at volume.

---

## WHITEBOARD BUILD CHECKLIST — before generating any whiteboard image

- [ ] Two-column VS structure (problem left / solution right) unless a comparison genuinely doesn't fit
- [ ] Teaches a mechanism or resolves a question (curiosity payoff, not just a benefit claim)
- [ ] A body/face/organ carries the transformation (not pure abstraction)
- [ ] Bold color fills + exactly one emotional icon pair, on the locked tallow color convention
- [ ] Real product jar composited into the scene
- [ ] Product/mechanism name at the bridge between columns
- [ ] One bold bottom-line payoff in large script (identity/relief, not "soft skin")
- [ ] Reads for 3-5 seconds with a clear eye-path (dwell = CPM lever)
- [ ] Looks photographed/organic, not designed (real frame, hand-drawn, no brand polish)
- [ ] Legible at mobile feed size
- [ ] Compliance: no banned phrases, functional low points only
