# AD1: Why It Actually Worked (evidence vs guesses) · 2026-10-24

Source: founder's Ads Manager screenshot (last year, one CBO, 15 ads) + the AD1 video file.
**Rule for this doc:** only call something a cause if AD1 is *different* from the other ads on it. If every ad had it, it can't explain why AD1 won.

## The numbers that separate AD1

| Ad | Spend | Impr. | Hook rate (3s plays ÷ impr.) | CPM | CTR | CPC | Avg watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AD1** | **$838.77** | 27,203 | **49.7%** | **$30.83** | 4.23% | **$0.73** | 6 s |
| AD5 | $83.53 | 2,090 | 44.4% | $39.97 | 3.49% | $1.14 | 5 s |
| AD7 | $29.60 | 572 | 34.3% | $51.75 | 2.97% | $1.74 | 5 s |
| AD3 | $19.93 | 473 | 38.1% | $42.14 | 4.44% | $0.95 | 5 s |
| AD8 | $11.97 | 152 | 34.9% | $78.75 | 4.61% | $1.71 | 6 s |
(Ads under $5 spend left out: too little data.)

### What's unique to AD1
1. **Highest hook rate: ~50%** vs 34–44% for the rest. More people stopped scrolling.
2. **Lowest CPM: $30.83** vs $40–79 for every other ad. Meta charged less to show it.
3. CPC was the lowest **because of** 1 and 2.

### What's NOT unique to AD1 (so not the reason it won)
- **CTR:** AD3 (4.44%) and AD8 (4.61%) clicked as well or better. The caption/offer didn't out-click the others.
- **Watch time:** 5–6 s for everyone. The middle of the video (peel, game count, gameplay) didn't hold people longer than the other ads did.

**Conclusion: AD1 won in the first 1–2 seconds (the stop) and on delivery cost (CPM). It did NOT win on the click or on the middle of the video.**

## Causes, ranked

### 1. The opening doesn't look like an ad ⭐ strongest evidence
Every other ad's thumbnail/first frame is the **product in a hand or on a table**. AD1 opens on **a Target store at night**, then walks in. It reads as a "Target finds" TikTok, a format people already watch, so they stop before their "ad" filter kicks in. That matches the one metric where AD1 clearly beat the rest: the hook rate.

### 2. Comments → cheaper delivery ⭐ strong, needs one check
Its CPM was 25–60% lower than everything else. Meta charges less for ads people engage with. The "they don't sell this at Target" correction comments (triggered by the caption **and** the shelf shot) are the likely source. **Check:** the comment and share counts on AD1 vs the others.

### 3. The CBO snowball (a confound, not a creative reason)
Once AD1 got the early spend, it gathered data, delivery got cheaper, and it got more spend. The other ads were starved (most spent under $5). Some of AD1's lead may simply be that it went first. **Check:** the launch dates of each ad.

### 4. Season
It ran into December gift buying. Every ad shared that, so it helps explain why *any* ad worked, not why AD1 beat the rest.

### 5. The middle of the video (peel, "15,833 games", famous characters, gameplay)
Useful for selling once people are watching, but the watch time matched the other ads. **Keep it, don't iterate on it first.**

## What this changes for iterating

1. **Protect the opening.** Never start an iteration on the product. Keep the "I'm at a store" first 2–3 seconds.
2. **Test new openings in the same format first**, not caption swaps. The opening is the proven variable:
   - "RUN don't WALK to **Walmart**" + Walmart exterior footage
   - **GameStop / Best Buy / the mall** exterior at night
   - **Christmas version:** a decorated store or a tree, same caption
3. **Caption call-out swaps (partner, parents, grandparents) come second.** CTR wasn't where AD1 won, so these are lower-value tests. Run them on whichever opening wins.
4. **Track hook rate and CPM per ad**, not just ROAS. They're the two numbers AD1 won on, so they're the early signal for a new winner (target: hook ≥ 45%, CPM below the account average).
5. **Give each iteration its own spend floor** (ABO or minimum spend), so the CBO snowball doesn't starve tests again.
6. **The money problem is after the click.** ~1.3% of clicks bought. The new PDP + bundles is the lever there, not the ad.
