# Voiceover scripts: "I didn't know what to get him" (v1, 2026-11-11)

**Status:** drafts for founder review. **No ElevenLabs audio until a script is approved.**

## The brief (founder)
- **Problem:** "I don't know what to get this person for Christmas" (husband, boyfriend, dad, son; sometimes mom or daughter).
- **Ideal outcome:** the person loves the gift.
- **Proven selling point:** nostalgia. It lets them relive their childhood.
- **Must feel native.** If it opens like an ad, it gets scrolled. The Target "find" video won because people post that kind of video every day.
- **The hook should not make people ask "what are they selling?"** It should start a story.
- **End on the outcome (they loved it), not the offer.** The offer goes in the caption and on the PDP.

## Why the founder's draft works (and what the earlier scripts got wrong)
| Founder's draft does | Earlier scripts did |
|---|---|
| Opens with **her** news ("I found the perfect gift for my husband"). It reads like a friend's update | Opened with a claim about the product ("If he has everything…") |
| Names **her** problem ("I'm super lost with that stuff") | Skipped the problem |
| Nostalgia comes from **his** memories ("the games he told me he used to play") | Nostalgia was a spec ("every game he grew up on") |
| Ends with **him loving it** ("on it more than his computer") | Ended with features or the offer |

**The key move:** the viewer's "this would be perfect for my ___" moment happens when the story's person sounds like *her* person. So each script picks **one recipient** and **one specific memory**, so the right viewer feels it hard.

## Structure every script follows
1. **Hook (0–3 s): her situation, in her words.** A story opener, not a product line.
2. **Problem (3–7 s):** "I never know what to get him" in a specific, relatable way.
3. **Nostalgia turn (7–14 s):** a real-sounding memory of *his* (a game, a moment), then the find: it's all on here.
4. **Outcome (14–20 s):** a small, believable moment that shows he loves it.
5. **No offer, no price** in the voiceover.

Rules: 5th-grade words, no em dashes, short sentences, sounds spoken (contractions, "like", "okay"). One recipient per script.

## Script 1 · The sneaky question ⭐ (husband/boyfriend) · builds on the founder's draft
**Native format:** storytime with a tip ("here's what I did"). Common on TikTok all November.

**On-screen hook:** "I finally figured out what to get my husband 😭"
**Voiceover:**
> Okay, I think I finally figured out what to get my husband this year.
> He's really into games, and I know nothing about games. Like, nothing.
> So a few weeks ago I just casually asked him what he played as a kid. Mario Kart, Pokémon, Zelda. I wrote it all down in my notes.
> Then I found this. It has every single one of them on it. Plus like fifteen thousand more.
> I gave it to him early because I couldn't wait. He hasn't touched his PC since.

**Why it should work:** the "sneaky research" is a move she can copy (a reason to keep watching), and the list of games does the nostalgia without a spec sheet.
**Footage:** hook = her hand typing a list of game names in the phone Notes app (screen record, easy to make). Body = AD 7 white unboxing. Outcome = man's hands playing on a couch, tree lights behind (T01 hands-on footage, or generate).
**Variant hook for boyfriend:** swap "husband" for "boyfriend" (separate ad, same footage).

## Script 2 · "He says he doesn't want anything" (dad)
**Native format:** the yearly "my dad is impossible to shop for" complaint. Very common, very relatable.

**On-screen hook:** "my dad every year: 'I don't need anything'"
**Voiceover:**
> Every year my dad says he doesn't want anything for Christmas. And every year I panic.
> But he always tells this story about staying up all night playing Mario with his brother.
> So when I found this, I knew. Every game from when he was a kid, on one little handheld.
> He opened it and went quiet for a second. Then he just started playing.
> He's been texting me his high scores all week.

**Why it should work:** "I don't need anything" is the line every viewer has heard from someone. The ending is the emotional payoff (texting high scores = he loves it *and* it connects them).
**Footage:** hook = text over a shot of wrapped presents. Body = T09 gift bag or T03 red/teal unboxing. Outcome = phone screen with a text bubble ("beat my score from 1994 😤"), easy to mock up.

## Script 3 · The mom who remembers (adult son) · fits our real buyers (women, often multi-unit)
**Native format:** mom storytime. The nostalgia is hers too: she bought those games.

**On-screen hook:** "my son is 30 and I still never know what to get him"
**Voiceover:**
> My son is thirty, and I still never know what to get him for Christmas.
> But I remember exactly what he begged me for as a kid. Game Boy games. Every birthday, every Christmas.
> So I found this. It has all of them. Every single one I used to wrap for him, all in one.
> When he opened it, he just looked at me and said, "Mom. You remembered."

**Why it should work:** the nostalgia is *shared*, so it hits harder than "he grew up on these". The last line is the ideal outcome in four words. It also opens the door to a duo ("one for him and one for my grandson").
**Footage:** hook = an old Game Boy-era photo feel (generate: 90s kid on a carpet with a handheld, film look). Body = AD 7 unboxing. Outcome = a hand holding the console with a Game Boy game on screen.

## Script 4 · Reply to a comment (any recipient) · uses our proven comment-reply format
**Native format:** TikTok "replying to @user". T01 and T05 used it and got cheap delivery.

**On-screen hook (comment bubble):** "what do you even get a guy who has everything??"
**Voiceover:**
> Okay, I had the exact same problem with my boyfriend last year.
> He has everything. Every console, every gadget. I was so lost.
> Then I remembered he always talks about the games he played growing up. The old ones.
> So I got him this. Every one of those games, all on one handheld.
> He played it the whole drive to his parents' house. Didn't even pick the music.

**Why it should work:** the comment *is* the viewer's question, so the hook is her problem said by someone else. Very native, doesn't read as an ad.
**Footage:** hook = comment-reply sticker on AD 7's first frame (we already make the bubbles). Outcome = T04 (playing in a car) fits "the whole drive" perfectly.
