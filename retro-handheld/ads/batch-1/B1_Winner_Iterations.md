**Rule:** change ONE variable per iteration, so we know what moved. Keep the b-roll the same unless that is the variable being tested.

## Why it worked (best read, before seeing the video)

1. **Call-out:** "Gamers" picks the viewer in one word.
2. **Urgency with humour:** "RUN don't WALK" is a familiar phrase turned around. It reads like a friend's tip, not an ad.
3. **A real store name:** "Target" makes it feel like a find you can actually get, and suggests it's a mainstream product, not junk.
4. **Product b-roll:** the product is on screen from second 1, so clicks are pre-qualified (the opposite of the register fan's curiosity clicks).

### Founder insight: the comment engine (2026-10-16)
Lots of comments said **"they don't sell this at Target."** The hook invites a correction, and people love to correct. Comments → more engagement → Meta delivered it cheaper. The hook works *because* it is slightly "wrong."

**Comment replies (keep the thread going, never claim it's in Target):**
- "That's why you walk 😄 Save yourself the trip, link's in the post"
- "Exactly. Skip the trip, it ships to your door"
- To "where do I get it": "Right here! Tap Shop Now 👇"
- Pin one reply that says where to buy. Hide only spam or abuse, not "not at Target" comments: those are the fuel.

**Other likely reasons it worked:** tag-a-friend/gift comments ("@babe this is what I want") · nostalgia recognised in 1 second of b-roll · reads like a creator tip, not an ad · a broad call-out lets Meta find buyers · "Target" makes it feel mainstream, not junk · cheap-looking price for what it is · Q4 timing.

**Round 1b: hooks built to get corrected** (test after Round 1)
- "Gamers, RUN don't WALK to Walmart for this" (same mechanic, different store)
- "Nobody's talking about this at Target"
- "Found the Christmas gift every 90s kid wants" (invites "80s kid here" replies)
- Keep it a playful hook, never a false claim that a store stocks it.

What we keep in every iteration: the **"[Who], RUN don't WALK…"** structure + the product on screen from frame 1.

## AD1 shot-by-shot (from the video file, 2026-10-24)

**11.8 s · 9:16 · no voiceover · upbeat Christmas music · text only (works with sound off).** The exact hook is **"Gamers, RUN don't WALK to Target for this! 🎁"**, in a white TikTok-style caption bubble on screen the whole time. For the first 3.5 s there is also a second, larger copy at the top that flips mirrored (a native "glitchy edit" look). It's repurposed TikTok footage.

| Time | Shot | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.5 s | A real Target store at night, filmed from the parking lot | Hook: matches the caption, a "Target finds" haul video |
| 1.5–2.5 s | Walking through the doors | Story movement, POV |
| 2.5–3.5 s | Gaming aisle, the box on the shelf, picked up | "Found it." The product is shown as if it's sold at Target |
| 3.5–4.5 s | At home: translucent teal unit in hand, sticker on the back | Cut to ownership; the color pops |

| 8–9 s | Game list | "It's all on there" |

### Why it worked (confirmed by the footage)
1. **Native "Target finds" format.** It looks like a TikTok haul, not an ad. The store footage proves the caption, so it reads as a real tip.
2. **The video SHOWS it on a Target shelf.** That explains the "they don't sell this at Target" comments: people went looking, or knew better. The correction engine came from the visuals, not just the words.
3. **Instant recognition.** Famous characters and system logos on the screen do the nostalgia work in under a second.
4. **A value number on screen:** "15,833 games".
5. **Satisfying beat:** the film peel at ~5 s.
6. **Short and loopable:** 11.8 s with a 5–6 s average watch, so many people watched half and it looped.
7. **Gift context:** the 🎁 emoji + Christmas music; no voiceover needed.

### ⚠️ Risks to decide on before re-running (founder call)
- **Third-party IP on screen:** console logos and recognisable characters and titles. This breaks our standing "no real game names or screens" rule and is the most likely reason for an ad rejection or IP report this year. It's also probably part of *why* it worked. Options: (a) run the original as-is as the control, accepting the risk; (b) an IP-safe rebuild that blurs or crops the screens and leads with the "15,833 games" count and gameplay of non-famous titles. Test both.
- **Implied retail claim:** it shows the box on a Target shelf, but we sell online. Meta can flag this as misleading, and some buyers feel tricked. The caption is fine as a joke; the shelf shot is the risky part.
- **Footage rights:** the clips came from someone else's TikTok. Re-shooting the at-home part with our own unit removes that risk and lets us control colors and screens.
