## The selection filter

Before matching anything, our own constraints knock out whole families of format. **This is the most useful part of the exercise** — it stops us reaching for a swipe-file format that our rules forbid.

### ⛔ Disqualified outright

| Format | Why it's out |
|---|---|
| **The Free Ad** | Puts offer terms in the image. Banned, Part 8 rule 3 |
| **Classy Offer** | Same — the offer *is* the creative. Banned |
| **Specific Pain Point** *(as executed in the swipe file)* | The reference execution pairs the pain line with a **price slash + guarantee badge**. The pain-naming half is usable; the promotional furniture is not |
| **Stealing Credibility** | Requires naming a brand or trend. We never name a competitor (§9a-i), and there is no neutral cultural referent here |
| **Minimal Us vs Them** *(against a product)* | Banned — no competitor named, no feature comparison. **But see below: it is allowed against the contractor quote**, which is a non-product alternative |
| **Negative Review Inversion / Taboo / Common Saying Hijack** | All three are Brand-Aware or Product-Aware formats. We target Unaware → Problem Aware |
| **Product Photography + Headline** | We have no product photography, and hero-product ads are the register the incumbents already own |
| **Text-message / DM screenshot** | Killed on tallow — bad CTR, worse CPC |

### ✅ What the constraints leave, and why each survives

- **Indirect Avatar** — buyer ≠ user. Purpose-built for SA6.
- **Infographic / educational** — New Information is our required Stage 4 response.
- **This Is Not [Product]** — category reframing, Stage 4-5, and it happens to carry the box-fan pre-empt perfectly.
- **Simplified Us vs Them** — two images, transformation, no competitor.
- **Escalation / Exaggeration** — the workaround stack, and it's *true* here.
- **Relatable Hook** — names a situation, not a feature.
- **Solution Exaggeration** — staccato, specific, no product needed.
- **Quote / Text-Heavy** — carries the attribution lever.
- **Pubity Style** — feed-camouflage without long copy.
- **Accidental Ideal Outcome** — understated by construction, which matches our rescaled claim.

## 🔑 The research constraint that drives every image choice

> **They describe this problem in DEGREES, not metaphors.** Four targeted searches for icebox / meat locker / fridge / sauna returned nothing in their own words. What they reach for is a thermometer reading: *13° overnight*, *55 degrees*, *5 degrees colder*.

So: **numbers on screen beat imagery of cold.** No frost, no icicles, no shivering-person stock photos. A thermometer is both the most honest image we have and the one that matches how they already talk.

Second constraint: **the claim must stay partial.** *"Much more comfortable,"* never *"fixed"* or *"matches the rest of the house"* — that exact phrasing is burned (Part 8 rule 1b).

# The map

Six angles. Each gets one native and two non-natives in different visual registers, so no two cells for one angle can be taxed by the same familiarity.

---

## ANGLE 1 — SA1-A3: You don't need a contractor ⭐ BEST-EVIDENCED

*Sourced: "somewhere between $3-4,000" · "quoted over 3k" · "That seems rather high and I don't like the broad range" · and a case where the pro's fix failed and the homeowner ended on a space heater.*

| | Format | Execution |
|---|---|---|
| **Native** | Long-form (D) | The full sequence: the quote, the vagueness, what they did instead |
| **Non-native 1** | **Minimal Us vs Them** *(against the quote, not a product)* | Left: "Them" — the itemised quote, *$3,400*, three days, someone in your house, *"somewhere between"*. Right: two screws and an outlet. **"None of that."** The Carepod structure exactly — list *their* problems, let the viewer infer our benefit |
| **Non-native 2** | **Solution Exaggeration** | Staccato, specific, no product: **"Two screws. One outlet. Not $3,400."** Their own distrust is of the *vague range*, so our number is exact and theirs is the one that wobbles |

**Why these two:** the price contrast is the entire angle, and both formats deliver a contrast without a product shot. Us-vs-Them against a *service quote* is the one use of that format our rules permit — the comparison the page is allowed to make is the contractor, the thermostat, the space heater and doing nothing.

## ANGLE 2 — SA1-A1: You were right about the room. Here's what nobody told you.

*The New Information play. Nobody in the category explains why one room starves.*

| | Format | Execution |
|---|---|---|
| **Native** | Long-form (D) | Cold-framed. Self-diagnosis → the duct run → the contested vent-closing advice corrected |
| **Non-native 1** | **Infographic / duct-run diagram** | Cross-section: air handler → branches → last room gets a trickle. Hand-drawn register, not corporate. *The cheapest explanation to consume* |
| **Non-native 2** | **This Is Not [The Product]** | **"This isn't a fan for the room. It's a fan for the vent."** Category reframing in the Huel structure — and it simultaneously carries the box-fan pre-empt, which is the objection nearly every one of these buyers holds |

**Why "This Is Not" is the strongest non-native in the whole document:** the swipe file's own warning is *"it has to make sense, do not overstretch it."* Here the reframe is literally true and it does two jobs at once — it reframes the category *and* it answers the failed box fan without naming a competitor.

## ANGLE 3 — SA2: The space heater is a confession, not a fix

*Sourced: space heaters and oil-filled radiators are the dominant purchase in every pass. One poster runs a heater, a timed blanket and a heated bed pad.*

| | Format | Execution |
|---|---|---|
| **Native** | Long-form (D) | The stack of things bought, in order, and what each one didn't do |
| **Non-native 1** | **Escalation / Exaggeration** | One frame, the whole workaround stack in a real bedroom: space heater, second duvet, draft stopper at the door, heated throw. **True, not exaggerated** — the research describes exactly this. Caption: *"Everything in this room is here because the vent isn't working."* |
| **Non-native 2** | **Simplified Us vs Them** *(Nutella structure)* | Two images, six words. Left: a space heater. **"This is a second heater."** Right: a vent. **"This is the heat you already pay for."** |

**Why:** this angle is about *accumulation*, and accumulation is a visual idea. It needs no claim at all — the picture is the argument, which is the safest possible ground given rule 1b.

## ANGLE 4 — SA6-A1: You keep checking that room ⭐ BEST FORMAT FIT

*Sourced: Roxyrocks' 13° nursery and the guilt · tjschuldt noticing his son's room · Izzie1213 unhooking a working fan for a sound-sensitive son · buttonedup researching on her boyfriend's behalf.*

| | Format | Execution |
|---|---|---|
| **Native** | Long-form (D) | First person, checking the room at night, what they tried for someone else |
| **Non-native 1** | **Indirect Avatar** ⭐ | The swipe file's canonical buyer≠user format ("TEEN ALWAYS GRUMPY?" → the parent buys). Ours: **"THE COLDEST ROOM IN YOUR HOUSE IS THE ONE THEY SLEEP IN."** Question-headline to the buyer, mechanism beneath, no product hero |
| **Non-native 2** | **Two-panel thermometer (H)** | Hallway 21°. That room 15°. Nothing else in frame. **This is their own language** — they reach for a thermometer, not a metaphor |

**Why Indirect Avatar is the single best structural match in the catalog:** SA6 is *defined* by the buyer not being the user. That is the one condition this format exists for, and it is rated Stage 3-4, which is where we sit.

> 🚨 **Strictest claim rules in the project apply to every cell here.** Temperature, degrees, the monitor reading, the waking, the not-checking. **Never** SIDS, safe sleep, risk, danger, hazard, or any health framing. We sell the not-worrying, never the thing they're worried about.

## ANGLE 5 — SA6-A2: You're buying around it instead of fixing it

*Sourced: the cold-rated baby sleeping bag bought instead of heating the room; the heated bed pad "so at least the bed is warm."*

| | Format | Execution |
|---|---|---|
| **Native** | Long-form (D) | The things bought to work around one room, and the moment of noticing that's what they were |
| **Non-native 1** | **Accidental Ideal Outcome** | Understated by construction, which is exactly what rule 1b demands: **"I stopped buying things for that room by accident."** No overclaim available in this structure |
| **Non-native 2** | **Relatable Hook** | Names the situation, not the product, in the "12-HOUR SHIFT SHOES" structure: **"THE ROOM YOU BUY BLANKETS FOR."** Then outcome bullets beneath |

---

## ANGLE 6 — SA3: It's not your thermostat to touch

*Sourced: four landlord exchanges — the non-responsive one, the "I'll take a look at it," the one who won't raise the heat because lower-floor tenants are "dying down there," and the one who bought a portable radiator and cut rent $40.*

| | Format | Execution |
|---|---|---|
| **Native** | Long-form (D) | The renter's sequence: asking, being deflected, working around it |
| **Non-native 1** | **Quote / Text-Heavy** | The landlord line, set as a quote, attributed to a landlord. ***"They won't raise the heat because they are 'dying' down there."*** **This is the attribution lever** — the account's finding that the same sentence converts from a named third party and doesn't from the advertiser |
| **Non-native 2** | **Relatable Hook** | **"THE COLDEST APARTMENT IN A HEATED BUILDING."** Situation-naming, plus the reversibility beat: *it goes back exactly how you found it* |

**⚠️ Gate:** confirm the unit is genuinely reversible and needs only a standard outlet before spending on this angle. If a renter can't install it, we're advertising to people who can't buy.

# Register spread — the anti-familiarity check

The point of the portfolio is that no two cells look alike. Across the 18:

| Visual register | Cells |
|---|---|
| Long-form native | 6 (one per angle — **the cap**) |
| Comparison / split | 3 (Us vs Them, Simplified, Two-panel) |
| Typographic / statement | 3 (Solution Exaggeration, Relatable Hook ×2) |
| Explanatory / drawn | 2 (Infographic, This Is Not) |
| Documentary photo | 2 (Escalation stack, thermometer) |
| Quote | 1 |
| First-person understatement | 1 |

**Natives are exactly one third.** That satisfies the founder rule and the familiarity argument at the same time.

# Sequencing against the $600 cap

18 cells is far beyond one test. Ship in waves:

**Wave 1 — 4 cells, no persona needed, shootable now.** Angle 1 Solution Exaggeration · Angle 2 This Is Not · Angle 3 Escalation stack · Angle 4 Two-panel thermometer. One cell from each of the four strongest angles, four different registers, $50/day.

**Wave 2 — the natives**, once the persona page exists, against whichever angle Wave 1 favoured.

**Wave 3 — the remainder**, funded by what Wave 1 and 2 teach.

This inverts the earlier batch-1 plan (hold angle, vary format) into **hold nothing, vary both** — justified because each of the four Wave 1 cells is a different angle *and* a different register, so a clear winner tells us about both at once, and a flat result tells us the offer is the problem rather than the creative.
