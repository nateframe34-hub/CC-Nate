|---|---|
| **Core desire** | That room the same temperature as the rest of the house, **for someone they're responsible for** |

### Primary text

Kids kick the blankets off. Every kid. Every night.

None of that makes the room warmer. It just helps them live with a cold one.

Here's why that one room is cold. Your furnace heats the air in one place and pushes it through ducts to every room. The rooms closest to it get the most. The room at the end of the line gets whatever's left.

Put your hand on the vent in that room while the heat is on. If the air is barely moving, that's it.

This replaces the vent cover that's already there. Two small fans pull the warm air your furnace is already making into the room. And it has its own thermostat. Set it to 69. When the room drops below that, it runs. When it gets there, it stops.

So the room holds the number all night. Even after the blanket comes off.

Two screws and a plug. Fits 4x10 and 6x10 floor vents, with a free size swap if you measure wrong.

## B3C3: Accidental Ideal Outcome

**Why this template:** the word "accidentally" makes the reader ask *how?*, and the swipe's "unique numbers" (12 pounds, 52, 56.4%) make it believable. Our outcome is the behaviour that stops (the nightly check), which is the A3 angle itself, stated as a result.
**Copy the swipe's parts:** dark charcoal background · a two-line handwritten-style headline in white · the product photo with a thick white sticker-style outline on the left · three bullet points in the same handwritten font on the right · a tiny disclaimer at the bottom.

### In-image text
> # I Accidentally Stopped Checking His Room At Night
> • His room went from 63° to 69°
> • Blanket stays off. Room stays warm.
> • Same two screws as the old vent
> *small:* Results vary by home.

### Primary text

I didn't set out to stop checking on him. It just happened.

My grandson sleeps over every Friday, in the spare room at the end of our hall. That room runs cold. For most of last winter I walked down the hall three times a night to put his blanket back on.

Then I put a cheap thermometer in there. Hallway thermostat: 70. His room: 63.

That room is at the end of the duct run. The warm air from the furnace reaches the rooms close to it first. By the time it gets to his room, it's a trickle.

So I swapped his vent cover for one with two small fans and its own thermostat. It pulls the warm air the furnace is already making into the room. I set it to 69.

The second Friday, I slept until six.

He still kicks the blanket off. The room just doesn't get cold anymore.

$89.99 for our Fall Sale. Two screws and a plug, and 60 days to try it.
