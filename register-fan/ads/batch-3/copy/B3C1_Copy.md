## Body Copy

My grandson Theo sleeps over on Friday nights.

He's four. He sleeps in our spare room at the end of the hall. And for most of last winter, I walked down that hall three times a night to check on him.

Not because anything was wrong. Because the room was cold.

----------

The first time I noticed was in November. I tucked him in at 7:30 and the room felt fine. At ten, I went in to grab a book and my arms got goosebumps.

He'd kicked the blanket off. His feet were like ice.

I put the blanket back on him. Then I went to bed and lay there thinking about it.

At midnight I got up and checked again. Blanket off. Cold feet.

At three, same thing.

That was the start of my Friday routine. Three trips down the hall. Every Friday. All winter.

----------

So I did what I think a lot of grandmas would do. I bought things.

A thicker comforter. Fleece pajamas with feet on them. A second blanket for the end of the bed. A little space heater. I ran it for one night, then decided I didn't want it running in his room all night.

My husband Dave said, "He's fine. Kids run warm."

He's right that Theo was fine. But I wasn't. I was the one getting up at three in the morning.

----------

Then my daughter Megan put a cheap baby monitor in there that shows the room temperature. Mostly so I'd stop texting her about it.

The hallway thermostat said 70.

The monitor in Theo's room said 63.

Seven degrees. Same house. Same night. Same furnace running.

That's when I stopped buying blankets.

Because I realized every single thing I'd bought was a way to live with a cold room. Not one of them made the room any warmer. A thicker comforter only works if a four-year-old stays under it. And no four-year-old stays under anything.

----------

Here's the part that's a little embarrassing.

I already knew why that room was cold. I just hadn't put it together.

Last summer I had the opposite problem with my upstairs office. It was the hot room. My sister Kerry finally explained it to me. Your furnace or AC makes the air in one place, then pushes it through ducts to every room. The rooms close to the unit get it first, and they get a lot. Every turn and every branch takes a little away. The room at the end of the line gets what's left.

My office was at the end of one line.

Theo's room is at the end of the other.

I went in and put my hand on his vent while the heat was running. Warm air was coming out. Barely. A trickle. The same trickle I'd felt in my office in July.

----------

I'll be honest. Part of me didn't want to buy one more thing for that room. I already had a closet full of blankets I'd bought for it.

But I'd seen what fixed my office. It's a vent cover with two small fans built in. It pulls the air out of the duct and into the room, instead of waiting for it to drift in. It's called an Evenroom.

And the part that matters for a kid's room is the little screen on the front. It has its own thermostat. You set the temperature you want for that room. When the room drops below it, it runs. When it gets there, it stops.

So I ordered a second one. $89.99.

----------

It took about ten minutes. Two screws out, the old cover off, the new one in the same hole, same two screws. I plugged it in and set it to 69.

The first Friday, I still got up at ten. Habit. The monitor said 68. I stood in the doorway for a minute, then went back to bed.

At midnight I didn't get up.

The second Friday, I slept until six. I woke up and didn't know where I was for a second, because I couldn't remember the last time I'd slept through a Friday.

Theo had kicked his blanket off again, of course. He was sprawled sideways across the bed in his regular pajamas. The fleece ones were still in the drawer. He didn't need them.

----------

The thing I didn't expect was Megan.

She picked him up that Saturday and he was chatty and wide awake. She said, "Did he sleep okay? He's in a good mood."

I said, "I think we both slept."

She looked at me funny. Then she went and looked at the little screen on the vent. "Mom. Why didn't you do this last year?"

Good question.

----------

It's not magic. That room is still the coolest room in the house on a bad January night. It's over the garage, and there's only so much you can do about that.

But it holds the number I set. The monitor reads 68 or 69 all night now. And the blanket pile in the hall closet hasn't moved since December.

Dave was right that Theo was fine. What changed was me. I sleep through Fridays now.

My friend Renee at work put one in her son's room over their garage. She told me the same thing I would have told you. "I didn't realize how many times a night I was getting up until I stopped."

A lot of people I know have a room like that. The one you check.

----------

If you have one too, try what we did. Put a cheap thermometer in that room and compare it to the thermostat on the same night. If it's a lot colder, it's probably not the room. It's where the room sits on the line.

There's a 60-day return if it doesn't help. I didn't need it. Twice now.

The link is below.

I used to walk down that hall three times a night. Now I walk down it once, in the morning, to see what Theo did with his blanket.
