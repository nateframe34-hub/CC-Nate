## Primary text

Nine degrees, one hallway apart, in the same house, on the same afternoon. With a fan running in the hot one all day.

The fan isn't broken and neither is the air conditioning. A fan moves the air that's already in the room. It doesn't bring any in. So it cools whoever's sitting in front of it and leaves the room at the temperature it found it.

The reason that one room runs hot is where it sits. Cold air gets made in one place and pushed through ducts to everywhere else. The first rooms on the run get it at full strength. Every branch and bend takes a little off. The last room gets the leftovers.

Which is why the vent in there can be blowing — you can feel it on your hand — and the room still never catches up. There's a difference between air coming out and enough air arriving.

This is a vent cover with fans in it. It goes in the same hole, with the same two screws, and pulls the cold air the rest of the way in rather than waiting for it to turn up. You set a temperature for that one room and leave it alone.

It won't make the hot room the coolest room in the house. It'll stop it being the one nobody uses in the afternoon.
