# B1C1b: The Nap I Couldn't Take

**Sub-Avatar:** SA1 The Fan Runner (sleep payoff)
**Angle:** A1, Cools the room instead of just moving the hot air around it *(Supporting: A5 a fan only cools the spot in front of it · A2 the fans go away)*
**Character type:** Peer + Skeptic Convert
**Hook format:** Scene + recognition + open loop
**Awareness level:** Problem Aware → Solution Aware
**Page:** Julie Brennan. Same upstairs room as B1C1 (west window, desk and a bed). Second native for Batch 1, run head to head with B1C1.
**Mechanism, per founder (first-hand):** the vent doesn't stop the sun. It pulls in enough cold air that the room stops climbing when the sun hits it.

## Body Copy

I got home from work at 3:15 last July and all I wanted was a nap.

It had been one of those weeks. I'd been up at 5:30 every day, and by Thursday I was running on fumes. I told my husband Dave I was going upstairs for an hour and nobody was allowed to wake me up.

I lasted twenty minutes.

----------

Our upstairs room has a bed in it. It also has a big window that faces west.

From about three o'clock on, the sun comes straight through that window and lands right on the bed. I'd pulled the blinds. Didn't matter. The room felt like a thousand degrees.

I lay there on top of the covers. Then I threw off the sheet. Then I flipped my pillow to the cold side, which stopped being cold in about a minute.

When I finally gave up and sat up, there was a damp outline of me on the sheet.

I went downstairs and lay on the couch instead. I didn't sleep there either. I was too annoyed.

----------

The next day I put a thermometer up there. At four in the afternoon it said 84.

The rest of the house was 72.

So I did what I think everybody does. I tried to fix it.

----------

First, blackout curtains. The heavy ones with the silver backing. They made the room dark, which was nice for a nap. But it was still 82 in there. Now it was just a dark, hot room.

Then a box fan, pointed at the bed.

Then a second fan, a tall one by the window, because one obviously wasn't enough.

And here's the thing. When I lay right in front of the box fan, I felt okay. But I don't sleep in one spot. I roll over. And the second I rolled out of that stream of air, I was hot again.

It took me way too long to figure out what was going on.

A fan doesn't make a room cooler. It just blows the air that's already in the room across your skin. If that air is 84 degrees, you're getting blasted with 84-degree air. It feels better while it's hitting you. The room stays at 84.

I'd bought two fans to cool a room. And a fan can't cool a room. It can only cool the spot right in front of it.

----------

We had an HVAC guy out the summer before. He said the AC was working fine. He was right. The kitchen was fine. The living room was fine. Dave was in the basement wearing a hoodie.

So why was this one room so bad?

My sister Kerry is the one who finally explained it. She had the same problem in her back bedroom.

Your AC cools the air in one place. Then it pushes it through ducts to every room in the house. The rooms close to the unit get the cold air first, and they get a lot of it. Every turn and every branch takes a little away. The room at the end of the line gets what's left.

Our upstairs room is at the end of the line.

I went up and put my hand on the vent. Cold air was coming out. Barely. A trickle.

And that's the part that clicked for me. The sun was heating that room up every afternoon, sure. But a room that's getting plenty of cold air can fight that off. Mine couldn't. It was getting a trickle against a wall of sun, and the sun was winning by three o'clock every single day.

----------

Kerry's husband had put a new vent cover in their back bedroom. It has two small fans built in. They pull the cold air out of the duct and push it into the room, instead of waiting for it to drift in.

I'll be honest, I rolled my eyes. I'd already bought two fans and a set of curtains for that room. I didn't need a third fan.

"It's not a fan for the room," Kerry said. "It's a fan for the vent."

It was $89.99. It's called an Evenroom. I ordered one mostly so she'd stop talking about it.

----------

Dave put it in on a Sunday morning. Two screws came out, the old cover came off, the new one went in the same hole with the same two screws. He plugged it into the outlet by the bed. It has a little screen on the front, so I set it to 72.

Monday at four I went up and checked. 78. Better, but not great.

Tuesday, 76.

By the end of the week, at the worst part of the afternoon, it was 74.

Here's what I didn't expect. It's not that the sun stopped. The sun still comes through that window every day at three. I can still feel it on my arm if I stand there. But the room doesn't climb anymore. It used to start at 74 at lunch and hit 84 by four. Now it starts at 72 and sits around 74 all afternoon.

The cold air finally gets there fast enough to keep up.

----------

That Friday I came home, went upstairs, and slept for an hour and a half.

I woke up under the sheet. Not on top of it. I lay there for a second trying to remember the last time that had happened in July.

----------

Dave noticed before I said anything. He came up the next weekend to grab something and stood in the doorway. "It's not bad up here," he said. Then, "Can I use this room for naps too?"

No, Dave.

My daughter Megan was over a few weeks later and took her son upstairs for his nap. She came back down and said, "Did you get a new AC up there?"

Nope. Same AC. It just shows up now.

----------

It's not magic. On the hottest afternoons, that room is still the warmest one in the house. There's a big west window, and I'm not going to pretend there isn't.

But it doesn't turn into an oven anymore. And I can take a nap in it.

Both fans are in the garage. The blackout curtains are still up, because I like the dark. But they're not doing the work anymore.

I told my coworker Renee about it, and she put one in her bedroom. Same thing, a west window. She texted me a week later: "Took a nap. On a Tuesday. In August."

A lot of people I know have a room like this. The one you can't use after three o'clock.

----------

If you've got one too, go put your hand on the vent in there with the AC running. If air is coming out but barely moving, the sun isn't the whole problem. It just has nothing to fight against.

There's a 60-day return if it doesn't help. I read that part twice. I didn't need it.

I came home exhausted that day in July and couldn't take a nap in my own house. Now that room is where I go to take one.
