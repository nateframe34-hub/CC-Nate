# BATCH 1 = ANGLE 1 — SA1-A3: "You don't need a contractor"

**Why this angle leads the batch:** it is the **best-evidenced angle we have.** Sourced verbatim — *"somewhere between $3-4,000"*, *"quoted over 3k"*, *"That seems rather high and I don't like the broad range"* — plus a documented case where the professional's fix (a sheet-metal baffle) *"didn't help much"* and the homeowner ended up on a 500W space heater anyway.

**The insight the whole ad set rests on:** their distrust is not only of the *price*. It's of the **vagueness**. "Somewhere between $3-4,000" is a $1,000 shrug. So every ad here answers vagueness with precision — our number is exact and small; theirs is large and wobbles.

---

## B1C1 — Native long-form · runs from the Julie Brennan page

**Format:** Native / camouflage, long primary text · **TOF** · **Right column EXCLUDED** (can't render primary text)

**What it tests:** whether the full argument, told as one person's sequence, out-converts a single-frame claim. This is the only format that can carry the whole chain — the quote, the vagueness, the failed professional fix, the thing they did instead.

**Thought process.** The account's best-ever ad (B17C1) worked on the **attribution lever**: the same mechanism sentence produced 12 purchases when quoted from a named person with nothing to gain, and 0 across ~1,002 impressions when asserted by the advertiser. A native is the only format where that lever is available at full strength. The story premise is handed to us by the research — someone got a quote, balked at the range, and solved it for under a hundred dollars.

**Story premise (to be written through `native-ad-writer`):**
Homeowner with one cold room. Two winters of putting it off. Gets a contractor out; the quote is "somewhere between $3,000 and $4,000" and the vagueness bothers them more than the number. They don't do it. A third winter. Then they find out the room is simply last on the duct run — nobody had told them that — and that the fix is a vent cover with fans in it. Room closes most of the gap. **Not all of it.** That partial outcome is the credibility.

**Beat structure:**
1. The quote, and the word "somewhere"
2. Two winters of not doing it
3. The workarounds that stacked up in the meantime
4. The explanation nobody had given them — the duct run
5. What they did instead: two screws and an outlet
6. **Partial result, stated honestly** — the gap closed from six degrees to about one
7. No offer terms in the image; price appears in text only, late

**Image:** Format F — authorless POV. A hand held flat against a floor register, phone-camera quality, real domestic room slightly out of focus behind it. No product. No text.

> **Image prompt:** *Candid iPhone photo, first-person point of view, looking down at a beige metal floor register in a carpeted bedroom. A woman's hand is held flat, palm down, an inch above the vent slats, the way you'd check whether air is coming out. Late afternoon winter light from a window out of frame, slightly cool and flat. The carpet is a real lived-in beige, one corner of a bed visible at the top edge, a power outlet on the baseboard behind. Ordinary, unstyled, slightly imperfect framing. Real phone-camera quality with mild grain. No text, no overlays, no product.* 4:5, 1080×1350.

## B1C2 — Solution Exaggeration (statement static)

**Format:** Typographic statement · **TOF** · all placements

**What it tests:** whether the angle survives with **no image at all** — the cheapest, most placement-portable carrier we have. If this performs, we have a format we can produce ten variants of in an hour.

**Thought process.** The swipe file's Solution Exaggeration format ("14+ HOURS. NO SWELLING.") works through **staccato declaration plus a specific, odd number.** Round numbers read as fabricated; measured numbers read as true. We have a real number from the research and it's usefully specific. The structure also inverts the trust dynamic: our figure is exact, theirs is a range. And it sits maximally far from a native in visual register, which is the anti-familiarity job.

**The creative:**

> ## Six degrees warmer.
> ## Not $3,400.

Terracotta on warm off-white, Archivo, very large, nothing else in frame. Both **`Six degrees`** and **`$3,400`** in mono tabular figures.

**Why this line and not "Two screws and an outlet. Not $3,400."** That earlier version led with an *install feature*. Per Part 8.1d the outcome leads: what they want is a warmer room, not an easier screwdriver. "Two screws" survives in the primary text, where it removes friction *after* the outcome has done the selling. No product, no logo lockup beyond a small wordmark, no offer terms.

> **Design direction:** *Plain typographic static, 4:5. Warm off-white background `#FAF7F2`. Two lines of large, tightly-set Archivo bold in near-black `#1C1A17`, left-aligned, generous margins. "Six degrees" and "$3,400" both set in monospace tabular figures, "$3,400" in terracotta `#B4553A`. Small Evenroom wordmark bottom-left in terracotta at 40% scale of body. Nothing else. No product, no icon, no badge, no price, no CTA.*

**Primary text direction:** the short version of the native — the quote, the vagueness, the duct run, the partial result. 120-180 words.

**Headline:** *The quote was "somewhere between $3,000 and $4,000."*
**Description:** *It's the last room on the duct run.*

## B1C3 — Minimal Us vs Them (vs the quote) ⚠️ leans MOF

**Format:** Split comparison · **TOF, but see caveat** · all placements

**What it tests:** whether a comparison works on a cold reader when the "them" is **a service quote rather than a competing product.**

**Thought process.** The Carepod structure is the strongest version of this format: list *their* problems, then say almost nothing about yourself. *"None of that."* The persuasion is that the viewer infers your benefit themselves — self-generated conclusions are more trusted than stated ones. Using it against a contractor quote does two things at once: it keeps us clear of the competitor-naming rule entirely, and it targets the vagueness rather than just the price.

**⚠️ Honest caveat.** Comparison formats assume the reader is weighing options, which is MOF behaviour. This is the one ad in the batch that may underperform cold for structural reasons rather than because the angle is wrong. **If it underdelivers, do not read that as a verdict on angle A3** — read B1C1 and B1C2 for that.

**The creative:**

Left panel, labelled **The quote**:
- "Somewhere between $3,000 and $4,000"
- Three days
- Someone in your house
- Booked for next month

Right panel, labelled **This**:
- **Six degrees warmer.**

*(Was "Two screws." — an install feature. The outcome leads; "two screws" moves to the small print beneath, where it removes friction after the outcome has sold.)*

> **Design direction:** *Split static, 4:5. Left half warm grey `#EDE9E3`, right half terracotta `#B4553A`. Left: four short lines in near-black, each with a small dash, "The quote" as a small caps label above. Right: "This" as the label, then "Six degrees warmer." in large white Archivo bold with "six degrees" in mono tabular, one small line beneath in 40% opacity white: "Two screws and an outlet." Generous whitespace. Dollar figures in monospace tabular. No product, no price for our item, no CTA, no badge.*

**Headline:** *Six degrees warmer.*
**Description:** *The other option was three days and a range.*
