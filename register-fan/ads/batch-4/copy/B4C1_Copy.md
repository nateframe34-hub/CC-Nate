## Body Copy

Every night at 9:30 last winter, my husband Dave did the same thing.

He'd walk into our bedroom, bend down behind the dresser, and plug in the heater.

Then he'd say, "Give it twenty minutes." And we'd go watch TV until the room was warm enough to get into bed.

----------

Our bedroom is over the garage. It's the coldest room in the house from October to April. It always has been.

The first fall we lived here, Dave dug an oil-filled radiator out of the basement. The kind that looks like a little brown accordion. He set it up behind the dresser and that was that.

It worked. I'll give it that. By ten the room was okay.

But I never liked it.

I never liked leaving it on while we slept. So I'd turn it off before I fell asleep. By 2am the room was cold again. I'd pull the blanket up over my ears and wait for morning.

And in the morning, the room was 61 and the bathroom floor was ice.

----------

Here's what bugged me most, and it took me a while to say it out loud.

Our furnace was running. The whole time.

The kitchen was warm. The living room was warm. Dave's basement office was warm enough that he wore a hoodie down there instead of a coat.

We were paying to heat the whole house. And then paying again, every night, to heat one room with a second little heater. Two things heating one room.

That didn't seem right. But I didn't know what else to do.

----------

We tried the obvious stuff.

A thicker comforter. Flannel sheets. A draft stopper at the bottom of the door. I bought a heated mattress pad. Now the bed was warm and the room was still cold. Getting out of bed in the morning was worse.

We even turned the thermostat up two degrees. The kitchen got hot. Dave got grumpy. Our bedroom barely moved.

I thought maybe it was the windows. Or the insulation over the garage. I figured we were looking at some big expensive project, and I wasn't ready for that.

----------

My sister Kerry is the one who explained it. She'd already helped me with my upstairs office last summer, which was the hot room.

"Put your hand on the vent in your bedroom while the furnace is running," she said.

I did. Warm air was coming out. Barely. A trickle.

Here's what's going on, and I wish someone had told me years ago.

Your furnace heats the air in one place. Then it pushes it through ducts to every room. The rooms closest to it get the warm air first, and they get a lot of it. Every turn and every branch takes a little away.

The room at the end of the line gets what's left.

Our bedroom is at the end of the line. It's over the garage, out past everything else. By the time the warm air got there, it was a trickle.

So the furnace was doing its job. The heat just wasn't arriving.

And that radiator behind the dresser wasn't fixing anything. It was making up for the heat we'd already paid for that never showed up.

----------

I already had one of these in my office from the summer, so I knew what it was.

It's a vent cover with two small fans built into it. It pulls the warm air out of the duct and into the room, instead of waiting for it to drift in. It has its own little thermostat on the front. You set the temperature you want for that room, and it runs until the room gets there.

It's called an Evenroom. $89.99.

Dave was skeptical. "So it's a fan," he said. He'd watched me buy a lot of things for that room.

"It's a fan for the vent," I said. "Not for the room."

He gave me a look. But he put it in.

----------

It took him about ten minutes on a Saturday. Two screws out of the old vent cover. Old cover off. New one in the same hole. Same two screws. He plugged it in and I set it to 68.

That first night, Dave went to plug in the radiator at 9:30 out of habit. I said, "Let's just see."

At ten, the room was 66. Not toasty. But fine.

By the end of the first week, it was holding 68 by the time we went to bed. And it was still 67 at six in the morning. I didn't have to turn anything off before I fell asleep, because there was nothing to turn off.

The thing I didn't expect was the bathroom floor. The bathroom shares a wall with our bedroom. For the first time in years, it wasn't ice in the morning.

----------

About two weeks in, I came home from work and the radiator was gone from behind the dresser.

Dave had wiped it down, wound up the cord, and put it back in its box in the basement.

"Figured we don't need it," he said. Like it was his idea.

I let him have that one.

----------

It's not magic. Our bedroom is over a garage. On the worst nights in January, it's still the coolest room in the house.

But it's a room we can walk into at night without waiting twenty minutes first. And nobody in our house has plugged anything in behind that dresser since November.

I told my coworker Renee about it, and she had the same thing going on in her son's room. Space heater, every night, all winter. She got one too. She told me her son asked where the heater went. Then he just stopped asking.

A lot of people I know have a heater like that. The one that comes out every October.

----------

If you've got one too, try this. Turn the heater off one night and put your hand on that room's vent while the furnace is running. If air is coming out but it's barely moving, the heat is being made. It just isn't getting there.

There's a 60-day return if it doesn't help. I didn't need it.
