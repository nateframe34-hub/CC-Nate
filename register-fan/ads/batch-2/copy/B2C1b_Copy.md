## Body Copy

I turned our thermostat down two degrees at a time for most of last summer.

It never once changed the room I was trying to fix.

----------

It was a Tuesday in July. 3:10 in the afternoon. I was at my desk upstairs with a wet ring under my water glass and my shirt stuck to the back of the chair.

So I did what I always did. I walked down the stairs to the thermostat in the hallway. It said 72. I pushed the down arrow twice. 70.

Then I went back up and sat down and waited.

At four I went down and made it 68.

At five I stopped pretending and carried my laptop to the kitchen table.

----------

Here's the part I didn't notice for a long time.

When I turned the house down, the house got colder. The kitchen got cold. The living room got cold. My husband Dave works in the basement, and he started keeping a blanket over his knees at his desk. In July.

My office didn't change at all.

I only figured that out because I bought a little $9 thermometer and put it on my desk. I wanted proof that the room was as bad as I said it was.

It was worse.

Hallway thermostat: 68.

My office: 81.

I had the whole house set to 68 degrees and my office was sitting at 81. Thirteen degrees. Same house, same afternoon, same AC running as hard as it could.

----------

I stood there with that little thermometer in my hand and felt pretty dumb.

Because I'd been doing this for two summers. Every hot day. Down two degrees, wait, down two more, give up, go to the kitchen. I thought I was cooling my office. I was cooling everything except my office.

The fans hadn't worked either. I had a box fan on the floor and a tower fan by the window. They felt nice while they blew on me. But a fan can't make a room colder. It just moves the hot air around.

We'd even had an HVAC guy out the August before. He checked everything. He said the system was fine and the thermostat was fine. Both were true. That was the frustrating part.

As he was leaving, he said, "Some rooms are just far from the unit."

I didn't know what that meant yet.

----------

My sister Kerry explained it on the phone a few weeks later. She'd had the same problem with her back bedroom.

"Where's your thermostat?" she asked.

"In the hallway."

"Then that's the only room it knows about."

Here's what she meant.

The thermostat can only feel the air right next to it. When the hallway gets to the number you set, it thinks the whole house got there. So it shuts the AC off.

It has no idea my office is still at 81. Nothing in the house does.

And there's a second part. Your AC makes the cold air in one place and pushes it through ducts to every room. The rooms close to the unit get it first, and they get a lot. Every turn takes a little away. The room at the end of the line gets what's left.

My office is at the end of the line. So the cold air was being made. Air was coming out of my vent. I could feel it with my hand. It was just a trickle.

That's why turning the house down never worked. I was asking the whole house to change so one room might get a little more of a trickle.

----------

Kerry's husband had fixed their back bedroom with a new vent cover. It has two small fans in it that pull the cold air out of the duct and into the room.

But the part that sold me was the little screen on the front.

It's a thermostat. Just for that room.

You set the temperature you want in that one room. If the room is warmer than that, it runs. When the room gets there, it stops. The thermostat in the hallway goes back to doing its job for the rest of the house.

I'll be honest. I rolled my eyes a little. I had two fans, blackout curtains and a $9 thermometer already sitting in that room. I didn't want one more thing.

But it was $89.99, and the HVAC guy's quote for "balancing the ducts" had a comma in it. So I ordered one. It's called an Evenroom.

----------

It took ten minutes on a Sunday. I took the two screws out of the old vent cover. I dropped the new one in the same hole and put the same screws back in. Then I plugged it into the outlet by the baseboard.

I set it to 73.

Then I went downstairs and put the house thermostat back to 74. That felt like a leap of faith.

Monday, not much. I was expecting a fridge and it wasn't one.

Wednesday at three, my little thermometer said 76.

By Friday it said 75, at the worst part of the afternoon.

The thing I didn't expect was my feet. I didn't realize I'd been walking up and down those stairs five or six times a day to push that down arrow. By the second week, I just didn't. There was nothing to go down there for. The room had its own number.

----------

Around week three, Dave came upstairs to ask me something. He stood in the doorway and said, "Huh. It's not bad up here."

Then he said, "Is that why the house is warmer?"

I told him I'd put it back to 74. That night the blanket went back in the hall closet. I think he'd been waiting two summers to put it away.

----------

It's not magic. On a bad afternoon, my office is still the warmest room in the house. It has a big west window, and there's only so much you can do about the sun.

But it's a room I can work in now. The fans are in the garage. And the thermostat in the hallway stays on 74 all day, every day.

I told my coworker Renee about it, and she got one for her son's room over the garage. A week later she texted me a photo of the little screen. It said 72. Then she wrote, "I've been turning our whole house down for that room for three years."

A lot of people I know have been doing the same thing.

----------

If you have one room that's always hotter than the rest, try this. Put a cheap thermometer in that room. Then go look at your thermostat in the hallway on the same afternoon.

If the numbers don't match, the thermostat isn't broken. It just can't feel that room.

There's a 60-day return if it doesn't help. I read that part twice. I didn't need it.
