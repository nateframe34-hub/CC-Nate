# BATCH 2 = ANGLE 2 — SA1-A1: "You were right about the room"

**Why this angle exists:** the **New Information play** — the one thing genuinely unowned in this category. Nobody explains *why* a specific room starves. Competitors claim improvements ("300% more airflow"); none explains the problem.

**⚠️ What this angle is NOT any more.** It used to be *"It isn't you, and it isn't your AC."* **The self-blame premise was retracted 2026-09-18** — three direct searches found nothing, and every self-diagnosis quote blames the house, the builder or the duct run, never the occupant. An ad opening with absolution answers an objection these people don't have. The surviving beat is **confirmation plus explanation**: you were right, and here's the part nobody told you.

---

## B2C1 — Native long-form · runs from the Julie Brennan page

**Format:** Native / camouflage · **TOF** · **Right column EXCLUDED**

**What it tests:** whether the explanation lands harder as a story than as a diagram. Same format as B1C1, different argument — so across the batch we also get a clean native-vs-native angle read.

**Thought process.** This is the angle where a native has the most room, because the mechanism takes a few sentences to land and a static has to compress it to a line. The story should arrive at the explanation the way the research subjects did — from a stranger, not from a company. The strongest real-world beat available: contested advice. Some forum posters recommend closing vents in other rooms to force air toward the cold one; more knowledgeable posters say flatly *"You really can't close registers and think air will be 'forced' into a different part of the house,"* and one person who tried it made a different room worse. **Correcting a piece of advice the reader has probably already been given is the most valuable thing in this ad.**

**Beat structure:**
1. The room, and the comparison that makes it obvious
2. Their own theory — corner room, over the garage, farthest from the furnace
3. The advice they were given: close the other vents
4. It didn't work, and one other room got worse
5. **The explanation** — last on the run, pressure lost at every branch, a trickle instead of a push
6. Hand on the vent — the checkable moment
7. What changed, partially

**Image:** Format F variant — the vent itself, no hand. Authorless.

> **Image prompt:** *Candid iPhone photo of a white wall register low on a bedroom wall, shot slightly off-angle from standing height, as if someone crouched to look at it. Winter afternoon light, cool and flat. A strip of beige carpet, a baseboard, the corner of a nightstand leg at the frame edge. Utterly ordinary, slightly imperfect framing, mild grain. No text, no overlay, no hand, no product.* 4:5, 1080×1350.

## B2C2 — Duct-run diagram (infographic) ⭐

**Format:** Explanatory diagram · **TOF** · all placements

**What it tests:** the **New Information response delivered at the lowest possible comprehension cost.** If this wins, the category's whole opportunity is education, and the PDP's mechanism section becomes the most important thing we own.

**Thought process.** The tallow precedent is the reason this is designed carefully rather than confidently: **B32C3 put an optical claim on a whiteboard and died at $36.53 with 0 purchases**, because there was no physical process to draw — the board carried no information and became decoration. The test the post-mortem implies: *is there a real spatial process here?* Yes. Air loses pressure at every branch and bend; the last room gets a trickle. That is genuinely drawable, and drawing it is the clearest explanation a reader can receive in one glance.

Hand-drawn register, not corporate infographic — a corporate one reads as a brand asserting; a sketched one reads as someone explaining.

> **Design direction:** *Hand-drawn-feeling cross-section diagram, 4:5, on warm off-white `#FAF7F2`. A simplified house in section: furnace/air handler at the left, one main duct running right, four branch ducts peeling off upward into four rooms. Arrows in terracotta `#B4553A` show airflow, drawn THICK at the first room and progressively thinner at each branch until the last arrow is a thin wisp. The final room is tinted with the diagram-cool slate `#5B7185`; the first three rooms are untinted. Small mono tabular labels on each room: 70°, 69°, 68°, 62°. One handwritten-feeling line beneath: "Every branch takes its cut." Ink-like irregular strokes, not vector-perfect. No product, no logo beyond a small wordmark, no price, no CTA.*

**Headline:** *Why the last room never keeps up*
**Description:** *It isn't the furnace. It's the distance.*

## B2C3 — This Is Not [The Product] ⭐ strongest single non-native

**Format:** Category reframe · **TOF** · all placements

**What it tests:** whether **reframing the category** beats explaining the mechanism. It also carries the box-fan pre-empt — the objection nearly every one of these buyers holds, because nearly all of them have already tried a fan and concluded fans don't work.

**Thought process.** The Huel structure — *"This is a meal. Not a protein shake."* — works by anticipating a misread and correcting it, which opens a curiosity gap: *if it's not that, what is it?* The swipe file's warning on this format is explicit: *"it has to make sense. Do not overstretch it. If the dots don't get connected, the ad will flop."*

Here the reframe is **literally true**, which is why it's the strongest non-native in the document. And it does two jobs in one line: it reframes the category *and* it turns the reader's failed box fan from evidence against us into evidence for us. Left alone, that failed fan is the single biggest unspoken objection in the funnel.

**The creative:**

> ## This isn't a fan for the room.
> ## It's a fan for the vent.

> **Design direction:** *Split static, 4:5. Left two-thirds: a real photograph of a plain white wall register, straight on, warm domestic light. Right third: a terracotta `#B4553A` panel carrying the two lines in white Archivo bold, the word "vent" emphasised. Beneath, two short mono lines — **outcomes, not features** (Part 8.1d): "The cold room gets six degrees closer to the rest of the house." / "The space heater goes back in the cupboard." No price, no guarantee, no CTA, no badge.*

**Primary text direction:** open on the box fan they already own. *A fan moves the air that's already in the room. In winter that air is cold — moving it around makes it feel worse.* Then the pivot: this one sits in the vent and pulls. Close on the partial, honest outcome.

**Headline:** *You already tried a fan. That's not this.*

*(Feature lines "Runs when the duct is running" and "Two screws" were cut from the image — both describe what the product does, not what the room becomes.)*
**Description:** *It pulls from the duct, not the room.*
