# M4 Method (The Moonlighters): Analysis vs Our Launch

**Source:** "The M4 Method Guide | How To Run & Optimize Facebook Ads In The Andromeda Era", Sam Piliero, The Moonlighters. Last updated 2026-07-23. Read 2026-09-30 from Nate's PDF screenshot.
**Read with:** `Kill_Rules.md`, `Launch_Campaign_SA1.md`, `Ad_Format_Strategy.md`.

---

## 1. What the document is

A free lead magnet for an agency (Moonlighters) and a coaching programme ("The Blueprint"). The last third is a sales pitch. The claims (80+ brands, $70M spend tested, BarkBox, "43% ROAS increase in 90 days") are self-reported and unverifiable. The advice in the middle is mostly standard, sound 2026 Meta practice.

**Who it's written for, in its own words:** *"brands that have ads running but cannot scale profitably. Beginners and those just getting started should go HERE."* **We are the beginner case.** No pixel history, no customers, a $600 test cap and $60–80/day. Most of the document assumes $1K+/day and months of data. Read it as a map of where we go after a winner, not a launch manual.

## 2. The framework in brief

| Phase | What it says |
|---|---|
| **M1: Campaign structure** | "Swimlanes": Prospecting (100% new people, excludes warm and customers), Retention (customers), Retargeting (optional), Scale (optional, winners only). Audience segments set up in Ads Manager so reporting separates new from existing. **CBO by default**, ABO only for "high-ticket or experimental spend." Creative **packs of 4–8 ads** launched together. Broad first; single interests only, never stacked. Always the Sales objective, never Traffic |
| **Creative testing** | Winners = **high spend + at or above target ROAS**, in the top 10–15% of creatives. **Never cherry-pick low spend + high ROAS.** Decide the criteria in advance |
| **Iteration** | 80/20: 80% variations of winners, 20% new formats. Aim to beat the baseline 2–5% every week. Use AI heavily |
| **Graduation** | Winners are **duplicated, not moved**, into a Scale campaign and into "Winners" interest ad sets. Never pause a winner to tidy up |
| **M2: Andromeda creative** | Creative is the targeting. **Concept = Avatar + Template.** Every good ad has Avatar ("this is talking to me"), Education ("that's the real problem"), Mechanism (how it works) and Offer. Scale by stacking: same avatar + new template, new avatar + a proven template |
| **Creative flywheel** | Launch, then **leave alone 7–14 days** (under $1K/day spend, wait 14), analyse against the KPI with incremental attribution, iterate winners, and feed in competitor concepts from the Ad Library |
| **M3: Fastest Horse** | Breakdowns by day of week, time of day, placement, platform, age, gender, age×gender, landing page and country. Shift budget toward the winners. Chase 3–5% per test |
| **M4: Cost caps** | Advanced. Only after about 90 days of stability. Using them earlier "will break" |

## 3. Where we already match it

- **Concept = Avatar + Template.** This is our system: batch = sub-avatar + angle, and formats come from the swipe templates. B1 and B2 are "same avatar, new template" stacking, exactly as the guide describes.
- **Education + Mechanism + Avatar + Offer.** Our natives and B2C3 carry all four. The "Oh... THAT'S the real problem" beat is our duct-run and hallway-thermostat reveal.
- **Creative packs.** Our ad sets hold 3–4 ads each, one concept per pack. The guide's diagram shows packs with 3–6 ads per concept. We're in range.
- **Sales objective, broad targeting.** Purchase optimisation, broad, US. ✅
- **Steal like an artist.** The swipe-file templates are this.
- **Graduation logic.** Our scale rule already says don't touch what's working.

## 4. Where it conflicts with our plan, and what to do

### 4a. CBO vs our ABO: **keep ABO for the test**
The guide says CBO by default and calls ABO a lack of trust in the algorithm. Its own exception is **"experimental spend,"** and our $600 validation phase is exactly that. Under CBO, Meta would push most of the budget into whichever angle gets early cheap clicks, and the other angles would never get a fair read. That's the failure this account already saw on tallow. **Switch to CBO when we graduate winners into a Scale campaign**, which is where the guide applies it.

### 4b. "Wait 14 days" vs our kill rules: **our rules win for the product decision**
At under $1K/day the guide waits 14 days before judging creative. At our ~$60–80/day, 14 days is $840–1,120, above our $600 cap. The two answer different questions:
- **Our kill rules decide whether the product is viable.** $300 with no purchase intent = kill the product. That's a product test, and it shouldn't wait.
- **The guide's 14-day wait is for picking between creatives.** It applies once we know the product sells.

**Worth adopting:** don't kill individual ads inside an ad set before about 7 days unless they're clearly broken. Meta needs time to spread spend. Our "no kills before day 4" is the floor; for ad-level decisions, lean toward day 7.

### 4c. Winner definition: **add a spend condition to our scale rule**
Our scale rule is ">2× ROAS". The guide's point is that a 15× ROAS on $20 is noise. **Update:** an ad qualifies to scale only at **>2× ROAS on at least ~$150 spent**, and it has to be among the top spenders in its ad set. That matches our $150 evaluation window.

### 4d. Swimlanes and exclusions: **set up now, costs nothing**
Before launch:
- **Audience segments** (Ads Manager → Advertising settings → Audience segments): define engaged audiences and existing customers, so the "new vs existing" breakdown works from the first purchase.
- **Prospecting excludes purchasers.** We have none yet, but add the purchaser custom audience as an exclusion now so it fills correctly.
- **No separate Retargeting or Retention campaign during the test.** The guide marks retargeting optional, and at $600 it would steal budget from the only question that matters. Our BOF formats (Free Ad, Classy Offer) wait until there's a pool of visitors worth retargeting, roughly 1,000+ site visitors.

### 4e. M3 breakdowns: **mostly not yet**
Day-of-week and age×gender splits need hundreds of purchases to mean anything. Their examples run on 1,495 purchases over $55K. We'll have 5–15. Reading breakdowns at our volume is reading noise. **The exceptions:**
- **Placement:** already acted on (the natives exclude the right-hand column). Check early whether Reels or Stories are eating spend without clicks.
- **Landing page:** only one page, so nothing to compare yet.

**Don't exclude age groups early.** The guide's gender×age example is dominated by women 55+ and 65+. Home-comfort products skew older, so broad should be left to find them.

### 4f. Cost caps: **not for us**
The guide says 90 days of stability first. Revisit only once a Scale campaign is running profitably.

### 4g. Frequency: **add to the watch list**
The guide recommends 2–4× over 7 days. At our spend frequency will stay low. If a single ad set's 7-day frequency passes ~4 before $300, the audience or creative is saturating and CPMs will rise.

## 5. What to take into the next phase (after a winner)

1. **Graduate by duplicating.** Copy winners into a CBO Scale campaign. Keep them running in the test ad set too.
2. **"Winners" interest ad sets.** Test the winning creatives against single interests (e.g. home improvement, HVAC, home office), never stacked. The creative is held constant, so the interest is the only variable.
3. **80/20 iteration.** 80% of new ads are variations of the winner: headline, hook line, image, colour. 20% are new templates from the swipe file.
4. **Weekly 2–5% goal.** Judge each new pack against the current winner, not against zero.
5. **Then the M3 breakdowns,** once purchases are in the hundreds.

## 6. What to be sceptical of

- **Results claims:** "43% ROAS increase across all clients in 90 days", "90K to 900K in under 40 days" and similar are marketing, with no methodology or controls. The BarkBox story was 2019–2021, before Andromeda.
- **"99% of advertisers…" and "the smartest human on the planet"** are rhetoric. The ABO/CBO point is a reasonable default for scaled accounts, not a law.
- **"Incremental attribution"** is mentioned once and never explained. For us, check the first real order in Events Manager against Shopify, as `Offer_Structure.md` already says.
- **Survivorship:** the case studies are brands that already had product-market fit. The guide says nothing about how to tell whether a product sells at all, which is the only question we're answering right now.
