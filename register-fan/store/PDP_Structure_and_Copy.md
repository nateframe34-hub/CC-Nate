## The three decisions this page has to win

Not "communicate benefits." A visitor arriving from a native ad has three specific questions, and the page exists to answer them in this order:

1. **Is this actually my problem?** (They think their AC is broken, or that it's their fault.)
2. **Will it fit my vent?** (The highest-friction step, and the top unspoken objection on any fitment product.)
3. **Why not the $22 one on Amazon?** (The structural risk that killed the last brand.)

Anything on the page that doesn't serve one of those three is decoration.

---

## Lessons carried from the tallow PDP

- **Drop-off clusters at the first section that names doubts.** The tallow "Sound familiar?" block was where scrolling visitors left. Handle objections *positively* — a collapsible titled "Why Beef Tallow" outperforms one titled "Does it smell like beef?". Same information, no doubt planted for the people who don't open it.
- **Collapsibles between the mechanism and the buy box** absorb objections without costing scroll depth.
- **The sticky ATC must return them to the buy box**, not restate the guarantee.
- **Cross out the compare-at price properly** or the bundle math reads as a mistake.
- **Real branding stays in imagery.** Never strip labels or brand marks from real props.

## 3. The permission line

> ### If one room in your house is always hotter than the rest — it isn't you, and it isn't your AC.
>
> You've probably already tried the obvious things. Blackout curtains. A box fan. Closing the vents in the rooms that are already fine. Turning the thermostat down two more degrees and cooling the entire house to fix one bedroom.
>
> None of it worked, and somewhere in there you started wondering whether you were doing something wrong.
>
> You aren't. The cold air is being made. It just isn't reaching that room.

**Why this leads.** The SA1 research found a belief nobody had named: *"I'm on my 5th house where this has been the case. I feel like it has to be something I'm doing."* They blame themselves. Removing that blame is the same permission-shift beat that made B17C1 the account's best ad. It also opens with two angles the visitor already believes (A: the AC isn't broken, the air isn't arriving; C: you shouldn't have to cool the whole house for one room), which is the correct place to start with a low-awareness buyer.

**No product mentioned yet.** This section's only job is recognition.

## 6. Objection collapsibles

Positively titled. No doubt named in any header.

- **How it works**
- **Will it fit my vent?**
- **How loud is it?**
- **What the modes do**
- **Installation**

**How loud is it** is the one that must not oversell. Noise is the top incumbent complaint after installation. Say it runs quieter than the box fan they're currently using and give the sleep mode as the answer. Do not claim silence.

**What the modes do** exists because the remote and display are the new return risk — a buyer who can't find the right mode concludes the unit is broken.

## 7. Why one room is hotter than the rest

> ### The air is being made. It's just not getting there.
>
> Your AC cools air at one place — the unit — and pushes it through ducts to every room. The rooms nearest the unit get it first, at full pressure. The rooms at the end of the run get whatever's left after every turn, every branch, and every foot of duct has taken its cut.
>
> If your problem room is above a garage, over an addition, at the end of a hallway, or on the top floor, it's almost certainly last on that run.
>
> Put your hand on that vent while the AC is running. Air is probably coming out. It's just barely moving — a trickle instead of a push. That trickle is the entire problem. The room doesn't have a temperature problem. It has a delivery problem.
>
> **A register booster fan sits in that vent and pulls. Instead of waiting for air the duct can barely push, it draws the cold air the rest of the way in.**

**Why this section matters most.** The research read this as a **Stage 2 market** — only one mechanism claim exists in the category ("improves airflow") and nobody explains *why* a room is hot. Being the first to explain the mechanism is the whole opportunity. A Stage 2 market is won by the clearest explanation, not the loudest claim.

**Compliance:** temperature and airflow only. No health, safety or risk language anywhere, including the nursery use case.

## 8. Why the fan you already tried didn't work

> ### A box fan moves the air that's already in the room.
>
> That's why it didn't fix it. The air in that room is warm. A fan pushes warm air around and makes it feel slightly better for as long as it's pointed at you, and the room stays exactly the same temperature.
>
> This isn't a fan for the room. It's a fan for the vent. It doesn't move the air you have — it brings in the air you're already paying to cool.

**Why it's here and not framed as an angle.** Angle B contradicts what the visitor currently believes, so it can't lead. But nearly every SA1 buyer has already tried a fan and concluded fans don't work. Left unaddressed, that experience reads as evidence against this product. Placed immediately after the mechanism, it converts their failed attempt into proof the mechanism is right.

## 10. Two comparisons

> ### What the alternatives cost
>
> **Having a contractor look at it:** a diagnostic visit, then a quote. Rebalancing a duct run or adding a return is commonly quoted in the thousands. It may well be the right permanent fix. It is not a thing you do this week, in a room that's hot today.
>
> **Cooling the whole house harder:** turning the thermostat down two degrees to fix one room means paying to over-cool every room that was already fine, every hour the system runs.
>
> **The $20 vent fan:** it has no thermostat and no remote. It runs whenever it's plugged in, whether or not the duct is blowing, which means it runs at night and it runs when the system is off.
>
> **This one has its own temperature setting.** You tell that room what temperature you want it to be. It runs when the duct is actually delivering and stops when it isn't. It's the closest thing to giving one room its own thermostat without opening a wall.

**This section carries the price-check answer**, and it has to be specification-based rather than a brand story. That is the lesson from the tallow post-mortem: a cheaper identical product one search away is fatal, so the product must not be identical. The thermostat and remote are what make it a different tier, so they belong in the comparison, not in a features list.

## 11. Guarantee

> **60 days. If your room doesn't change, send it back.**
>
> Install it, run it through a hot stretch, and see whether that room finally matches the rest of the house. If it doesn't, email us and we'll refund you.
>
> Return shipping is on us if the unit is faulty. Otherwise it's on you — we'd rather tell you that here than surprise you later.

### The qualification — keep it, don't soften it

> **When this won't help.** This works if air comes out of your vent but weakly, or if the room is at the end of a long duct run. If **no** air comes out of that vent at all, a booster fan won't help — that's a blocked or disconnected duct, and no fan can pull air that isn't arriving. Check before you buy: hold your hand to the vent with the system running.

Costs some conversions, saves more returns than it costs, and does credibility work nothing else on the page can — nobody selling a gimmick tells you when it won't work.
