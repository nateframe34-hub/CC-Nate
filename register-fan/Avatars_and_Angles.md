## Product & Sophistication Gate

- **Functional product:** it makes one room the same temperature as the rest of the house, without touching the HVAC system, without a contractor, and without over-cooling every other room.
- **Mechanism:** the vent itself pulls. A duct at the end of a long run can only push a trickle into the last room; fans mounted in the register draw that conditioned air the rest of the way in. **The fans on the vent are the mechanism** — that is what makes it different from the passive grille it replaces.
- **Sophistication: Stage 2.** The category makes exactly one claim — "improves airflow" — and nobody explains *why* a room is hot. The market has heard a claim but has never heard a mechanism.
- **Awareness: low.** A 286-comment thread on this exact problem contained zero mentions of register booster fans. A buyer wrote: *"our realtor told me about this as I didn't even know it was a thing."*

**→ Required strategic response: New Information.** Not a bigger claim, and not a new mechanism — the mechanism is barely known, so it doesn't need replacing. At Stage 2 with low awareness, the winner is **the clearest explanation of why the problem exists.** Every angle below carries an explanation, not an assertion.

**Sophistication gap check:** the product can credibly deliver on temperature and on avoiding a contractor. It **cannot** credibly deliver on air quality, filtration, allergens, or safety. No angle is built on those. (See the compliance note under SA2.)

**On "Air flow" as a sub-avatar — the same call you made about angle 4.**

Airflow is the mechanism, not a desire. Nobody wants airflow; they want the room to be cooler, or to stop smelling closed-up, or to sleep. If we build a sub-avatar on airflow we will write ads that sell a specification, and the research contains no one asking for airflow in their own words. **It stays where it belongs — in the mechanism explanation, as the reason the temperature angles work.** The genuine desire hiding under it is "the room feels stale," which is SA5, and it needs research before it earns an ad.

## Core Avatar

**Desire:** *"I want this one room to be the same temperature as the rest of the house."*

**Why this one:** highest on all three dimensions. It is lived daily, it renews every season without any action from us, and it is broad — every housing stock in North America produces rooms at the end of a duct run. Every other desire in the map is either a narrowing of this one or is currently unevidenced.

**Core desire underneath:** Comfort, with Control as a strong secondary (the recurring theme is not just heat, it is *not being able to do anything about it*).

## Sub-Avatars (ranked)

### SA1 — The Room at the End of the Run ⭐ STRONGEST

- **Desire:** I want this one room to be the same temperature as the rest of the house.
- **Experience:** One room runs hot (or cold) while the rest of the house is fine. They have already tried the obvious fixes — blackout curtains, a box fan, closing the vents in other rooms, dropping the thermostat two degrees. Air *does* come out of the vent; it is just barely moving.
- **Emotion:** Frustration → underneath it, **anger** at a system they pay for that doesn't work, and **fear** in its low-grade form: that the fix is structural, expensive, and permanent.
- **Behavior:** Repeatedly re-attempting failed solutions. Buying another fan. Adjusting a thermostat that changes nothing in that room. Closing and reopening other rooms' vents. Checking the vent with their hand.
- **Demographic:** none needed.
- **Verbatim:**
  - *"I'm on my 5th house/apartment where this has been the case. I feel like it has to be something I'm doing."*
  - *"Saved us from reventing parts of our house"*
  - *"Amazing temp went from 73 to 69."*
  - *"our realtor told me about this as I didn't even know it was a thing"*
- **Evidence strength:** strongest in the set. Multiple independent forum sources, corroborated by Amazon outcomes, plus founder and friend.

#### Angles

**Angle 1: It isn't you, and it isn't broken — the air is being made, it just isn't arriving.** ⭐ LEAD
Reason to buy: their AC is fine and they are not doing anything wrong, so the fix is not a repair or a habit change — it is delivery, at the one vent that is starving.
Hooks:
1. Your AC isn't broken. The cold air just isn't reaching that room.
2. One room hotter than the rest? It isn't you.
3. Put your hand on that vent. Air is coming out — it's just barely moving.

**Angle 2: You shouldn't have to cool the whole house to fix one room.**
Reason to buy: stop paying to over-cool every room that was already fine.
Hooks:
1. Stop cooling seven rooms to fix one.
2. Two degrees colder everywhere, for the one room that's still hot.
3. Give that room its own temperature instead.

**Angle 3: You don't need a contractor for this.**
Reason to buy: the permanent fix is a quote in the thousands and a person in your house; this is two screws and an outlet, today.
Hooks:
1. Two screws and an outlet. Not a $4,500 quote.
2. Fix it this afternoon, without anyone in your house.
3. You were quoted thousands to re-duct one room.

**Pre-empt, not an angle: "a fan only moves the air already there."** They have tried a box fan and concluded fans don't work. Unaddressed, that experience is evidence *against* us. It belongs immediately after the mechanism, where it converts into proof. It is not a reason to buy on its own.

### SA2 — Sleeping in the Hot Room

- **Desire:** I want to sleep without lying there hot.
- **Experience:** The problem room is a bedroom. It is fine at bedtime and wrong by 2am, or it never cools down at all. Fans get pointed at the bed. Bedding gets thrown off.
- **Emotion:** Frustration → **anger**, with the specific edge that it is happening at the moment they are least able to tolerate it.
- **Behavior:** Running a fan all night. Sleeping with fewer covers than they want. Getting up to check the other rooms and confirming, again, that it's only this one.
- **Verbatim:** *"Her room is no longer a sauna"* · *"Husband used to complain all the time his room was to hot. Now he says it's to cold. lol"*
- **Evidence strength:** Good — **nearly every piece of outcome language in the research names a bedroom.** That is the clearest unforced pattern in the data and it was not in the original avatar list.

This is a genuinely separate desire from SA1, not a restatement: sleep is more urgent than comfort, it recurs nightly rather than daily, and it is the version of the problem people describe on behalf of *someone else* — a partner, a child — which is a different and often stronger buying posture.

#### Angles

**Angle 1: The room is fine at bedtime and wrong by 2am.** ⭐ Strongest hook material in the set
Reason to buy: the problem is nightly and the fix runs while you sleep, on its own thermostat.
Hooks:
1. Fine at bedtime. Wrong by 2am.
2. You don't need a colder house. You need a colder bedroom.
3. The one room that never cools down is the one you sleep in.

**Angle 2: A fan pointed at the bed is not air conditioning.**
Reason to buy: they already run a fan all night; it moves warm air and the room stays the same temperature.
Hooks:
1. The fan isn't cooling the room. It's just blowing warm air at you.
2. You've been running a fan all night for two summers.
3. Cool the room, not the person.

**Angle 3: Set the bedroom's temperature and stop managing it.**
Reason to buy: the unit has its own thermostat — the room holds a number instead of needing attention.
Hooks:
1. Tell that room what temperature to be.
2. Set it once. Stop getting up to adjust things.
3. The closest thing to a second thermostat, without opening a wall.

> ⚠️ **Compliance — the nursery trigger.** The most intense version of SA2 is a baby's room, and its intensity comes from safety. That is the trap named in `tallow-cream/POST_MORTEM.md`: the most urgent version of the problem is the least compliant. **Allowed:** room temperature, degrees, the monitor reading, the baby waking, sleeping through, not having to get up and check. **Banned outright:** SIDS, safe sleep, risk, danger, overheating-as-hazard, anything health-framed. Sell the temperature and the not-worrying. Never the risk. The founder's "new parents" idea belongs here, under these rules — it is a **door into SA2**, not an avatar of its own.

### SA3 — The Thermostat Standoff ⚠️ HYPOTHESIS, NOT YET EVIDENCED

- **Desire:** I want to stop arguing about the thermostat.
- **Core desire:** Relationships.
- **Experience (inferred):** Two people in one house who want different temperatures, in rooms that already run different temperatures.
- **Evidence strength:** **None in the current research.** Not one thermostat-conflict quote surfaced in the SA1 mining or the reviews. The one adjacent data point cuts *against* framing it as conflict — *"Husband used to complain all the time his room was to hot. Now he says it's to cold. lol"* is affectionate, not a standoff.

**Assessment:** a good instinct with a real cultural foothold — thermostat conflict is a recognised trope, which means the *relatability* is pre-installed and a hook can land without setup. But it is currently a trope we recognise, not a desire we found. Two cautions:

1. **The product only wins this argument in a specific configuration** — where the people are in *different rooms*. It does nothing for two people in the same room wanting different temperatures, which is the version of the trope everyone actually pictures. Sold carelessly, it promises something the product cannot deliver.
2. It competes with SA1 and SA2 for the same buyer while being weaker on urgency.

**Recommendation:** do not build a batch on it. It is a strong *concept* to ride inside an SA1 or SA2 ad — one line, not a premise. If it earns attention there, research it properly then.

### SA4 — The System You're Not Allowed To Touch ⚠️ PARTIALLY EVIDENCED

- **Desire:** I want control over my room's temperature when I have no control over the building's.
- **Experience:** Renters, apartments, dorms, offices, basement suites — the thermostat is somewhere else, or belongs to somebody else, and the room is at the mercy of it.
- **Emotion:** Frustration → **anger** at powerlessness. This is the purest Control avatar in the set.
- **Behavior:** Asking a landlord, super, RA or facilities and being told nothing can be done. Buying portable units. Working around it.
- **Verbatim (partial):** *"I'm on my 5th house/**apartment**…"* — the only direct trace.
- **Evidence strength:** thin but not absent. The desire is coherent and the powerlessness is the strongest emotion available in the whole map.

**The disqualifier to check before spending on it:** this product **replaces a fixture and needs an outlet.** Renters can do that and undo it; many dorms cannot, and offices generally cannot. Before writing a dorm-room ad, confirm the unit is genuinely reversible and that the audience can install it — otherwise we are selling to people who cannot buy. Dorm is also seasonally narrow (an August-September window) and skews to a low-budget buyer at a $99.94 checkout.

**Recommendation:** hold. **Renters and basement suites are the viable slice**, not dorms. Test after SA1/SA2 have produced a winner, and only with the reversibility confirmed and an "it goes back exactly how you found it" beat in the copy.

### SA5 — The Room That Feels Closed-Up ⚠️ NEEDS RESEARCH BEFORE USE

- **Desire (candidate):** I want this room to stop feeling stuffy and stale.
- **Evidence strength:** **none yet.** This is the honest version of the founder's "air flow" sub-avatar — the desire underneath the mechanism.

This may well be real; stale-room language is common in home forums. But we have not gone and found it, and the product's ability to deliver on it is **unverified** — moving more conditioned air through a room plausibly helps it feel less closed-up, but we have no customer saying so.

**Explicitly excluded from this avatar:** allergies, air quality, filtration, dust. The unit has no filter. Those are claims the product cannot deliver, and building on a desire the product can't live up to is the documented cause of "the desire is clearly there but the ads don't work."

**Recommendation:** one focused research pass — mine "stuffy room," "stale air one room," "room feels closed up" in home and apartment forums — before it gets an ad. It is the natural home for the founder's home-office idea if the desire validates.

## Testing Priority

1. **SA1, Angle 1 — "It isn't you, and it isn't broken."** Strongest evidence, broadest scope, and it carries the New Information response the Stage 2 market requires. It also opens on the permission shift, which is the beat that made B17C1 the account's best-performing ad.
2. **SA2, Angle 1 — "Fine at bedtime. Wrong by 2am."** The sharpest single line in the set, on the most urgent version of the desire. This is the batch's second cell, not a later batch.
3. Everything else waits for a winner.

**You do not need one ad per angle.** A single native concept can carry all three SA1 angles in sequence — the permission shift opens, the whole-house waste sits mid-body, and the no-contractor beat closes — using the strongest hook as the entry. That is exactly how B17C1 carried three angles in one ad. Test *concepts* against each other, not angles.
