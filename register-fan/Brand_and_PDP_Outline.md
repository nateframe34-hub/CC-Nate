# PART A — BRAND

## A1. Brand name — recommend **EVENROOM**

Three candidates, judged on: does it name the outcome, is it free of category jargon, does it avoid the incumbents' search term, does it survive being said out loud.

| Name | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **EVENROOM** ⭐ | Names the **outcome**, not the hardware. "Even" is literally the desire — every quote in the research is a comparison between rooms. One word, spells itself, no HVAC jargon, no overlap with "register booster fan" | Slightly soft |
| **ROOMTEMP** | Uses the customer's own phrase. Plain and honest | Generic enough to be hard to own; weak trademark |
| **VESTA** | Roman goddess of the hearth — warm, domestic, premium | Almost certainly taken in the home category. Says nothing about the problem |

**Recommendation: Evenroom.** It passes the test that matters most — it describes the thing they want (*this room, even with the others*) without teaching them the category name that leads to a $59.99 Amazon listing.

**⚠️ Before committing:** check .com availability, USPTO, and Meta/Instagram handles. I can't verify any of those.

## A2. Product name — **The Vent Thermostat**

**Not "register booster fan."** That is the incumbents' Amazon search term and their SEO. Naming our product with it hands an unaware reader the vocabulary to leave (§9a-i).

**"Vent Thermostat"** is the better name for three reasons:

1. **It's accurate.** You set a target temperature for a room; the unit runs when the duct is delivering and stops when it isn't. That *is* a thermostat for one vent.
2. **It carries the category reframe for free.** Our strongest non-native ad is *"This isn't a fan for the room. It's a fan for the vent."* The product name completes that argument.
3. **It reframes the price.** A $99 "fan" is expensive. A $99 "thermostat for a room that never had one" is cheap next to a $3,400 duct quote.

Full: **Evenroom Vent Thermostat**. Variants: *4×10* and *6×10*.

## A3. Colours

The category is white plastic and blue-lit tech. The design study's headline finding is **commit to ONE accent and thread it everywhere** — that is what separates the top-scoring pages from "a clean Shopify template with content dropped in."

| Role | Colour | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** | Warm off-white `#FAF7F2` | Domestic, not clinical. Makes thermometer numbers pop |
| **Accent (the brand colour)** | **Deep clay / terracotta `#B4553A`** | Every CTA, every checkmark, every section band, every hover |
| **Text** | Near-black `#1C1A17` | Warm black, not pure black |
| **Diagram cool** | Muted slate `#5B7185` | **Used ONLY inside diagrams**, never as brand colour |
| **Diagram warm** | The accent | Same |

**Why terracotta and not blue or red.** This product sells in both seasons — cooling in summer, heating in winter. **A blue brand reads as cooling-only; a red brand reads as heating-only.** Terracotta is temperature-neutral, reads architectural and domestic rather than technological, and appears nowhere in the category.

**The system detail worth having:** warm and cool appear *only* inside diagrams, to show the temperature difference. The brand itself stays warm. That way the colour system is doing explanatory work, not decoration.

## A4. Type

| Role | Face | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Headers | **Archivo** (or Instrument Sans) | Sturdy, confident, architectural. Not apothecary-serif — we're documentary, not artisanal |
| Body | **Inter** | Neutral, readable at length |
| **Every temperature** | **JetBrains Mono / Roboto Mono, tabular figures** | ⭐ The identity detail. **Every degree reading on the site renders in mono** — 68° / 61°. Costs nothing, instantly distinctive, and congruent with the ads |

The mono-degrees rule is the cheapest brand asset available. It makes measurement the brand's visual language, which is exactly what the research says these people speak.

## A5. ★ The congruence unlock

The design study's most important strategic point: **the PDP must be congruent with the world the click came from.** For tallow that meant putting the whiteboard diagrams on the page. Here it's cleaner:

> **Our ads are thermometers and a duct diagram. Those same two images become the PDP's design language.**

The two-thermometer image from the ad appears full-bleed below the hero. The duct-run diagram from the ad becomes the mechanism section, redrawn. The visit reads as one continuous explanation rather than an ad and then a shop.

# PART B — THE PDP

## B0. ⭐ The PDP's job — and it is NOT the ad's job

**Founder, 2026-09-24.**

| | Job |
|---|---|
| **The ads** | **Channel the desire.** Move them up the awareness ladder far enough to click. They do not answer objections |
| **The PDP** | **Inform, answer every objection, and CLOSE** — with enough urgency and value that comparison shopping never starts |

**They arrive already wanting it.** The ad did that. The page's task is to remove every reason not to buy, and then make buying *today* feel obviously right.

### What "make them feel they're getting an amazing deal" can honestly mean here

⚠️ **We cannot win a price comparison.** A $59.99 branded unit with an app exists. If the reader's mental benchmark becomes "other vent fans," we lose, and no copy fixes that.

**So the deal feeling has to be anchored against the comparisons they actually arrived with** — which are the ones the ads used, and the ones in their head already:

| Anchor | The deal |
|---|---|
| **The contractor quote** | $3,400 and three days, versus $89 and an afternoon |
| **Another winter of it** | Three winters of a heater running nightly costs more than this does |
| **The 2-pack** | **$80 a room.** The strongest genuine value frame we own |
| **The guarantee** | 60 days makes "now" safe — risk removal is what converts urgency into action instead of anxiety |

**Congruence matters here too:** the ad anchored against the quote, so the page must continue that anchor rather than introducing a new one. A page that switches comparisons re-opens the question the ad closed.

### Where urgency lives on the page

**Seasonal, and it's real** — it gets colder every week, and the problem they've tolerated is about to be nightly. No countdown timers, no fake stock counters. The calendar is doing the work.

Three placements, escalating:
1. **Hero subline** — light. *"Before it's properly cold."*
2. **After the alternatives section** — the cost of another winter, stated plainly.
3. **Final CTA** — the strongest statement of it, paired with the guarantee so it reads as *safe to act now*, not *hurry up*.

**Not urgency:** anything invented. We are drop-shipping; a false scarcity claim on a first campaign risks the account for a few points of conversion.

## B1. Three constraints that shape the whole page

**1. 🚨 We have ZERO reviews, and the swipe says every high-converting PDP shows them early.** This is the page's biggest structural weakness and it cannot be designed around. What substitutes, in order of strength: the **mechanism explanation** (nobody else offers it), the **honest "when this won't help"** section, and the **guarantee**. Do not fake it, do not use stock testimonials, and **add a reviews module the week the first real review exists.**

**2. Toups & Co has the shortest page and the highest conversion (5.9%).** Length is not the lever. Every section below has to earn its place.

**3. The page must not make the reader Solution Aware.** No competitor named, no "register booster fan," no cheaper-alternative comparison. The only alternatives this page acknowledges are the contractor quote, the thermostat, the space heater, and doing nothing.

## B2. Section order

| # | Section | Why it exists | Why it's HERE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Announcement bar** — *Ships from the US · 60-day returns* | Two trust facts we can prove | Above everything. Costs one line, answers "is this a real company" before anything else |
| 2 | **Hero** — gallery, name, recognition subtitle, price, size step, bundle, ATC | The buy box | Top, because a reader already sold by the ad shouldn't have to scroll. **The subtitle does the recognition work for everyone else** |
| 3 | **Congruence band** — the two-thermometer image, full bleed | Confirms they're in the right place | **First thing below the fold.** It is literally the image they clicked. The swipe study's rule: alternate airy product sections with full-bleed bands |
| 4 | **"Why one room never keeps up"** — the duct diagram, redrawn | ⭐ **New Information — our only real differentiator.** Nobody in the category explains why | **Early, because it is the conviction engine.** Everything after it is easier once they understand the cause |
| 5 | **"Why the fan you already tried didn't work"** | Pre-empts the objection nearly every buyer holds | **Immediately after the mechanism.** Placed there it converts their failed box fan into *proof* the mechanism is right. Placed anywhere else it reads as defensive |
| 6 | **The attribute grid** — WHAT IT IS / HOW IT WORKS / FITS / POWER / MODES / GUARANTEE | Nécessaire's systematic credentialing. Turns claims into structured data | After conviction, before objections. It reads as confidence, and it's scannable for the skimmer |
| 7 | **"Will it fit?"** — measure step + size selector repeated | **The highest-friction question in the funnel** | Here, not at the top, because a reader won't go and get a tape measure until they want the thing. But **before** the deeper objections, because an unanswered fit question blocks everything downstream |
| 8 | **Install — three steps**, real photos | Kills "is this a project?" | Straight after fit. Fit and install are the same anxiety and should be resolved together |
| 9 | **"When this won't help"** — the blocked-duct qualification | Prevents the returns that killed the incumbent's reviews, and does credibility work nothing else can | Late enough not to scare off early, early enough to stop a bad purchase. **Nobody selling a gimmick tells you when it won't work** |
| 10 | **What the alternatives cost** — the quote, the thermostat, the space heater, nothing | Value justification | **After conviction, never before.** Price comparison before belief just reads as expensive |
| 10b | **The cost of another winter** ⭐ NEW | **The urgency beat.** Converts "good idea" into "this year, not next" | Straight after the alternatives, where the value frame is already open. This is where the page does the job the ad deliberately left it |
| 11 | **Guarantee** — 60 days, stated plainly including who pays return shipping | Last objection — and **it is what makes acting NOW feel safe rather than rushed** | Directly before the close. Urgency without risk-removal produces anxiety, not orders |
| 12 | **FAQ** | Long-tail objections without spending scroll | Bottom. Anyone reading this far is working to convince themselves |
| 13 | **Final CTA** — size step + bundle repeated in full | Nobody should scroll back up | End |
| — | **Sticky ATC** | Returns to the buy box — **not** a guarantee restatement | Persistent, mobile |

## B3. Section detail

### 2 — Hero

- **Name:** Evenroom Vent Thermostat
- **Subtitle (the recognition line):** *For the one room that never keeps up with the rest of the house.*
- **Price:** ~~$119.99~~ **$89.99** · 2-pack $159.99, free shipping
- **Size step and bundle live in the hero**, not further down — they are the two purchase decisions and splitting them across the page costs conversions.
- Gallery: real photography only. Product, the display close up, the remote, installed in a wall register, a hand for scale.

### 3 — Congruence band

Full-bleed. Two thermometers, hallway and bedroom, several degrees apart, mono figures. One line:

> **Same house. Same afternoon.**

No product. No claim. It exists to say *yes, this page is about your problem.*

### 4 — Why one room never keeps up

> Your furnace heats air in one place and pushes it through ducts to every room. The rooms nearest it get full pressure. The rooms at the end of the run get whatever's left after every turn and every branch has taken its cut.
>
> Put your hand on that vent with the heat running. Air is probably coming out — it's just barely moving. That trickle is the whole problem. **The room doesn't have a temperature problem. It has a delivery problem.**

Diagram beneath, redrawn from the ad. **This section gets the most words on the page** because it is the one thing no competitor offers.

### 5 — Why the fan you already tried didn't work

> A box fan moves the air that's already in the room. That air is cold. Moving it around makes it feel worse, not better.
>
> **This isn't a fan for the room. It's a fan for the vent.** It doesn't move the air you have — it pulls in the air you're already paying to heat.

### 9 — When this won't help

> This works if air comes out of your vent but weakly, or if the room sits at the end of a long duct run.
>
> **If no air at all comes out of that vent, this won't help.** That's a blocked or disconnected duct, and no fan can pull air that isn't arriving. Check before you buy: hold your hand to the vent with the system running.

Keep it. It costs some conversions and saves more returns than it costs.

### 10 — What the alternatives cost

Contractor quote (thousands, and it's not happening this week) · turning the thermostat up for the whole house · a second appliance in the room · or nothing, which is what most people do for years.

**No product comparison. No competitor. No "cheap ones."**

### 11 — Guarantee

> **60 days. If the room doesn't change, send it back.**
>
> Return shipping is on us if the unit is faulty. Otherwise it's on you — we'd rather tell you here than surprise you later.

## B4. What we are deliberately NOT doing at launch

| Swipe-file standard | Our call |
|---|---|
| Reviews above the fold | **Can't — we have none.** Add the week the first real one exists. **Mockup placeholders live in `store/PLACEHOLDER_reviews_DO_NOT_PUBLISH.md` — design only, every entry marked `[PH]`, grep for it before any deploy** |
| UGC / real faces | None yet |
| Cross-sell "you may also like" | Single product. Revisit at two SKUs |
| Retail / press logos | None |
| Noise claim | **Never.** Not quiet, not decibels, not comparative — Part 8 |
